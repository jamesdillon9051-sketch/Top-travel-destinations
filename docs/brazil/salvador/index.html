Welcome to Salvador da Bahia

Salvador was founded in 1549 as the first capital of Portuguese Brazil, and served as the colony's capital until 1763. It became one of the greatest slave ports in the Americas, with millions of Africans brought here to work on sugar plantations, and their legacy shaped the city's culture. Today, it is the most African city in Brazil, and its music, religion, food and dance reflect that heritage.

The historic centre, the Pelourinho, is one of the finest colonial ensembles in Latin America, and UNESCO listed it in 1985. Pastel-coloured mansions and baroque churches line cobbled squares and steep lanes, with the gilded interior of the Church of São Francisco as a highlight. The city is divided by a cliff into an Upper City and a Lower City, linked by the Elevador Lacerda, an Art Deco lift from the 1930s.

Salvador is also a city of beaches, of a huge bay, of Carnival, and of candomblé. Drummers play in the squares, capoeira groups practise in the streets, and baianas in white dresses sell acarajé. It is a warm, vibrant, welcoming place, though visitors should take care in the historic centre at night.

Why visit

Why visit Salvador da Bahia?

Salvador shows the African soul of Brazil, and is one of the most culturally rich cities in the country.

  • A colonial masterpiece

    The Pelourinho has baroque churches and pastel colonial houses, with a lively atmosphere.

  • Afro-Brazilian culture

    Candomblé, capoeira, drumming and dance.

  • Bahian food

    Acarajé, moqueca, vatapá and other dishes cooked with palm oil and coconut milk.

  • Beaches and the bay

    City beaches, islands and the Bay of All Saints.

  • Carnival

    One of Brazil's largest, with electric trucks and street parties.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Salvador da Bahia

The main sights are in the Pelourinho and the Upper City, with beaches and forts along the coast.

  1. 01

    The Pelourinho

    The historic centre, with cobbled squares, pastel houses, museums and churches. The name refers to the pillory in the square where enslaved people were once whipped.

    Tip Visit in daylight, and stay with the crowds in the evening.

  2. 02

    The Church and Convent of São Francisco

    A baroque church with an interior of carved wood covered in gold leaf, and a cloister decorated with blue and white Portuguese tiles.

    Tip Photography is often restricted.

  3. 03

    Elevador Lacerda

    An Art Deco lift that carries passengers between the Upper City and Lower City, with views of the Bay of All Saints.

    Tip The ride takes about 30 seconds and costs very little.

  4. 04

    Mercado Modelo

    A handicrafts market in the Lower City, with souvenirs, Bahian art and food stalls.

    Tip Bargain, and watch your belongings.

  5. 05

    Farol da Barra

    A 17th-century fort and lighthouse at the entrance to the bay, with a museum and one of the city's best sunset spots.

    Tip Go at sunset.

  6. 06

    Bonfim Church

    A pilgrimage church known for the coloured ribbons tied to its railings for luck, the fitas do Bonfim.

    Tip Take a ribbon home.

Things to do

Best things to do in Salvador da Bahia

Salvador is about culture, food and time by the sea.

  1. Watch capoeira

    The Afro-Brazilian martial art and dance is practised in squares and academies.

    Tip Tip performers if you take photographs.

  2. Try acarajé from a baiana

    Deep-fried black-eyed pea fritters filled with shrimp and sauces, sold by women in traditional white dress.

    Tip Ask for it with everything.

  3. Listen to live music

    Percussion groups, samba de roda and pagode play in the Pelourinho and bars.

    Tip Tuesday nights are busy in the Pelourinho.

  4. Visit a candomblé house

    Afro-Brazilian religious ceremonies are open to visitors in some terreiros, with respect.

    Tip Go with a knowledgeable guide.

  5. Take a boat to the islands

    Day trips to Itaparica, Morro de São Paulo or the Bay of All Saints.

    Tip Book in advance.

  6. Relax at the beach

    City beaches such as Porto da Barra and beaches north of the centre.

    Tip Keep valuables safe.

Local food

What to eat in Salvador da Bahia

Bahian cooking is the most African cuisine in Brazil, based on palm oil (dendê), coconut milk, shrimp, peppers and coriander.

  • Acarajé

    Fritters of black-eyed peas fried in palm oil, filled with vatapá, caruru and shrimp. The baianas who sell them are recognised as cultural heritage.

    Where to try it Street stalls in the Pelourinho and Rio Vermelho.

  • Moqueca baiana

    A fish or shrimp stew cooked in coconut milk and palm oil in a clay pot.

    Where to try it Restaurants along the coast.

  • Vatapá and caruru

    Rich, creamy sauces of bread, shrimp, peanuts, palm oil and okra, eaten with rice.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants.

  • Bobó de camarão

    Shrimp in a creamy cassava purée with coconut milk.

    Where to try it Bahian restaurants.

  • Tropical fruit and cachaça

    Fresh juices from local fruit and a range of flavoured cachaças.

    Where to try it Markets and bars.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Salvador da Bahia

Stay in the Pelourinho for atmosphere, or in Barra and Rio Vermelho for beaches and restaurants.

  • Pelourinho and Santo Antônio

    Guesthouses and boutique hotels in colonial houses.

    Best for: History and atmosphere

  • Barra

    Hotels and apartments near the sea.

    Best for: Beaches and safety

  • Rio Vermelho

    A bohemian neighbourhood of bars and restaurants.

    Best for: Nightlife and food

Transport

Getting to and around Salvador da Bahia

Salvador is about 1,200 km north-east of Rio.

Getting there

  • Flight to Salvador (SSA)

    Direct flights from Rio (about 2 hours 30 minutes), São Paulo and other cities, and some from Europe and Africa.

    The airport is 30–40 minutes from the centre.

  • Bus

    Long-distance buses run from Recife, Aracaju and other cities.

    Fly for long distances.

  • From the islands

    Ferries from Itaparica and Morro de São Paulo arrive at the Lower City.

    Check schedules.

Getting around

The Upper and Lower Cities are linked by lifts and streets, and ride-hailing is best for beaches.

  • Elevador Lacerda and Plano Inclinado

    Lifts and funiculars link the Upper and Lower Cities.

    Cheap and quick.

  • Uber and app taxis

    The best way to move, especially at night.

    Use the app.

  • Walking

    The Pelourinho is walkable, with steep cobbles.

    Wear good shoes.

When to go

Best time to visit Salvador da Bahia

Salvador is tropical and warm year-round, with a rainy season from April to July.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Good

    Hot, humid and busy, with Carnival.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Quiet

    Warm, with the start of the rains.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Quiet

    Mild and rainy, with June festivals.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm, sunny and drier.

Worth planning around: Carnival week if you want quiet, and April to July for heavy rain.

Travel tips

Salvador da Bahia travel tips

A few tips help you enjoy Salvador safely.

  • Use the Pelourinho in the daytime, and take a taxi at night.
  • Keep valuables out of sight and avoid isolated streets.
  • Use Uber or 99 rather than walking between areas.
  • Ask before photographing baianas and street performers, and tip.
  • Book Carnival accommodation months ahead.
  • Try acarajé from a licensed baiana.
  • Respect candomblé and church rules about photography.

FAQ

Salvador da Bahia: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Salvador?

Three days for the historic centre, food and beach.

Is Salvador safe?

Tourist areas are policed, but petty crime is common. Use apps at night, and keep valuables hidden.

What is the best time to visit?

September to March. April to July is rainy.

What is acarajé?

A fritter of black-eyed peas fried in palm oil and filled with shrimp and sauces.

Is Salvador better than Rio?

They are different. Salvador is more African and cultural, and Rio has more scenery.

Can I visit a candomblé ceremony?

Some houses welcome visitors. Go with a guide and be respectful.
