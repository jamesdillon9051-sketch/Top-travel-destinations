Welcome to Rio de Janeiro

Rio de Janeiro was founded by the Portuguese in 1565, and it grew rich on the gold, sugar and coffee trades. It was the capital of Portugal's empire for a time in the early 19th century, and then of Brazil until 1960, when the capital moved to Brasília. It is still the country's cultural heart, and the world's image of Brazil.

Its setting is extraordinary. Steep granite mountains, cloaked in rainforest, rise directly from the sea, dividing the city into neighbourhoods separated by tunnels and beaches. The great sights are among the most photographed anywhere: the statue of Christ the Redeemer on Corcovado, the cable car to Sugarloaf Mountain, and the crescent of Copacabana. UNESCO inscribed the city's landscapes 'between the mountain and the sea' in 2012.

Rio is also a city of contrasts, with rich neighbourhoods and favelas built on the hillsides, and crime is a real concern. But its culture, from samba and bossa nova to football and Carnival, is irresistible, and its people, the cariocas, are famously friendly and relaxed.

Why visit

Why visit Rio de Janeiro?

Rio is one of the most beautiful and lively cities in the world, and the first stop for most visitors to Brazil.

  • Spectacular scenery

    Mountains, beaches and a rainforest within the city limits.

  • Iconic sights

    Christ the Redeemer and Sugarloaf are among the world's great views.

  • Beach life

    Copacabana and Ipanema are social spaces, with sport, drinks and people-watching.

  • Music and nightlife

    Samba in Lapa, bossa nova bars, and street parties throughout the year.

  • Football and Carnival

    Maracanã stadium and the samba schools bring the city's passions to life.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Rio de Janeiro

Rio's sights are spread out, so group them by area and plan around the weather.

  1. 01

    Christ the Redeemer (Cristo Redentor)

    A 30-metre Art Deco statue on the summit of Corcovado, 710 metres above the city, completed in 1931. Reach it by the historic rack railway through the Tijuca forest, or by van.

    Tip Go early, and check the cloud cover first.

  2. 02

    Sugarloaf Mountain (Pão de Açúcar)

    A 396-metre granite peak at the mouth of Guanabara Bay, reached by two cable-car stages. The views at sunset, with the city lit up, are famous.

    Tip Go about an hour before sunset.

  3. 03

    Copacabana and Ipanema

    Two of the world's most famous beaches. Copacabana is a long, lively curve of sand and mosaic pavement, and Ipanema is more elegant, with Arpoador Rock at its end, a favourite sunset spot.

    Tip Keep only what you need on the beach, and use kiosks for drinks.

  4. 04

    Selarón Steps and Lapa

    A staircase covered in tiles from around the world, created by the Chilean-born artist Jorge Selarón, beside the Lapa Arches, the centre of the city's music and nightlife.

    Tip Visit the steps in the morning, and Lapa at night with a group.

  5. 05

    Santa Teresa

    A hilltop neighbourhood of old mansions, artists' studios and cafés, reached by a historic yellow tram or by taxi.

    Tip Go by app taxi, and walk in daylight.

  6. 06

    Tijuca National Park

    One of the world's largest urban rainforests, with waterfalls, viewpoints and trails, replanted in the 19th century after coffee planting.

    Tip Hire a guide or join a tour.

  7. 07

    Maracanã Stadium

    The legendary stadium that hosted the 1950 and 2014 World Cup finals, and is the home of several football clubs. Tours run on non-match days.

    Tip Watch a match if you can.

Things to do

Best things to do in Rio de Janeiro

Rio is best enjoyed by mixing sights, beaches and evenings out.

  1. Watch the sunset at Arpoador

    Locals applaud as the sun sets behind the Two Brothers mountains.

    Tip Arrive early for a spot.

  2. Hike to a viewpoint

    Trails lead up to Morro Dois Irmãos and Pedra da Gávea.

    Tip Go with a guide, in daylight.

  3. Go to a samba night in Lapa

    Live samba and choro in bars and clubs.

    Tip Go with a group, and use ride-hailing apps.

  4. See a football match

    A match at the Maracanã is a memorable experience of drums, songs and flags.

    Tip Book through a reputable tour.

  5. Visit the Botanical Garden

    A green retreat with palms, orchids and monkeys.

    Tip Go in the morning.

  6. Try a favela tour with a local guide

    A responsible guided walk by a resident shows the community, its culture and challenges.

    Tip Choose a community-led operator.

Local food

What to eat in Rio de Janeiro

Rio's food is casual and beach-centred, with local specialities such as feijoada and street snacks.

  • Feijoada

    Black bean stew with pork, served with rice and collard greens, traditionally on Saturdays.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants in Lapa and Santa Teresa.

  • Beach snacks

    Grilled cheese (queijo coalho), shrimp skewers, fresh coconut water and mate, sold by vendors.

    Where to try it Copacabana and Ipanema.

  • Pastel and coxinha

    Fried pastries and chicken croquettes, sold in botecos and bakeries.

    Where to try it Corner bars.

  • Caipirinha

    Cachaça, lime and sugar, the national cocktail.

    Where to try it Beach kiosks and bars.

  • Açaí

    Cold, thick purple bowls with granola and banana.

    Where to try it Juice bars across the city.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Rio de Janeiro

Choose Ipanema or Copacabana for the beach, Santa Teresa for atmosphere, or Botafogo for value.

  • Ipanema and Leblon

    Stylish hotels and apartments near the beach.

    Best for: Safety and restaurants

  • Copacabana

    Big hotels along the beachfront, more crowded.

    Best for: Convenience and value

  • Santa Teresa and Botafogo

    Guesthouses, hostels and boutique hotels.

    Best for: Atmosphere and budget

Transport

Getting to and around Rio de Janeiro

Rio is Brazil's second-largest international gateway.

Getting there

  • Flight to Galeão (GIG)

    Direct flights from Europe, North America and other South American cities.

    Use an app taxi or a pre-booked transfer.

  • Flight from São Paulo

    About 1 hour between the two cities, with many daily flights to Santos Dumont.

    The airport is central.

  • Bus

    About 6 hours from São Paulo and 4 hours from Paraty.

    Book leito seats for comfort.

Getting around

Use the metro and ride-hailing apps, and avoid walking at night.

  • Metro

    Clean and efficient, linking Copacabana, Ipanema, the centre and the north.

    Avoid rush hours.

  • Uber and 99

    The safest and easiest way to move, especially at night.

    Check the plate.

  • Walking and cycling

    Beach promenades are walkable and have cycle lanes.

    Keep valuables hidden.

When to go

Best time to visit Rio de Janeiro

Rio is hot and humid in summer, and mild and drier in winter. Carnival, in February or March, is the peak.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Good

    Hot and humid, with storms. Carnival and New Year, with high prices.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm and less humid, with lower prices.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Best time

    Mild and dry, with clear views.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm and sunny, with fewer crowds.

Worth planning around: Carnival and New Year if you want low prices and quiet.

Travel tips

Rio de Janeiro travel tips

Rio rewards awareness and common sense.

  • Use Uber or 99 rather than street taxis, especially at night.
  • Carry only what you need on the beach, and leave valuables at the hotel.
  • Keep your phone out of sight in the street.
  • Do not walk on the beaches or quiet streets at night.
  • Visit Christ the Redeemer early, and check the cloud.
  • Use a reputable guide for hikes and favela tours.
  • Book Carnival accommodation and tickets many months ahead.

FAQ

Rio de Janeiro: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Rio?

Three or four days for the main sights and beaches.

Is Rio safe?

It has serious street crime, but millions visit safely. Take precautions, use apps, stay in well-known areas and avoid isolated places.

When is Carnival?

In February or March, before Lent. The dates change each year.

What is the best time to visit Rio?

April to October is drier and cooler.

Can I swim at Copacabana?

Yes, but currents can be strong, and water quality varies. Swim near lifeguards.

Is it worth going up both Christ the Redeemer and Sugarloaf?

Yes. They give different views, and both are highlights.
