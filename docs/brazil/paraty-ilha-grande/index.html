Welcome to Paraty

Paraty, on Brazil's Costa Verde, or Green Coast, was founded in the 17th century, and it became a major port for shipping gold from Minas Gerais to Portugal along the Estrada Real. When the gold route moved to Rio, Paraty faded, and its centre was left nearly untouched, a perfectly preserved colonial town of whitewashed houses with brightly painted doors, cobbled streets and old churches. UNESCO inscribed it in 2019, together with Ilha Grande, for its culture and biodiversity.

The streets of the centre are closed to cars, and were designed with a slope and stones that allow the sea to flush them at high tide. Restaurants, galleries and pousadas fill the old houses, and the waterfront is busy with boats. The surrounding hills produce cachaça, the sugarcane spirit, and Paraty is famous for its distilleries.

Offshore, the bay is dotted with islands, and schooner trips are a highlight. Just to the east, Ilha Grande, a large island with no cars, has rainforest trails, empty beaches and a former prison. Together, they make an ideal escape from Rio and São Paulo, and are best visited from March to October, avoiding the busy summer weeks.

Why visit

Why visit Paraty?

Paraty and Ilha Grande combine colonial charm with tropical scenery, a short trip from Rio.

  • A preserved colonial centre

    Walk through streets, churches and squares that look much as they did in the 18th century.

  • Boat trips

    Schooners take you to islands and beaches in the bay.

  • Ilha Grande

    A car-free island of jungle, trails and beaches.

  • Cachaça and food

    Distilleries, seafood and Brazilian cooking.

  • Culture and festivals

    Literary festival, music and art galleries.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Paraty

Give Paraty a day, and Ilha Grande a night or two.

  1. 01

    The historic centre

    A grid of cobbled streets with whitewashed colonial houses, brightly painted doors, churches such as Santa Rita and Nossa Senhora dos Remédios, and small museums.

    Tip Explore in the morning, before the crowds and the heat.

  2. 02

    Schooner trips in Paraty Bay

    Traditional wooden boats take you to islands and beaches, with swimming, snorkelling and lunch on board.

    Tip Book a half- or full-day trip.

  3. 03

    Cachaça distilleries

    Small distilleries around Paraty produce artisanal cachaça, and offer tours and tastings.

    Tip Book a tour with tastings.

  4. 04

    Trindade

    A fishing village and beaches about 30 km south of Paraty, with a natural rock pool, Cachadaço, and a relaxed atmosphere.

    Tip Take a bus or taxi.

  5. 05

    Ilha Grande and Lopes Mendes beach

    A car-free island with a village, Abraão, and beaches including Lopes Mendes, a 3-km stretch of pale sand often ranked among the best in Brazil, reached by a trail or boat.

    Tip Spend at least a night on the island.

  6. 06

    Pico do Papagaio

    A hike on Ilha Grande to a peak with views across the island and the sea.

    Tip Go with a guide.

Things to do

Best things to do in Paraty

Paraty and Ilha Grande are for slow days by the sea and on trails.

  1. Take a schooner trip

    Swim, snorkel and enjoy a seafood lunch on board.

    Tip Book the morning trip.

  2. Taste local cachaça

    Try different aged and flavoured spirits.

    Tip Buy a bottle to take home.

  3. Hike on Ilha Grande

    Trails lead through Atlantic rainforest to beaches.

    Tip Wear repellent.

  4. Snorkel at Lagoa Azul

    A calm lagoon with clear water and fish.

    Tip Go in the morning.

  5. Attend the FLIP festival

    The Paraty International Literary Festival, held in the town in the middle of the year, attracts writers and readers from around the world.

    Tip Book accommodation far ahead.

  6. Kayak the bay

    Paddle through mangroves and quiet coves.

    Tip Go with a guide.

Local food

What to eat in Paraty

Paraty's food is coastal Brazilian, with fish, shrimp and local specialities.

  • Fish and seafood moqueca

    Fish and shrimp stews in coconut milk.

    Where to try it Waterfront restaurants.

  • Azul-marinho

    A local dish of fish stewed with unripe banana, giving a bluish tint.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants.

  • Cachaça

    Aged and flavoured spirits, including cachaça with honey and cinnamon (gabriela).

    Where to try it Distilleries and bars.

  • Tapioca and cuscuz

    Cassava pancakes and cornmeal dishes.

    Where to try it Cafés.

  • Tropical fruit

    Bananas, jackfruit and pineapple.

    Where to try it Markets.

Transport

Getting to and around Paraty

Paraty is on the coast road between Rio and São Paulo.

Getting there

  • Bus from Rio

    About 4 to 4.5 hours along the Costa Verde, with several daily services.

    Book seats in advance.

  • Bus or car from São Paulo

    About 5 to 6 hours.

    Take a bus or rent a car.

  • Boat to Ilha Grande

    Boats and ferries link Paraty and Angra dos Reis with Abraão.

    Check the schedule.

Getting around

The centre is on foot, and the coast is by bus and boat.

  • Walking

    The historic centre is car-free.

    Wear sturdy shoes.

  • Taxi and bus

    To Trindade and beaches.

    Check the timetable.

  • Boat

    To the bay's islands and Ilha Grande.

    Book in advance.

When to go

Best time to visit Paraty

Paraty has a humid tropical climate, with rain all year and a wetter summer.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Think twice

    Hot, humid, rainy and crowded, with high prices.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm, less crowded and often clear.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Best time

    Mild and drier, with clear days.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Warming, with some showers.

Worth planning around: Christmas to Carnival, when the town is crowded and expensive.

Travel tips

Paraty travel tips

A few tips make the trip easier.

  • Book accommodation early for holidays and festivals.
  • Wear comfortable shoes for the cobbles.
  • Bring insect repellent, sunscreen and a rain jacket.
  • Take cash for small stalls and boats.
  • Check tides and the weather for boat trips.
  • Take ferries early in the morning.
  • Do not leave belongings unattended on the beach.

FAQ

Paraty: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Paraty?

Two to three days, and more if you include Ilha Grande.

Can I visit as a day trip from Rio?

It is a long day. A night or two is better.

What is the best time to visit?

April to October.

Is Ilha Grande worth it?

Yes, for beaches, hiking and a relaxed atmosphere. Allow at least one night.

Is Paraty safe?

Generally, yes. It is a small, relaxed town.

Can I drive in the centre?

No, the historic centre is closed to cars.
