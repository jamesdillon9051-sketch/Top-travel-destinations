Welcome to Iguazu Falls

Iguazu Falls is a system of 275 waterfalls spread over almost 3 km of the Iguazu River, on the border between Brazil and Argentina. The name means big water in the Guarani language. The falls drop up to 80 metres over a basalt cliff into a gorge cloaked in subtropical rainforest, and the volume of water is greater than that of Niagara. UNESCO inscribed the national parks on both sides of the border, in 1984 and 1986.

The highlight is the Devil's Throat (Garganta del Diablo), a huge U-shaped chasm where about half of the river plunges in a roaring, misty cataract. Standing on a walkway over its edge is one of the world's great natural experiences.

The falls are best seen from both sides. The Brazilian side, with a 1.2-km walkway, offers wide panoramas and takes half a day, and the Argentine side has more trails, close-up views and a train to the Devil's Throat, and needs a full day. Foz do Iguaçu, the Brazilian gateway, and Puerto Iguazú in Argentina are both easy bases.

Why visit

Why visit Iguazu Falls?

Iguazu is one of the greatest waterfalls on earth, and an easy addition to a trip to Brazil or Argentina.

  • Sheer scale

    A wall of water more than 2.7 km wide, with spray visible from far away.

  • The Devil's Throat

    A thundering chasm where you stand right at the top.

  • Two national parks

    Different experiences on the Brazilian and Argentine sides.

  • Wildlife

    Coatis, toucans, butterflies and, if you are lucky, caiman and monkeys.

  • Easy access

    Airports on both sides and a border crossing in half an hour.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Iguazu Falls

Give the Brazilian side half a day and the Argentine side a full day.

  1. 01

    The Brazilian side

    A clifftop trail with sweeping views of the Argentine falls, ending on a walkway that reaches the base of the Devil's Throat. Shorter and less crowded than the Argentine side.

    Tip Go at opening time, and bring a raincoat.

  2. 02

    The Argentine side

    More extensive, with the Upper and Lower Circuits, and the Devil's Throat platform, reached by a small train and a long walkway. You get closer to more falls.

    Tip Allow a full day, and go early.

  3. 03

    Macuco Safari boat trip

    A jungle truck ride and a speedboat that takes you right beneath the falls, and soaks you completely.

    Tip Bring dry clothes and a waterproof bag.

  4. 04

    Bird Park (Parque das Aves)

    A sanctuary near the Brazilian entrance, with toucans, macaws, flamingos and parrots in large walk-through aviaries.

    Tip Visit after the falls.

  5. 05

    Itaipu Dam

    One of the largest hydroelectric dams in the world, shared by Brazil and Paraguay, with tours of the power station.

    Tip Book a tour in advance.

Things to do

Best things to do in Iguazu Falls

Iguazu is mostly about walking, looking and getting wet.

  1. Walk to the edge of the Devil's Throat

    Feel the vibration of the water from the walkway.

    Tip Go early to avoid the crowds.

  2. Ride the Macuco boat

    Get soaked under the falls in a speedboat.

    Tip Book the first boat of the day.

  3. Hike the Lower Circuit in Argentina

    See the falls from below, with views of the gorge.

    Tip Wear shoes with grip.

  4. Take a helicopter flight

    A short flight over the falls gives a panoramic view.

    Tip Book in advance.

  5. Visit the Bird Park

    Walk through aviaries and see rare birds.

    Tip Half a day is enough.

  6. See the Three Borders

    The point where Brazil, Argentina and Paraguay meet at the confluence of rivers.

    Tip Go at sunset.

Local food

What to eat in Iguazu Falls

Food near Iguazu is a mix of Brazilian, Argentine and Paraguayan traditions.

  • Churrasco and rodízio

    Grilled meats served in endless rounds at Brazilian churrascarias.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Foz do Iguaçu.

  • Surubi and river fish

    Fish from the Paraná River, grilled or fried.

    Where to try it Riverside restaurants.

  • Chipa

    Cheesy cassava bread rolls from Paraguay and Argentina.

    Where to try it Bakeries and street stalls.

  • Empanadas

    Baked or fried pastries with meat, cheese or vegetables.

    Where to try it Puerto Iguazú.

  • Mate and tereré

    Herbal tea drunk hot or cold, a social ritual.

    Where to try it Everywhere.

Transport

Getting to and around Iguazu Falls

Iguazu is in the far south-west of Brazil, and is reached by air.

Getting there

  • Flight to Foz do Iguaçu (IGU)

    Direct flights from Rio (about 2 hours), São Paulo and other Brazilian cities.

    The airport is 20 minutes from the falls.

  • Flight to Puerto Iguazú (IGR)

    Direct flights from Buenos Aires, about 2 hours.

    Good for combining with Argentina.

  • Bus

    Long-distance buses from São Paulo take about 15 hours.

    Fly instead.

Getting around

Buses and taxis link the towns and both parks, and border formalities apply between sides.

  • Local buses

    Buses run from Foz to the Brazilian park, and cross the border to Argentina.

    Carry passports.

  • Taxi or tour

    Convenient for crossing the border and visiting both sides.

    Agree the price.

  • On foot

    Trails and walkways inside the parks.

    Wear good shoes.

When to go

Best time to visit Iguazu Falls

Iguazu has a subtropical climate, with hot, humid summers and mild winters, and rain all year. The falls are fullest in the wetter months.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Good

    Hot and humid, with big flows and crowds.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm, with good flow and fewer visitors.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Mild and drier, with lower flow. Some walkways can be busier.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm, with good flow and fewer crowds.

Worth planning around: January, when it is very hot and busy, and periods of extreme flooding, when walkways can close.

Travel tips

Iguazu Falls travel tips

A few tips make the visit smoother.

  • Go early to avoid crowds and heat.
  • Carry your passport for the border crossing, and check visa rules for Argentina.
  • Bring a raincoat or poncho, and a waterproof bag.
  • Do not feed the coatis, who can bite.
  • Wear shoes with grip, since walkways are wet.
  • Check whether the Devil's Throat walkway is open, since floods can close it.
  • Use insect repellent.

FAQ

Iguazu Falls: frequently asked questions

Which side is better, Brazil or Argentina?

The Argentine side has more trails and closer views, and the Brazilian side has better panoramas. Most visitors see both.

How many days do you need?

Two days, one for each side.

Do I need a visa for Argentina?

It depends on your nationality. Check the rules for both countries, and carry your passport.

What is the best time to visit?

March to May and August to October.

Will I get wet?

Yes, particularly on the walkways and on the boat. A poncho and a waterproof bag are useful.

Is the Devil's Throat always open?

No. High water can close the walkway to it.
