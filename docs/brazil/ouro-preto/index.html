Welcome to Ouro Preto

Ouro Preto, or Black Gold, was founded around 1698 after gold was discovered in the hills of what would become Minas Gerais, which means General Mines. Within a few decades it became the greatest gold-mining town in the Americas, and one of the largest cities on the continent, and it built churches, mansions and fountains with the wealth from the mines. It was known as Vila Rica, and it later became the state capital until 1897. UNESCO listed it in 1980, one of the first places in Brazil to be recognised.

The town is spread across steep hills, and its cobbled streets climb and drop between whitewashed houses with colourful doors and windows. More than a dozen baroque churches are decorated with carved wood and gold leaf. Many are associated with the sculptor and architect Aleijadinho, Antônio Francisco Lisboa, who created some of the finest baroque works in the Americas despite a disabling illness, and with the painter Manuel da Costa Ataíde.

It is also the site of the Inconfidência Mineira, a 1789 plot to free Brazil from Portuguese rule, whose martyr, Tiradentes, gave his name to the main square. The town has a university, and a lively student atmosphere, which contrasts with its historic character. It is a quiet town of hills and mist, with excellent food and a hospitable culture.

Why visit

Why visit Ouro Preto?

Ouro Preto is the finest colonial town in Brazil, and a highlight of the interior.

  • Baroque masterpieces

    Churches decorated with gilded carving, painted ceilings and sculptures by Aleijadinho.

  • Gold-rush history

    Mines, museums and mansions from the era that shaped Brazil.

  • A perfect hill town

    Steep cobbled streets, colonial houses and mountain views.

  • Mineiro cuisine

    Hearty, comforting food such as tutu de feijão, pão de queijo and doce de leite.

  • Other colonial towns

    Mariana, Tiradentes and São João del-Rei are within reach.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Ouro Preto

The centre is small and walkable, and a full day covers the churches.

  1. 01

    Church of São Francisco de Assis

    A masterpiece of Brazilian baroque, designed by Aleijadinho, with a ceiling painted by Ataíde. The façade, with its sculpted medallions, is a highlight.

    Tip Visit early, and check the opening times.

  2. 02

    Praça Tiradentes

    The main square, with a statue of Tiradentes and the Museu da Inconfidência in a former town hall and jail, which tells of the 1789 conspiracy and the gold-rush era.

    Tip Start here.

  3. 03

    Matriz de Nossa Senhora do Pilar

    A church richly decorated with hundreds of kilograms of gold, and one of the most lavish in Brazil, with a museum of sacred art.

    Tip Photography is often not allowed.

  4. 04

    Mina do Chico Rei

    A former gold mine that visitors can enter, with a story of an African king who bought his freedom.

    Tip Wear comfortable shoes.

  5. 05

    Mariana

    An older and quieter colonial town, 12 km away, with baroque churches and the oldest city in Minas Gerais. A tourist train links it with Ouro Preto.

    Tip Take the train.

  6. 06

    Museu do Oratório

    A museum of small household shrines, with more than a thousand pieces.

    Tip A short visit.

Things to do

Best things to do in Ouro Preto

Ouro Preto is for slow walking and church-going.

  1. Walk the cobbled streets

    Explore the hills and viewpoints.

    Tip Wear good shoes.

  2. Visit the baroque churches

    See carved altars and painted ceilings.

    Tip Buy a combined ticket.

  3. Explore a gold mine

    Go underground at the Chico Rei mine.

    Tip Bring a jacket.

  4. Take the train to Mariana

    A scenic ride on a historic train.

    Tip Check the timetable.

  5. Try local sweets and cachaça

    Doce de leite, goiabada and cachaça from Minas.

    Tip Buy from local shops.

  6. Watch the sunset

    From viewpoints such as Morro da Forca and Praça Tiradentes.

    Tip Bring a jacket.

Local food

What to eat in Ouro Preto

Minas Gerais has the most beloved regional cooking in Brazil, hearty and warming.

  • Tutu de feijão

    A thick bean purée with cassava flour, served with pork, greens and rice.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants.

  • Frango com quiabo

    Chicken stewed with okra, a Minas classic.

    Where to try it Family restaurants.

  • Pão de queijo

    Chewy cheese rolls made with local cheese.

    Where to try it Cafés and bakeries.

  • Doce de leite and goiabada

    Sweet milk caramel and guava paste, eaten with white cheese.

    Where to try it Shops and cafés.

  • Cachaça mineira

    Artisanal cachaça from Minas Gerais.

    Where to try it Bars and shops.

Transport

Getting to and around Ouro Preto

Ouro Preto is about 100 km south-east of Belo Horizonte.

Getting there

  • Bus from Belo Horizonte

    About 2 to 2.5 hours, several daily services.

    Book in advance.

  • Car from Belo Horizonte or Rio

    About 1.5 to 2 hours from Belo Horizonte, and about 7 hours from Rio.

    Parking is limited in town.

  • Flight to Belo Horizonte (CNF)

    Direct flights from Rio, São Paulo and other cities.

    Take a bus or transfer.

Getting around

The centre is walkable, and steep.

  • Walking

    The best way, though hills are steep.

    Wear good shoes.

  • Taxi

    Useful for hills and viewpoints.

    Agree the fare.

  • Bus

    Local buses link the town centre and Mariana.

    Check the timetable.

When to go

Best time to visit Ouro Preto

Ouro Preto is in the highlands, so it is cool and misty, with a dry winter and a rainy summer.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Think twice

    Warm and wet, with heavy rain.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Good

    Mild and drying out.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Best time

    Dry, sunny days and cold nights.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Warming, with the first rains.

Worth planning around: January and February, when rain is heavy. Semana Santa (Easter week) is a special, crowded time.

  1. Day1

  2. Day2

    A colonial neighbour.

Travel tips

Ouro Preto travel tips

A few tips help you enjoy the town.

  • Wear comfortable shoes for the steep cobbles.
  • Bring a jacket, since evenings are cool.
  • Visit churches in the morning, and check opening times, since many close for lunch.
  • Buy a combined ticket to churches and museums.
  • Stay a night to enjoy the town after day trippers leave.
  • Respect photography rules in churches.
  • Book ahead for Easter week.

FAQ

Ouro Preto: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Ouro Preto?

Two days for the churches, museums and Mariana.

Is Ouro Preto worth visiting?

Yes, for baroque architecture and colonial history.

Who was Aleijadinho?

Antônio Francisco Lisboa, a sculptor and architect of the 18th century who created many of Minas Gerais's baroque masterpieces.

What is the best time to visit?

May to September.

How do I get there?

By bus from Belo Horizonte, about 2 to 2.5 hours.

Can I visit as a day trip?

Yes, from Belo Horizonte, but a night is better.
