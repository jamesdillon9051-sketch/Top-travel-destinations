Brazil covers nearly half of South America, with beaches, rainforest, wetlands and colonial cities. This guide covers how the regions divide, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to move around, when to go, what it costs and three routes that work.

How Brazil divides

Brazil is about 4,300 km from north to south, and its regions differ greatly in climate, culture and cuisine. Five zones organise most trips.

The south-east. Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo are the two great cities, about 430 km apart. On the coast between them, Paraty and Ilha Grande have colonial streets and islands, and inland, Ouro Preto is a colonial gold town.

The south. Iguazu Falls lie on the border with Argentina and Paraguay, in the far south-west of the country.

The north-east. Salvador is the centre of Afro-Brazilian culture, and the region has long beaches, the dunes of Lençóis Maranhenses and the island of Fernando de Noronha.

The Amazon. Manaus is the gateway to the world's largest rainforest and its rivers.

The centre-west. The Pantanal is a vast wetland with the best wildlife viewing in South America.

Worth knowing: The practical unit is two or three of these, linked by domestic flights. Attempting more turns the trip into an exercise in airports.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If you have one week

    Rio de Janeiro with a short trip to Paraty, or Rio combined with Iguazu Falls by a two-hour flight.

  • If you love wildlife

    The Pantanal for jaguars and giant otters, and the Amazon for birds, monkeys and river life.

  • If you want beaches

    Fernando de Noronha for pristine sand and diving, Ilha Grande for islands, and Rio for city beaches.

  • If you want culture and history

    Salvador for Afro-Brazilian heritage, Ouro Preto for baroque gold-town architecture, and São Paulo for museums and food.

  • If you want something spectacular

    Iguazu Falls and Lençóis Maranhenses are among the most striking landscapes on earth.

  • If you care about food

    São Paulo for its restaurants and street food, Salvador for Bahian cooking, and Minas Gerais for comfort dishes.

Flying across a continent

Brazil's distances are huge. Rio to Manaus is about 2,800 km, and Rio to Salvador about 1,200 km.

Flights. The only sensible way between regions. LATAM, GOL and Azul fly to all the main cities. Book early, and check baggage rules.

Long-distance buses. Comfortable and cheap between cities up to about 8 hours apart, such as Rio to São Paulo or Rio to Paraty.

City transport. Metros in Rio and São Paulo are efficient. Ride-hailing apps such as Uber and 99 are the safest way to move at night.

Boats. Essential in the Amazon and the Pantanal, and useful for Ilha Grande and Paraty's bay.

Worth knowing: Avoid arriving in a new city late at night, and pre-book transfers from airports and bus stations.

When to go

Brazil's seasons are the reverse of Europe and North America. Summer runs from December to February, and winter from June to August.

The south-east coast, including Rio, is hot and humid in summer, and drier and more comfortable from April to October.

The Amazon has two seasons: high water from December to May, when boats reach the flooded forest, and low water from June to November, when jungle trails open up.

The Pantanal is best in the dry season from June to October, when animals gather around water and are easy to spot. The wet season is green but harder to travel in.

The north-east is warm year-round, with a rainy season around April to July. Lençóis Maranhenses is best from June to September, when the lagoons are full.

Worth knowing: Carnival, in February or March, and New Year are the peak periods, and prices rise sharply.

Money, costs and safety

Brazil is moderately priced, cheaper than Europe or North America but not as cheap as much of Asia. A restaurant meal costs US$10–25, a comfortable hotel US$60–150, and domestic flights US$50–200.

Beach-resort areas, Fernando de Noronha and the Amazon lodges cost more. Fernando de Noronha, in particular, has park fees and high prices for food and accommodation.

Cards are widely accepted, but carry some cash for markets and small places. Tip 10% where a service charge is not included.

Crime is a concern in large cities, especially petty theft and muggings. Keep valuables hidden, use ride-hailing apps and follow local advice about areas to avoid.

Three routes that work

  1. One week: Rio and Iguazu

    Four nights in Rio de Janeiro, and two in Iguazu Falls, with a two-hour flight between them.

    Tip Fly into Rio and out of Foz do Iguaçu.

  2. Ten days: coast and culture

    Rio, Paraty and Salvador, with a flight from Rio to Salvador at the end.

    Tip Take the bus to Paraty, and fly for the long leg.

  3. Two weeks: the classic

    The itinerary on the country page: Rio, Paraty, Iguazu and Salvador.

    Tip Swap Salvador for the Amazon or the Pantanal if you want wildlife.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Brazil

Can I see Rio and the Amazon in one trip?

Yes, with a flight. Rio to Manaus takes about four hours. Ten days is the minimum for Rio, Iguazu and the Amazon, and two weeks is more comfortable.

Do I need to speak Portuguese?

Not strictly. English is common in hotels and tourist areas, but a few phrases of Portuguese help everywhere, and Spanish is not a substitute.

Is it better to travel independently or on a tour?

Independent travel works in cities and along the coast. Tours are more practical for the Amazon, the Pantanal and Fernando de Noronha, where logistics are complicated.

When should I book?

Flights and hotels for Carnival, New Year and July should be booked three to six months ahead. Wildlife lodges in the Pantanal fill early in the dry season.
