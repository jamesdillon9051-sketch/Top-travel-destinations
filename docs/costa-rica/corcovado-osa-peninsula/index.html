Welcome to Corcovado and the Osa Peninsula

The Osa Peninsula, in the southwest of Costa Rica, is wild, remote and astonishingly rich. National Geographic once called it the most biologically intense place on Earth, and Corcovado National Park protects a large block of Pacific lowland rainforest where jaguars, tapirs, scarlet macaws, four species of monkeys and thousands of insects live.

The park is not easy to visit. A guide is required, trails are hot and muddy, and accommodation inside is limited to ranger stations such as Sirena. Many visitors choose day trips from Drake Bay by boat, while multi-day treks take you to the heart of the forest. Puerto Jimenez, on the Golfo Dulce side, is the other main gateway.

Elsewhere on the peninsula, you can go whale watching, kayaking and snorkelling, and relax in eco-lodges on remote beaches. It is more expensive and less comfortable than other parts of Costa Rica, but the wildlife experience is among the best in Central America.
