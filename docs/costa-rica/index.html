Why go to Costa Rica

Costa Rica is a small country in Central America, but it holds about five percent of the planet's biodiversity. Cloud forests, volcanoes, rainforest canals, dry forests and two oceans lie within a few hours of each other, and roughly a quarter of the land is protected.

The country has no army, a long tradition of democracy and conservation, and a laid-back philosophy known as pura vida. Tourism is well developed, with eco-lodges, guided walks, canopy tours and surf schools, so it is easy to visit even for first-time travellers.

San Jose is the capital and gateway, but most visitors head straight for the volcano and hot springs at Arenal, the cloud forest at Monteverde, or the beaches of the Pacific. For wilder experiences, the Caribbean canals of Tortuguero and the remote Osa Peninsula are among the best wildlife destinations in the Americas.

Food and drink

Costa Rica foods to try

Costa Rican food is simple and fresh, built on rice, beans, plantain and fruit, with Caribbean touches on the east coast.

  • Gallo pinto

    Rice and black beans fried together, the national breakfast.

    Where to try it Sodas and hotels everywhere.

  • Casado

    A set plate of rice, beans, salad, plantain and meat or fish.

    Where to try it Local sodas.

  • Ceviche

    Raw fish cured in lime.

    Where to try it Coastal restaurants.

  • Patacones

    Fried plantain slices.

    Where to try it Snack stalls.

  • Costa Rican coffee

    Some of the world's best arabica.

    Where to try it Cafes and farms.

  • Rondon

    A Caribbean coconut seafood stew.

    Where to try it Limon province.

Before you go

Practical travel information for Costa Rica

The essentials, current for 2026. Costa Rica is welcoming and straightforward, but check official sources before travelling.

Capital
San Jose
Language
Spanish is the official language. English is widely spoken in tourist areas.
Currency
Costa Rican colon (CRC). Roughly 500-550 colones to the US dollar in recent years. US dollars are widely accepted.
Cards and cash
Cards are widely accepted. Carry some cash for small shops, buses and rural areas. ATMs are common in towns.
Entry and visas
Many nationalities, including the US, UK, EU and Australia, can enter visa-free for up to 90 days, but you may need to show proof of onward travel. Check current rules.
Power sockets
Type A and B (US-style), 120 V, 60 Hz. Bring an adaptor if needed.
Emergency number
911 for all emergencies.
Tipping
Tipping is not required, as restaurants add a 10% service charge, but extra tips are appreciated for guides and drivers.
Safety
Costa Rica is generally safe, but petty theft is common. Never leave valuables in cars, be careful at beaches with strong currents, and use licensed guides.
Internet and SIM
Good in towns, patchy in remote areas. SIMs from Kolbi, Movistar and Claro are inexpensive.
Time zone
Central Standard Time (UTC-6) all year, with no daylight saving.

When to go

Best time to visit Costa Rica

The dry season runs from December to April and is the most popular time to visit.

  • December-April

    Best time

    Dry, sunny and busy.

  • May-June

    Good

    Green and quiet.

  • July-October

    Quiet

    Wet afternoons, cheaper prices.

  • November

    Think twice

    Wettest month on much of the Pacific.

Worth planning around: September and October on the Pacific side, when roads can flood.

Trip length

How long to spend in Costa Rica

Costa Rica is small, but roads are slow. Two or three bases are plenty.

  • 5-7 days

    San Jose, Arenal and Manuel Antonio.

    A classic start.

  • 10 days

    Add Monteverde and Tortuguero.

    More wildlife.

  • 14 days

    Add the Caribbean or Guanacaste.

    A broader trip.

  • 3 weeks

    Add Corcovado and the Nicoya Peninsula.

    For slow travellers.

Getting around

Transportation in Costa Rica

Shuttles and rental cars are the most flexible, buses the cheapest. Domestic flights save time.

  • Shuttle

    Door-to-door between tourist hubs.

    Book ahead.

  • Rental car

    Flexible, but roads can be rough.

    Choose a 4x4 for Monteverde and Osa.

  • Public bus

    Cheap and slow.

    Good for budget travellers.

  • Domestic flight

    Sansa and Skyway.

    Quick to beach towns.

  • Boat

    To Tortuguero and across the Gulf of Nicoya.

    Often part of tours.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Costa Rica

Costa Ricans, called Ticos, are friendly, easygoing and proud of their peaceful, green country. The phrase pura vida means pure life and is used as hello, goodbye and thank you.

  • Say pura vida

    It is the national greeting.

  • Be patient

    Life runs on tico time.

  • Dress casually

    But cover up in towns.

  • Respect nature

    Keep to trails.

  • Tip modestly

    A little goes a long way.

  • Greet people

    A smile and hola.

Do

  • Learn basic Spanish phrases
  • Follow park and trail rules
  • Bring a reusable bottle
  • Respect wildlife distances
  • Use licensed guides
  • Book turtle tours with registered operators

Don't

  • Do not feed wildlife
  • Do not litter
  • Do not leave valuables in cars
  • Do not swim in rip currents
  • Do not take sand or shells
  • Do not touch turtles

Travel tips

Costa Rica travel tips

The things that most often catch visitors out in Costa Rica.

  • Book hotels and tours ahead for Christmas and Easter.
  • Roads are slow, so allow extra time.
  • Bring rain gear even in the dry season.
  • Carry insect repellent and sun protection.
  • Use only licensed guides in parks.
  • Never leave valuables in a parked car.
  • Carry some cash for small places.
  • Check tide and currents before swimming.

FAQ

Visiting Costa Rica: frequently asked questions

Is Costa Rica safe for tourists?

Generally yes, with normal care against theft and rip currents.

How many days do I need?

Ten to fourteen days for a good trip.

Do I need a visa?

Many nationalities do not for stays up to 90 days, but check.

What is the best time to visit?

December to April for dry weather, May to November for green season savings.

Is Costa Rica expensive?

It is moderate to high for Central America.

Can I use US dollars?

Yes, widely, but colones are better for small purchases.
