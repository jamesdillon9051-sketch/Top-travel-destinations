Welcome to the Cape Winelands

South Africa has been making wine since 1659, when the first Cape vintage was pressed by the colony's Dutch founder, Jan van Riebeeck. The most important regions are in the mountain-ringed valleys east of Cape Town, where warm, dry summers and cool sea breezes are ideal for grapes. The area, called the Cape Winelands, includes the university town of Stellenbosch, the French-influenced village of Franschhoek, the larger town of Paarl, and the historic estates of Constantia on the edge of Cape Town.

The estates are as beautiful as the wine. Many are grouped around white, gabled Cape Dutch manor houses, some dating from the 17th and 18th centuries, with oaks, rose gardens and vineyards against a backdrop of dramatic mountains. Wine tasting is often paired with food, and restaurants in the region rank among the best in the country. The Cape's signature grapes include Chenin Blanc, Pinotage, a local cross, Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah.

Wine tourism is well organised, with tasting rooms, tours, a Wine Tram in Franschhoek and cycling routes. It is close enough to Cape Town for a day trip, but a night or two is better, particularly in Franschhoek or Stellenbosch, where a stay on a vineyard estate is part of the experience.

Why visit

Why visit the Cape Winelands?

The Winelands combine some of the most beautiful landscapes in South Africa with excellent wine and food.

  • World-class wine

    Estates producing Chenin Blanc, Pinotage, Cabernet and more, with tastings for all levels.

  • Superb scenery

    Vineyards and mountains, with oak-lined roads and Cape Dutch buildings.

  • Fine dining

    Restaurants and farm-to-table lunches at estates.

  • History

    Stellenbosch, South Africa's second-oldest town, and Franschhoek's Huguenot heritage.

  • Easy access

    About 45 minutes from Cape Town's airport.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Cape Winelands

Give each town a day, or combine them into a tour.

  1. 01

    Stellenbosch

    A university town founded in 1679, with oak-lined streets, Cape Dutch houses and museums, surrounded by more than 100 wine estates.

    Tip Walk Dorp Street, and book a tasting.

  2. 02

    Franschhoek

    A village settled by French Huguenots in 1688, now known as the country's food and wine capital, with a memorial museum, boutiques and top restaurants.

    Tip Book the Wine Tram.

  3. 03

    Paarl

    A long town below a granite dome, with old estates, the Afrikaans Language Monument and a wine route.

    Tip Visit an old estate.

  4. 04

    Babylonstoren

    A farm and estate near Franschhoek with a huge kitchen garden, restaurants and a hotel in Cape Dutch buildings.

    Tip Book lunch in advance.

  5. 05

    Groot Constantia

    South Africa's oldest wine estate, founded in 1685, in Constantia on the edge of Cape Town, with a museum and tastings.

    Tip Combine with other Constantia estates.

  6. 06

    Cape Dutch estates

    Manor houses such as Boschendal, Vergelegen and others, with tours, gardens and restaurants.

    Tip Choose two or three estates.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Cape Winelands

Wine, food and scenery make up the days.

  1. Taste wine at several estates

    Visit three or four, with a lunch break.

    Tip Book in advance and hire a driver.

  2. Ride the Franschhoek Wine Tram

    A hop-on hop-off tram and bus that links estates.

    Tip Book a day pass.

  3. Cycle through the vines

    Quiet roads and tracks link estates.

    Tip Rent an e-bike.

  4. Have a farm-to-table lunch

    Many estates serve seasonal menus with wine pairings.

    Tip Reserve early.

  5. Hike in the mountains

    Trails in the Jonkershoek or Franschhoek mountains.

    Tip Go early.

  6. Visit an art gallery or craft market

    Stellenbosch and Franschhoek have galleries and markets.

    Tip Check opening days.

Local food

What to eat in the Cape Winelands

The Winelands' food is estate-led, with produce from farms and gardens and a mix of Cape traditions.

  • Chenin Blanc

    The Cape's signature white, in styles from fresh to oaked.

    Where to try it Every estate.

  • Pinotage

    A South African red, a cross of Pinot Noir and Cinsault.

    Where to try it Stellenbosch and Paarl.

  • Cape Winelands cuisine

    Modern dishes with local ingredients, such as springbok, trout and fruit.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Franschhoek.

  • Cheese and olive oil

    Artisan cheese, olives and preserves at farm shops.

    Where to try it Estates and markets.

  • Malva pudding

    A warm sponge dessert.

    Where to try it Restaurants.

Transport

Getting to and around the Cape Winelands

The Winelands are 45 to 75 km from Cape Town.

Getting there

  • Drive from Cape Town

    About 45 minutes to Stellenbosch, 1 hour to Franschhoek, 50 minutes to Paarl.

    Hire a car.

  • Tour or driver

    Day tours from Cape Town are widely available.

    Best if you plan to drink.

  • Train to Stellenbosch

    Limited services.

    Check availability.

Getting around

A driver or a tour is the easiest way to taste wine.

  • Driver or tour

    Book for the day.

    Avoid drinking and driving.

  • Wine Tram

    In Franschhoek.

    Book ahead.

  • Bicycle

    For short hops.

    Wear a helmet.

When to go

Best time to visit the Cape Winelands

The Winelands have hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    Hot, sunny and green, with alfresco lunches.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Harvest season, golden vines and pleasant weather.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Cool and wet, with fires and low prices.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Green, with blossom.

Worth planning around: The hottest days of January, and busy Easter and Christmas periods.

Travel tips

The Cape Winelands travel tips

A few tips make wine touring better.

  • Book tastings and lunches in advance.
  • Hire a driver or take a tour. Do not drink and drive.
  • Visit two or three estates a day, not more.
  • Ask about cellar tours and pairings.
  • Try wines you don't know, such as Chenin Blanc and Pinotage.
  • Wear sun protection and comfortable shoes.
  • Buy wine directly from estates.

FAQ

The Cape Winelands: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in the Winelands?

Two or three days. A day trip from Cape Town is also possible.

What is the best time to visit?

November to April.

Which town is best?

Franschhoek for food and charm, Stellenbosch for variety, and Paarl for a quieter feel.

Do I need a driver?

It is strongly recommended if you plan to drink.

What wine is South Africa known for?

Chenin Blanc, Pinotage, Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah.

Is it expensive?

Tastings are inexpensive, and wine and food are good value.
