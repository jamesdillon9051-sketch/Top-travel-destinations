Hermanus and the Overberg

Welcome to Hermanus

Hermanus, a seaside town on Walker Bay, about 120 km east of Cape Town, is regarded as one of the best places in the world to see whales from land. From about June to November, southern right whales come into the shallow waters of the bay to mate and give birth, and they can be seen just a few metres from the rocks along a 12-km cliff path that runs along the shore. Whale watching is at its best in September and October.

The town has a long tradition of whale-watching. A 'whale crier', dressed in traditional costume, blows a kelp horn to tell visitors where whales have been sighted, and each September the town holds a Whale Festival. Away from the sea, Hermanus has a pleasant old harbour, craft shops and restaurants, and the Hemel-en-Aarde Valley, a cool-climate wine region known for pinot noir and chardonnay, is close by.

The wider Overberg region has more to offer. Gansbaai, the great white shark capital of the world, offers shark-cage diving, Cape Agulhas marks the southernmost tip of Africa, De Hoop Nature Reserve has dunes and whales, and Betty's Bay has a colony of African penguins. It makes a good weekend from Cape Town, or a stop on the way to the Garden Route.

Why visit

Why visit Hermanus?

Hermanus offers easy, close whale watching, and a coast full of wildlife.

  • Whales from the shore

    Southern right whales close to the cliff path, from June to November.

  • Sharks and marine life

    Shark-cage diving at Gansbaai, and seals and dolphins.

  • Southernmost Africa

    Cape Agulhas, where two oceans meet.

  • Wine

    Pinot noir and chardonnay in the Hemel-en-Aarde Valley.

  • Easy from Cape Town

    About 1.5 hours by road.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Hermanus

Hermanus is compact, and the surrounding region has several excursions.

  1. 01

    The Hermanus Cliff Path

    A 12-km walking path along the sea cliffs, with viewing platforms where whales can be seen very close to shore, plus rock pools and fynbos.

    Tip Walk between the Old Harbour and Grotto Beach.

  2. 02

    Voëlklip and Grotto beaches

    Sandy beaches on Walker Bay, backed by mountains, good for walks and views of the whales.

    Tip Swim at Grotto Beach in summer.

  3. 03

    The Old Harbour and Whale Crier

    A small historic harbour with a museum, and the whale crier, who announces sightings with a kelp horn.

    Tip Listen for the horn.

  4. 04

    Gansbaai and Shark Alley

    A fishing town about 40 km east, where boats take visitors to see great white sharks from cages or from the deck. It is a controversial activity, and operators differ.

    Tip Choose a responsible operator.

  5. 05

    Cape Agulhas

    The southernmost tip of Africa, marked by a plaque, where the Indian and Atlantic Oceans meet, with a historic lighthouse.

    Tip A long day trip.

  6. 06

    Hemel-en-Aarde Valley

    A cool-climate wine valley near Hermanus, producing some of South Africa's best pinot noir and chardonnay.

    Tip Book tastings.

When to go

Best time to visit Hermanus

Hermanus has a Mediterranean climate, with mild, wet winters and dry summers.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Peak whale season, with wildflowers.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Good

    Warm and dry, but no whales.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Good

    Warm and calm.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Whales arrive, with cool, wet weather.

Worth planning around: Whale watching outside June to November.

Travel tips

Hermanus travel tips

A few tips help you see the whales.

  • Go in September or October for the most whales.
  • Walk the cliff path in the morning, when the sea is calm.
  • Bring binoculars and a jacket.
  • Book boat trips and shark dives in advance.
  • Do not approach whales in the water.
  • Choose responsible operators.
  • Book accommodation ahead for the Whale Festival.

FAQ

Hermanus: frequently asked questions

When is the best time to see whales?

June to November, with September and October the peak.

Do I need a boat?

No. Whales can be seen from the cliff path.

How far is Hermanus from Cape Town?

About 120 km, or 1.5 hours.

Can I visit as a day trip?

Yes, though a night lets you enjoy the evening.

Is shark-cage diving safe?

Operators follow safety rules, but it is a controversial activity, so choose carefully.

What else is nearby?

Gansbaai, Cape Agulhas, De Hoop and the Hemel-en-Aarde wine valley.
