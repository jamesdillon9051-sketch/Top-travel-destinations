Welcome to the Drakensberg

The Drakensberg, Afrikaans for Dragon Mountains and known to the Zulu as uKhahlamba, the Barrier of Spears, is the highest range in southern Africa. Its great basalt escarpment runs for about 200 km along the border between South Africa's KwaZulu-Natal province and Lesotho, rising to more than 3,400 metres, with sandstone foothills, deep valleys, grassy slopes and rivers. It was inscribed by UNESCO in 2000 for its spectacular scenery, biodiversity and its rock art.

The most famous sight is the Amphitheatre, a rock wall about 5 km long and 1,200 metres high in the Royal Natal National Park, with the Tugela Falls plunging in five drops over the edge, the second-highest waterfall in the world at about 948 metres. Elsewhere, Cathedral Peak, Champagne Castle and Giant's Castle offer hiking, and the sandstone shelters hold more than 35,000 paintings by the San people, who lived here for thousands of years.

It is a place for walkers, riders, birdwatchers and those who enjoy mountain air. Small resorts and lodges are scattered through the valleys, and the days are best spent on trails, with a swim in a rock pool at the end. Weather can change fast, with summer thunderstorms and winter snow, and a car is the best way to reach the trailheads.

Why visit

Why visit the Drakensberg?

The Drakensberg is South Africa's finest mountain scenery, with excellent walks for all abilities.

  • Dramatic escarpment

    Sheer basalt walls, spires and amphitheatres.

  • Tugela Falls

    One of the highest waterfalls in the world, reached by a hike.

  • Hiking for everyone

    Easy waterfall walks to multi-day treks.

  • San rock art

    Thousands of paintings in shelters, some up to 3,000 years old.

  • Mountain resorts

    Family-friendly lodges with horse riding and swimming holes.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Drakensberg

The range has several distinct areas, each with its own character.

  1. 01

    The Amphitheatre and Royal Natal National Park

    A great curving rock wall with the Tugela Falls behind. Trails lead to the Tugela Gorge, and a hike to the top via chain ladders from Sentinel car park reaches the escarpment.

    Tip Go on a clear day.

  2. 02

    Tugela Falls

    A waterfall in five drops totalling about 948 metres, often cited as the second highest in the world. It flows best after rain, and can freeze in winter.

    Tip Check the flow before you go.

  3. 03

    Cathedral Peak

    A sharp, cathedral-like peak in the central Drakensberg, with hiking trails, rock pools and Didima Gorge, and access to San rock art.

    Tip A good base for walks.

  4. 04

    San rock art at Giant's Castle and Cathedral Peak

    The Drakensberg holds one of the world's greatest collections of rock art, with paintings of eland, humans and spirit beings, at sites such as Main Caves and Eland Cave.

    Tip Visit with a guide.

  5. 05

    Giant's Castle Game Reserve

    A high-altitude reserve with eland, other antelope and the rare bearded vulture, which can be seen at a hide in winter.

    Tip Book the vulture hide.

  6. 06

    Sani Pass

    A 4x4 route over the escarpment into Lesotho, with steep hairpins, and the highest pub in Africa at the top.

    Tip Go on a guided tour.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Drakensberg

Walking and outdoor activities make up most of the days.

  1. Hike to Tugela Gorge

    A 5-hour walk through a spectacular gorge to the base of the Amphitheatre.

    Tip Start early.

  2. Ride a horse

    Trails through the foothills and valleys.

    Tip Book at your lodge.

  3. See San rock art

    Guided walks to painted shelters.

    Tip Book in advance.

  4. Swim in a rock pool

    Cool off in mountain streams.

    Tip Check the water levels.

  5. Fly fish for trout

    Tip Book a guide.

  6. Watch birds

    Bearded vultures, cranes and sunbirds.

    Tip Bring binoculars.

When to go

Best time to visit the Drakensberg

The Drakensberg has hot, wet summers with thunderstorms and cold, dry winters, with snow on the peaks.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Good

    Warm and green, with afternoon thunderstorms.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Clear, warm days and cool nights.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Dry and sunny, but very cold at night, with snow.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Mild, with wildflowers.

Worth planning around: Summer afternoons for hiking, when storms and lightning are common.

Travel tips

The Drakensberg travel tips

A few tips help you stay safe in the mountains.

  • Check the weather. Storms and mist can arrive suddenly.
  • Register at park offices before hiking.
  • Bring layers, sun protection and waterproofs.
  • Take a guide for longer routes.
  • Do not touch rock paintings.
  • Start walks early to avoid afternoon storms.
  • Fill up with fuel and cash before arriving.

FAQ

The Drakensberg: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in the Drakensberg?

Three days is ideal.

Is Tugela Falls really the second highest waterfall?

It is often described so, at about 948 metres in five drops, though rankings vary by definition.

What is the best time to visit?

March to May and September to November.

Can I hike without a guide?

Many day walks are fine without one. Take a guide for longer routes and rock art.

Is it cold?

Nights are cold, even in summer, and winters can be freezing.

Is the Drakensberg near Johannesburg?

It is about 4 to 5 hours by road.
