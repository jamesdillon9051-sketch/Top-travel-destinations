Welcome to St Lucia

iSimangaliso Wetland Park, on the north-east coast of KwaZulu-Natal, stretches for about 220 km along the Indian Ocean, from the Mozambique border to Maphelane. Its name means miracle and wonder in Zulu, and it was South Africa's first UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1999. The park protects a mosaic of habitats, including coral reefs, long beaches, coastal forest, dunes, lakes and swamps, and it is home to hippos, crocodiles, elephants, rhinos, leopards, turtles, whales, dolphins and hundreds of species of bird.

The centre of the park is Lake St Lucia, Africa's largest estuarine lake, and the small town of St Lucia, at its mouth, where hippos occasionally wander at night and boat trips leave to see them and the crocodiles. To the east, a sandy road leads through coastal forest to Cape Vidal, with warm-water reefs and beaches, and further north Sodwana Bay is a top diving destination, while Kosi Bay and the Maputaland coast have lakes and turtle nesting beaches. Leatherback and loggerhead turtles nest on the beaches from November to January.

Nearby, Hluhluwe–iMfolozi Park, one of the oldest game reserves in Africa, has the Big Five and is known for its white rhino conservation success. Together, they make a rewarding trip for wildlife lovers who want a mix of bush and beach. The region is a malaria area, and it is best visited in the drier winter months.

Why visit

Why visit St Lucia?

iSimangaliso combines beach, wetland and bush wildlife, with a relaxed atmosphere and good value.

  • Hippos and crocodiles

    Estuary cruises get close to some of the biggest hippo populations in Africa.

  • Beaches and reefs

    Warm water, coral, snorkelling and diving, without the crowds.

  • Turtles and whales

    Nesting turtles from November to January, and humpback whales from June to November.

  • Big-game nearby

    Hluhluwe–iMfolozi and other reserves have rhinos, lions and elephants.

  • Birdlife

    Over 500 species, including fish eagles and pelicans.

Main attractions

Top things to see in St Lucia

The park has several areas, and most visitors base themselves in St Lucia town.

  1. 01

    St Lucia estuary boat cruise

    A two-hour boat trip up the estuary, where you can see hippos, crocodiles, fish eagles and other birdlife from close range.

    Tip Book the late-afternoon trip.

  2. 02

    Cape Vidal

    A beach and camp on the coast, about 30 km from St Lucia, with reefs for snorkelling, a lookout for whales and a wildlife-rich drive through forest and grassland.

    Tip Go early in the morning.

  3. 03

    Sodwana Bay

    One of South Africa's top diving spots, with warm water and reef fish, about 1.5 hours north of St Lucia.

    Tip Dive schools offer courses.

  4. 04

    Hluhluwe–iMfolozi Park

    One of the oldest protected areas in Africa, established in 1895, with the Big Five and one of the best places to see white and black rhinos.

    Tip Go in the dry season.

  5. 05

    Turtle nesting beaches

    Leatherback and loggerhead turtles come ashore to nest on the beaches of Maputaland, and guided night trips are available from November to January.

    Tip Book a guided tour, and follow the rules.

  6. 06

    Kosi Bay

    A remote set of lakes and channels on the Mozambique border, with traditional fish traps and pristine beaches.

    Tip A 4x4 is needed.

When to go

Best time to visit St Lucia

iSimangaliso has a subtropical climate, with hot, humid summers and mild, dry winters.

  • Summer (November–March)

    Good

    Hot and humid, with turtles nesting and rain.

  • Autumn (April–May)

    Best time

    Warm, dry and clear.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Best time

    Mild and dry, with whales and good game viewing.

  • Spring (September–October)

    Best time

    Warm and dry.

Worth planning around: January and February for heat, humidity and malaria risk.

Travel tips

St Lucia travel tips

A few tips help you stay safe.

  • Do not walk around St Lucia at night, when hippos come ashore.
  • Never swim in the lake or estuary, where there are crocodiles.
  • Check malaria advice and take precautions.
  • Book turtle tours, cruises and camps in advance.
  • Take binoculars, a hat and sunscreen.
  • Follow rangers' instructions.
  • Carry cash for park entry fees.

FAQ

St Lucia: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in iSimangaliso?

Three days is ideal.

Is it a malaria area?

Yes. Consult a travel clinic.

What is the best time to visit?

April to October, with turtles from November to January.

Can I swim at the beach?

At some beaches, such as Cape Vidal, with care. Never swim in the lake.

Can I see rhinos?

Yes, at Hluhluwe–iMfolozi.

Is it safe?

Yes, if you follow wildlife safety rules.
