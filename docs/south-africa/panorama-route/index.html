Welcome to the Panorama Route

The Panorama Route is a scenic drive along the northern part of the Drakensberg escarpment in Mpumalanga, where the high plateau drops away in a series of cliffs, gorges and forests to the hot lowveld below. It links a series of natural attractions in a short distance, and is a natural companion to a visit to Kruger National Park, which lies just below the escarpment to the east.

The centrepiece is the Blyde River Canyon, about 25 km long and up to 750 metres deep, one of the largest canyons in Africa and among the largest green canyons in the world, cloaked in subtropical vegetation. Its viewpoints include the Three Rondavels, three round rock pinnacles that resemble traditional huts, and God's Window, where the ground falls away to the lowveld. Bourke's Luck Potholes, cylindrical holes worn into the rock where two rivers meet, and waterfalls such as Lisbon, Berlin and Mac Mac add to the scenery.

The route is also historic. Pilgrim's Rest, a preserved gold-rush village from the 1870s, has a living museum, and the towns of Graskop and Sabie offer craft shops, cafés and adventure activities. The route can be done in a day, though two days allow a slower pace, and it is best in the dry winter months when the views are clearest.

Why visit

Why visit the Panorama Route?

The Panorama Route is one of the most scenic short drives in South Africa, and easy to combine with Kruger.

  • A great canyon

    Blyde River Canyon has huge cliffs, green slopes and a dam-fed river.

  • Panoramic viewpoints

    God's Window, the Three Rondavels and other lookouts.

  • Waterfalls and potholes

    Lisbon Falls, Mac Mac Falls and Bourke's Luck Potholes.

  • Gold-rush history

    Pilgrim's Rest is a preserved mining village.

  • Easy access

    Sealed roads and short walks between viewpoints.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Panorama Route

Most sights are short stops off the R532 road.

  1. 01

    God's Window

    A viewpoint on the edge of the escarpment, from which the land drops about 900 metres to the lowveld, with views to Kruger on clear days. Mist can hide it.

    Tip Go early, before the cloud.

  2. 02

    The Three Rondavels

    Three round, grass-topped rock pinnacles, resembling traditional huts, rising above the Blyde River Canyon, with the dam far below.

    Tip A short walk from the car park.

  3. 03

    Bourke's Luck Potholes

    Cylindrical potholes carved by whirling water where the Treur and Blyde rivers meet, reached by footbridges over the gorge.

    Tip Allow an hour.

  4. 04

    Lisbon Falls and Berlin Falls

    Waterfalls dropping over the escarpment into the forest, with short paths from car parks.

    Tip Best in summer after rain.

  5. 05

    Mac Mac Falls

    A 65-metre twin waterfall in a forested gorge near Sabie.

    Tip A short stop.

  6. 06

    Pilgrim's Rest

    A gold-rush village from 1873, preserved as a museum, with old shops, houses and a pub.

    Tip Combine with Graskop.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Panorama Route

The route is about scenery, with a few active options.

  1. Drive the route

    Stop at viewpoints and waterfalls.

    Tip Start early.

  2. Ride the Graskop Gorge Lift

    A glass lift that descends into a forested gorge, with walkways.

    Tip Book in advance.

  3. Try the Big Swing

    A giant swing over a gorge.

    Tip For thrill seekers.

  4. Take a boat trip on the Blyde Dam

    See the canyon from below.

    Tip Book a morning trip.

  5. Walk in the forest

    Short trails to waterfalls.

    Tip Wear good shoes.

  6. Visit Pilgrim's Rest

    Wander the historic village.

    Tip Buy a museum ticket.

Local food

What to eat in the Panorama Route

Food is simple, with pancakes, farm stalls and local trout.

  • Graskop pancakes

    Large, thin pancakes with sweet or savoury fillings, a Graskop speciality.

    Where to try it Harrie's Pancakes and cafés.

  • Trout

    Fresh trout from mountain streams.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Dullstroom and Sabie.

  • Braai

    Barbecue with steak and boerewors.

    Where to try it Lodges.

  • Local fruit

    Avocados, mangoes and macadamia nuts.

    Where to try it Farm stalls.

  • Coffee and cake

    In roadside cafés.

    Where to try it Graskop.

Transport

Getting to and around the Panorama Route

The route is about 350 km from Johannesburg, and 1.5 to 2 hours from Kruger's gates.

Getting there

  • Drive from Johannesburg

    About 4 to 5 hours to Graskop.

    Good roads.

  • Fly to Nelspruit or Hoedspruit

    Then a drive of about 1.5 to 2 hours.

    Hire a car.

  • Tours

    Day tours from Kruger area lodges.

    Book in advance.

Getting around

A car is the only practical way to see the route.

  • Hire car

    The best option.

    Drive carefully on mountain roads.

  • Tour

    A guided day trip.

    Book ahead.

  • On foot

    Short walks at each stop.

    Wear good shoes.

When to go

Best time to visit the Panorama Route

The escarpment has warm, wet summers with frequent mist, and mild, dry winters with clear views.

  • Summer (November–March)

    Good

    Green and lush, but hot, misty and stormy.

  • Autumn (April–May)

    Best time

    Clear, warm and dry.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Best time

    Sunny, clear and cool, with excellent views.

  • Spring (September–October)

    Good

    Warming, with clear days.

Worth planning around: Misty summer mornings, when the viewpoints are hidden.

Itinerary

Two days on the Panorama Route

The canyon on the first day, and the waterfalls and villages on the second.

  1. Day1

    The canyon

    Viewpoints.

    • Morning Drive to God's Window.
    • Afternoon The Three Rondavels and Bourke's Luck Potholes.
    • Evening Dinner in Graskop.
  2. Day2

    Waterfalls and history

    Falls and gold.

    • Morning Lisbon and Berlin Falls.
    • Afternoon Pilgrim's Rest.
    • Evening Depart or continue to Kruger.

Travel tips

The Panorama Route travel tips

A few tips help you get the best views.

  • Go early, before the mist and the crowds.
  • Check the weather and visibility forecast.
  • Bring layers, since it can be cool on the escarpment.
  • Watch for street vendors and be polite.
  • Lock your car and do not leave valuables inside.
  • Take cash for entry fees at some sites.
  • Combine with Kruger for a full trip.

FAQ

The Panorama Route: frequently asked questions

How long does the Panorama Route take?

It can be done in a day, but two days allow a relaxed pace.

What is the best time to visit?

April to September, when the air is clear.

Can I combine it with Kruger?

Yes. It is one to two hours from the gates, and a natural add-on.

Is it safe?

Generally yes. Lock your car and keep valuables out of sight.

Do I need a car?

Yes, or a tour.

Is the Blyde River Canyon the third-largest canyon in the world?

It is often described that way, though it depends on how canyons are measured.
