Welcome to the Engadin

The Engadin is the high valley of the Inn river in the south-east canton of Graubünden, running at about 1,800 metres above sea level between wide peaks. It is a place of large skies, blue-green lakes, larch woods and low stone villages with sgraffito-decorated houses, where Romansh, Switzerland's fourth language, is still spoken. St. Moritz, the valley's resort, claims 322 days of sunshine a year, and is where winter tourism began in 1864.

St. Moritz has a reputation for glamour, with luxury hotels, polo on snow, horse races on the frozen lake and the Cresta Run, but the surrounding valley is just as good for ordinary visitors. Lake Sils and Lake Silvaplana are two of the most beautiful Alpine lakes, paths climb through larches to hut restaurants, and quiet villages such as Sils Maria, where Nietzsche wrote, and Pontresina offer a calmer stay.

The Engadin is also the heart of the scenic railways of Graubünden. The Bernina Express crosses the Bernina Pass from the Engadin to Italy, and the Albula and Bernina lines, with their curved viaducts and spirals, are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. The Glacier Express, slowest of the fast trains, crosses the Landwasser Viaduct and runs to Zermatt. Three days is a good time to enjoy the valley.

Why visit

Why visit the Engadin?

The Engadin combines sunshine, lakes and the most famous railways in Europe.

  • Iconic railways

    The Bernina and Glacier Express.

  • Turquoise lakes

    Sils, Silvaplana and St. Moritz.

  • Sunshine and light

    A high valley with big skies.

  • Hiking and skiing

    Wide trails and snow-sure slopes.

  • Romansh villages

    Painted stone houses.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Engadin

Sights are spread along the valley, linked by train and bus.

  1. 01

    Lake St. Moritz

    A lake ringed by hotels and pines, which freezes in winter for horse racing and other events.

    Tip Walk the 4-km lake path.

  2. 02

    Landwasser Viaduct

    A curved, six-arch stone viaduct 65 metres high, opened in 1904, on the UNESCO-listed Albula line.

    Tip Photograph it from the viewpoint at Filisur.

  3. 03

    Brusio spiral viaduct

    A circular viaduct on the Bernina line, where the train loops around to lose height on the way to Italy.

    Tip Ride the Bernina Express.

  4. 04

    Lago Bianco and the Bernina Pass

    A high pass at 2,253 metres, with a pale lake beside the railway and views of glaciers.

    Tip Ride in the window seat on the right.

  5. 05

    Lake Sils and Sils Maria

    A long lake with a peninsula, and a quiet village where Nietzsche lived.

    Tip Walk along the shore.

  6. 06

    Muottas Muragl

    A funicular to a viewpoint over the Engadin lakes.

    Tip Go at sunset.

Best things to do in the Engadin

The Engadin is for trains, lakes and walks.

  1. Ride the Bernina Express

    The panoramic route to Zermatt.

    Tip Book ahead.

    An easy lakeside path.

    Tip Start in the morning.

  4. Ski or snowshoe

    Winter runs and trails.

    Tip Book equipment.

  5. Visit Pontresina

  6. Sail or windsurf

    On Lake Silvaplana.

    Tip Winds are best in the afternoon.

Getting to and around the Engadin

The Engadin is reached by rail from Chur.

Getting there

  • Train from Chur

    About two hours via the Albula line to St. Moritz.

    UNESCO-listed and scenic.

  • Train from Zurich

    About 3.5 hours with a change in Chur.

    Frequent.

  • Bernina Express from Tirano

    From Italy over the Bernina Pass.

    Reserve a seat.

Travel tips

The Engadin travel tips

A few tips help you plan.

  • Reserve scenic trains early.
  • Ask your hotel for a guest card.
  • Stay outside St. Moritz to save money.
  • Bring warm layers.
  • Watch the weather.
  • Try the walnut tart.
  • Book ski gear in advance.

FAQ

The Engadin: frequently asked questions

Is St. Moritz worth visiting?

Yes, for the lakes and railways, even if you skip the luxury.

How many days do you need?

Three.

What is the Bernina Express?

A panoramic train from Chur or St. Moritz to Tirano, Italy.

Is the Glacier Express worth it?

For scenery yes, though slow and pricey.

What language is spoken?

German and Romansh, with English widely understood.

When is the best time?

June to September, or winter for skiing.
