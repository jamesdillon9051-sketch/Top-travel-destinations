Welcome to Jotunheimen

Jotunheimen, literally 'home of the giants,' genuinely holds all of Norway's peaks over 2,300 metres, including Galdhøpiggen, the country's highest point, and a landscape of real high-alpine terrain rare this far north in Europe.

It's also genuinely home to the Besseggen ridge, widely considered Norway's most famous single-day hike — a narrow ridge walk with two differently coloured lakes visible on either side, popular enough that it can draw real crowds in peak summer.

Beyond Besseggen, Jotunheimen genuinely rewards those with more time: a network of staffed DNT mountain huts links multi-day routes across the range, and Sognefjellet, Northern Europe's highest mountain pass road, gives access to genuinely dramatic scenery without technical climbing.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Jotunheimen

Jotunheimen's essential sights genuinely span its highest peaks, its most famous ridge walk, and the road connecting them.

  1. 01

    Besseggen Ridge

    Genuinely Norway's most famous day hike, a narrow ridge with turquoise Lake Gjende on one side and deep blue Lake Bessvatnet on the other.

    Tip Take genuinely the boat across Lake Gjende to start the hike from Memurubu, making the route one-way and downhill-heavy.

  2. 02

    Galdhøpiggen

    Genuinely Norway's highest peak at 2,469 metres, reachable by a demanding but non-technical hike in summer, including a glacier crossing.

    Tip The genuinely glacier crossing near the summit requires a guide — never attempt it independently.

  3. 03

    Storebjørn

    Genuinely one of Jotunheimen's striking peaks, its name meaning 'big bear,' visible across much of the central park.

    Tip Several genuinely trailheads offer good views of Storebjørn without requiring a summit attempt.

  4. 04

    Sognefjellet Road

    Genuinely Northern Europe's highest mountain pass road, crossing dramatic high-alpine terrain between Jotunheimen and the Sognefjord region.

    Tip The genuinely road is open only from around May to October — check current status before planning a crossing.

  5. 05

    Spiterstulen

    A genuinely popular mountain lodge and trailhead, the most common starting point for Galdhøpiggen ascents.

    Tip Book genuinely accommodation here well ahead in peak summer — it's a busy trailhead hub.

  6. 06

    Valdresflya

    A genuinely vast, open high mountain plateau, crossed by a scenic road with sweeping views and a real chance of wild reindeer sightings.

    Tip The genuinely plateau's open scale is best appreciated by stopping at one of several roadside viewpoints.

  7. 07

    Loftet

    Genuinely one of the range's many striking, less-visited peaks, offering a quieter alternative to the busier Besseggen trail.

    Tip Genuinely quieter trails like this reward hikers willing to research beyond the single most famous route.

Things to do

Best things to do in Jotunheimen

Jotunheimen genuinely rewards serious hiking, whether a single iconic day route or a multi-day hut-to-hut trek.

  1. Hike the Besseggen Ridge

    Genuinely the essential Jotunheimen experience, a demanding but non-technical full-day hike with unforgettable views throughout.

    Tip Allow genuinely 6-8 hours and start early — the trail can get crowded by midday in peak summer.

  2. Summit Galdhøpiggen

    A genuinely serious full-day undertaking to stand on Norway's highest point, including a guided glacier crossing.

    Tip Book genuinely a certified glacier guide through Spiterstulen or Juvasshytta — never cross independently.

  3. Drive Sognefjellet's high mountain pass

    A genuinely spectacular way to see Jotunheimen's high peaks without hiking, crossing Northern Europe's highest road pass.

    Tip Several genuinely viewpoints along the road make worthwhile stops even on a through-drive.

  4. Cross Lake Gjende by boat

    A genuinely popular way to shorten or extend the Besseggen hike, with a scenic boat service running the length of the lake.

    Tip Book genuinely boat tickets ahead in peak summer — services can fill up on busy days.

  5. Stay at a DNT mountain lodge

    A genuinely comfortable way to experience multi-day hiking, with staffed huts offering hot meals and real beds along the trail network.

    Tip Membership genuinely in the Norwegian Trekking Association (DNT) gets you discounted hut rates.

  6. Spot wild reindeer on the high plateaus

    A genuinely real possibility across Jotunheimen's open highland areas, particularly around Valdresflya.

    Tip Bring genuinely binoculars and keep a respectful distance if you do spot a herd.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants in Valdres and Gudbrandsdalen, near Jotunheimen's edges.

  • Fårikål

    Genuinely hearty mountain-lodge fare, a welcome hot meal after a long day's hike.

  • Brunost (brown cheese)

Transport

Getting to and around Jotunheimen

Jotunheimen is genuinely reached by a scenic drive from Oslo or Bergen, with no airport of its own within the park.

Getting there

  • Rental car from Oslo

    Genuinely the most flexible way in, about 4-5 hours to the main trailheads.

    Route E6 and Route 51 connect Oslo to Jotunheimen's eastern trailheads.

  • Rental car from Bergen via Sognefjellet

    A genuinely spectacular approach, crossing Northern Europe's highest mountain pass road directly into the range.

    Only open roughly May to October — check current status.

  • Bus to Otta or Lom

    A genuinely workable option for visitors without a car, with onward local transport to trailheads.

    Connects to Oslo and other major towns, though less flexible than driving.

Getting around

Within Jotunheimen, getting around genuinely means hiking, with the Lake Gjende boat and mountain roads connecting trailheads.

  • On foot

    Genuinely the only way to reach the range's interior — this is a hiking destination, not a drive-through one.

    A well-marked trail network connects trailheads and DNT huts throughout the park.

  • Lake Gjende boat

    Genuinely essential for many Besseggen hikers, shortening or extending the classic route.

    Runs regularly between Gjendesheim and Memurubu in season.

  • Rental car between trailheads

    Genuinely necessary for reaching different parts of the park, since trailheads are spread across a wide area.

    Roads connecting trailheads are well maintained but seasonal at higher elevations.

When to go

Best time to visit Jotunheimen

Jotunheimen's genuinely high elevation means a shorter hiking season than lower-lying parts of Norway, with snow lingering into early summer some years.

  • Mid-summer (July–August)

    Best time

    Genuinely the safest window, with snow cleared from most trails and DNT huts fully staffed.

  • Late June and September

    Good

    Genuinely quieter, though some snow can linger on higher routes into late June.

  • Winter (October–May)

    Good

    Genuinely for experienced winter mountaineers only — most facilities and roads close for the season.

  • Any summer weekday

    Good

    Genuinely quieter than weekends on the Besseggen trail specifically, worth considering if your schedule allows.

Worth planning around: Avoid genuinely attempting Besseggen or Galdhøpiggen outside roughly late June to September, when snow and ice make both routes genuinely hazardous.

Travel tips

Jotunheimen travel tips

Jotunheimen demands more preparation than Norway's coastal regions — plan accordingly.

  • Book DNT genuinely hut beds and Lake Gjende boat tickets well ahead in peak summer.
  • Never genuinely attempt a Galdhøpiggen glacier crossing without a certified guide.
  • Pack genuinely full hiking gear, even for a single day route — mountain weather changes fast.
  • Check genuinely Sognefjellet's seasonal opening status before planning your route around it.
  • Bring genuinely more cash and supplies than expected — facilities are limited outside main lodges.
  • Consider genuinely a DNT membership if planning multiple hut stays — it pays for itself quickly.

FAQ

Jotunheimen: frequently asked questions

Is the Besseggen ridge hike dangerous?

Not genuinely for reasonably fit hikers on a clear day, though the narrow ridge sections and real elevation gain demand respect and proper gear.

How long is the Besseggen hike?

About genuinely 14km one-way, taking 6-8 hours for most hikers, often shortened using the Lake Gjende boat.

Do you need a guide to climb Galdhøpiggen?

Yes, genuinely, for the standard glacier-crossing route — never attempt this section independently.

When is Jotunheimen open for hiking?

Genuinely roughly late June to September for the main routes, with snow often lingering into early summer at altitude.

How do you get to Jotunheimen?

By genuinely rental car from Oslo or Bergen, or by bus to a gateway town like Otta or Lom with onward local transport.

Is Jotunheimen good for multi-day trekking?

Yes, genuinely excellent — the DNT staffed hut network links multi-day routes across the range for hikers of varying experience.
