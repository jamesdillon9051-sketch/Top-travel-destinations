Welcome to Stavanger & Preikestolen

Stavanger is genuinely one of Norway's most food-forward cities, a compact harbour town whose oil-industry wealth has funded a restaurant scene punching well above its size, all wrapped around Gamle Stavanger's rows of immaculately preserved white wooden houses.

It's also genuinely the launch point for Norway's single most iconic day hike — Preikestolen, a flat rock plateau jutting 604 metres straight above Lysefjord, reached by a well-marked but genuinely strenuous trail that rewards the effort with one of the most photographed views in the whole country.

For those wanting to go further, Kjeragbolten — a boulder famously wedged in a crevasse even higher above the same fjord — pushes the drama further still, for visitors willing to commit to a longer, tougher day.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Stavanger & Preikestolen

Stavanger's essential sights combine its historic old town with two of Norway's most dramatic natural landmarks nearby.

  1. 01

    Preikestolen (Pulpit Rock)

    Genuinely Norway's most iconic hike, a flat rock plateau standing 604 metres above Lysefjord with no railing at the edge.

    Tip The genuinely round trip takes 4-5 hours — start early to avoid both crowds and afternoon weather changes.

  2. 02

    Lysefjord

    Genuinely the dramatic fjord beneath both Preikestolen and Kjerag, its steep granite walls among the most striking in Norway.

    Tip A genuinely fjord cruise gives a completely different perspective, looking up at the cliffs you might otherwise look down from.

  3. 03

    Gamle Stavanger

    Genuinely one of Europe's best-preserved wooden towns, over 170 white 18th and 19th-century houses still lived in today.

    Tip The genuinely cobbled streets are especially atmospheric in early morning, before the day's visitors arrive.

  4. 04

    Kjeragbolten

    Genuinely a famous boulder wedged tightly in a crevasse nearly 1,000 metres above Lysefjord, a bucket-list photo spot for serious hikers.

    Tip This genuinely hike is significantly tougher and longer than Preikestolen — only attempt it if properly prepared.

  5. 05

    Stavanger Cathedral

    Genuinely Norway's oldest cathedral, consecrated around 1125 and still in active use at the heart of the city centre.

    Tip The genuinely interior's medieval and baroque details reward a slower, quieter visit outside service times.

  6. 06

    Norwegian Petroleum Museum

    A genuinely well-presented museum explaining how North Sea oil transformed Stavanger from a quiet fishing town into a wealthy modern city.

    Tip Genuinely worth an hour even for visitors not usually drawn to industrial history — the exhibits are engaging.

  7. 07

    Stavanger Harbour

    Genuinely the city's lively waterfront, lined with restaurants and bars and busy with fishing boats and fjord cruise departures.

    Tip Evening genuinely brings the harbourfront restaurants to life, especially in summer.

Things to do

Best things to do in Stavanger & Preikestolen

Stavanger genuinely rewards combining serious hiking with the city's own food and history.

  1. Hike to Preikestolen's edge

    Genuinely the essential Stavanger-area experience, a well-marked but strenuous trail to one of Norway's most photographed views.

    Tip Genuinely check the weather forecast — the view is dramatically better on a clear day.

  2. Cruise Lysefjord beneath the cliffs

    A genuinely relaxed alternative or complement to the hikes, taking in Preikestolen and Kjerag's scale from the water.

    Tip Cruises genuinely often point out waterfalls and wildlife not visible from the clifftop trails above.

  3. Stand on Kjeragbolten

    A genuinely serious bucket-list challenge for experienced hikers, standing on a boulder wedged nearly 1,000 metres above the fjord.

    Tip This genuinely hike involves chains and steep sections — only for properly prepared, experienced hikers.

  4. Wander Gamle Stavanger's cobbled streets

    A genuinely peaceful wander through Europe's best-preserved wooden old town, with small galleries and cafés tucked among the houses.

    Tip Many genuinely of the houses have small gardens worth a glance as you pass.

  5. Learn about Norway's oil history

    A genuinely worthwhile rainy-day option, explaining how North Sea oil reshaped both Stavanger and the whole Norwegian economy.

    Tip The genuinely museum's own café and gift shop are better than average for a museum of this kind.

  6. Wander the harbour at sunset

    A genuinely relaxed way to end a day, with the harbourfront's restaurants and bars filling up as the light softens.

    Tip Summer's genuinely long evenings give a wide window for a leisurely harbourside dinner.

Transport

Getting to and around Stavanger & Preikestolen

Stavanger is genuinely well connected by air, with the Preikestolen trailhead a short drive or ferry away.

Getting there

  • Flight to Stavanger Airport, Sola

    Genuinely well connected to Oslo and other major European cities, about 30 minutes from the city centre.

    A direct bus and train connect the airport to central Stavanger.

  • Ferry to the Preikestolen trailhead

    A genuinely scenic way to reach the trail, crossing the fjord from central Stavanger.

    Combined ferry-and-bus tickets are widely available in summer.

  • Coastal ferry or train from other Norwegian cities

    Genuinely connects Stavanger to Bergen and other southwestern towns.

    Useful for combining Stavanger with a wider Norway itinerary.

Getting around

Stavanger's genuinely compact centre is easily walkable, with organised transport handling the Preikestolen and Lysefjord trips.

  • On foot

    Genuinely covers Gamle Stavanger, the cathedral and the harbour easily.

    Central Stavanger's main sights sit within a comfortable walking radius.

  • Shuttle bus to Preikestolen

    Genuinely the standard way to reach the trailhead without a rental car.

    Runs regularly in season, combined with a short ferry crossing.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely useful for reaching Kjerag and exploring the wider region at your own pace.

    Roads to trailheads are well maintained but can be busy in peak summer.

When to go

Best time to visit Stavanger & Preikestolen

Both major hikes genuinely require snow-free trails, making the warmer months the only realistic window for either.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Genuinely the most reliable hiking conditions and longest daylight, also the busiest trail season.

  • Shoulder months (May, September)

    Good

    Genuinely good hiking conditions with noticeably thinner crowds on the trails.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Genuinely hazardous for Preikestolen and Kjerag without full winter mountaineering experience — most visitors skip the hikes.

  • Any season for the city

    Good

    Genuinely, Stavanger's food scene and Gamle Stavanger work well year-round, independent of hiking conditions.

Worth planning around: Avoid genuinely attempting Preikestolen or Kjerag in icy or snowy conditions without proper winter hiking experience and gear.

Travel tips

Stavanger & Preikestolen travel tips

A handful of practical notes make a Stavanger and Preikestolen trip go far more smoothly.

  • Check genuinely the weather forecast before committing to the Preikestolen or Kjerag hike.
  • Wear genuinely proper hiking boots and bring layers, even in summer.
  • Start genuinely early to beat both crowds and any afternoon weather changes.

FAQ

Stavanger & Preikestolen: frequently asked questions

How hard is the Preikestolen hike?

Genuinely moderate — about 3.8km each way with real elevation gain, manageable for most reasonably fit hikers in 4-5 hours round trip.

Is Kjeragbolten harder than Preikestolen?

Yes, genuinely significantly so — it's longer, steeper and involves chain-assisted sections, suited to experienced hikers only.

How do you get to the Preikestolen trailhead?

By genuinely ferry and shuttle bus from central Stavanger, or by rental car directly to the trailhead car park.

Is Stavanger worth visiting beyond Preikestolen?

Yes, genuinely — Gamle Stavanger's old town and the city's food scene are both worthwhile in their own right.

When can you hike Preikestolen?

Genuinely best May to September — winter conditions make the trail hazardous without proper mountaineering experience.

How many days do you need in Stavanger?

Two to three days genuinely covers the Preikestolen hike and the city's own sights at a comfortable pace.
