Welcome to Bergen

Bergen is genuinely Norway's traditional gateway to the fjords, a colourful harbour city ringed by seven mountains and centred on Bryggen, a UNESCO World Heritage wharf of tilted, brightly painted wooden buildings dating back to the city's Hanseatic trading days.

The mountains that surround it are genuinely part of the city's identity, not just scenery — funiculars and cable cars up Fløyen and Ulriken give sweeping views over the harbour within minutes of the centre, and locals treat the surrounding trails as a normal part of daily life.

Bergen's genuinely famous rain has become something of a local joke, but the city makes a strong case for itself even on a wet day, with world-class museums, an excellent fish market and Bryggen's wharf all genuinely worth exploring regardless of weather.

Why visit

Why visit Bergen?

Bergen's case rests on genuine history and mountain scenery packed into a compact, walkable harbour city.

  • Bryggen is genuinely a UNESCO World Heritage site

    Tilted, colourful wooden buildings dating to the city's medieval Hanseatic trading days, still standing right on the harbour.

  • It's genuinely Norway's traditional fjord gateway

    The classic starting point for cruises and day trips into the western fjords, including Hardangerfjord and Sognefjord.

  • The surrounding mountains are genuinely part of daily life

    Funiculars and cable cars put sweeping views within minutes of the harbour, not a special-occasion trip.

  • The fish market is genuinely a real working institution

    Fresh, high-quality seafood sold right on the harbour, not a purely touristic display.

  • It's genuinely more compact than Oslo

    Most major sights sit within an easy walk of Bryggen, making it a relaxed, unhurried city to explore.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Bergen

Bergen's essential sights combine its historic wharf with the mountains rising directly above the city.

  1. 01

    Bryggen

    Genuinely Bergen's defining sight, a UNESCO-listed row of tilted, brightly painted wooden buildings dating to the city's medieval Hanseatic trading era.

    Tip Duck genuinely into the narrow passageways behind the main facades — much of Bryggen's character is hidden just out of sight.

  2. 02

    Mount Fløyen

    A genuinely sweeping viewpoint over the city and harbour, reached by a historic funicular that's been running since 1918.

    Tip Walk genuinely back down through the forest trails rather than taking the funicular both ways.

  3. 03

    Fisketorget (Fish Market)

    A genuinely real working fish market, selling fresh catch, smoked salmon and prepared seafood right on the harbour front.

    Tip Prices genuinely favour buying a bit less from several stalls rather than a lot from one — compare before ordering.

  4. 04

    Bergenhus Fortress

    A genuinely significant medieval fortress guarding the harbour entrance, including the 13th-century Håkon's Hall.

    Tip The genuinely fortress grounds are free to wander, with fine harbour views included.

  5. 05

    Mount Ulriken

    Genuinely Bergen's highest peak, reached by cable car, with hiking trails and views extending well beyond the city itself.

    Tip The genuinely trail between Ulriken and Fløyen (Vidden) is a serious but rewarding full-day hike for fit visitors.

  6. 06

    Hanseatic Museum

    A genuinely evocative preserved trading house, giving real insight into the harsh daily life of the city's medieval German merchants.

    Tip The genuinely original, unheated interiors make clear just how tough Hanseatic trading life really was.

  7. 07

    Troldhaugen

    Genuinely the lakeside home of composer Edvard Grieg, preserved with his piano and personal belongings, a short trip from the centre.

    Tip Summer genuinely brings lunchtime concerts in the lakeside concert hall — check the schedule ahead.

Things to do

Best things to do in Bergen

Bergen genuinely rewards mixing its historic wharf with the mountains and water that surround it.

  1. Wander Bryggen's wooden alleys

    A genuinely absorbing wander through the narrow passageways behind Bryggen's main facades, now home to small craft shops and galleries.

    Tip Early genuinely morning, before the day-trip crowds arrive, gives the most atmospheric photos.

  2. Ride the funicular up Fløyen

    A genuinely quick, easy way to get one of Bergen's best views, whether for a short visit or as the start of a longer hike.

    Tip Sunset genuinely rides are popular in summer — book ahead if visiting during peak season.

  3. Sample fresh seafood at the fish market

    A genuinely essential Bergen experience, sampling fresh catch, smoked salmon or a seafood platter right on the harbour.

    Tip Lunchtime genuinely offers the freshest selection before the day's catch starts running low.

  4. Ride the cable car up Ulriken

    A genuinely quick way to Bergen's highest peak, with a restaurant and hiking trails waiting at the top.

    Tip Clear genuinely days offer views extending well out to the surrounding fjords and islands.

  5. Visit Troldhaugen for a lakeside concert

    A genuinely lovely way to spend an afternoon, exploring Grieg's preserved home and, in summer, catching a lakeside piano concert.

    Tip Book genuinely concert tickets ahead — the small hall fills up quickly in summer.

  6. Day-trip to a nearby fjord

    A genuinely easy way to see real fjord scenery without committing to a multi-day trip, with Hardangerfjord and Sognefjord both reachable in a day.

    Tip Organised genuinely day tours handle the transport logistics for visitors without a rental car.

Local food

What to eat in Bergen

As Norway's genuine historic fish trading capital, Bergen's food leans heavily on fresh and preserved seafood.

  • Salmon (gravlax)

    Genuinely exceptional in Bergen, sold fresh or cured at the fish market and served throughout the city's restaurants.

    Where to try it The fish market and harbourside restaurants.

  • Brunost (brown cheese)

    A genuinely essential taste of Norway, widely available at cafés and breakfast tables across the city.

    Where to try it Cafés and hotel breakfasts throughout Bergen.

  • Fiskesuppe (fish soup)

    A genuinely creamy, warming fish soup, a Bergen specialty and a real comfort on the city's famously rainy days.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout the city centre.

  • Klippfisk (dried salted cod)

    Genuinely central to Bergen's historic trading wealth, dried and salted cod once exported worldwide from this very harbour.

    Where to try it The fish market and traditional Bergen restaurants.

  • Fresh shrimp

    A genuinely popular harbourside treat, peeled and eaten fresh, sometimes bought straight from a boat.

    Where to try it The fish market and harbourfront vendors.

  • Cinnamon buns (kanelboller)

    A genuinely essential Norwegian bakery treat, worth pairing with a coffee at one of Bergen's many independent cafés.

    Where to try it Bakeries and cafés throughout the city centre.

Transport

Getting to and around Bergen

Bergen is genuinely well connected to Oslo by one of the world's great scenic train journeys, plus direct flights.

Getting there

  • The Bergen Line train from Oslo

    A genuinely spectacular roughly 7-hour journey crossing Norway's mountains directly.

    Widely considered one of the world's great scenic rail routes in its own right.

  • Flight to Bergen Flesland

    Genuinely the fastest option from Oslo or international destinations, about 30 minutes from the city centre.

    A light rail line connects the airport directly to central Bergen.

  • Fjord cruise or ferry

    A genuinely scenic way to arrive if combining Bergen with a wider fjord itinerary.

    Several coastal and fjord routes connect Bergen to other western Norway towns.

Getting around

Bergen's genuinely compact centre is easily walkable, with funiculars and cable cars covering the surrounding heights.

  • On foot

    Genuinely covers Bryggen, the fish market and Bergenhus Fortress easily.

    Central Bergen's main sights sit within a comfortable walking radius of each other.

  • Funicular and cable car

    Genuinely the standard way to reach Fløyen and Ulriken's viewpoints above the city.

    Both run frequently and are quick, scenic rides in their own right.

  • Light rail (Bybanen)

    Genuinely useful for reaching Troldhaugen and other sights slightly further from the centre.

    Runs frequently and connects to the airport as well.

When to go

Best time to visit Bergen

Bergen is genuinely famous for rain in any season, but summer brings the longest daylight and most reliable conditions for fjord day trips.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Genuinely the longest daylight and most reliable weather for fjord cruises and mountain viewpoints.

  • Shoulder months (May, September)

    Good

    Genuinely good weather odds with thinner crowds than peak summer.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Genuinely atmospheric and moody, with museums and Bryggen's covered passageways offering shelter from the rain.

  • Any season

    Good

    Genuinely, Bergen's famous rain means no season is guaranteed dry — pack a proper waterproof regardless.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Bergen's genuinely rainy reputation means every season carries some chance of wet weather, so plan for it rather than around it.

Practical notes that make a Bergen stay noticeably smoother.

FAQ

Bergen: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Bergen?

Two to three days genuinely covers Bryggen, the fish market and a mountain viewpoint at a comfortable pace.

Is Bergen the best base for fjord trips?

Yes, genuinely — it's Norway's traditional fjord gateway, with day cruises and multi-day routes both departing from the city.

Does it really rain that much in Bergen?

Yes, genuinely — Bergen is famously one of Europe's rainiest cities, so a proper waterproof is worth packing regardless of season.

How do you get from Oslo to Bergen?

The genuinely scenic Bergen Line train takes about 7 hours, or a direct flight takes under an hour.

Is Bergen expensive?

Yes, genuinely, like most of Norway — supermarket meals and a hostel or guesthouse help manage costs.

What is Bergen known for?

Genuinely its UNESCO-listed Bryggen wharf, its seven surrounding mountains, and its role as the historic gateway to Norway's western fjords.
