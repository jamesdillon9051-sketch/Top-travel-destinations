Welcome to Svalbard

Svalbard is genuinely a different category of destination from the rest of this guide — an Arctic archipelago roughly halfway between mainland Norway and the North Pole, still home to more polar bears than the roughly 2,500 people who live there.

Longyearbyen, the world's northernmost settlement of its size, serves as the genuine gateway to everything else — a small, surprisingly well-equipped town where independent travel outside the settlement is genuinely restricted for safety, given the real presence of polar bears across the archipelago.

Visiting genuinely means joining guided expeditions, whether by boat, snowmobile or dog sled, out into a landscape of glaciers, fjords and tundra that looks, and largely is, close to untouched — a genuine bucket-list trip rather than a casual add-on to a Norway itinerary.

Why visit

Why visit Svalbard?

Svalbard's case rests on genuinely offering one of the last true Arctic wildernesses reachable by a scheduled flight.

  • It's genuinely one of the best places to see wild polar bears

    Svalbard holds one of the world's healthiest polar bear populations, viewable on guided boat and snowmobile expeditions.

  • Longyearbyen is genuinely a real, functioning town

    Not a research outpost alone — shops, restaurants and hotels make the Arctic genuinely accessible.

  • The landscape is genuinely close to untouched

    Glaciers cover over half the archipelago, with tundra and jagged peaks filling most of the rest.

  • The Global Seed Vault is genuinely a real, working piece of history

    A striking, purpose-built facility safeguarding the world's crop diversity, visible from outside even though it's closed to visitors.

  • It's genuinely unlike anywhere else in this guide

    A true Arctic wilderness trip, for travellers wanting something well beyond a standard Norway itinerary.

Svalbard's essential sights genuinely span Longyearbyen itself and the wilderness reached only by guided expedition beyond it.

    Genuinely the archipelago's most famous residents, spotted from a safe distance on guided boat or snowmobile expeditions.

    Tip Never genuinely venture outside Longyearbyen without an armed, experienced guide — this is a real safety requirement, not a formality.

    Svalbard Global Seed Vault

    A genuinely striking facility built into a mountainside, safeguarding backup seed samples from gene banks worldwide against global catastrophe.

    Tip The genuinely vault itself is closed to visitors, but the distinctive entrance is a short, worthwhile walk from town.

  5. 05

    Svalbard Museum

    A genuinely excellent introduction to the archipelago's natural history, mining past and Arctic wildlife.

    Tip Genuinely worth visiting on arrival — it gives real context for everything you'll see afterward.

  6. 06

    Reinsdyrflya Tundra

    Genuinely home to a distinct, stocky subspecies of reindeer found only on Svalbard, grazing across open tundra plains.

    Tip Svalbard's genuinely reindeer are notably unafraid of people, given the near-total absence of natural predators on land.

  7. 07

    Dog sledding terrain

    Genuinely open snow and tundra terrain surrounding Longyearbyen, home to several working dog sledding kennels.

    Tip Meeting genuinely the dogs at a kennel, even without a full sledding trip, is a worthwhile stop in itself.

Things to do

Best things to do in Svalbard

A Svalbard visit genuinely revolves around guided expeditions — independent travel outside Longyearbyen is restricted for real safety reasons.

  1. Spot polar bears on a guided boat trip

    Genuinely the trip most visitors come for, scanning the coastline and ice from a safe, expert-guided boat.

    Tip Summer genuinely boat expeditions generally offer better sighting odds than winter's limited daylight.

  2. Go dog sledding across the tundra

    A genuinely classic Arctic experience, mushing across snow-covered terrain with a team of eager huskies.

    Tip Dress genuinely in serious layers — even short trips expose you to real Arctic cold.

  3. Cruise past Nordenskiöldbreen

    A genuinely relaxed way to take in Svalbard's glacial scale, watching ice calve into the fjord from a safe distance.

    Tip Bring genuinely binoculars — seals and seabirds are often spotted near the glacier front too.

  4. See the Global Seed Vault's entrance

    A genuinely quick but worthwhile stop, walking distance from Longyearbyen, even though the vault itself stays closed to visitors.

    Tip The genuinely walk here also gives good views back over Longyearbyen and the surrounding fjord.

  5. Snowmobile across a glacier

    A genuinely thrilling way to cover real distance across Svalbard's ice-covered interior, only possible with a guided operator.

    Tip This genuinely activity runs only in winter and early spring, when snow and ice cover is reliable.

  6. Learn Arctic survival history at the museum

    A genuinely worthwhile rainy or windy-day option, covering the archipelago's mining, trapping and exploration history.

    Tip Allow genuinely about an hour to take in the exhibits properly.

Transport

Getting to and around Svalbard

Svalbard is genuinely reached only by direct flight from mainland Norway — there is no road or ferry access.

Getting there

  • Flight to Svalbard Airport, Longyear

    Genuinely the only practical way in, with direct flights from Oslo and Tromsø.

    The airport sits just a few minutes from central Longyearbyen.

  • Expedition cruise

    A genuinely popular way to see the wider archipelago beyond Longyearbyen, often starting or ending with a flight.

    Multi-day cruises circle parts of Svalbard, going well beyond what's reachable on day trips.

Getting around

Longyearbyen itself is genuinely walkable, but everything beyond town requires a licensed guide.

  • On foot within Longyearbyen

    Genuinely fine for exploring the town's centre, museum and seed vault viewpoint.

    Even short walks beyond town limits carry real polar bear risk — stay within marked safe zones.

  • Guided boat expeditions

    Genuinely the standard way to see glaciers, wildlife and the wider fjord system.

    Operators range from small groups to larger expedition vessels.

  • Snowmobile or dog sled (winter/spring)

    Genuinely the way to cover ground across Svalbard's snow-covered interior.

    Always guided, given both terrain and wildlife safety considerations.

When to go

Best time to visit Svalbard

Svalbard genuinely offers two very different seasons, both worth visiting for different reasons.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Genuinely 24-hour daylight, open water for boat expeditions, and the best wildlife-viewing conditions.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Good

    Genuinely excellent for snowmobile and dog sledding trips, with lengthening daylight returning.

  • Polar night (November–January)

    Good

    Genuinely 24-hour darkness, a dramatic and unusual experience, with a real chance of the northern lights.

  • Deep winter (February)

    Good

    Genuinely extremely cold with limited daylight returning — a demanding time to visit even for prepared travellers.

Worth planning around: Avoid genuinely underestimating Svalbard's cold and safety requirements in any season — this is a true Arctic environment demanding real preparation.

Travel tips

Svalbard travel tips

Svalbard genuinely demands more preparation and respect for safety than anywhere else in this guide.

  • Never genuinely venture outside Longyearbyen's marked safe zone without an armed, licensed guide.
  • Book genuinely guided expeditions well ahead — capacity is limited and demand is high in peak season.
  • Pack genuinely serious cold-weather gear, even in summer.
  • Bring genuinely more cash and supplies than you think you'll need — Longyearbyen is the only real town.
  • Respect genuinely all wildlife viewing distances set by your guide, for your safety and the animals'.
  • Budget genuinely significantly more than mainland Norway — Svalbard's remoteness makes everything more expensive.

FAQ

Svalbard: frequently asked questions

Is it safe to visit Svalbard?

Yes, genuinely, with proper precautions — stay within Longyearbyen's safe zone unless with an armed, licensed guide, and the real polar bear risk is well managed by local operators.

How do you get to Svalbard?

By genuinely direct flight from Oslo or Tromsø — there's no road, rail or ferry access to the archipelago.

Will you definitely see a polar bear?

No, genuinely never guaranteed — but Svalbard offers some of the best realistic odds anywhere on Earth with a guided expedition.

Is Svalbard expensive?

Yes, genuinely significantly more than mainland Norway, reflecting the real cost of operating this far into the Arctic.

How many days do you need in Svalbard?

Three to five days genuinely allows a proper mix of Longyearbyen's sights, a wildlife boat trip and a dog sledding or snowmobile excursion.

Not genuinely inside — it's closed to visitors for security reasons, though the distinctive entrance is viewable from outside.
