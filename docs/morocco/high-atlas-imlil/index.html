Welcome to Imlil

The High Atlas is the mountain range that runs across Morocco from the Atlantic to Algeria, and it holds the highest peaks in North Africa. Imlil, at about 1,740 metres, is a small village at the head of the Mizane Valley, roughly 60 km from Marrakech, and the starting point for most walks in Toubkal National Park. Jebel Toubkal (4,167 metres) rises above it.

The area is home to Amazigh (Berber) communities who farm terraced fields and walnut orchards, and who have hosted trekkers for decades. The villages are built of stone and earth, and the paths between them, once used by mules and shepherds, offer some of the finest walking in the country.

The September 2023 earthquake, which was centred in the Al Haouz province of the High Atlas, caused severe damage and killed close to 3,000 people, and the Imlil area was among those affected. Rebuilding has continued, and many guesthouses and trails have reopened, but conditions vary, so check locally before you travel.

Why visit

Why visit Imlil?

Imlil is the quickest escape from Marrakech into real mountains, with a warm welcome and superb walking.

  • North Africa's highest peak

    Toubkal is a straightforward, non-technical climb in summer, though demanding at altitude.

  • Village-to-village walks

    Easy day walks and multi-day treks connect Amazigh villages with mule tracks.

  • Cool air and space

    A refreshing change from the heat of Marrakech in summer.

  • Mountain hospitality

    Guesthouses and mountain gîtes serve home-cooked tagines, with tea on the terrace.

  • Close to Marrakech

    A day trip or a two-day stay is easily arranged.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Imlil

The scenery is the main attraction, and the walks range from short to demanding.

  1. 01

    Jebel Toubkal

    The highest summit in North Africa at 4,167 metres, usually climbed in two days, with a night at the Toubkal refuge (Neltner Refuge, at about 3,200 metres).

    Tip Allow time to acclimatise, and take a guide.

  2. 02

    Aroumd (Armed) village

    A stone village on the slope above Imlil, with terraced fields and guesthouses, on the route to the refuge.

    Tip A pleasant half-day walk from Imlil.

  3. 03

    Sidi Chamharouch

    A whitewashed shrine and a small settlement beside a river, revered locally, on the path to the refuge. Non-Muslims cannot enter the shrine.

    Tip Stop for mint tea.

  4. 04

    The Imlil valley walks

    Trails lead through walnut and apple orchards to villages such as Tamatert, Tacheddirt and Ait Souka, with panoramic views.

    Tip Hire a local guide for less-marked paths.

  5. 05

    Toubkal National Park

    A protected area of rocky peaks, juniper woods and high pastures, with Barbary macaques and birds of prey.

    Tip Bring a map and rain jacket.

Things to do

Best things to do in Imlil

Most visitors walk, with the option of climbing Toubkal.

  1. Climb Jebel Toubkal

    A two-day ascent from Imlil with a night at the refuge, best from May to October when the route is free of snow.

    Tip In winter, crampons and an ice axe and a guide are needed.

  2. Walk to a village

    Half-day and full-day routes visit nearby villages, with lunch in a family home.

    Tip Book a local guide through your guesthouse.

  3. Take a mule trek

    Mules carry luggage and, if you wish, you.

    Tip Agree the price beforehand.

  4. Stay in a mountain guesthouse

    Small hotels and gîtes offer terraces, home cooking and starry skies.

    Tip Book ahead in high season.

  5. Try a multi-day trek

    Routes such as the Toubkal circuit or the Mgoun massif link villages over several days.

    Tip Use a licensed guide and agency.

  6. Visit the Ourika Valley

    A neighbouring valley with waterfalls at Setti Fatma and popular day-trip restaurants.

    Tip Best on weekdays.

Local food

What to eat in Imlil

Mountain food is simple and filling, based on bread, vegetables, eggs and tagines.

  • Vegetable tagine

    Slow-cooked vegetables in a clay pot, often with lamb or chicken.

    Where to try it Guesthouses.

  • Fresh bread and olive oil

    Warm home-baked bread with local olive oil and honey.

    Where to try it Breakfast at guesthouses.

  • Walnuts and almonds

    Locally grown nuts.

    Where to try it Village shops.

  • Mint tea

    Served on every trek, often with a view.

    Where to try it Everywhere.

  • Couscous

    A Friday dish, with vegetables and meat.

    Where to try it Family guesthouses.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Imlil

Imlil and nearby villages have guesthouses and mountain lodges, and the Toubkal refuge is for climbers.

  • Imlil

    Guesthouses and small hotels along the valley.

    Best for: A base for walks

  • Aroumd and higher villages

    Guesthouses on the mountainside, reached on foot or by mule.

    Best for: Quiet

  • The Toubkal refuge

    A mountain hut with dorm beds and meals.

    Best for: The summit climb

Transport

Getting to and around Imlil

Imlil is about 60 km south of Marrakech, up a valley road.

Getting there

  • Grand taxi or shared taxi from Marrakech

    About 1 hour 30 minutes from Marrakech, with shared taxis available from taxi stands in the city.

    Agree the fare.

  • Private driver

    An easy option, and useful for a day trip.

    Arrange through your riad.

  • Organised tour or trek

    Agencies in Marrakech offer one- to three-day trips.

    Check the guide's qualifications.

Getting around

Everything beyond the road end is on foot or by mule.

  • Walking

    The main way to see the valley.

    Wear boots and take water.

  • Mules

    Carry luggage to the refuge and villages.

    Agree the price in advance.

  • Taxis

    Shared taxis run between Imlil and neighbouring villages.

    Ask locally.

When to go

Best time to visit Imlil

In summer the mountains are a cool escape, but winter brings snow and cold.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Wildflowers and blossom, with snow on the summits.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Hot in the lowlands, cool and clear in the mountains. Busy on the Toubkal path.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Clear, stable weather and colour in the walnut groves.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Think twice

    Snow and ice make Toubkal a winter mountaineering route.

Worth planning around: Winter for Toubkal, unless experienced and equipped.

Travel tips

Imlil travel tips

The mountains are simple to reach but should be treated with respect.

  • Take a local guide for Toubkal. It is strongly recommended, and may be required.
  • Acclimatise, since altitude can cause headaches and nausea.
  • Wear sturdy boots, and carry layers and sun protection.
  • Check the weather and trail conditions before going.
  • Check the post-earthquake situation locally, including road and accommodation status.
  • Book guesthouses and the refuge in advance in summer.
  • Tip guides and muleteers appropriately.

FAQ

Imlil: frequently asked questions

How hard is it to climb Toubkal?

In summer, it is a non-technical walk, but at high altitude and steep in places. Fitness and acclimatisation matter. In winter, it needs mountaineering gear and experience.

Do I need a guide?

It is strongly recommended and may be required in some conditions. Use a licensed guide through your guesthouse or a reputable agency.

How long does it take to climb?

Usually two days: about 5–6 hours to the refuge, then 3–4 hours to the summit and a descent to Imlil.

Was Imlil affected by the earthquake?

Yes. The September 2023 earthquake damaged villages in the area. Many have been rebuilt or reopened, but check current conditions before you go.

Can I visit Imlil on a day trip from Marrakech?

Yes, though it is a long day. Staying a night is more rewarding.

When is the best time to go?

May to October for Toubkal, and spring and autumn for valley walks.
