Welcome to Casablanca

Casablanca is a big, busy, modern city with more than three million people in the city and many more in the metropolitan area. It grew fast under French rule in the early 20th century, when planners laid out wide boulevards and built Art Deco apartment blocks, cinemas and civic buildings that still make up much of the downtown. Today it is the commercial capital of Morocco and its main port.

It is not a conventional tourist city, and many travellers only pass through the airport. But the Hassan II Mosque, completed in 1993, is a spectacular building, with a minaret of around 210 metres, a retractable roof, and a section built out over the Atlantic. It is one of the few working mosques in Morocco that non-Muslims can enter on a guided tour.

The rest of the city rewards a day or two of exploring: the Art Deco streets of the centre, the Habous quarter with its 1920s neo-Moorish architecture, the Corniche along the ocean, and a lively food scene ranging from fish grills to modern Moroccan cooking. The film Casablanca (1942) was shot in Hollywood, but its spirit is celebrated at Rick's Café, opened in 2004.

Why visit

Why visit Casablanca?

Casablanca shows modern Morocco, and it is worth a day if you are passing through.

  • A monumental mosque

    The Hassan II Mosque is one of the largest in the world, with astonishing craftsmanship in its carved wood, marble and tile.

  • Art Deco streets

    The downtown is full of 1920s and 1930s buildings, which have been increasingly restored.

  • Habous quarter

    A quiet neo-Moorish district of arcades, bookshops and pastry shops.

  • Modern Moroccan life

    Cafés, galleries, restaurants and shops show a side of the country that the imperial cities do not.

  • A convenient gateway

    Casablanca's airport and rail links connect to Rabat, Marrakech and Fes.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Casablanca

A day covers the major sights, and an extra day allows a slower pace.

  1. 01

    The Hassan II Mosque

    The great mosque, built partly over the sea between 1986 and 1993, with a 210-metre minaret and space for about 25,000 worshippers inside and many more in the courtyard. Guided tours take non-Muslim visitors inside.

    Tip Book a tour in advance and check the visiting times.

  2. 02

    The Corniche

    The seaside promenade of Ain Diab, with cafés, beach clubs and restaurants facing the ocean.

    Tip A pleasant place for a sunset.

  3. 03

    The Habous quarter

    A 1920s neo-Moorish quarter built by the French, with arches, souks, a royal palace and the Mahakma du Pacha.

    Tip Buy pastries and dates in the shops.

  4. 04

    The Art Deco downtown

    Buildings around Place Mohammed V, the Marché Central and the boulevards, including the former Sacré-Cœur cathedral, now a cultural venue.

    Tip Walk with a map of Art Deco buildings.

  5. 05

    The Museum of Moroccan Judaism

    A museum in the Oasis district about the history of Jews in Morocco, one of the few of its kind in the Arab world.

    Tip Check opening times.

  6. 06

    The old medina

    A small, lively walled quarter next to the port, less polished than Marrakech.

    Tip Go in daylight, and watch your belongings.

Things to do

Best things to do in Casablanca

Casablanca is best taken as a series of short, varied outings.

  1. Tour the Hassan II Mosque

    See the prayer hall, the ablution rooms and the hammam underneath.

    Tip Dress modestly.

  2. Walk the Corniche

    A stroll along the ocean promenade past cafés and beach clubs.

    Tip Go in the late afternoon.

  3. Have coffee in an Art Deco café

    Old cafés in the centre have been restored and are a good place to watch the city.

    Tip Try a café crème and a pastry.

  4. Eat fresh fish

    Simple seafood restaurants near the port serve the day's catch.

    Tip Choose a busy place.

  5. Shop in the Habous

    Local crafts, books and food sold in a calm quarter.

    Tip A good place to buy gifts without hassle.

  6. Visit Rick's Café

    A restaurant modelled on the film, with a piano bar.

    Tip Book for dinner.

Local food

What to eat in Casablanca

Casablanca has the best variety of food in Morocco, from street stalls to fine dining.

  • Grilled fish and seafood

    Sardines, calamari, prawns and sea bass, grilled or fried near the port.

    Where to try it Restaurants near the port and the Corniche.

  • Tagine and couscous

    Classic dishes in family restaurants.

    Where to try it Restaurants across the city.

  • Pastries

    French and Moroccan pastries in bakeries.

    Where to try it The Habous quarter.

  • Café culture

    Strong coffee, fresh orange juice and pastries in cafés.

    Where to try it Cafés downtown.

  • Street snacks

    Sandwiches, fried fish and snails.

    Where to try it Markets and the old medina.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Casablanca

Casablanca has a good range of hotels, chiefly in the centre and along the coast.

  • The centre

  • Ain Diab and the Corniche

    Beachfront hotels, restaurants and clubs.

Transport

Getting to and around Casablanca

Casablanca is Morocco's main air and rail hub.

Getting there

  • Flight to Mohammed V (CMN)

    Direct flights from Europe, the Middle East, Africa and North America.

    A train runs from the airport to the city.

  • Train from Rabat or Marrakech

    About 1 hour from Rabat and 2 hours 40 minutes from Marrakech, with the high-speed Al Boraq from Tangier in about 2 hours 10 minutes.

    Book first class.

  • Bus

    CTM services run to all major cities.

    Book the day before.

Getting around

Casablanca is large, and taxis and the tram are the best ways to cover it.

  • Tram

    Modern tram lines run across the city.

    Cheap and reliable.

  • Petits taxis

    Red taxis serve the city.

    Insist on the meter.

  • Walking

    The centre is walkable.

    Take care at busy crossings.

When to go

Best time to visit Casablanca

Casablanca has a mild Atlantic climate, with humid summers and cool, wet winters.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Mild and sunny.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Good

    Warm and humid, cooler than the interior.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm and pleasant.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cool and rainy.

Worth planning around: Winter rain if you plan a coastal walk.

Travel tips

Casablanca travel tips

A few tips help for a short visit.

  • Book the mosque tour in advance.
  • Take the train from the airport, which is quicker than a taxi.
  • Use petits taxis with the meter.
  • Dress modestly for the mosque.
  • Consider staying a night on the way to or from a flight.
  • Keep valuables safe in crowded areas.

FAQ

Casablanca: frequently asked questions

Is Casablanca worth visiting?

For one day, yes. The Hassan II Mosque alone is worth seeing, and the Art Deco downtown is interesting.

Can non-Muslims enter the Hassan II Mosque?

Yes, on guided tours at set times. Dress modestly.

Was the film Casablanca shot here?

No, it was filmed in Hollywood. Rick's Café is a modern restaurant inspired by the film.

How far is Casablanca from Marrakech?

About 240 km, by train in about 2 hours 40 minutes.

Is Casablanca safe?

Generally yes, with the usual big-city precautions.

How many days do you need?

One day is enough, and two if you want a slower pace.
