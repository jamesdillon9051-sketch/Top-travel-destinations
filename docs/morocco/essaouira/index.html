Welcome to Essaouira

Essaouira was known to the Portuguese as Mogador, and they built a fortress on the site in the early 16th century. The present town was laid out in the 1760s by Sultan Mohammed III, who wanted a port for trade with Europe and hired European engineers, including the Frenchman Théodore Cornut, to design its grid of streets and walls. It became Morocco's chief Atlantic trading port for a time, with a large Jewish and Muslim merchant community. UNESCO listed the medina in 2001.

Today Essaouira is a small, relaxed town, and a favourite for Moroccans as well as foreigners. The medina is easy to navigate, the ramparts are lined with old cannons, the port still launches blue fishing boats, and the constant Atlantic wind keeps the air cool, even in summer.

It has long attracted artists and musicians, from Orson Welles, who shot part of Othello here, to the Gnaoua World Music Festival, held every June. Galleries, craftspeople who work thuya wood and cooperatives making argan oil add to the appeal.

Why visit

Why visit Essaouira?

Essaouira is an easy, friendly contrast to Marrakech, and a good place to relax.

  • A compact, walkable medina

    Small, calm and largely free of hassle, with blue-shuttered houses and craft workshops.

  • Ramparts and port

    The Skala walls, old cannons and the busy harbour are highlights.

  • Seafood

    Freshly caught fish grilled to order at the port stalls.

  • Beach and wind sports

    A long sandy beach and reliable wind for kitesurfing and windsurfing.

  • Arts and music

    Galleries, Gnaoua music and a creative community.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Essaouira

The sights are small and close together, and a day and a half covers them.

  1. 01

    The Skala de la Ville

    The sea bastion with rows of old bronze and iron cannons pointing across the Atlantic, and ramparts you can walk. It is a fine place at sunset.

    Tip Go at sunset for the light.

  2. 02

    The fishing port

    A working harbour of blue wooden boats, nets and squawking gulls, with fish auctions in the morning and grill stalls at lunch.

    Tip Choose your fish from the stall and have it grilled.

  3. 03

    The medina

    A grid of lanes with craft workshops, thuya woodwork, argan oil sellers and small galleries.

    Tip Thuya wood is a local speciality.

  4. 04

    The beach

    A long sandy bay to the south of the town, usually windy, popular with kitesurfers and horse riders.

    Tip Windy days are common, so bring a jacket.

  5. 05

    The Mellah and Jewish heritage

    The old Jewish quarter, with a synagogue and a cemetery, recalls the community that once made up a large part of the town.

    Tip Ask at your riad about opening.

Things to do

Best things to do in Essaouira

Essaouira is best enjoyed slowly, with a mix of sea, food and shopping.

  1. Eat grilled fish at the port

    Pick sardines, sole or prawns from the stall and eat at a communal table.

    Tip Agree the price before it is cooked.

  2. Walk the ramparts

    A loop of the walls gives views of the sea and the harbour.

    Tip Go at sunset.

  3. Try kitesurfing or windsurfing

    The Atlantic wind attracts riders, with schools and rentals on the beach.

    Tip Book a lesson at a reputable school.

  4. Shop for crafts

    Thuya wood boxes, argan oil, painted ceramics and Gnaoua instruments.

    Tip Argan cooperatives outside town let you see how the oil is made.

  5. Listen to Gnaoua music

    Music shops and cafés play and sell Gnaoua instruments, and the June festival fills the town.

    Tip Book accommodation well ahead for the festival.

  6. Ride a horse or camel on the beach

    Rides at sunset along the shore south of the town.

    Tip Choose operators who look after their animals.

Local food

What to eat in Essaouira

Essaouira is about seafood, grilled simply with lemon and served with bread and salad.

  • Grilled sardines and fish

    Freshly caught sardines, sole, sea bream and prawns, grilled over charcoal.

    Where to try it The port stalls.

  • Fish tagine

    Fish cooked with tomatoes, peppers and preserved lemon.

    Where to try it Restaurants in the medina.

  • Argan oil and amlou

    A nutty local oil, and amlou, a paste of argan oil, almonds and honey.

    Where to try it Cooperatives and shops.

  • Pastries

    Almond pastries and sweets with mint tea.

    Where to try it Cafés on the main square.

  • Sea urchins and oysters

    Fresh shellfish, sold from market stalls.

    Where to try it The port.

Transport

Getting to and around Essaouira

Essaouira is about 175 km west of Marrakech.

Getting there

  • Bus from Marrakech

    About 3 hours on CTM or Supratours.

    Book the day before.

  • Private driver or shared taxi

    About 2 hours 30 minutes by road.

    Agree the price.

  • Flight to Essaouira–Mogador (ESU)

    Limited flights from Casablanca and some European cities.

    Check the schedule.

Getting around

The medina is small and largely pedestrian.

  • Walking

    Everything in the medina and along the beach is within reach.

    Wear comfortable shoes.

  • Petits taxis

    Useful for the beach and out-of-town cooperatives.

    Agree the fare.

When to go

Best time to visit Essaouira

Essaouira has a mild, breezy climate, and stays cooler than Marrakech in summer.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Mild and sunny.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Warm but windy, and a cool relief from the interior. Busy.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm and calmer.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cool, windy and sometimes wet.

Worth planning around: The Gnaoua festival weekend, if you dislike crowds.

Travel tips

Essaouira travel tips

A few things make the visit easier.

  • Bring a windbreaker. Even in summer, the wind is strong.
  • Eat at the port stalls, but agree prices first.
  • Book ahead for the Gnaoua festival in June.
  • Buy thuya wood from workshops that use certified sources.
  • Use a bus or driver from Marrakech, avoiding driving at night.
  • Wear sun protection, since the wind masks the heat.

FAQ

Essaouira: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Essaouira?

Two days are enough. It can also be a day trip from Marrakech, though a night is nicer.

Can you swim in Essaouira?

The beach is windy, with strong Atlantic currents, and the water is cold. Most people paddle or watch the kitesurfers.

When is the Gnaoua festival?

Usually in June. Check dates, and book accommodation early.

Is Essaouira safe?

Yes, it is generally relaxed. Use normal precautions.

Is it a good day trip from Marrakech?

It is doable, at about three hours each way, but staying a night is better.

Is Essaouira good for kitesurfing?

Yes, it is one of Morocco's main wind sport centres, with strong summer winds.
