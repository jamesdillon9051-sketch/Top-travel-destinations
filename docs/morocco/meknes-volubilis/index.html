Welcome to Meknes

Meknes was a modest city until the second half of the 17th century, when Sultan Moulay Ismail (r. 1672–1727) made it his capital. Over 55 years he built miles of walls, monumental gates, palaces, granaries and stables, which turned Meknes into one of Morocco's four imperial cities. UNESCO inscribed the historic city in 1996.

It is a quieter, less touristy alternative to Fes or Marrakech, with a friendly medina and a relaxed pace. The great gate of Bab Mansour, completed in 1732, is one of the finest in Morocco, and the sultan's mausoleum is one of the few in the country that non-Muslims can enter.

About 30 km north lie the ruins of Volubilis, a Roman town in the province of Mauretania Tingitana, inscribed by UNESCO in 1997. It flourished from the 1st to 3rd centuries AD on the olive and grain trade, and its mosaics, capitol and triumphal arch remain in a landscape of rolling fields. Nearby, Moulay Idriss is a hillside pilgrimage town holding the tomb of the founder of Morocco's first Islamic dynasty.

Why visit

Why visit Meknes?

Meknes and Volubilis together make one of the best day or two-day excursions in Morocco.

  • Roman Morocco

    Volubilis is the best-preserved Roman site in the country, with mosaics still in their original position.

  • Imperial monumental architecture

    Bab Mansour, the Heri es-Souani granaries and the walls show the ambitions of Moulay Ismail.

  • Fewer crowds

    A calmer medina than Fes or Marrakech, with less hassle.

  • A holy hill town

    Moulay Idriss stacks white houses on two hills, and is a pleasant place for lunch.

  • A base near Fes

    Meknes is only an hour from Fes, and a good stop between Fes and Rabat.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Meknes

Give Meknes a morning and Volubilis an afternoon, or stay overnight and take it slowly.

  1. 01

    Bab Mansour

    The great ceremonial gate, finished in 1732, decorated with zellige tile and marble columns, some taken from Volubilis.

    Tip Photograph it in the late afternoon.

  2. 02

    The Mausoleum of Moulay Ismail

    The tomb of the sultan, a series of elegant courtyards with zellige, carved plaster and a fountain. It is open to non-Muslims, unusual in Morocco.

    Tip Dress modestly and remove your shoes as requested.

  3. 03

    Heri es-Souani

    The massive granaries and former stables of the sultan, with rows of great arches in a vast space, built to house grain and horses.

    Tip Combine with the neighbouring water tank.

  4. 04

    Place el-Hedim and the medina

    A large square facing Bab Mansour, with cafés and snake charmers, leading into the medina and its craft stalls.

    Tip The medina is easy to explore alone.

  5. 05

    Bou Inania Madrasa, Meknes

    A 14th-century Marinid college with decorated courtyards, quieter than the one in Fes.

    Tip Check opening.

Things to do

Best things to do in Meknes

This is a place for history, and for wandering.

  1. Explore the Roman ruins at Volubilis

    Walk the paved main street, the basilica and the capitol, and see the mosaics, including the Labours of Hercules and Diana bathing.

    Tip Go in the morning, and hire a guide at the entrance.

  2. Visit Moulay Idriss

    A hilltop town holding the tomb of Idris I, the founder of Morocco's first Islamic dynasty. It is a pilgrimage site.

    Tip Non-Muslims cannot enter the shrine, but the town has views.

  3. Walk the imperial city

    Follow the walls and gates from Bab Mansour to the mausoleum.

    Tip Go early to avoid the heat.

  4. Taste local wine

    The Meknes region is Morocco's main wine-growing area, with vineyards and tastings.

    Tip Ask your hotel about tasting visits.

  5. Shop in the medina

    Damascene metalwork, ceramics and textiles, in a calm setting.

    Tip Prices are often lower than in Fes.

  6. Combine with Fes

    Use Meknes as a day trip from Fes or a stop on the way between Rabat and Fes.

    Tip Take the train.

Local food

What to eat in Meknes

Meknes is known for its olives and fruit, and its food is simple, Fassi-influenced Moroccan cooking.

  • Olives

    The region is one of Morocco's big olive producers, and olives and oil are sold in the market.

    Where to try it The main market.

  • Tagines and couscous

    Classic dishes in local restaurants.

    Where to try it Restaurants in the medina.

  • Local wine

    Red and rosé wines, from vineyards near Meknes.

    Where to try it Restaurants and wine tastings.

  • Grilled meat

    Kebabs and kefta sandwiches.

    Where to try it Stalls on Place el-Hedim.

  • Pastries and mint tea

    Local sweets in cafés.

    Where to try it Cafés around Place el-Hedim.

Transport

Getting to and around Meknes

Meknes lies about 60 km west of Fes, on the main train line.

Getting there

  • Train from Fes or Rabat

    About 45 minutes to 1 hour from Fes and about 2 hours from Rabat.

    Meknes El Amir Abdelkader is the main station.

  • Grand taxi to Volubilis

    About 30 minutes from Meknes. Taxis often include Moulay Idriss.

    Agree the price and the waiting time.

  • Private driver or tour

    Day trips from Fes cover Meknes, Volubilis and Moulay Idriss.

    Arrange through your riad.

Getting around

The medina is on foot, and you need a taxi for Volubilis.

  • Walking

    The medina and Place el-Hedim are walkable.

    Wear sun protection.

  • Petits taxis

    Serve the imperial city and the new town.

    Insist on the meter.

  • Grand taxi

    Shared or chartered to Volubilis and Moulay Idriss.

    Agree the fare.

When to go

Best time to visit Meknes

Meknes has hot summers and cool, wet winters. Spring and autumn are best for Volubilis, which has little shade.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Green fields and mild temperatures.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Think twice

    Hot, and the Roman site has little shade.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Pleasant, and quieter.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cool, with rain.

Worth planning around: The midday heat in summer.

Travel tips

Meknes travel tips

A few tips help you plan the visit.

  • Go to Volubilis in the morning, when it is cooler.
  • Hire a guide at the Volubilis entrance for the mosaics.
  • Bring a hat, sunscreen and water for the ruins.
  • Dress modestly for the mausoleum.
  • Combine with Fes by train, or stay in Meknes.
  • Agree taxi fares and waiting time in advance.

FAQ

Meknes: frequently asked questions

Is Meknes worth visiting?

Yes, for its imperial monuments and quiet medina, and as a base for Volubilis. It is less touristy than Fes.

How long do you need for Volubilis?

About two hours, with a guide.

Can I do Meknes and Volubilis as a day trip from Fes?

Yes. It is an easy and popular day trip, by train and taxi or private driver.

Can non-Muslims enter the mausoleum of Moulay Ismail?

Yes, it is one of the few in Morocco open to non-Muslims.

What is Moulay Idriss?

A holy hill town that holds the tomb of Idris I, Morocco's first Islamic ruler.

When is the best time to visit?

Spring and autumn.
