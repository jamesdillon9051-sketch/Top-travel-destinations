Welcome to Eisenstadt & Lake Neusiedl

Eisenstadt is Burgenland's small, low-key provincial capital, best known as the long-time home of composer Joseph Haydn, who spent decades in the service of the Esterházy princely family whose palace still dominates the town.

A short drive away, Lake Neusiedl is genuinely unlike anywhere else in Austria — a shallow steppe lake shared with Hungary, ringed by extensive reed beds rather than mountains, and warm enough for swimming through much of the summer despite averaging barely a metre in depth.

The flat, sunny plain surrounding the lake has made this Austria's warmest wine-growing region, producing genuinely excellent reds and dessert wines rarely found outside the country, and the combination of Haydn's legacy, lake activities and wine tasting gives Burgenland a distinctly different, quieter character than Austria's Alpine regions.

Why visit

Why visit Eisenstadt & Lake Neusiedl?

The region's case is that it offers a genuinely different Austria — flat, warm and lake-centred — a real contrast to the Alpine scenery most visitors expect.

  • Lake Neusiedl is genuinely unlike anywhere else in Austria

    A shallow steppe lake with extensive reed beds, a landscape found nowhere else in the country.

  • Haydn's legacy is genuinely well preserved

    His house and the Esterházy Palace where he composed for decades both remain open to visitors.

  • The wine is genuinely excellent and underrated

    Burgenland's reds and dessert wines rank among Austria's best, rarely exported and worth tasting on-site.

  • It's genuinely quieter than the Alpine regions

    A relaxed, low-key pace with a fraction of the visitor numbers found in Salzburg or Tyrol.

  • It's genuinely easy to combine with Vienna

    Under an hour from the capital, making a worthwhile day trip or overnight extension.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Eisenstadt & Lake Neusiedl

The region's essential sights, spanning Eisenstadt's Haydn heritage and the lake beyond it.

  1. 01

    Esterházy Palace

    The Esterházy princely family's genuinely grand baroque palace, where Joseph Haydn served as court composer for decades, its Haydnsaal concert hall still hosting performances today.

    Tip Time a visit around a concert in the Haydnsaal, genuinely one of the world's most acoustically celebrated concert halls.

  2. 02

    Joseph Haydn's House

    The composer's former home in Eisenstadt, now a genuinely well-curated museum of his life and work, where several major compositions were written.

    Tip A short, worthwhile stop to pair with the Esterházy Palace for a fuller picture of Haydn's decades in the town.

  3. 03

    Lake Neusiedl

    A genuinely unusual shallow steppe lake shared with Hungary, ringed by extensive reed beds and warm enough for swimming through much of summer.

    Tip The lake's average depth is barely a metre — a genuinely different swimming experience from Austria's deep Alpine lakes.

  4. 04

    Rust

    A genuinely charming lakeside wine town, its UNESCO-recognised old town famous for storks nesting on its chimneys each summer.

    Tip Look up while walking the old town streets — the storks' rooftop nests are genuinely a beloved local tradition.

  5. 05

    Neusiedler See–Seewinkel National Park

    A genuinely important wetland ecosystem around the lake's southern shore, home to significant bird populations and a unique steppe landscape.

    Tip Bring binoculars if visiting during migration season, when the park's bird populations are genuinely at their most active.

Things to do

Best things to do in Eisenstadt & Lake Neusiedl

Beyond Haydn's legacy and the lake itself, the region rewards wine tasting and its genuinely open, flat landscape.

  1. Taste Burgenland wine at a local winery

    The region's warm, sunny climate produces genuinely excellent reds and dessert wines, best tasted directly at family-run wineries around the lake.

    Tip Rust and the surrounding villages hold genuinely the highest concentration of quality wineries in the region.

  2. Cycle around Lake Neusiedl

    A genuinely well-maintained, largely flat cycle path circles the entire lake, including a stretch across the border into Hungary.

    Tip The full loop takes a full day — many visitors cycle a shorter section rather than the complete circuit.

  3. Swim in the lake

    Lake Neusiedl's warm, shallow water makes it genuinely popular for swimming through much of the summer, a distinctly different experience from Alpine lakes.

    Tip Public bathing areas (Strandbad) around the lake offer facilities and easy access through the reed beds.

  4. Go birdwatching in the national park

    The Seewinkel wetlands are genuinely one of Central Europe's most significant birdwatching areas, particularly during spring and autumn migration.

    Tip Guided birdwatching tours through the national park offer genuinely better sightings than an independent visit.

  5. Attend a Haydn concert

    Regular performances in the Esterházy Palace's Haydnsaal keep the composer's music genuinely active in the town where he spent much of his career.

    Tip Check the concert calendar ahead, particularly during the annual Haydn Festival held in the palace.

Local food

What to eat in Eisenstadt & Lake Neusiedl

Burgenland's food centres on its own wine and a genuinely warmer-climate Austrian cuisine than the Alpine regions.

  • Burgenland red wine (Blaufränkisch)

    The region's signature red grape, genuinely excellent from Burgenland's warm, sunny vineyards, increasingly recognised internationally.

    Where to try it Family-run wineries throughout Rust and the villages around Lake Neusiedl.

  • Ausbruch dessert wine

    A genuinely distinctive sweet dessert wine from Rust, made from grapes affected by noble rot, a regional specialty with centuries of tradition.

    Where to try it Wineries in and around Rust, several with genuinely deep historical ties to the wine's production.

  • Burgenländischer Gulasch

    A regional take on goulash, genuinely hearty and warming, reflecting Burgenland's historical ties to neighbouring Hungary.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout Eisenstadt and the lake villages.

  • Fresh fish from Lake Neusiedl

    Local fish from the shallow lake, prepared simply, a genuinely distinctive regional specialty found nowhere else in Austria.

    Where to try it Lakeside restaurants around Rust and other villages on Lake Neusiedl's shore.

  • Wiener schnitzel

    Widely available across the region as elsewhere in Austria, genuinely good from Eisenstadt's traditional restaurants.

    Where to try it Gasthauses throughout Eisenstadt and the lake villages.

Transport

Getting to and around Eisenstadt & Lake Neusiedl

The region is an easy train or car trip from Vienna, making it a genuinely accessible day trip or overnight extension.

Getting there

  • Train from Vienna

    Regional trains connect Vienna to Eisenstadt in under an hour.

    A separate onward bus or short taxi ride reaches Rust and the lake villages from Eisenstadt.

  • Rental car

    The most flexible way to explore the lake's villages and wineries at your own pace.

    The flat terrain makes this a genuinely easy, relaxed region to drive compared to Austria's Alpine roads.

  • Bus from Vienna

    Direct buses connect Vienna to Rust and other lake villages.

    A genuinely convenient option if avoiding a rental car or train transfer in Eisenstadt.

Getting around

Eisenstadt's centre is walkable, while the lake villages and wineries are best explored by car or bicycle.

  • Walking

    Eisenstadt's Esterházy Palace and Haydn's House both sit within a genuinely compact, walkable town centre.

    The town itself is small enough to cover comfortably on foot in a few hours.

  • Bicycle

    A genuinely well-maintained path circles Lake Neusiedl, the best way to explore multiple lakeside villages.

    Rental is widely available in Rust and other lakeside towns.

  • Rental car

    The most practical way to combine Eisenstadt with several wine villages around the lake in one day.

    The region's flat terrain makes for genuinely easy, relaxed driving.

When to go

Best time to visit Eisenstadt & Lake Neusiedl

Burgenland's warm climate makes it genuinely pleasant across a longer season than Austria's Alpine regions, with spring and early autumn the most comfortable windows.

  • Spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Mild, warming weather, ideal for cycling around the lake and wine tasting before peak summer heat.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Genuinely warm, sometimes hot, ideal for swimming in the lake but with less comfortable cycling conditions at midday.

  • Autumn (September)

    Best time

    Comfortable temperatures and the wine harvest in full swing, a genuinely excellent window for the region.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Shoulder

    Quiet and cold, with many wineries and lake facilities operating reduced hours outside the main season.

Worth planning around: Winter if wine tasting or lake activities are your priority — most facilities genuinely reduce or pause operations outside the main season.

Travel tips

Eisenstadt & Lake Neusiedl travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to an Eisenstadt and Lake Neusiedl trip.

  • Check the Esterházy Palace's concert calendar ahead if the Haydnsaal's acoustics and a live performance interest you.
  • Call ahead to smaller wineries for tastings if visiting outside peak summer season.
  • Rent a bicycle in Rust for the most flexible way to explore the lake's villages.
  • Bring binoculars if visiting the national park during spring or autumn bird migration.
  • Pack swimwear in summer — the lake's warm, shallow water is genuinely popular for swimming.
  • Combine Eisenstadt with Vienna rather than treating it as a standalone destination — the rail connection is genuinely convenient.

FAQ

Eisenstadt & Lake Neusiedl: frequently asked questions

Is Lake Neusiedl worth visiting compared to Austria's Alpine lakes?

Yes, genuinely, as a different kind of destination rather than a direct comparison — it offers a unique shallow steppe-lake landscape, warmer swimming and wine country unlike anywhere else in Austria, rather than competing on Alpine scenery.

How many days do you need in this region?

Two days covers Eisenstadt's Haydn sights and a lakeside wine village comfortably, with more time rewarding a fuller lake cycle or national park birdwatching visit.

How do I get from Vienna to Eisenstadt?

By regional train, taking under an hour — genuinely one of the most convenient day-trip connections from the Austrian capital.

Is this region expensive?

No, genuinely one of Austria's more affordable regions — mid-range travel runs roughly €75–130 per person per day, noticeably below the Alpine resort regions.

What is the best time of year to visit?

May to June and September offer the most comfortable weather for cycling and wine tasting. Summer is warmer and better for swimming in the lake, though midday heat can make cycling less comfortable.
