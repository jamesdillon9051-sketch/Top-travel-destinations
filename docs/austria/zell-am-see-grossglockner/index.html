Welcome to Zell am See & the Grossglockner High Alpine Road

Zell am See sits on the shore of the genuinely picturesque Zeller See, a turquoise Alpine lake ringed by mountains, close enough to Austria's highest peak, the Grossglockner, to serve as the natural base for exploring both the lake and the glacier country above it.

The region's centrepiece is the Grossglockner High Alpine Road, a genuinely spectacular 48-kilometre high-mountain road opened in 1935, climbing to over 2,500 metres through hairpin turns with views over glaciers, waterfalls and Austria's highest summit throughout.

That combination of a relaxed lakeside town and one of Europe's most dramatic drives makes the area a genuinely worthwhile stop for visitors wanting serious Alpine scenery without committing to a full ski-resort stay in Kitzbühel or Innsbruck.

Why visit

Why visit Zell am See & the Grossglockner High Alpine Road?

The region's case is that the Grossglockner High Alpine Road delivers Alpine drama unmatched anywhere else in Austria, with a genuinely relaxed lakeside base to return to.

  • The Grossglockner road is genuinely one of Europe's best mountain drives

    48 kilometres of hairpin turns through glacier country, climbing to over 2,500 metres.

  • Zeller See is genuinely one of Austria's prettiest Alpine lakes

    Turquoise water ringed by mountains, offering swimming, boating and lakeside walking.

  • The glacier views are genuinely spectacular

    The Pasterze, Austria's largest glacier, is directly viewable from the Grossglockner road's Kaiser-Franz-Josefs-Höhe viewpoint.

  • It's genuinely less crowded than Austria's bigger-name Alpine resorts

    Comparable scenery to Kitzbühel or Innsbruck's surroundings, with noticeably fewer visitors.

  • It works well as both a base and a day trip

    Genuinely flexible for a dedicated stay or as an extension from Salzburg or Innsbruck.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Zell am See & the Grossglockner High Alpine Road

The region's essential sights, spanning Zell am See's lake and the Grossglockner road above it.

  1. 01

    The Grossglockner High Alpine Road

    A genuinely spectacular 48-kilometre toll road climbing through hairpin turns to over 2,500 metres, with views over glaciers and Austria's highest peak throughout.

    Tip Check the road's opening dates before visiting — it typically operates from May to October and closes for winter.

  2. 02

    Kaiser-Franz-Josefs-Höhe and the Pasterze glacier

    The Grossglockner road's most famous viewpoint, offering a genuinely direct view of the Pasterze, Austria's largest glacier, though it has retreated considerably in recent decades.

    Tip A funicular or walking path descends closer to the glacier's current edge for those wanting a nearer view.

  3. 03

    Zeller See

    A genuinely picturesque turquoise Alpine lake at the centre of Zell am See, ringed by mountains and offering swimming, boating and lakeside walking paths.

    Tip A full walk or cycle around the lake's roughly 12-kilometre perimeter path is a genuinely pleasant half-day activity.

  4. 04

    Edelweissspitze

    The Grossglockner road's highest viewpoint, reachable by a short side road, offering genuinely 360-degree panoramic views over the surrounding peaks.

    Tip A worthwhile detour off the main road — allow extra time for the narrow access route and the view itself.

  5. 05

    Schmittenhöhe

    The mountain rising directly above Zell am See, reachable by cable car, offering genuinely sweeping views over the lake and surrounding Alps.

    Tip The cable car runs year-round, serving skiers in winter and hikers in summer alike.

Things to do

Best things to do in Zell am See & the Grossglockner High Alpine Road

Beyond the famous road and the lake, the region rewards both mountain activity and a relaxed lakeside pace.

  1. Drive the Grossglockner High Alpine Road

    The single most essential activity in the region, a genuinely unforgettable drive through high Alpine scenery unmatched elsewhere in Austria.

    Tip Start early in the day for the clearest weather and lightest traffic on the narrow mountain road.

  2. Swim or boat on Zeller See

    The lake's genuinely clean, cool water and mountain backdrop make it a popular summer swimming and boating spot.

    Tip Several public swimming areas around the lake are free to access, alongside paid lidos with more facilities.

  3. Hike or ski on the Schmittenhöhe

    The mountain above Zell am See offers genuinely excellent hiking in summer and skiing in winter, both reachable directly by cable car from town.

    Tip Summer hiking trails from the top station suit a genuinely wide range of fitness levels.

  4. Cycle the Tauern cycle path

    A genuinely well-maintained long-distance cycle route passes through the Zell am See area, following river valleys through the wider region.

    Tip Bicycle rental is widely available in Zell am See for visitors without their own equipment.

  5. Visit the Kitzsteinhorn glacier

    A genuinely accessible glacier ski area near Kaprun, offering summer skiing and glacier viewing even outside winter season.

    Tip A cable car and the '3K K-onnection' reach the glacier directly, worthwhile even for non-skiers for the high-altitude views.

Local food

What to eat in Zell am See & the Grossglockner High Alpine Road

The region's food centres on Salzburg province's Alpine specialties, served in genuinely scenic lakeside and mountain settings.

  • Kaiserschmarrn

    A shredded, caramelised pancake with fruit compote, genuinely a beloved mountain-hut classic across the Grossglockner road's rest stops.

    Where to try it Mountain huts along the Grossglockner High Alpine Road and restaurants in Zell am See.

  • Pinzgauer Kasnocken

    A regional cheese spätzle dish from the Pinzgau area around Zell am See, genuinely rich and satisfying after a day of hiking or driving.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout Zell am See.

  • Fresh lake fish

    Fish from Zeller See, grilled or pan-fried, genuinely one of the local specialties at lakeside restaurants.

    Where to try it Restaurants directly on Zeller See's shore.

  • Tiroler Gröstl

    A genuinely hearty pan-fried dish of potatoes, onions and roast meat, widely available as essential Alpine comfort food.

    Where to try it Traditional gasthauses throughout Zell am See.

  • Alpine cheese and dairy

    Mountain farms across the Pinzgau region produce genuinely good local cheese, often sold directly at farm shops.

    Where to try it Farm shops and markets around Zell am See and along the Grossglockner road.

Transport

Getting to and around Zell am See & the Grossglockner High Alpine Road

Zell am See is reached by train or road from Salzburg or Innsbruck, both genuinely convenient gateway cities.

Getting there

  • Train from Salzburg

    Regional trains connect Salzburg to Zell am See in about 1.5 hours.

    The town's train station sits within easy walking distance of the lake and centre.

  • Rental car for the Grossglockner road

    Essential for driving the Grossglockner High Alpine Road itself, which isn't well served by public transport.

    Hire specifically for this leg if travelling the rest of your trip by train.

  • Train or bus from Innsbruck

    A regional connection from Innsbruck, typically changing once, taking roughly 2–2.5 hours.

    A rental car offers more flexibility if combining Innsbruck and Zell am See in one trip.

Getting around

Zell am See town is genuinely walkable, while the Grossglockner road and glacier require a car or organised tour.

  • Walking

    Zell am See's town centre and lakeside promenade are genuinely walkable, with the Schmittenhöhe cable car reachable on foot.

    A full lake-perimeter walk takes several hours — plan accordingly or cycle instead.

  • Rental car

    The only practical way to drive the Grossglockner High Alpine Road and reach some outlying viewpoints.

    The road's toll and narrow hairpin sections require genuine driving confidence.

  • Organised bus tour

    A genuinely relaxed alternative to self-driving the Grossglockner road, available from Zell am See and Salzburg.

    Worthwhile if you'd rather enjoy the views without focusing on the driving.

When to go

Best time to visit Zell am See & the Grossglockner High Alpine Road

The region genuinely splits into two seasons — the high alpine road is only open roughly May to October, while winter brings skiing on the Schmittenhöhe and Kitzsteinhorn.

  • Late spring to early summer (May–June)

    Good

    The Grossglockner road typically reopens in May, though higher sections can retain snow and require caution early in the season.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Best time

    The most reliable window for driving the full Grossglockner road and enjoying Zeller See's lake activities.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Still open for driving with thinning crowds, though the road typically closes for the season by late October.

  • Winter (November–April)

    Shoulder

    The Grossglockner road closes entirely, but skiing on the Schmittenhöhe and nearby Kitzsteinhorn glacier remains excellent.

Worth planning around: Attempting the Grossglockner road outside its official open season (roughly May to October) — it closes completely for winter and reopening dates can shift with conditions.

Travel tips

Zell am See & the Grossglockner High Alpine Road travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Zell am See and Grossglockner trip.

  • Check the Grossglockner road's current opening dates before planning your trip — it closes completely in winter.
  • Start the Grossglockner drive early in the day for the clearest weather and lightest traffic.
  • Rent a car specifically for the Grossglockner leg if travelling the rest of your trip by train.
  • Pack proper layers even in summer — temperatures at 2,500 metres run considerably cooler than the valley.
  • Book Zell am See accommodation ahead for both summer lake season and winter ski season peaks.
  • Consider an organised bus tour of the Grossglockner road if you'd rather not focus on driving the hairpin turns yourself.

FAQ

Zell am See & the Grossglockner High Alpine Road: frequently asked questions

Is the Grossglockner High Alpine Road worth the toll?

Yes, genuinely — it's widely considered one of Europe's most spectacular mountain drives, with views over glaciers and Austria's highest peak that justify both the toll and the drive time.

When is the Grossglockner road open?

Typically from May to October, though exact dates shift year to year depending on snow conditions — check current opening dates before planning your visit, since the road closes completely for winter.

How many days do you need in this region?

Two to three. Two covers Zeller See and a full Grossglockner road drive; three allows the Schmittenhöhe or Kitzsteinhorn glacier without feeling rushed.

Do I need a car for this region?

For the Grossglockner road, yes — it isn't well served by public transport, though organised bus tours offer an alternative. Zell am See itself is genuinely walkable without a car.

What is the best time of year to visit?

June to September for driving the Grossglockner road and lake activities. December to March brings excellent skiing on the Schmittenhöhe and Kitzsteinhorn glacier, though the high alpine road itself is closed.
