Welcome to Bregenz & Lake Constance

Bregenz sits at Austria's westernmost tip, on the shore of Lake Constance where Austria, Germany and Switzerland all meet, a genuinely different corner of the country both geographically and culturally from anywhere further east.

The city's best-known feature is the Bregenz Festival's floating stage, a genuinely spectacular open-air venue built directly on the lake for opera performances each summer, one of the most striking stage designs anywhere in Europe.

Beyond the festival, Vorarlberg's distinct regional culture, closer in dialect and character to neighbouring Switzerland than to Vienna, gives Bregenz a genuinely different feel from the rest of Austria, and its position on Lake Constance makes it a natural jumping-off point for exploring all three bordering countries.

Why visit

Why visit Bregenz & Lake Constance?

Bregenz's case is that it offers a genuinely different Austria — a Lake Constance setting and Vorarlberg culture unlike anywhere else in the country.

  • The floating stage is genuinely spectacular

    One of Europe's most striking open-air performance venues, built directly on Lake Constance for the annual summer festival.

  • Lake Constance's setting is genuinely different

    A large lake shared by three countries, with mountains and vineyards framing the shoreline.

  • Vorarlberg's culture is genuinely distinct

    Closer in dialect and character to Switzerland than to Vienna, giving the region its own identity.

  • It's genuinely well positioned for a three-country trip

    Germany and Switzerland are both easily reachable from Bregenz around the lake.

  • It's genuinely quieter than Austria's bigger-name destinations

    A relaxed lakeside pace with far fewer visitors than Salzburg or Innsbruck.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Bregenz & Lake Constance

Bregenz's essential sights, spanning the lakeside festival area and the old town above it.

  1. 01

    The Bregenz Festival floating stage

    A genuinely spectacular open-air stage built directly on Lake Constance, hosting major opera productions each summer with elaborate, ever-changing set designs.

    Tip Book festival tickets many months ahead if visiting specifically for a performance — the summer run genuinely sells out.

  2. 02

    Bregenz's Oberstadt (upper old town)

    A genuinely well-preserved medieval old town on a hill above the lake, with a distinctive Martinsturm tower and cobbled streets.

    Tip The Martinsturm's onion-dome tower, one of the largest of its kind in Central Europe, is genuinely worth climbing for the view.

  3. 03

    Pfänder cable car

    A cable car climbing directly above Bregenz, offering genuinely sweeping views over Lake Constance and, on clear days, into Germany and Switzerland.

    Tip The summit's free-roaming alpine ibex enclosure is a genuinely worthwhile add-on to the viewpoint itself.

  4. 04

    Lake Constance's shoreline promenade

    A genuinely pleasant lakeside walking and cycling path, with views across to Germany and Switzerland on the opposite shores.

    Tip Rent a bicycle for a longer ride along the shoreline, genuinely one of the most relaxed ways to see the lake.

  5. 05

    Vorarlberg Museum

    A genuinely striking modern museum building covering the region's distinct history and culture, a worthwhile stop for context on Vorarlberg's identity.

    Tip The building's textured concrete facade is itself genuinely worth a look, a notable piece of contemporary architecture.

Things to do

Best things to do in Bregenz & Lake Constance

Beyond the festival and old town, Bregenz rewards its lake setting and access to three countries at once.

  1. Attend a Bregenz Festival performance

    The summer opera festival's floating stage productions are genuinely among the most visually spectacular performances in European opera.

    Tip Even without attending a performance, the stage is genuinely worth seeing up close from the lakeside promenade.

  2. Take a boat on Lake Constance

    Regular ferries cross Lake Constance to towns in Germany and Switzerland, offering a genuinely easy way to visit all three countries in one day.

    Tip A day pass on the lake's ferry network is a genuinely good value way to combine several shoreline towns.

  3. Ride the Pfänder cable car

    A genuinely quick way to gain a sweeping panoramic view over the lake and surrounding countries from directly above Bregenz.

    Tip Hiking trails from the top station offer a genuinely rewarding way back down for those wanting more time on the mountain.

  4. Cycle around Lake Constance

    A genuinely well-established long-distance cycle route circles the entire lake, crossing between Austria, Germany and Switzerland.

    Tip Most visitors cycle a shorter section near Bregenz rather than the full multi-day loop.

  5. Explore Vorarlberg's mountain villages

    The region behind Bregenz holds genuinely charming Alpine villages with a distinct Vorarlberg character, worth a day trip inland from the lake.

    Tip A rental car offers the most flexibility for exploring Vorarlberg's smaller mountain villages beyond the lake itself.

Local food

What to eat in Bregenz & Lake Constance

Bregenz and Vorarlberg's food scene leans toward Alpine dairy and Central European influences shared with neighbouring Switzerland.

  • Käsespätzle

    Vorarlberg's version of cheese spätzle, genuinely rich and hearty, closely related to Swiss and southern German versions of the dish.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout Bregenz's old town.

  • Fresh lake fish (Felchen)

    A whitefish native to Lake Constance, grilled or pan-fried, genuinely one of the region's most distinctive local specialties.

    Where to try it Lakeside restaurants along Bregenz's shoreline promenade.

  • Vorarlberg cheese

    The region's Alpine dairy farms produce genuinely excellent cheese, closer in style to Swiss mountain cheeses than typical Austrian varieties.

    Where to try it Local markets and farm shops throughout Vorarlberg's mountain villages.

  • Wiener schnitzel

    Widely available across the region as elsewhere in Austria, genuinely good from Bregenz's traditional restaurants.

    Where to try it Gasthauses throughout Bregenz's old town.

  • Riebel

    A genuinely traditional Vorarlberg cornmeal dish, historically a humble staple food, now enjoying something of a local culinary revival.

    Where to try it Traditional Vorarlberg restaurants, increasingly reappearing on modern regional menus.

Transport

Getting to and around Bregenz & Lake Constance

Bregenz is reached by train from within Austria, or conveniently via Zurich or Munich given its far-western position.

Getting there

  • Train from Innsbruck

    Regional trains connect Innsbruck to Bregenz in about 2.5 hours.

    A genuinely scenic route through Vorarlberg's mountains and along the lake shore.

  • Zurich Airport, Switzerland

    A genuinely convenient international gateway, roughly 1.5 hours from Bregenz by train.

    Many international visitors find Zurich a more practical entry point than flying into Austria directly for this region.

  • Train from Vienna

    A long but scenic direct connection from Vienna, taking roughly 8 hours across the whole country.

    Most visitors combine Bregenz with a Munich or Zurich gateway rather than a direct Vienna train given the distance.

Getting around

Bregenz's lakeside area and old town are genuinely walkable, with ferries and cable cars covering the wider region.

  • Walking

    The lake promenade, festival grounds and Oberstadt old town all sit within a genuinely compact, walkable area.

    The climb up to the Oberstadt is short but genuinely steep in places.

  • Lake Constance ferries

    Connect Bregenz to towns in Germany and Switzerland across the lake.

    A day pass covers multiple crossings, genuinely good value for visiting several shoreline towns.

  • Pfänder cable car

    Reaches the mountain directly above the city for panoramic views.

    Runs year-round, with hiking trails available from the summit in summer.

When to go

Best time to visit Bregenz & Lake Constance

Bregenz is genuinely at its best during the summer festival season, though the wider Lake Constance shoulder months offer a quieter, still-pleasant alternative.

  • Spring (May–June)

    Good

    Mild, increasingly warm weather, genuinely pleasant for lake walks and cycling before the summer festival crowds.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Best time

    The Bregenz Festival's core season, with the floating stage's performances and genuinely warm lake-friendly weather.

  • Autumn (September)

    Good

    Comfortable temperatures and thinning crowds after the festival season ends.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Shoulder

    Quiet and cold, with lake activities and the festival both dormant outside the main season.

Worth planning around: Winter if the festival or lake activities are your priority — both are genuinely seasonal and largely inactive outside summer.

Travel tips

Bregenz & Lake Constance travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Bregenz trip.

  • Book Bregenz Festival tickets many months ahead if visiting specifically for a performance.
  • Consider Zurich or Munich as more convenient international gateways than flying into Austria directly.
  • Ride the Pfänder cable car on a clear day for the best three-country views.
  • Buy a day pass for the Lake Constance ferries if planning to visit both Germany and Switzerland in one trip.
  • Pack for genuinely changeable lake-and-mountain weather regardless of season.
  • Visit outside the July-August festival peak for a quieter, less crowded version of the same lakeside setting.

FAQ

Bregenz & Lake Constance: frequently asked questions

Is Bregenz worth visiting outside the festival season?

Yes, genuinely — the Pfänder cable car, old town and Lake Constance's shoreline are all worthwhile year-round, though the floating stage's performances are the region's single most famous draw.

How many days do you need in Bregenz?

Two days covers the lake, old town and Pfänder cable car comfortably, with more time rewarding a cross-border ferry trip to Germany or Switzerland.

How do I get to Bregenz?

By train from Innsbruck, or conveniently via Zurich or Munich airports given the city's far-western position — many international visitors find these more practical gateways than flying into Austria directly.

Can I visit Germany and Switzerland from Bregenz?

Yes, easily — regular ferries cross Lake Constance to towns in both countries, making a three-country day genuinely straightforward from Bregenz.

What is the best time of year to visit?

June to August for the Bregenz Festival and warmest lake weather. May, June and September offer a quieter, still-pleasant alternative for those less focused on the festival itself.
