Welcome to Phnom Penh

Phnom Penh sits where the Mekong, Tonle Sap and Bassac rivers meet. A former French colonial city, it has wide boulevards, a riverside promenade, and a mix of golden royal buildings, markets and modern cafes. It is busier and more intense than Siem Reap, but worth a few days.

The Royal Palace and the Silver Pagoda, with its floor of silver tiles, are the main sights, along with the National Museum of Khmer art. The Central Market, Russian Market and riverside offer shopping and street food. Wat Phnom, the hill that gave the city its name, is a small temple at the centre.

The city also holds the memory of the Khmer Rouge years. The Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum and the Choeung Ek Killing Fields are heavy but important visits, and should be approached with respect. Evenings are good for sunset boat rides and the riverside bars.

Travel tips

Phnom Penh travel tips

Tips.

  • Dress modestly at the palace and memorials.
  • Be respectful at Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek.
  • Watch bags on motorbikes.
  • Use apps for taxis.
  • Carry small US dollar notes.
  • Drink bottled water.
  • Avoid walking alone late.
