Why go to Cambodia

Cambodia is a country of rice fields, rivers and ruins, home to the temples of Angkor, built by the Khmer Empire between the 9th and 15th centuries. The great temples draw most visitors, but the country also has colonial towns, quiet beaches and rural landscapes.

Its modern history includes the horrors of the Khmer Rouge years, which are remembered in memorials in Phnom Penh. Visiting these sites is moving, and Cambodians today are warm, resilient and proud of their culture, with a growing arts scene and tourism industry.

Travel is easy and cheap, with buses connecting main cities and tuk-tuks everywhere. Siem Reap is the usual starting point, then Phnom Penh, with side trips to Battambang, Kampot and the islands. The dry season from November to February is the best time to go.

Food and drink

Cambodia foods to try

Khmer food is milder than Thai, with sour, salty and sweet flavours, built on rice, freshwater fish and fragrant herbs.

  • Fish amok

    Fish steamed in coconut curry paste, traditionally in banana leaf.

    Where to try it Restaurants across the country.

  • Lok lak

    Stir-fried beef with a lime and pepper dipping sauce.

    Where to try it Local restaurants.

  • Kuy teav

    A noodle soup eaten for breakfast.

    Where to try it Street stalls.

  • Num banh chok

    Rice noodles with green fish curry.

    Where to try it Morning markets.

  • Kampot pepper crab

    Crab stir-fried with local pepper.

    Where to try it Kep.

  • Fried insects

    A crunchy snack for the brave.

    Where to try it Markets.

Before you go

Practical travel information for Cambodia

The essentials, current for 2026. Cambodia is welcoming and straightforward, but check official sources before travelling.

Capital
Phnom Penh
Language
Khmer is the official language. English is widely spoken in tourist areas.
Currency
Cambodian riel (KHR) and US dollars both circulate. Around 4,000 riel to the dollar.
Cards and cash
US dollars are used in cities. ATMs dispense dollars. Carry small notes, and riel for small purchases.
Entry and visas
Most nationalities need a visa, available as an e-visa or on arrival. Check current rules and fees.
Power sockets
Type A, C and G, 230 V, 50 Hz. Bring an adaptor.
Emergency number
117 for police, 119 for ambulance, 118 for fire.
Tipping
Tipping is not required, but small tips are appreciated for guides and drivers.
Safety
Cambodia is generally safe, but watch for bag-snatching from motorbikes and petty theft. Stay on marked paths in rural areas because of unexploded mines, and drink bottled water.
Internet and SIM
Good in cities, patchy in remote areas. SIMs from Cellcard, Smart and Metfone are cheap.
Time zone
Indochina Time (UTC+7), with no daylight saving.

When to go

The dry, cool season from November to February is best for temples and sightseeing.

    Best time

    Think twice

Trip length

How long to spend in Cambodia

Cambodia is small, but travel is slow. Three or four bases work well.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Cambodia

Cambodians are polite, gentle and respectful of hierarchy and religion. The traditional greeting is the sampeah, palms pressed together, and keeping face matters.

  • Greet with a sampeah

    Palms together, a slight bow.

  • Dress modestly

    Cover shoulders and knees at temples.

  • Remove shoes

    At temples and homes.

  • Respect monks

    Women should not touch monks.

  • Keep calm

    Raised voices cause loss of face.

  • Ask before photos

    Especially of monks and people.

Do

  • Learn a few Khmer words
  • Dress modestly at temples
  • Bargain politely
  • Carry small US dollar notes
  • Use bottled water
  • Respect memorials

Don't

  • Do not touch heads
  • Do not point feet at people or Buddhas
  • Do not climb restricted structures
  • Do not give money to begging children
  • Do not litter at heritage sites
  • Do not leave marked paths in rural areas

Travel tips

Cambodia travel tips

The things that most often catch visitors out in Cambodia.

  • Buy Angkor passes only at the official ticket office.
  • Carry small US dollar notes.
  • Dress modestly at temples.
  • Start early to avoid heat.
  • Agree tuk-tuk prices in advance.
  • Stay on marked paths in rural areas.
  • Hold bags away from the road side.
  • Drink bottled water.

FAQ

Visiting Cambodia: frequently asked questions

Is Cambodia safe for tourists?

Generally yes, with normal care against petty theft.

How many days do I need?

Ten to fourteen days.

Do I need a visa?

Most do. Check e-visa options.

What is the best time to visit?

November to February.

Is Cambodia cheap?

Yes, one of the cheapest in the region.

Can I use US dollars?

Yes, widely.
