Welcome to Banteay Srei and Kulen Mountain

Banteay Srei, about 25 kilometres north of Angkor Wat, is a small 10th-century Hindu temple made of rose-coloured sandstone, covered in intricate carvings that are among the finest in Khmer art. Its name means "citadel of women", perhaps for the delicate work. It is easily reached by tuk-tuk in under an hour.

Further north, the Kulen Mountain is a sacred sandstone plateau where Khmer king Jayavarman II proclaimed independence in 802. The Kbal Spean site, the "River of a Thousand Lingas", has carvings of Hindu symbols on the riverbed, reached by a forest walk. At the top of Kulen, a giant reclining Buddha, a waterfall and swimming pools draw local families.

Banteay Srei and Kbal Spean combine neatly into a half-day, while Kulen needs a full day. These are included on a Angkor pass, except Kulen which has its own fee. The sites are less crowded, and a nice change from the main temples.
