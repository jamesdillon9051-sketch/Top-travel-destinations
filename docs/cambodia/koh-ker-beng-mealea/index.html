Welcome to Koh Ker and Beng Mealea

Beyond the Angkor park lie some of the most atmospheric Khmer temples. Beng Mealea, about 65 kilometres east of Siem Reap, is a vast 12th-century temple left largely as found, with towers and galleries collapsed under trees and moss. Wooden walkways help you explore, and it feels like the jungle temples of old photographs.

Koh Ker, a further 90 minutes north, was briefly the capital of the Khmer Empire in the 10th century. Dozens of temples are scattered through the forest, including Prasat Thom and its great seven-tier pyramid, which visitors can climb for views over the treetops. Because of its remoteness, Koh Ker is rarely crowded.

Most visitors do both in a long day trip with a driver. Roads are generally good, though the Koh Ker area still has marked mine clearance zones, so stay on paths. It is a fine way to see temples without the crowds.
