Welcome to Esteros del Iberá

The Esteros del Iberá is one of the largest freshwater wetlands in South America, an area of about 13,000 square kilometres of lagoons, marshes and floating islands, fed by rainfall rather than rivers, in the province of Corrientes. Its shallow, still waters are covered with aquatic plants, and much of the surface is made up of camalotes, floating mats of vegetation. It is home to more than 350 species of bird, and to caimans, capybaras, marsh deer, howler monkeys and many others.

For decades, poaching and cattle ranching reduced the wildlife, but a large conservation project, begun by the American philanthropist Douglas Tompkins and continued by his foundation and the Argentine government, has bought and donated land, creating Iberá National Park in 2018. The project has reintroduced species that had disappeared from the area, including giant anteaters, pampas deer, giant otters and, in recent years, jaguars, and it is one of the most ambitious rewilding projects in South America.

Iberá is remote and quiet. The main base is the small village of Colonia Carlos Pellegrini, on the edge of a large lagoon, where lodges and guesthouses offer boat trips, walks, horseback rides and night safaris. It is not an easy place to reach, and it is not for those who want a big-name attraction, but it is a peaceful and rewarding place for wildlife lovers.

Why visit

Why visit Esteros del Iberá?

Iberá offers close encounters with wetland wildlife in a quiet, uncrowded setting, and a chance to support conservation.

  • Wildlife everywhere

    Caimans, capybaras, marsh deer, howler monkeys and hundreds of bird species.

  • Quiet boat safaris

    Small motorless or low-noise boats drift through lagoons and among floating islands.

  • Rewilding in action

    A project bringing back anteaters, otters, deer and even jaguars.

  • Peace and stars

    Dark skies and a slow pace.

  • Gaucho culture

    Horse riding and estancia hospitality.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Esteros del Iberá

Most experiences are guided, from Colonia Carlos Pellegrini and nearby lodges.

  1. 01

    Laguna Iberá boat safari

    A boat trip on the large lagoon beside Carlos Pellegrini, with a guide who spots caimans, capybaras, snail kites and marsh deer among the floating islands.

    Tip Go at dawn or late afternoon.

  2. 02

    Caimans and capybaras

    Yacaré caimans bask on banks and capybaras graze in groups, often at close range, in a landscape where wildlife has little fear of people.

    Tip Keep a safe distance.

  3. 03

    Birdwatching

    More than 350 species, including jabiru storks, herons, spoonbills, ducks, kingfishers and the rare strange-tailed tyrant.

    Tip Bring binoculars.

  4. 04

    The Laguna Iberá lookout

    A viewpoint over the lagoon, near the village, for sunset.

    Tip A short walk.

  5. 05

    Rewilding centres

    Visitors can see where jaguars, giant otters and other species are prepared for release, though not the animals themselves in some cases.

    Tip Ask a guide about the project.

  6. 06

    Flying over the marshes

    The vast scale of the wetland is best seen from above, on a small-plane flight.

    Tip Book at your lodge.

Local food

What to eat in Esteros del Iberá

Food in Iberá is simple and regional, with river fish, beef and local sweets.

  • Surubí and pacú

    River fish, grilled or in stews.

    Where to try it Lodges and restaurants.

  • Chipa

    Cheesy cassava bread rolls.

    Where to try it Bakeries.

  • Asado

    Grilled beef, the traditional meal.

    Where to try it Lodges.

  • Mbaipy and sopa paraguaya

    Corn-based dishes of the Guaraní tradition.

    Where to try it Local restaurants.

  • Tereré

    Cold mate, a common drink.

    Where to try it Everywhere.

Transport

Getting to and around Esteros del Iberá

Iberá is remote, and reached by road, with some unpaved sections.

Getting there

  • Fly to Corrientes or Posadas, then drive

    About 4 to 5 hours from either airport, with the last stretch unpaved.

    Hire a car or arrange a transfer.

  • Transfer or tour

    Lodges arrange transfers from airports and Iguazú.

    Book in advance.

  • Bus to Mercedes, then transfer

    Buses reach Mercedes, about 120 km from Carlos Pellegrini.

    Book a shuttle.

Getting around

Everything is guided and close to Carlos Pellegrini.

  • Boat

    The main way to see wildlife.

    Book at your lodge.

  • On foot or by bicycle

    Around the village and trails.

    Bring repellent.

  • 4WD vehicle

    For longer excursions.

    Arranged by lodges.

When to go

Best time to visit Esteros del Iberá

Iberá has a humid subtropical climate, with hot, rainy summers and mild winters. Winter is cooler and drier, with fewer insects.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Think twice

    Hot, humid and rainy, with mosquitoes.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Pleasant temperatures, good wildlife.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Best time

    Mild days, cool nights and fewer insects.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm, with birds in breeding plumage.

Worth planning around: January and February for heat and insects.

Travel tips

Esteros del Iberá travel tips

A few tips make the visit more comfortable.

  • Visit between March and November, avoiding the summer heat and insects.
  • Bring insect repellent, sun protection, binoculars and a hat.
  • Book lodges and transfers in advance.
  • Carry cash, since there are few ATMs.
  • Do not feed animals, and keep a safe distance.
  • Take a night safari for different wildlife.
  • Allow time to slow down.

FAQ

Esteros del Iberá: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Iberá?

Three days is ideal.

Can I see jaguars?

The rewilding project has released jaguars, but sightings are rare. Caimans, capybaras and birds are much easier.

What is the best time to visit?

March to November.

How do I get there?

By road from Corrientes or Posadas, about 4 to 5 hours, or by lodge transfer.

Is Iberá like the Pantanal?

Similar, though quieter and less visited. The Pantanal has more large mammals.

Is it safe?

Yes, with guides and normal precautions.
