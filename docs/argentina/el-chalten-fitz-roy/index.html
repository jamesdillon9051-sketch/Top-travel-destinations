Welcome to El Chaltén

El Chaltén was founded in 1985, largely to establish an Argentine presence in an area disputed with Chile, and it has grown into a village of about 3,000 people with a single purpose: hiking. It sits in the Los Glaciares National Park, in a valley beneath Fitz Roy (3,405 metres), whose Tehuelche name, Chaltén, means smoking mountain, for the cloud that often clings to its summit, and its neighbour Cerro Torre. Together, these granite spires form some of the most spectacular mountain scenery in the world.

The great appeal of El Chaltén is that the trails begin at the edge of the village, and require no permits, guides or park fees. A day's walk can reach an alpine lake with the towers reflected in it, and there is a choice of routes, from easy lakeside strolls to demanding ascents. For serious climbers, the granite walls are a legendary challenge.

The weather is the wild card. Patagonia's wind, rain and sun can change within an hour, and clear days are precious. The village is small, with brewpubs, simple hotels and a relaxed atmosphere, and it is at its best between November and March, though autumn brings golden beech forests and fewer people.

Why visit

Why visit El Chaltén?

El Chaltén offers some of the finest day hiking in the world, right from the village.

  • Trails from the door

    No permits or fees, and well-marked paths from the village.

  • Iconic peaks

    Fitz Roy and Cerro Torre, among the most dramatic mountains in the Andes.

  • Day walks for all levels

    From a two-hour stroll to a full-day climb to a glacial lake.

  • Glaciers and lakes

    Turquoise lagoons and hanging glaciers.

  • A friendly village

    Craft beer, hearty food and a community of walkers.

Main attractions

Top things to see in El Chaltén

The walks are the attractions, and a few classic routes stand out.

  1. 01

    Laguna de los Tres and Fitz Roy

    The classic hike, about 20 km round trip and 8 to 10 hours, ending with a steep final climb to a lake directly beneath Fitz Roy's summit.

    Tip Start early, on a clear day, and bring warm layers.

  2. 02

    Laguna Torre and Cerro Torre

    An easier day hike of about 18 km round trip and 6 to 7 hours, to a glacial lake beneath the needle of Cerro Torre, often wrapped in cloud.

    Tip Best on a clear day.

  3. 03

    Mirador de los Cóndores and Las Águilas

    A short hike of about 1 hour up from the village, to viewpoints over the town, valley and mountains.

    Tip Perfect for arrival day.

  4. 04

    Chorrillo del Salto waterfall

    A short, easy walk of about 3 km to a waterfall in the forest, a good option for a windy day.

    Tip A family-friendly walk.

  5. 05

    Loma del Pliegue Tumbado

    A high-altitude route of about 20 km to a ridge with a panorama of Fitz Roy and the ice field.

    Tip Best on a clear day.

  6. 06

    The Huemul Circuit

    A multi-day trek around a glacial lake, with a river crossing and stunning views.

    Tip Take a guide.

Transport

Getting to and around El Chaltén

El Chaltén is 215 km north of El Calafate, reached by a good road.

Getting there

  • Bus from El Calafate

    About 3 hours, with several daily services.

    Book in advance in summer.

  • Transfer or hire car

    About 3 hours by road, with wide views.

    Fuel up in El Calafate.

  • From Chile

    Via El Calafate, or via Villa O'Higgins by ferry and trail in summer.

    For adventurous travellers.

When to go

Best time to visit El Chaltén

Patagonia's climate is cool and windy, with snow in winter. Summer has the best hiking weather, and autumn brings beautiful colours.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    Longest days, warmest weather and the most people.

  • Autumn (March–April)

    Best time

    Fewer crowds and golden forests.

  • Winter (May–September)

    Think twice

    Snowy, with most services closed.

  • Spring (October–November)

    Good

    Windy and cool, with fewer visitors.

Worth planning around: Winter, when the village is nearly empty and trails are snowbound.

Travel tips

El Chaltén travel tips

The mountains are close, but the weather demands respect.

  • Check the weather and go on the clearest day you can.
  • Carry layers, waterproofs, gloves and a hat, and a windproof jacket.
  • Bring lunch, water and snacks, since there is no food on the trails.
  • Register with park rangers and follow the rules for camping and fires.
  • Start early. The days are long in summer.
  • Bring cash, as ATMs are unreliable.
  • Book accommodation ahead in January.

FAQ

El Chaltén: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in El Chaltén?

Three days is ideal, for two big hikes and a rest day.

Do I need a guide?

Not for the main day hikes, which are well marked. Guides are recommended for glacier treks and multi-day routes.

Is there an entry fee?

The main trails have no entry fee, but check the current park rules.

What is the best time to visit?

November to March, with April for autumn colours.

How hard is the Laguna de los Tres hike?

It is a long day of about 20 km with a steep final section. It suits reasonably fit walkers.

Can I see Fitz Roy without hiking?

Yes, from viewpoints near the village and along the road, weather permitting.
