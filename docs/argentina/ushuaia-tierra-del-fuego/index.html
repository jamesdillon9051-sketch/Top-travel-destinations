Welcome to Ushuaia

Ushuaia, on the Beagle Channel at the southern tip of South America, is widely called the southernmost city in the world, at 54° 48' south, although some tiny settlements in Chile lie further south. It lies at the foot of the Martial Mountains, on the large island of Tierra del Fuego, which is shared by Argentina and Chile, and it was founded in 1884, first as a mission and then as a penal colony. Its former prison, now a museum, is one of its main sights.

The city now lives on tourism, fishing and its role as the main departure port for cruises to Antarctica, which sail from about November to March. A visit here does not need an Antarctic trip, though, since the surroundings are spectacular: boat trips on the Beagle Channel pass sea lions, cormorants and lighthouses, penguin colonies can be visited on Isla Martillo and Estancia Harberton, and Tierra del Fuego National Park offers forest and lake walks and the end of Ruta 3, the road that begins in Buenos Aires.

The climate is cool and changeable even in summer, and the light is long and strong, with sunset after 10pm in December. It is at its best from November to March, and winter offers skiing and quiet. It is a place of dramatic landscapes and the thrill of standing near the end of the world.

Why visit

Why visit Ushuaia?

Ushuaia is a unique destination at the edge of the map, with wildlife and wilderness at its doorstep.

  • The end of the world

    The southernmost city, where the Andes plunge into the sea.

  • Beagle Channel cruises

    Sea lions, cormorants, lighthouses and mountain views.

  • Penguins

    Magellanic and Gentoo penguins on Isla Martillo, one of the few places where you can walk among them.

  • Tierra del Fuego National Park

    Forests, lakes and coastal paths.

  • Gateway to Antarctica

    Most Antarctic cruises leave from here.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Ushuaia

Most sights are on the water or in the nearby national park.

  1. 01

    The Beagle Channel and Les Éclaireurs Lighthouse

    The strait named after Darwin's ship, with boat trips to sea lion colonies, bird islands and the picturesque red-and-white lighthouse, often called the lighthouse at the end of the world.

    Tip Book a morning departure, and dress warmly.

  2. 02

    Tierra del Fuego National Park

    The country's southernmost national park, with coastal trails, southern beech forests, peat bogs and the sign marking the end of Ruta 3, at Lapataia Bay, 3,000 km from Buenos Aires.

    Tip Walk the coastal path.

  3. 03

    The End of the World Train

    A narrow-gauge steam railway that follows the route once used by prisoners to bring timber, through the national park.

    Tip Book in advance.

  4. 04

    Isla Martillo penguin colony

    An island with Magellanic and Gentoo penguins, visited on small-boat tours, and one of the few places where visitors can land and walk among penguins.

    Tip Book with a licensed operator, and go from October to March.

  5. 05

    The Maritime and Prison Museums

    The former prison houses museums on the penal colony, maritime history and Antarctic exploration.

    Tip Allow a couple of hours.

  6. 06

    Martial Glacier

    A short chairlift and walk above the town to a small glacier, with views of the channel.

    Tip Go early.

Things to do

Best things to do in Ushuaia

Ushuaia is a place for water, walks and a sense of adventure.

  1. Sail the Beagle Channel

    See sea lions, seabirds and lighthouses.

    Tip Choose a small boat.

  2. Walk among penguins

    A guided landing on Isla Martillo.

    Tip Book far ahead.

  3. Hike in the national park

    Coastal and forest trails.

    Tip Bring waterproofs.

  4. Ride the End of the World Train

    A steam train through the forest.

    Tip Combine with a park visit.

  5. Eat king crab

    Centolla is a local speciality.

    Tip Try it in a local restaurant.

  6. Ski or go dog-sledding

    Winter sports at Cerro Castor.

    Tip Best from June to September.

Local food

What to eat in Ushuaia

Ushuaia's food is seafood and Patagonian lamb, at high prices.

  • Centolla (king crab)

    The sweet meat of the southern king crab, served hot or cold.

    Where to try it Seafood restaurants.

  • Patagonian lamb

    Roasted over an open fire.

    Where to try it Parrillas.

  • Fresh fish

    Hake, trout and mussels.

    Where to try it Restaurants on the waterfront.

  • Craft beer

    Local microbreweries.

    Where to try it Brewpubs.

  • Hot chocolate and pastries

    Warming after the cold.

    Where to try it Cafés.

Transport

Getting to and around Ushuaia

Ushuaia is reached by air, or by long road trips through Chile.

Getting there

  • Flight to Ushuaia (USH)

    Direct flights from Buenos Aires (about 3 hours 30 minutes), El Calafate and other cities.

    Book early for summer.

  • Bus and ferry from Río Gallegos

    A long journey via Chile and the Strait of Magellan.

    About 12 to 14 hours.

  • By cruise

    Some Antarctic and Patagonian cruises start and end here.

    Book well ahead.

Getting around

The town is small, and tours and taxis reach the sights.

  • Walking

    The centre and waterfront.

    Wear warm layers.

  • Taxi and minibus

    To the national park and trailheads.

    Agree the fare.

  • Boat

    Beagle Channel trips.

    Book at the port.

When to go

Best time to visit Ushuaia

Ushuaia has a cold, windy, changeable oceanic climate, with cool summers and cold winters.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    Long days, mild weather and the busiest season.

  • Autumn (March–April)

    Good

    Beautiful autumn colours and fewer visitors.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Quiet

    Cold and snowy, with skiing and few visitors.

  • Spring (October–November)

    Good

    Cool, with penguins arriving.

Worth planning around: Winter for boat trips and penguins, since services close.

Travel tips

Ushuaia travel tips

A few tips help you prepare.

  • Bring layers, waterproofs, gloves and a hat, even in summer.
  • Book penguin landings and the train ahead.
  • Check the weather and wind for boat trips.
  • Bring sunscreen, since the sun is strong.
  • Book Antarctic cruises many months ahead, or look for last-minute deals in Ushuaia.
  • Carry cash.
  • Allow extra time for flights, which are prone to delays.

FAQ

Ushuaia: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Ushuaia?

Three or four days.

Is it really the southernmost city?

It is widely called so, though small settlements in Chile, such as Puerto Williams, lie further south.

Can I see penguins?

Yes, on Isla Martillo and at Estancia Harberton, from about October to March.

What is the best time to visit?

November to March.

Can I go to Antarctica from Ushuaia?

Yes, cruises leave from about November to March. Book well ahead.

Is Ushuaia expensive?

Yes, particularly for accommodation, food and cruises.
