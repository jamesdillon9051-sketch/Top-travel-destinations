Welcome to Bariloche

San Carlos de Bariloche sits on the southern shore of Lake Nahuel Huapi, a huge glacial lake in the Andes of northern Patagonia, surrounded by forested mountains and the snow-capped Cerro Tronador. It was settled in the early 20th century by Swiss, German and other European immigrants, and their influence shows in its alpine-style timber architecture, its chocolate shops and its restaurants serving fondue, smoked trout and venison.

Nahuel Huapi National Park, established in 1934, is the oldest in Argentina and encloses the town and the lakes. Its landscape offers many activities: a scenic loop called the Circuito Chico around the Llao Llao peninsula, chairlifts and cable cars to viewpoints, forest hikes, lake cruises to islands, and, in winter, skiing at Cerro Catedral, one of the largest ski areas in South America.

Further afield, the Route of the Seven Lakes leads north to San Martín de los Andes, passing a series of lakes and forests, and Villa La Angostura is a quieter alternative, with a small centre and waterfront. The region is popular with Argentines, particularly in January, July and for school graduation trips, and it is best to visit in autumn or spring, when it is quieter and the colours are striking.

Why visit

Why visit Bariloche?

Bariloche and the Lake District are among the most scenic parts of Argentina, with a mix of outdoor activities and comfort.

  • Lakes and mountains

    Turquoise water, forested peaks and snowy summits in every direction.

  • Outdoor variety

    Hiking, skiing, kayaking, cycling and horse riding.

  • Scenic drives

    The Circuito Chico and the Route of the Seven Lakes are two of the great drives in Argentina.

  • Chocolate and food

    Artisan chocolate, craft beer, trout and lamb.

  • Year-round appeal

    Ski in winter, hike in summer, and see the colours in autumn.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Bariloche

The best sights are around the lake, and a car or bike makes exploring easy.

  1. 01

    The Circuito Chico

    A 60-km loop west of town along the lake shore, with viewpoints, small lakes, beaches, forest, chocolate cafés and the Llao Llao peninsula.

    Tip Drive, cycle or take a bus tour.

  2. 02

    Llao Llao

    A peninsula with a famous 1938 hotel, forest trails and lake views, at the end of the Circuito Chico.

    Tip Walk the Bosque Encantado trail.

  3. 03

    Cerro Catedral

    The ski resort about 20 km from town, with lifts, runs and views of the lake in winter. In summer, lifts open for hiking and mountain biking.

    Tip Take a lift to the top for the views.

  4. 04

    Cerro Campanario

    A short chairlift ride to a panoramic viewpoint over the lakes and islands, often ranked among the best views in the world.

    Tip Go on a clear afternoon.

  5. 05

    Lake Nahuel Huapi and Isla Victoria

    Boat trips on the lake reach Isla Victoria and the Arrayanes forest, with unusual cinnamon-barked trees.

    Tip Book a half-day trip.

  6. 06

    Villa La Angostura

    A charming village north of Bariloche, on a peninsula, with a small centre, lakes and walking trails.

    Tip A good quiet base.

Things to do

Best things to do in Bariloche

Outdoor activities and scenic drives make up the main things to do.

  1. Drive the Circuito Chico

    Stop at viewpoints, lakes and cafés.

    Tip Allow half a day.

  2. Ski at Cerro Catedral

    Great skiing from June to September, with lake views.

    Tip Book lift tickets and lessons ahead.

  3. Hike to a refuge

    Trails lead to mountain huts, such as Refugio Frey and Refugio López.

    Tip Check the weather.

  4. Try Bariloche chocolate

    Shops on Mitre Street sell chocolate in many forms.

    Tip Visit a chocolate factory.

  5. Drive the Seven Lakes route

    A scenic road with lakes, forest and mountain views, from Villa La Angostura to San Martín de los Andes.

    Tip Allow a full day.

  6. Kayak on the lake

    Paddle to hidden coves in summer.

    Tip Go in the morning.

Local food

What to eat in Bariloche

Bariloche's food is Patagonian-Alpine, with lamb, trout, venison, fondue and chocolate.

  • Chocolate

    Bariloche is Argentina's chocolate capital, with hot chocolate, truffles and eggs.

    Where to try it Shops and cafés on Mitre Street.

  • Smoked trout and salmon

    Local fish, smoked or grilled.

    Where to try it Restaurants and lodges.

  • Cordero and jabalí

    Patagonian lamb, wild boar and venison.

    Where to try it Parrillas.

  • Fondue

    Swiss-style cheese and chocolate fondue.

    Where to try it Alpine restaurants.

  • Craft beer

    Bariloche has a strong microbrewery tradition.

    Where to try it Brewpubs.

Transport

Getting to and around Bariloche

Bariloche is in the Andes of northern Patagonia, about 1,600 km from Buenos Aires.

Getting there

  • Flight to Bariloche (BRC)

    Direct flights from Buenos Aires (about 2 hours), El Calafate and other cities.

    The airport is about 20 minutes from town.

  • Bus

    Long-distance buses from Buenos Aires take about 20 hours, and from Mendoza about 17 hours.

    Fly for long distances.

  • From Chile

    Via the Puyehue pass, or the boat-and-bus Cruce de Lagos to Puerto Montt.

    A scenic crossing.

Getting around

A car is ideal, but buses and tours serve the main sights.

  • Hire car

    Good for the Circuito Chico and Seven Lakes.

    Roads can be icy in winter.

  • Local buses

    Reach Llao Llao and Cerro Catedral.

    Check timetables.

  • Bicycle

    Popular for the Circuito Chico.

    Roads can be busy.

When to go

Best time to visit Bariloche

Bariloche has a cool climate, with snowy winters and pleasant summers.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    Warm, with long days and hiking weather. Busy in January.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Golden beech forests and fewer visitors.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Snow and skiing, cold days and busy in July.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Blossom, and lower prices.

Worth planning around: Early December, when high school graduation groups fill the town, and the busiest weeks in January and July.

Travel tips

Bariloche travel tips

A few tips make the Lakes District more enjoyable.

  • Book ski lift tickets and accommodation ahead for July and January.
  • Drive the Circuito Chico early to avoid traffic.
  • Bring layers. The weather can change quickly.
  • Try the chocolate, and the craft beer.
  • Check road conditions in winter.
  • Avoid the town in early December, when it is full of school groups.
  • Book boat trips in advance.

FAQ

Bariloche: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Bariloche?

Three or four days for the main sights.

What is the best time to visit?

December to March for hiking, and April for autumn colours. June to September for skiing.

Is Bariloche only for skiing?

No. Summer and autumn are equally lovely, with lakes, hiking and drives.

What is Bariloche known for?

Chocolate, lakes, mountains and skiing.

Can I visit Chile from Bariloche?

Yes, by road via the Puyehue pass, or by the scenic Cruce de Lagos.

Is it better to hire a car?

It helps for the Circuito Chico and the Seven Lakes route, though buses and tours are available.
