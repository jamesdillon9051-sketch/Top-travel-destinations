Welcome to El Calafate

El Calafate is a small town on the southern shore of Lago Argentino, the largest lake in Argentina, in Patagonia. It has grown from a sleepy sheep-farming outpost into a busy tourist town of hotels, restaurants and tour operators, and its purpose is one: to serve as the gateway to the Los Glaciares National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981, which covers a vast landscape of ice, lakes and mountains at the edge of the Southern Patagonian Ice Field.

The star of the park is the Perito Moreno Glacier, about 30 km long and 5 km wide, that descends from the ice field to Lago Argentino. It is one of very few glaciers in the world that is stable or advancing rather than retreating, and its front, about 60 metres above the water, regularly calves huge blocks of ice with a thunderous crack. A network of walkways offers close views from the opposite bank, and boat trips and guided ice treks allow closer encounters.

The town is 80 km from the glacier, and it makes a good base for two or three days. Boat trips to the Upsala and Spegazzini glaciers, walks, estancia visits and evening lamb asados round out a stay, and it is the departure point for El Chaltén and Torres del Paine in Chile.

Why visit

Why visit El Calafate?

Perito Moreno is one of the world's most accessible and dramatic glaciers, and a highlight of Patagonia.

  • An active glacier

    Ice that groans, cracks and calves in view of the walkways.

  • Easy access

    Walkways, boats and treks for all abilities.

  • Other glaciers

    Boat trips reach Upsala and Spegazzini glaciers and icebergs.

  • Patagonian scenery

    Steppe, lakes and mountains beneath huge skies.

  • A base for El Chaltén

    The town links Patagonia's great sights.

Main attractions

Top things to see in El Calafate

The glacier is the main event, with boat trips and estancias as options.

  1. 01

    The Perito Moreno walkways

    About 4 km of steel walkways and viewing platforms facing the glacier, from which you can watch and hear the ice calving.

    Tip Go early or late, and be patient.

  2. 02

    Boat trip on Lago Argentino

    A boat trip along the glacier's face, at a safe distance, gives a sense of its scale, with cliffs of ice towering overhead.

    Tip Book combined tickets.

  3. 03

    Ice trekking (Minitrekking or Big Ice)

    Guided walks on the glacier's surface with crampons, through crevasses and ice caves, ending with a whisky on the ice.

    Tip Book in advance, and check fitness and age limits.

  4. 04

    Upsala and Spegazzini glaciers

    Full-day boat trips to other glaciers in the national park, passing through icebergs to great ice walls.

    Tip Go on a clear day.

  5. 05

    Lago Argentino

    A huge milky-turquoise glacial lake, with viewpoints along the shore of El Calafate and the road to the glacier.

    Tip Try Laguna Nimez, a bird reserve on the edge of town.

  6. 06

    An estancia visit

    Patagonian sheep ranches near El Calafate, such as Estancia Cristina, offer horseback riding, lunch and tours.

    Tip Book a day visit.

Things to do

Best things to do in El Calafate

Most visitors spend at least one day at the glacier, and the rest exploring.

  1. Walk the Perito Moreno balconies

    Listen for the cracks, and watch for calving.

    Tip Bring a windproof jacket.

  2. Trek on the glacier

    See crevasses and blue ice up close.

    Tip Wear waterproof layers.

  3. Sail to Upsala Glacier

    See enormous icebergs and a vast glacier front.

    Tip Book the full-day trip.

  4. Eat Patagonian lamb

    Cordero patagónico is slow-roasted on a cross over an open fire.

    Tip Try it at a local parrilla.

  5. Visit the Glaciarium

    A museum about the ice field, with an ice bar.

    Tip Good for a rainy day.

  6. Continue to El Chaltén

    A 3-hour bus ride takes you to Argentina's hiking capital.

    Tip Book the bus ahead.

Local food

What to eat in El Calafate

El Calafate's food centres on Patagonian lamb, along with trout, calafate berries and craft beer.

  • Cordero patagónico

    Lamb slow-roasted over an open fire, tender and lightly smoky.

    Where to try it Parrillas in El Calafate.

  • Trout

    Fresh river and lake trout, grilled or baked.

    Where to try it Restaurants on the main street.

  • Calafate berry desserts

    The town's namesake berry, in jam, ice cream and liqueur. According to legend, those who eat it will return.

    Where to try it Ice-cream shops.

  • Craft beer

    Local microbreweries serve fresh beer.

    Where to try it Brewpubs in town.

  • Mate and medialunas

    Traditional snacks.

    Where to try it Cafés.

Transport

Getting to and around El Calafate

El Calafate is in southern Patagonia, about 2,700 km from Buenos Aires.

Getting there

  • Flight to El Calafate (FTE)

    Direct flights from Buenos Aires (about 3 hours), Ushuaia and Bariloche.

    The airport is 20 minutes from town.

  • Bus from El Chaltén

    About 3 hours, with frequent services.

    Book in advance.

  • Bus from Puerto Natales, Chile

    About 5 to 6 hours, with a border crossing.

    Carry passports.

Getting around

Buses and tours reach the glacier, and the town is walkable.

  • Bus or tour to the glacier

    About 1 hour to the glacier, with regular buses and tours.

    Buy tickets in advance.

  • Hire car

    Gives freedom to visit at quieter times.

    Watch out for wind and gravel.

  • Walking

    The town centre.

    Wear warm layers.

When to go

Best time to visit El Calafate

Summers are cool, and winters are cold, with most services closed from May to September.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    Longest days, the warmest weather and the busiest time.

  • Autumn (March–April)

    Best time

    Fewer crowds, autumn colours and crisp days.

  • Winter (May–September)

    Think twice

    Cold, with limited services.

  • Spring (October–November)

    Good

    Cool and windy, with lower prices.

Worth planning around: Winter, when many hotels and tours close.

Travel tips

El Calafate travel tips

A few tips help you plan the glacier visit.

  • Book glacier trips and buses in advance in summer.
  • Bring windproof and waterproof layers, gloves and a hat.
  • Go to the walkways early or late, when tour groups are fewer.
  • Wear sturdy shoes, and sunglasses for the glare.
  • Carry cash for park entry and small purchases.
  • Check weather and wind forecasts.
  • Be patient and wait for calving.

FAQ

El Calafate: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in El Calafate?

Two to three days. One is for the glacier, and one for other trips.

Is the Perito Moreno Glacier still growing?

It has been relatively stable, unlike most glaciers, though it changes from year to year.

Can I walk on the glacier?

Yes, on guided ice treks with crampons, from Minitrekking to longer Big Ice tours.

What is the best time to visit?

October to April.

How far is the glacier from town?

About 80 km, 1 hour by road.

Can I combine El Calafate with Torres del Paine?

Yes. Buses cross the border to Puerto Natales, about 5 to 6 hours.
