Welcome to Mendoza

Mendoza is the heart of Argentine wine. The province produces about 70% of the country's wine, most of it in vineyards at altitudes of 700 to 1,500 metres on the eastern slope of the Andes, where hot days, cool nights and irrigation from mountain snowmelt create ideal conditions for grapes. Malbec, a French variety that has found its best expression here, is the signature, alongside Cabernet Sauvignon, Bonarda, Torrontés and other varieties.

The city of Mendoza itself is calm and elegant, with wide streets shaded by plane trees, irrigation channels, or acequias, and five plazas, rebuilt after a devastating earthquake in 1861. Restaurants and wine bars fill its streets, and the cafés are lively on summer evenings. Around it, the main wine regions are Luján de Cuyo, the oldest area with historic bodegas, Maipú, close to the city and easy to visit by bicycle, and the high-altitude Uco Valley, an hour or more away, with modern wineries in front of the Andes.

Beyond wine, Mendoza is a base for adventure. Aconcagua, at 6,961 metres the highest mountain outside Asia, is about three hours away, and river rafting, horse riding, hiking and skiing are all available. It is a sociable, sunny and delicious destination, and most visitors stay three or four days.

Why visit

Why visit Mendoza?

Mendoza is one of the world's great wine regions, with mountain scenery and a relaxed pace.

  • Malbec and much more

    Tastings at hundreds of wineries, from small family bodegas to architectural showpieces.

  • A stunning setting

    Vineyards backed by snowy Andean peaks.

  • Food and lunch

    Wine-region restaurants offer superb multi-course lunches and asados.

  • A pleasant city

    Tree-lined streets, plazas and a lively café and restaurant culture.

  • Adventure on the doorstep

    Aconcagua, rafting, hiking and skiing.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Mendoza

Wineries, the city and the Andes make up the visit.

  1. 01

    Luján de Cuyo

    The birthplace of Argentine Malbec, with historic bodegas and modern wineries. Many offer tours, tastings and lunch.

    Tip Book visits ahead, and choose three or four.

  2. 02

    Maipú

    The closest wine region to Mendoza, with a mix of old and new wineries, olive oil producers and a flat landscape suited to cycling.

    Tip Rent a bike, but do not overdo the tastings.

  3. 03

    The Uco Valley

    A high-altitude region about 1 to 1.5 hours away, with world-class wineries, striking architecture and views of the Andes.

    Tip Go with a driver, and book lunch.

  4. 04

    Mendoza's plazas

    Plaza Independencia, the central square, is surrounded by four smaller plazas, Spain, Italy, Chile and San Martín, each with its own style.

    Tip Go for a stroll in the evening.

  5. 05

    Aconcagua Provincial Park

    The park around the highest peak in the Americas, with viewpoints and short walks at the Laguna Horcones trailhead.

    Tip Go on a clear day.

  6. 06

    Puente del Inca

    A natural stone bridge over the Vacas River, stained yellow and orange by mineral springs, on the road to Chile.

    Tip Combine with Aconcagua.

Things to do

Best things to do in Mendoza

Wine dominates, with adventure and views available too.

  1. Take a wine tour

    Visit three or four wineries, taste, and have lunch.

    Tip Book a driver or a tour.

  2. Cycle through Maipú

    Ride between wineries and olive oil producers.

    Tip Start early.

  3. Try white-water rafting

    Rapids on the Mendoza River, near Potrerillos.

    Tip Book from October to April.

  4. Visit Aconcagua

    A day trip along the Andean road to the highest peak.

    Tip Start early, and bring layers.

  5. Have an asado lunch

    Grilled meats and wine, in the vineyards.

    Tip Book in advance.

  6. Ski in winter

    Las Leñas and Penitentes are the resorts.

    Tip Check the snow.

Local food

What to eat in Mendoza

Mendoza's cuisine pairs beef and empanadas with olive oil, vegetables and wine.

  • Asado and parrilla

    Grilled meats, especially beef, served with Malbec.

    Where to try it Bodegas and city restaurants.

  • Empanadas mendocinas

    Baked pastries with beef, onion, olives and hard-boiled egg.

    Where to try it Bakeries.

  • Olive oil

    Local producers make fine oil.

    Where to try it Farms in Maipú.

  • Malbec and Torrontés

    The region's wines.

    Where to try it Every winery.

  • Dulce de leche and alfajores

    Sweet treats.

    Where to try it Shops.

Transport

Getting to and around Mendoza

Mendoza is about 1,000 km west of Buenos Aires, at the foot of the Andes.

Getting there

  • Flight to Mendoza (MDZ)

    Direct flights from Buenos Aires, about 1 hour 45 minutes.

    The airport is close to the city.

  • Bus from Buenos Aires or Santiago

    About 14 hours from Buenos Aires, and 6 to 7 hours from Santiago over the Andes.

    The pass can close in snow.

  • Hire car

    Good for the wineries and the Andean road.

    Drink responsibly, or hire a driver.

Getting around

Taxis, tours and bicycles are the main ways to reach the wineries.

  • Wine tour or driver

    The easiest way to visit several wineries.

    Book in advance.

  • Bicycle

    Popular in Maipú and Luján de Cuyo.

    Wear sun protection.

  • Taxi and bus

    In the city and to nearby areas.

    Agree fares.

When to go

Best time to visit Mendoza

Mendoza has a sunny, dry climate, with hot summers and cold winters.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Good

    Hot, sunny and long days, with outdoor dining.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Harvest season, with warm days and golden vines. The Vendimia festival in late February and early March.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Quiet

    Cold, with snow in the mountains and skiing.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Mild, blooming and quieter.

Worth planning around: The hottest days in January and the busiest weekends during Vendimia.

Travel tips

Mendoza travel tips

A few tips make wine touring easier.

  • Book winery visits and lunch in advance.
  • Plan two or three wineries a day, not more.
  • Hire a driver, or use a tour, if you plan to drink.
  • Wear sun protection, and take water.
  • Try wines from different regions.
  • Buy wine directly at the winery.
  • Check the Andean pass before crossing to Chile in winter.

FAQ

Mendoza: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Mendoza?

Three or four days for wineries, the city and the mountains.

What is the best time to visit?

March to April for the harvest, and October to December for mild weather.

What wine should I try?

Malbec is the signature, and Torrontés, Cabernet Franc and Bonarda are also worth trying.

Which wine region should I visit first?

Luján de Cuyo for history, Maipú for easy cycling and the Uco Valley for altitude and views.

Can I see Aconcagua from Mendoza?

Not from the city, but on a day trip on the Andean road.

Is Mendoza safe?

Generally yes, with normal precautions.
