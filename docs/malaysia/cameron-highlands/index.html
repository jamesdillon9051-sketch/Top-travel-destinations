Welcome to Cameron Highlands

The Cameron Highlands is a hill station in Pahang, developed by the British in the 1920s specifically to escape the heat of the lowlands, sitting at over 1,500 metres above sea level.

The temperature drop is dramatic — daytime highs rarely exceed the low 20s Celsius, a striking change from the tropical heat almost everywhere else in Malaysia.

Tea plantations, strawberry farms and an ancient mossy forest fill the highlands, making it a popular two-day detour on a longer Peninsular Malaysia itinerary.

Why visit

Why visit Cameron Highlands?

The Cameron Highlands' case rests almost entirely on being a genuine change of climate and landscape within a few hours of the lowlands.

  • The climate is a real change of pace

    Cool mountain air replaces tropical heat, a welcome break partway through a Malaysia trip.

  • The tea plantations are photogenic

    Rolling terraced hills that look transplanted from somewhere else entirely.

  • The Mossy Forest is a striking ancient ecosystem

    A stunted, moss-draped cloud forest walked on raised boardwalks.

  • It's an easy add-on to a Peninsular itinerary

    Sitting between Kuala Lumpur and Ipoh or Penang, it fits naturally into a west coast route.

  • Strawberry farms make for an easy, family-friendly stop

    A novelty in tropical Malaysia, and a popular reason locals visit too.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Cameron Highlands

The Cameron Highlands' sights are spread across tea estates, farms and the Mossy Forest above Brinchang.

  1. 01

    Boh Tea Plantation

    Malaysia's best-known tea estate, its terraced hillsides among the most photographed scenes in the country.

    Tip Take the factory tour to see how the tea is processed, then relax with a cup at the estate café overlooking the plantation.

  2. 02

    The Mossy Forest

    An ancient, stunted cloud forest near Brinchang's summit, its trees draped thickly in moss and ferns.

    Tip Go early morning for the mist that gives the forest its atmosphere and its name.

  3. 03

    Raaju's Hill Strawberry Farm

    One of several strawberry farms in the highlands, popular for picking your own and strawberry-based treats.

    Tip Prices at farm cafés run higher than in town — factor that in if strawberry desserts are the goal.

  4. 04

    An Orang Asli village

    Indigenous communities living in and around the highlands, visited respectfully on some guided tours.

    Tip Only visit as part of a responsible, community-endorsed tour rather than showing up independently.

  5. 05

    Kampung Taman Sedia's farms

    A farming area outside Tanah Rata with strawberry, vegetable and flower farms open to visitors.

    Tip Combine with a stop at a roadside stall selling fresh highland produce.

  6. 06

    Brinchang town and its night market

    The highlands' main town, with a lively night market selling local produce, snacks and strawberry treats.

    Tip The night market runs Friday, Saturday and Sunday evenings — plan around it if possible.

  7. 07

    The highlands' terraced hillsides

    The wider landscape itself is worth slowing down for, terraced farmland and tea rolling across the hills.

    Tip Several roadside viewpoints along the main routes offer good photo stops without a formal detour.

Things to do

Best things to do in Cameron Highlands

The Cameron Highlands rewards a slower pace than the rest of a Malaysia itinerary, built around farms and walking trails.

  1. Tour a tea plantation and factory

    Boh's factory tour explains the full process from leaf to cup, finishing with tea tasting.

    Tip Arrive earlier in the day — the estate café and viewpoints get busy by midday.

  2. Walk the Mossy Forest boardwalks

    Raised wooden walkways lead through the stunted, moss-covered forest near Brinchang's summit.

    Tip Bring a light rain jacket — this altitude sees mist and drizzle even on otherwise dry days.

  3. Pick your own strawberries

    Several farms let visitors pick strawberries directly, a popular activity for families.

    Tip Weigh expectations against price — this is a novelty experience more than a bargain.

  4. Visit a local market for highland produce

    Cameron Highlands grows a large share of Malaysia's temperate vegetables and flowers, sold fresh at local markets.

    Tip The Kea Farm market near Brinchang is a popular, well-stocked stop.

  5. Hike one of the highlands' jungle trails

    A network of numbered trails around Tanah Rata and Brinchang range from easy walks to fuller day hikes.

    Tip Stick to marked trails and inform your accommodation of your route — visitors have gotten lost here before.

  6. Warm up with a steamboat dinner

    The highlands' cool evenings make a hot-pot-style steamboat dinner especially appealing.

    Tip A popular way to end a day here, and a genuine change from the rest of a tropical Malaysia trip.

Local food

What to eat in Cameron Highlands

Cameron Highlands food leans into the cool climate, with produce, tea and steamboat all shaped by the altitude.

  • Cameron Highlands tea

    Grown and processed on the highlands' own estates, best tried fresh at a plantation café.

    Where to try it Boh Tea Plantation's café and shops throughout Tanah Rata and Brinchang.

  • Fresh strawberries and strawberry desserts

    The highlands' signature produce, sold fresh or made into jams, cakes and shakes.

    Where to try it Strawberry farms and roadside stalls throughout the highlands.

  • Steamboat (hot pot)

    A shared pot of broth cooked at the table with vegetables, meat and seafood, well suited to the cool evenings.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout Tanah Rata and Brinchang.

  • Highland vegetables

    Temperate vegetables that don't grow well in lowland Malaysia, a genuine local specialty.

    Where to try it Local markets and restaurants throughout the highlands.

  • Scones and English-style teatime

    A holdover from the highlands' colonial-era origins, still served at several plantation cafés.

    Where to try it Boh Tea Plantation's café and heritage hotels in Tanah Rata.

  • Local honey

    Bee farms in the highlands sell raw honey, often available to sample before buying.

    Where to try it Bee farms and markets around Brinchang.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Cameron Highlands

The Cameron Highlands' accommodation ranges from simple guesthouses to colonial-era heritage hotels, mostly in Tanah Rata or Brinchang.

  • Tanah Rata

    The highlands' main town, well set up for visitors without a car.

    Best for: A central, walkable base with plenty of restaurants

  • Brinchang

    A smaller town slightly higher up, closer to the highlands' natural attractions.

    Best for: Proximity to the Mossy Forest and tea estates

Transport

Getting to and around Cameron Highlands

The Cameron Highlands are reached by road, with winding mountain roads making the final stretch slower than the distance suggests.

Getting there

  • Long-distance coach from Kuala Lumpur or Ipoh

    The standard option for visitors without a car.

    Takes around 3.5-4 hours from Kuala Lumpur, less from Ipoh.

  • Rental car or private transfer

    The most flexible option, allowing stops along the winding mountain road.

    The final approach involves many curves — those prone to motion sickness should plan accordingly.

  • Organised tour from Kuala Lumpur

    A convenient option for a focused day or overnight trip.

    Typically covers tea plantations, the Mossy Forest and a strawberry farm in one itinerary.

Getting around

The highlands are spread out enough that a car or organised tour helps, though Tanah Rata itself is walkable.

  • Rental car

    The most flexible way to combine tea estates, farms and the Mossy Forest in one trip.

    Roads are well maintained but winding — drive cautiously in mist or rain.

  • Local taxis

    A practical option for visitors without their own transport.

    Agree a price for multi-stop trips ahead of time.

  • On foot within Tanah Rata

    The town centre itself is compact and easily walkable.

    Attractions outside town need other transport to reach.

When to go

Best time to visit Cameron Highlands

The highlands' cool climate holds steady year-round, with rainfall the main seasonal variable.

  • December–March

    Best time

    Generally drier, with clearer conditions for the Mossy Forest and viewpoints.

  • April–June

    Good

    Warm days by highland standards, with a moderate chance of afternoon mist and rain.

  • July–November

    Good

    The wetter stretch of the year, with more frequent mist and rain.

  • Any season

    Good

    The cool climate itself holds steady year-round, unlike the lowlands' more dramatic seasonal shifts.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — pack a light jacket regardless of season, since the highlands stay cool year-round.

Itinerary

Two days in the Cameron Highlands

Built around tea plantations, the Mossy Forest and the highlands' farms.

  1. Day1

    Arrival and tea country

    Travel up and a first taste of the highlands.

    • Morning Travel from Kuala Lumpur or Ipoh.
    • Afternoon Visit Boh Tea Plantation and its factory tour.
    • Evening A steamboat dinner in Tanah Rata.
  2. Day2

    The Mossy Forest and farms

    A full day covering the highlands' natural side and produce.

    • Morning Walk the Mossy Forest boardwalks near Brinchang.
    • Afternoon Visit a strawberry farm and the Kea Farm market.
    • Evening A final highland dinner before descending.

Travel tips

Cameron Highlands travel tips

Practical notes that make a Cameron Highlands visit noticeably smoother.

  • Pack a light jacket — the highlands run noticeably cooler than the rest of Malaysia.
  • Book a tea plantation tour ahead in peak weekend periods.
  • Watch for motion sickness on the winding mountain roads if prone to it.
  • Visit the Mossy Forest early morning for the best mist and light.
  • Only visit Orang Asli villages through a responsible, community-endorsed tour.
  • Try steamboat dinner at least once — it suits the cool evenings especially well.

FAQ

Cameron Highlands: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in the Cameron Highlands?

Two days covers the tea plantations, Mossy Forest and a strawberry farm comfortably.

How do you get to the Cameron Highlands?

By coach or car from Kuala Lumpur or Ipoh, both taking a few hours on winding mountain roads.

Is it cold in the Cameron Highlands?

Cool rather than cold by most standards — daytime highs rarely exceed the low 20s Celsius, with cooler nights.

What is the Cameron Highlands known for?

Tea plantations, strawberry farms and the ancient Mossy Forest, all shaped by its cool mountain climate.

Do you need a car in the Cameron Highlands?

Not essential, but useful for reaching tea estates and farms spread beyond Tanah Rata.

Is the Cameron Highlands worth visiting?

Yes, especially as a change of pace partway through a warmer Malaysia itinerary.
