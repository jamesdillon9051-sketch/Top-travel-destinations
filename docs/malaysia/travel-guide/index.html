How to plan a Malaysia trip: which region to choose, when to go around two separate monsoon seasons, what it costs, and a practical ten-day Peninsular Malaysia itinerary.

How Malaysia divides

Malaysia splits into two physically separate halves across the South China Sea — a first trip realistically covers one.

Peninsular Malaysia's west coast. Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Malacca, Langkawi, the Cameron Highlands and Ipoh — cities, hill stations and heritage towns connected by good roads and rail.

Peninsular Malaysia's east coast. the Perhentian Islands and Taman Negara — beaches, diving and ancient rainforest, on a very different monsoon calendar from the west coast.

East Malaysia (Borneo). Kota Kinabalu and Kuching — Sabah and Sarawak, reached only by air, with Mount Kinabalu, orangutans and longhouse culture.

Worth knowing: Most first-time visitors pick the Peninsula's west coast — it works as a complete trip on its own and doesn't require a single domestic flight beyond arrival.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If this is your first trip

    Kuala Lumpur, Penang and Malacca, the classic Peninsular introduction.

  • If you want cooler weather and green scenery

    The Cameron Highlands, a genuine change of pace from the tropical lowlands.

  • If you want beach resorts with easy access

    Langkawi, reachable by a short flight or ferry with no monsoon complications on the west coast.

  • If you want serious diving and a backpacker pace

    The Perhentian Islands, best fit into a March–October trip.

  • If you want rainforest and wildlife over beaches

    Taman Negara, among the world's oldest rainforests.

  • If you want a dedicated Borneo trip

    Kota Kinabalu and Kuching, best planned as their own separate itinerary.

Getting around: flights, trains and one common mistake

The Peninsula's west coast is easy to cover by train and coach; Borneo and the east coast islands need their own flights.

Don't try to combine the Peninsula and Borneo in one short trip. Kota Kinabalu and Kuching both need a dedicated domestic flight and add several days on their own.

Use the KTM ETS train for the west coast corridor. Kuala Lumpur, Ipoh and Penang are connected by frequent, comfortable high-speed rail.

Check the east coast monsoon before booking. The Perhentian Islands largely close from November to February — this catches first-time planners out more than anything else.

Rent a car for the highlands. The Cameron Highlands are far easier to explore at your own pace with your own vehicle.

Worth knowing: Book Sabah or Sarawak flights well ahead if adding Borneo — seats on the popular Kuala Lumpur routes fill up in peak holiday periods.

When to go

Malaysia is hot and humid year-round, so 'the best time' really means picking a coast rather than a season.

December to February is the driest stretch for the west coast — Kuala Lumpur, Penang, Malacca, Langkawi and the highlands.

March to October is the main season for the east coast — the Perhentian Islands and Taman Negara's river access.

November to February brings the northeast monsoon to the east coast, closing most island resorts and complicating river trips inland.

Worth knowing: If a trip has to pick just one season, December to February works well for everywhere except the east coast islands and rainforest interior.

Four routes that work

  1. Five days: Kuala Lumpur and Malacca

    A focused city-and-heritage-town introduction, flying into Kuala Lumpur.

    Tip Malacca works well as a two-night side trip rather than a rushed day return.

  2. Ten days: the classic Peninsular loop

    The itinerary on the Malaysia country page: Kuala Lumpur, Malacca, the Cameron Highlands, Ipoh, Penang and Langkawi. A complete first look at the west coast.

    Tip Book Langkawi's cable car for early morning — cloud cover often rolls in by early afternoon.

  3. A week focused on the east coast

    The Perhentian Islands and Taman Negara, best combined between March and October.

    Tip Fly into Kota Bharu or Kuala Terengganu rather than routing everything through Kuala Lumpur.

  4. A dedicated Borneo add-on for a second trip

    Kota Kinabalu and Kuching, the parts of Malaysia most first-time visitors never see.

    Tip Book Mount Kinabalu climb permits months ahead — daily numbers are capped and popular dates sell out.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Malaysia

How many days do you need in Malaysia?

Ten to fourteen days covers Peninsular Malaysia's west coast properly, such as the itinerary in this guide. Borneo is best treated as a separate trip.

Should I combine Peninsular Malaysia and Borneo in one trip?

It's possible with three weeks or more, but most visitors get better value focusing on one region and returning for the other.

Do you need a car in Malaysia?

Not for the main west coast cities, which are well connected by train and coach, but a car helps for exploring the Cameron Highlands.

When should I avoid the Perhentian Islands?

November through February, when the northeast monsoon brings rough seas and most island resorts close for the season.
