Welcome to Kuching

Kuching is the capital of Sarawak, Malaysia's largest state on the island of Borneo, a relaxed riverside city that serves as the main base for exploring the surrounding rainforest.

Its name means "cat" in Malay, a link celebrated across the city in statues and a dedicated museum, though its real draw is what lies just outside it — wild orangutans, rainforest national parks and Iban longhouse culture.

Compared to Kota Kinabalu, Kuching leans more toward culture and rainforest day trips than beaches, making the two Borneo cities a different pair of gateways depending on what a trip is built around.

Why visit

Why visit Kuching?

Kuching's case rests on genuine access to wild orangutans and rainforest within day-trip range of a comfortable, walkable city.

  • Semenggoh's orangutans are wild

    Semi-wild orangutans roam free in the surrounding forest, visited rather than kept in enclosures.

  • Bako National Park packs a lot into a short trip

    Rainforest, proboscis monkeys and dramatic coastline reachable on a day trip from the city.

  • The waterfront is pleasant

    A landscaped promenade along the Sarawak River, lively with food stalls in the evening.

  • It's a genuine base for Iban longhouse culture

    Day trips and overnight stays at traditional longhouses are more accessible from here than from Kota Kinabalu.

  • The pace suits a slower few days

    A relaxed city, easier to settle into than a typical big Southeast Asian capital.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Kuching

Kuching's essential experiences combine the city's riverside centre with day trips to nearby wildlife and rainforest.

  1. 01

    Semenggoh Wildlife Centre

    A rehabilitation centre where semi-wild orangutans roam free in the surrounding forest, visited during scheduled feeding times.

    Tip Sightings aren't guaranteed outside fruiting season, when wild orangutans have less need to visit the feeding platform.

  2. 02

    Bako National Park

    Malaysia's oldest national park, its sea cliffs, mangroves and rainforest trails home to proboscis monkeys found almost nowhere else.

    Tip Visit on a day trip via boat from Bako village, reachable by bus or taxi from Kuching.

  3. 03

    The Sarawak River waterfront

    A landscaped riverside promenade lined with food stalls, gardens and views across to the Astana palace.

    Tip Come in the evening when the food stalls open and the heat of the day has eased.

  4. 04

    Kuching's cat statues

    Scattered across the city in reference to its name, from a large roundabout monument to smaller statues on street corners.

    Tip The main Cat Statue at the Padungan roundabout is the most photographed, but keep an eye out for smaller ones too.

  5. 05

    The Old Sarawak State Mosque

    A striking riverside mosque with golden domes, among the city's most recognisable landmarks.

    Tip Visible clearly from the opposite riverbank — a good photo spot even without visiting the interior.

  6. 06

    A Sarawak longhouse

    Traditional communal Iban houses, some open to respectful day trips or overnight stays outside the city.

    Tip Book through a responsible tour operator with genuine community relationships rather than an informal arrangement.

  7. 07

    The Sarawak Cultural Village

    A living museum recreating traditional houses of Sarawak's different ethnic groups, with cultural performances.

    Tip A useful, accessible introduction to Sarawak's cultures for visitors without time for a genuine longhouse visit.

Things to do

Best things to do in Kuching

Kuching rewards day trips into the surrounding rainforest as much as time spent in the city itself.

  1. See wild orangutans at Semenggoh

    Scheduled morning and afternoon feeding times offer the best chance of a sighting, though nothing is guaranteed.

    Tip Arrive well before the feeding time slot — spaces at the viewing platform fill up.

  2. Day-trip to Bako National Park

    A boat ride and short trek reveal sea cliffs, mangroves and a real chance of spotting proboscis monkeys.

    Tip Go early — both wildlife activity and boat conditions are better before midday.

  3. Walk the Sarawak River waterfront

    An easy evening activity, mixing river views with food stalls and a relaxed local atmosphere.

    Tip Take the small river taxi across to the Astana side for a different view back at the city.

  4. Visit or stay at a Sarawak longhouse

    A day trip or overnight stay offers a genuine look at Iban communal living and culture.

    Tip Choose an operator with an established, respectful relationship with the community you'll visit.

  5. Explore the Sarawak Cultural Village

    A half-day visit covering traditional architecture and performances from Sarawak's various ethnic groups.

    Tip Time your visit around the scheduled cultural performances for the fullest experience.

  6. Try Sarawak laksa for breakfast

    An essential Kuching food experience, distinct from laksa found elsewhere in Malaysia.

    Tip Try it at a busy local kopitiam rather than a tourist-oriented restaurant.

Local food

What to eat in Kuching

Kuching's food centres on Sarawak laksa and other dishes distinct from Peninsular Malaysian cooking, shaped by the state's own traditions.

  • Sarawak laksa

    A rich, spiced coconut-based noodle soup distinct from Penang's sour version, closely associated with Kuching.

    Where to try it Kopitiams throughout the city, especially for breakfast.

  • Kolo mee

    Dry egg noodles tossed in a light sauce, topped with minced pork or char siu, a Sarawak staple.

    Where to try it Noodle stalls and kopitiams throughout Kuching.

  • Manok pansoh (chicken cooked in bamboo)

    A traditional Iban dish of chicken and herbs cooked inside a bamboo tube over an open fire.

    Where to try it Restaurants specialising in Sarawakian or Iban cuisine.

  • Midin (jungle fern)

    A local jungle fern, typically stir-fried with garlic or belacan, found on most Sarawakian menus.

    Where to try it Local restaurants throughout Kuching.

  • Layer cake (kek lapis Sarawak)

    An elaborately patterned, multi-layered cake distinctly associated with Sarawak.

    Where to try it Bakeries and markets throughout the city.

  • Tuak (rice wine)

    A traditional fermented rice wine from Sarawak's indigenous communities, often served at longhouse visits.

    Where to try it Longhouse visits and some cultural restaurants in the city.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Kuching

Kuching's accommodation is generally good value, concentrated near the waterfront and old town.

Transport

Getting to and around Kuching

Kuching is reached by air, requiring a separate domestic flight from Peninsular Malaysia or Kota Kinabalu.

Getting there

  • Fly into Kuching International Airport

    The only realistic option for most visitors, with frequent flights from Kuala Lumpur.

    The airport sits about 20 minutes from the city centre.

  • Connect from Kota Kinabalu

    A domestic flight links Sarawak and Sabah's two main cities.

    Useful for a Borneo trip covering both states, though it adds meaningful travel time.

Getting around

Central Kuching is walkable, with taxis, ride-hailing or organised tours needed for day trips outside the city.

  • On foot

    Covers the waterfront, old town and cat statues comfortably.

    Most central sights sit within a compact, walkable core.

  • Ride-hailing apps or taxis

    Useful for reaching Semenggoh or the Sarawak Cultural Village.

    Cheap and reliable for point-to-point trips outside the centre.

  • Organised day tours

    The standard way to reach Bako National Park or a longhouse visit.

    Bundles transport, boat travel and guiding into one straightforward booking.

When to go

Best time to visit Kuching

Kuching sees a wetter stretch from November to February, similar to Kota Kinabalu's pattern.

  • March–October

    Best time

    Generally drier, with better conditions for Bako National Park and other outdoor day trips.

  • November–February

    Good

    The wetter stretch of the year, with more frequent rain.

  • Year-round

    Good

    Semenggoh's orangutan sightings and city sightseeing remain workable most of the year.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly, though heavier rain from November to February can affect Bako National Park's boat access.

Itinerary

Three days in Kuching

Built around the city, wild orangutans and a rainforest day trip.

  1. Day1

    The city and waterfront

    Settling into Kuching's riverside centre.

    • Morning Arrive and check in to your hotel.
    • Afternoon Explore the old town and Kuching's cat statues.
    • Evening Dinner and a walk along the Sarawak River waterfront.
  2. Day2

    Semenggoh and culture

    Wild orangutans and a look at Sarawak's traditions.

    • Morning Visit Semenggoh Wildlife Centre for the morning feeding.
    • Afternoon Explore the Sarawak Cultural Village.
    • Evening Try Sarawak laksa and other local dishes for dinner.
  3. Day3

    Bako National Park

    A full day trip into the rainforest.

    • Morning Boat to Bako National Park.

    • Evening Return to Kuching for a final dinner.

Travel tips

Kuching travel tips

Practical notes that make a Kuching visit noticeably smoother.

  • Check Semenggoh's feeding schedule and arrive early for the best chance of a sighting.
  • Book Bako National Park's boat transfer through an operator or the park itself in advance.
  • Only visit a longhouse through a responsible, community-endorsed tour operator.
  • Try Sarawak laksa for breakfast at least once — it's distinct from laksa elsewhere in Malaysia.
  • Pack for rainforest humidity even outside the wetter months.
  • Treat Kuching as a separate Borneo base from Kota Kinabalu unless you have time for both.

FAQ

Kuching: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Kuching?

Three to four days covers the city, Semenggoh's orangutans and a Bako National Park day trip comfortably.

Can you guarantee seeing orangutans at Semenggoh?

No — sightings depend on wild fruit availability, though scheduled feeding times offer a good chance, especially outside fruiting season.

How do you get to Kuching?

By domestic flight from Kuala Lumpur or other Malaysian cities — Sarawak has no land connection to the Peninsula.

Is Kuching better than Kota Kinabalu?

They suit different trips — Kuching leans toward culture and rainforest, while Kota Kinabalu leans toward islands and Mount Kinabalu.

What is Kuching's most famous dish?

Sarawak laksa, a rich, spiced coconut noodle soup distinct from laksa found elsewhere in Malaysia.

Is Kuching safe?

Yes, generally — a relaxed, safe city, with normal precautions around belongings in markets and on tours.
