Welcome to Arequipa & Colca Canyon

Arequipa is genuinely one of Peru's most underrated cities, its historic centre built almost entirely from sillar, a pale volcanic stone that gives the whole city a striking, luminous white character.

Beyond the city, Colca Canyon is genuinely one of the deepest canyons on Earth, roughly twice the depth of the Grand Canyon, and one of the most reliable places anywhere to watch Andean condors soar at close range.

Most visitors genuinely spend a day or two in Arequipa itself before heading out to Colca for an early-morning condor watch at the famous Cruz del Cóndor viewpoint, a trip that also passes through genuinely traditional Andean villages along the way.

The region's essential sights combine genuinely striking colonial architecture with one of South America's great wildlife experiences.

    Tip Genuinely the surrounding cathedral's rooftop tour offers sweeping views over the square and city.

  2. 02

Transport

Getting to and around Arequipa & Colca Canyon

Arequipa is genuinely well connected by flight and long-distance bus, Peru's second-largest city.

Getting there

  • Flight from Lima or Cusco

    Genuinely the fastest option, both under two hours.

    Frequent daily flights make Arequipa an easy addition to a wider Peru itinerary.

  • Bus from Puno

    Genuinely a comfortable overland option, taking around five to six hours.

    A practical way to combine Arequipa with Lake Titicaca.

  • Long-distance bus from Lima

    Genuinely a long but comfortable overnight option on reclining-seat services.

    Takes around 16 hours, a budget alternative to flying.

Getting around

Arequipa's historic centre is genuinely walkable, while Colca Canyon requires an organised tour or rental car.

  • On foot in central Arequipa

    Genuinely covers the Plaza de Armas and Santa Catalina Monastery easily.

    The historic centre is compact and pleasant to explore on foot.

  • Organised Colca Canyon tours

    Genuinely the standard way to reach Cruz del Cóndor, given the early start required.

    Widely available from Arequipa, typically as an overnight or two-day trip.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely useful for a more flexible Colca Canyon visit with an overnight in the valley.

    Roads to the canyon are generally well maintained but wind through high mountain passes.

When to go

Best time to visit Arequipa & Colca Canyon

Arequipa's genuinely mild, sunny climate makes it pleasant year-round, though the dry season offers the clearest Colca Canyon conditions.

  • Dry season (May–September)

    Best time

    Genuinely the clearest skies for condor watching and canyon views, also the busiest season.

  • Shoulder months (April, October)

    Good

    Genuinely fewer crowds with still-decent weather odds.

  • Wet season (November–March)

    Good

    Genuinely lush and green in the valley, though cloud cover can affect condor sightings and mountain pass conditions.

Travel tips

Arequipa & Colca Canyon travel tips

Practical notes that make an Arequipa and Colca Canyon visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book genuinely an overnight Colca Canyon tour rather than a rushed single-day trip if possible.
  • Arrive genuinely at Cruz del Cóndor by 8am for the most reliable condor sightings.
  • Pack genuinely warm layers for the Colca Valley — mountain pass temperatures drop sharply.
  • Allow genuinely at least two hours for Santa Catalina Monastery, larger than it first appears.

FAQ

Arequipa & Colca Canyon: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need for Arequipa and Colca Canyon?

Three days genuinely covers Arequipa's historic centre and a Colca Canyon condor-watching trip comfortably.

Is Colca Canyon deeper than the Grand Canyon?

Yes, genuinely — Colca Canyon is roughly twice as deep as the Grand Canyon at its most dramatic point.

Are condor sightings at Cruz del Cóndor guaranteed?

Not guaranteed, but genuinely very reliable, especially with an early morning arrival before around 9-10am.

What is Arequipa known for?

Genuinely its striking white volcanic-stone architecture, earning it UNESCO World Heritage status, alongside Santa Catalina Monastery.

Do you need to acclimatise before visiting Colca Canyon?

Genuinely helpful, yes — the surrounding valley sits at real altitude, and some viewpoints exceed 4,000 metres.

Can you visit Colca Canyon as a day trip from Arequipa?

It's genuinely possible but rushed, given the early start required — an overnight in the valley allows a far more comfortable pace.
