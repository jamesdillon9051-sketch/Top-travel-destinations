Welcome to the Amazon (Puerto Maldonado)

Puerto Maldonado is genuinely Peru's easiest gateway into the Amazon, a small jungle town at the meeting point of the Madre de Dios and Tambopata rivers, and the starting point for lodge stays deep in the rainforest.

The surrounding region is genuinely one of the most biodiverse places on Earth, part of the Tambopata National Reserve, home to giant river otters, hundreds of bird species and macaw clay licks found in only a handful of places worldwide.

Most visitors genuinely arrive on an all-inclusive multi-night lodge package, travelling upriver by boat to a genuinely remote setting with no road access, guided daily wildlife excursions built into the stay.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Amazon (Puerto Maldonado)

The Amazon genuinely rewards patience, with the best wildlife sightings coming from guided excursions at dawn and dusk.

  1. Spot giant river otters at Lake Sandoval

    A genuinely thrilling early-morning canoe trip, watching one of the Amazon's most endangered mammals in its natural habitat.

    Tip Genuinely bring a zoom lens or binoculars — otters keep a respectful distance from canoes.

  2. Watch macaws at a clay lick

    A genuinely unforgettable dawn spectacle, dozens of macaws descending in a wash of colour to eat mineral-rich clay.

    Tip Genuinely arrive before sunrise and stay quiet — sudden noise can scare the birds away.

  3. Search for caimans by night

    A genuinely atmospheric excursion, gliding silently by canoe as a guide's spotlight picks out caiman eyes along the shore.

    Tip Genuinely dress warmly — jungle nights on the water are cooler than daytime temperatures suggest.

  4. Explore Tambopata's rainforest trails

    A genuinely eye-opening guided night walk, revealing an entirely different set of species active after dark.

    Tip Genuinely a good guide can spot camouflaged insects and frogs that are otherwise invisible.

  5. Spot mealy parrots along the river

    A genuinely rewarding, easy sighting for birdwatchers, common along the Tambopata River's forested banks.

    Tip Genuinely bring binoculars — many bird sightings here are at a distance in the canopy.

  6. Watch birds dry their feathers at Lake Sandoval

    A genuinely peaceful sight, cormorants and other water birds spreading their wings to dry along the lake's edge.

    Tip Genuinely a quiet moment of canoe stillness improves the chances of a close, unhurried view.

When to go

Best time to visit the Amazon (Puerto Maldonado)

The Amazon's genuinely own separate dry season differs from the Andean highlands, with lower water levels concentrating wildlife near rivers.

  • Dry season (June–October)

    Best time

    Genuinely the easiest wildlife spotting, with lower water levels concentrating animals near rivers and clay licks.

  • Shoulder months (May, November)

    Good

    Genuinely fewer crowds with still-decent wildlife spotting conditions.

  • Wet season (December–April)

    Good

    Genuinely lush and dramatic, with higher water levels making some trails inaccessible, though birdlife remains excellent.

Worth planning around: Expecting guaranteed big-mammal sightings in any season — genuinely most Amazon wildlife is elusive, and birds, insects and reptiles are the more reliable highlights.

Travel tips

The Amazon (Puerto Maldonado) travel tips

Practical notes that make an Amazon lodge visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book genuinely a multi-night lodge package rather than a single day trip — wildlife sightings improve significantly with more time.
  • Pack genuinely lightweight, quick-dry clothing in neutral colours, plus a rain jacket regardless of season.
  • Bring genuinely strong insect repellent and consider anti-malarial medication after consulting a doctor.
  • Pack genuinely binoculars — most wildlife sightings here are at a distance.
  • Manage genuinely expectations on jaguar or big-cat sightings, which are rare even for experienced guides.
  • Confirm genuinely your lodge's cancellation policy given the real possibility of river-level or weather delays.

The Amazon (Puerto Maldonado): frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in the Amazon?

Three to four days genuinely allows for a proper lodge stay with multiple wildlife excursions, improving your odds significantly over a rushed single day.

Is Puerto Maldonado a good Amazon gateway?

Yes, genuinely one of the most accessible, reachable by a short flight from Cusco directly into the rainforest.

Genuinely likely for birds, insects, caimans and often giant otters, though large mammals like jaguars are rare, elusive sightings even for experienced guides.
