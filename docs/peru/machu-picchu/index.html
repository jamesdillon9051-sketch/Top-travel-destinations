Welcome to Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu is genuinely Peru's single most famous sight, a 15th-century Inca citadel perched on a ridge high above the Urubamba River, one of the only major ancient wonders the Spanish conquest never located or destroyed.

Rediscovered by the outside world in 1911, its genuinely extraordinary setting — surrounded by cloud forest peaks, most dramatically Huayna Picchu rising directly behind the ruins — has made it one of the most recognisable images in world travel.

Most visitors genuinely arrive by train from Cusco or the Sacred Valley to the small town of Aguas Calientes below, then take an early bus up to the site itself, timed to catch the classic view before the day's crowds build.

Machu Picchu genuinely rewards an early start, whatever else fills the rest of the day.

  1. Watch sunrise from the classic viewpoint

    Tip Genuinely catch the first bus up from Aguas Calientes, which departs before dawn.

  2. Hike Huayna Picchu

    A genuinely steep, exposed climb rewarded with a bird's-eye view down over the entire citadel.

    Tip Genuinely not for those uncomfortable with heights — sections of the trail are narrow with real drops.

  3. Examine the Intihuatana stone up close

    Tip Genuinely touching the stone is prohibited — a protective barrier now keeps visitors at a respectful distance.

    Tip Genuinely the town's hot springs are a welcome way to soothe tired legs afterward.

  6. Photograph the classic postcard view

Transport

Getting to and around Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu is genuinely reachable only by train or a multi-day trek — no road connects Aguas Calientes to the outside world.

Getting there

  • Train from Cusco or the Sacred Valley

    Genuinely the standard way in, run by PeruRail or Inca Rail to Aguas Calientes.

    Departures from Ollantaytambo in the Sacred Valley are generally shorter and cheaper than from Cusco itself.

  • The classic Inca Trail

    Genuinely a multi-day trek arriving at the Sun Gate above the citadel, for those who book permits far ahead.

    A profoundly rewarding, if strenuous, alternative to arriving by train.

  • Bus from Aguas Calientes to the citadel

    Genuinely the final short leg, a winding 20-minute ride up from the town to the entrance gate.

    Buses run continuously from early morning, with queues forming well before opening.

Getting around

Once at the site, Machu Picchu is genuinely explored entirely on foot, following marked circuit routes.

  • On foot within the site

    Genuinely the only way to explore the citadel, following one of several fixed, one-way circuit routes.

    Routes are assigned at entry and cannot be changed once inside, per current park rules.

  • Bus between Aguas Calientes and the entrance

    Genuinely the practical way up and down, though a steep hiking trail also exists for the fit and determined.

    Buy bus tickets in Aguas Calientes ahead of your entry time.

Best time to visit Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu is genuinely visitable year-round, with the dry season offering the most reliable views free of low cloud.

  • Dry season (May–September)

Travel tips

Machu Picchu travel tips

Practical notes that make a Machu Picchu visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book genuinely entry tickets, trains and any Huayna Picchu permit months ahead, especially for June–August travel.
  • Catch genuinely the first bus up from Aguas Calientes for the best light and thinnest crowds.
  • Bring genuinely your passport — it's required to enter, matching the name on your ticket.
  • Pack genuinely a poncho or light rain layer, even in the dry season, given the cloud forest setting.

FAQ

Machu Picchu: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need at Machu Picchu?

One full day genuinely covers the citadel itself; an overnight in Aguas Calientes allows a more relaxed, unrushed visit.

Do I need to book Machu Picchu tickets in advance?

Yes, genuinely essential — daily visitor numbers are capped, and popular entry slots sell out well ahead, especially in the June–August dry season.

Is Huayna Picchu worth climbing?

Yes, genuinely, for those comfortable with heights and steep trails — it offers a spectacular bird's-eye view, though tickets are separate and limited.

Can you visit Machu Picchu without hiking the Inca Trail?

Yes, genuinely — most visitors arrive by train to Aguas Calientes and bus up to the site, no trekking required.

What is Machu Picchu known for?

Genuinely being one of the few major Inca sites the Spanish conquest never found, its dramatic cloud forest setting now one of the world's most iconic views.

How high is Machu Picchu?

Genuinely around 2,430 metres, notably lower than Cusco, which makes it a comparatively easier stop for those still adjusting to altitude.
