Welcome to Lake Titicaca & Puno

Lake Titicaca is genuinely the world's highest navigable lake, a vast, deep-blue expanse straddling the Peru-Bolivia border at over 3,800 metres, ringed by traditional Andean communities.

Its most famous residents, the Uros people, genuinely live on floating islands built and continuously rebuilt from layers of totora reed, a way of life dating back centuries and still practised today, not staged for visitors.

Most visitors genuinely base themselves in Puno, a workaday lakeside city, using it to reach the Uros islands, Taquile's traditional weaving communities and the pre-Inca burial towers at nearby Sillustani.

Transport

Getting to and around Lake Titicaca & Puno

Puno is genuinely reached by bus or flight via nearby Juliaca, with Cusco the most common connecting point.

Getting there

  • Overnight or day bus from Cusco

    Genuinely comfortable on reclining-seat services, taking around six to seven hours.

    Some routes stop at scenic and historic sites along the way.

  • Flight to Juliaca

    Genuinely the fastest option, with a short onward drive of about 45 minutes to Puno.

    Connects to Lima and Cusco with regular daily flights.

  • Bus or train from Arequipa

    Genuinely a practical link for combining Titicaca with southern Peru.

    The scenic train route is a memorable, if slower, alternative to the bus.

Getting around

Boats are genuinely essential for reaching the lake's islands, with Puno itself easily walkable.

  • Organised boat tours

    Genuinely the standard way to reach the Uros islands, Taquile and Amantaní.

    Widely available from Puno's harbour, bookable a day or more ahead.

  • On foot in Puno

    Genuinely easy — the city centre is compact and walkable, altitude permitting.

    Take it slowly given Puno's elevation, even higher than Cusco.

  • Taxis for Sillustani

    Genuinely useful for reaching sites outside the city, such as Sillustani.

    Organised tours are also widely available for this route.

FAQ

Lake Titicaca & Puno: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need at Lake Titicaca?

Two to three days genuinely covers the Uros islands, Taquile and Sillustani comfortably.

Are the Uros floating islands still genuinely inhabited?

Yes, genuinely — around 1,200 Uros people still live on and maintain the islands, a real, ongoing way of life, not a recreation.

Is Puno's altitude higher than Cusco's?

Yes, genuinely — Puno sits at around 3,800 metres, noticeably higher than Cusco's 3,400 metres, so prior acclimatisation genuinely helps.

What is Lake Titicaca known for?

Genuinely being the world's highest navigable lake, home to the Uros floating islands and traditional weaving communities on Taquile.

Can you visit Lake Titicaca as a day trip?

The Uros islands genuinely work as a half-day trip from Puno, though an overnight island homestay offers a far richer experience.

How do you get from Cusco to Lake Titicaca?

Genuinely by overnight bus (around six to seven hours) or a short flight to nearby Juliaca, both common and comfortable options.
