How to plan a Peru trip: whether to focus on the Andes, the coast or the Amazon, when to go for the driest trails, what it costs, and a practical ten-day itinerary.

How Peru divides

Peru divides into three very different geographic zones — the coast, the Andean highlands and the Amazon — and a first trip realistically covers only one or two well.

The Andean highlands. Cusco, Machu Picchu, the Sacred Valley, Rainbow Mountain and Lake Titicaca — the historic and scenic heart of most Peru itineraries.

The coast. Lima, the Nazca Lines and Huacachina — the capital's food scene and a strip of desert coastline with its own distinct attractions.

The south. Arequipa and Colca Canyon — an underrated colonial city and one of the world's deepest canyons.

The Amazon. Puerto Maldonado — a different Peru entirely, dense rainforest and extraordinary biodiversity.

Worth knowing: Most first-time visitors combine Lima with the Andean highlands in a single trip. The Amazon and the far south coast are where a longer or second trip belongs.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If this is your first trip

    Lima, Cusco, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu, the classic route for a reason.

  • If you want dramatic high-altitude scenery

    Rainbow Mountain, one of the most photographed natural sights in the Andes.

  • If you want living Andean culture

    Lake Titicaca, home to the Uros floating islands and Taquile's weaving traditions.

  • If you want an underrated colonial city

    Arequipa, one of Peru's most beautiful cities, paired with condor-watching at Colca Canyon.

  • If you want extraordinary wildlife

    The Amazon, one of the most biodiverse places on Earth.

  • If you want something different from the Andes

    The Nazca Lines and Huacachina, a striking desert detour on Peru's southern coast.

Getting around: flights, trains, and one common mistake

Peru's scale and mountainous terrain mean no single mode of transport covers the whole country — a mix of domestic flights, trains and buses is standard.

Fly between regions, don't drive. Lima to Cusco or Arequipa takes over 24 hours by road versus about an hour and a half by air.

Book the Machu Picchu train well ahead. It's the only practical way to reach Machu Picchu without a multi-day hike.

Don't skip altitude acclimatisation. Arriving in Cusco at 3,400 metres and rushing straight into activity is the most common mistake first-time visitors make.

Use overnight buses for longer coastal routes. Nazca to Ica and similar routes are comfortable on Peru's reclining-seat overnight services.

Book Rainbow Mountain and Colca Canyon tours locally. Both involve early starts and high-altitude driving best left to experienced operators.

Worth knowing: Book your Machu Picchu tickets, train and Inca Trail permits (if hiking) months ahead for June–August travel — Peru's dry season sells out early.

When to go

Peru's three distinct regions have different ideal seasons, so 'the best time' depends on where your itinerary focuses. For the classic Andean route, May to September works best.

The Andean dry season brings reliable weather for Machu Picchu, Cusco and Rainbow Mountain, also the busiest and most expensive season.

The wet season, November to March, is lush and green, though trails can turn muddy and the Inca Trail itself closes each February.

The Amazon's own dry season, roughly June to October, offers the easiest wildlife spotting, with lower water levels concentrating animals near rivers.

The coast, including Lima and the desert south, is mild year-round, with its own separate garúa fog season affecting Lima specifically from June to September.

Worth knowing: Book Machu Picchu tickets and Inca Trail permits (if hiking) many months ahead for a June–August trip — this is the busiest and most tightly booked window of the year.

Four routes that work

  1. Six days: the highlights sprint

    Lima, Cusco and Machu Picchu, the most popular short Peru itinerary, though tight on acclimatisation time.

    Tip Fly into Cusco a day earlier than needed if possible, purely for extra altitude adjustment time.

  2. Ten days: the classic Andean loop

    The itinerary on the Peru country page: Lima, Cusco, the Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu, Rainbow Mountain and Lake Titicaca. A complete first look at Peru's classic highlights.

    Tip Do Rainbow Mountain only after at least three days at altitude — it's a serious high-altitude hike.

  3. A week focused on the south

    Arequipa and Colca Canyon paired with Nazca and Huacachina, skipping Cusco entirely.

    Tip This route avoids the highest altitudes, a good option for those concerned about altitude sickness.

  4. An Amazon add-on for a second trip

    Puerto Maldonado by guided lodge stay, one of the most biodiverse regions on Earth.

    Tip Book a multi-night lodge stay rather than a single day trip — wildlife sightings improve significantly with more time.

Ica Region

Massive ancient geoglyphs etched into Peru's southern desert, so large they're only fully legible from the air, and still not fully explained after a century of study.

Don't miss Watching the Hummingbird geoglyph resolve into perfect focus from a small plane window, a shape etched into the desert floor by people who never saw it from above.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Peru

How many days do you need in Peru?

Ten to fourteen days covers Lima, Cusco, the Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu and Lake Titicaca at a reasonable pace, which is the itinerary in this guide. Six days is enough for the highlights alone, and still worthwhile.

Is Machu Picchu worth the cost and effort?

Yes — it's one of the only major ancient wonders the Spanish conquest never found, and no photograph quite captures standing in the citadel itself.

Do you need to worry about altitude in Peru?

Yes, in Cusco and Puno especially — both sit above 3,300 metres, and rushing straight into activity without acclimatising is the most common visitor mistake.

When should I visit for the best weather?

May to September for the Andean dry season, offering the most reliable conditions for Machu Picchu, Cusco and Rainbow Mountain, though it's also the busiest and priciest time to visit.
