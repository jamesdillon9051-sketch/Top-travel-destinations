Welcome to Cusco

Cusco is genuinely the former capital of the Inca Empire, a high-altitude city at 3,400 metres where Spanish colonial churches and mansions still sit directly on the massive, precisely cut stone foundations the Inca left behind.

It's also genuinely Peru's essential gateway, the near-universal starting point for Machu Picchu, the Sacred Valley and the wider Andean highlands, making it a stop on almost every Peru itinerary.

Most visitors genuinely spend a few days here at the start of their trip, partly for the historic centre's dense concentration of Inca and colonial sites, and partly out of real necessity — acclimatising to the altitude before heading further into the mountains.

Why visit

Why visit Cusco?

Cusco's case rests on genuinely unmatched Inca stonework layered beneath a beautifully preserved colonial city.

  • Sacsayhuamán's stonework is genuinely extraordinary

    Massive stone blocks fitted so precisely a knife blade can't slip between them, without mortar.

  • It's genuinely the historic capital of the Inca Empire

    The former centre of the largest empire in pre-Columbian America.

  • Colonial architecture sits genuinely directly on Inca foundations

    A visible, tangible layering of history found in few other cities on Earth.

  • It's genuinely the essential gateway to Machu Picchu

    Nearly every route to the citadel and the Sacred Valley starts here.

  • The altitude genuinely demands, and rewards, a slower pace

    A few unhurried days here set up the rest of an Andean trip properly.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Cusco

Cusco's essential sights combine genuinely extraordinary Inca stonework with beautifully preserved Spanish colonial architecture.

  1. 01

    The Plaza de Armas

    Genuinely the heart of the city, a grand colonial square built directly over the Inca's own central plaza, Huacaypata.

    Tip The genuinely square is at its most atmospheric in the evening, when the surrounding buildings light up.

  2. 02

    Cusco Cathedral

    Genuinely a massive 16th-century cathedral built using stones taken from the nearby Sacsayhuamán fortress.

    Tip Genuinely look for the cathedral's famous painting of the Last Supper, which features a guinea pig on the table.

  3. 03

    Sacsayhuamán

    Genuinely one of the Inca Empire's greatest engineering achievements, zigzagging walls of stone blocks weighing over 100 tonnes each.

    Tip Genuinely walk or take a short taxi up — the site sits at a noticeably higher altitude than the city centre.

  4. 04

    Qorikancha

    Genuinely once the Inca Empire's most important temple, dedicated to the sun god, its precise stonework still visible beneath a colonial convent built on top.

    Tip Genuinely look closely at the contrast between the Inca base and the Spanish colonial construction above it.

  5. 05

    San Blas

    Genuinely Cusco's most charming neighbourhood, steep cobbled streets lined with artisan workshops and a beautifully carved colonial church.

    Tip Genuinely wear good shoes — the neighbourhood's streets are steep, uneven, and add up at altitude.

  6. 06

    San Pedro Market

    Genuinely Cusco's main local market, a lively, authentic mix of produce, juice stalls and local street food.

    Tip Genuinely try a fresh fruit juice from one of the market's many juice stands.

  7. 07

    The Twelve-Angled Stone

    Genuinely a single, famous stone block cut with twelve distinct angles, fitted perfectly into a surviving Inca wall on Hatunrumiyoc street.

    Tip Genuinely easy to miss — look for the small crowd usually gathered around it.

Things to do

Best things to do in Cusco

Cusco genuinely rewards mixing its major Inca and colonial sites with a slower wander through its historic streets.

  1. Wander San Blas's artisan streets

    A genuinely relaxed way to spend an afternoon, browsing workshops and galleries along steep, atmospheric cobbled streets.

    Tip Genuinely take it slowly at this altitude — the neighbourhood's hills are more tiring than they look.

  2. Explore Qorikancha's Inca stonework

    A genuinely worthwhile hour, examining stonework so precise it has survived major earthquakes that destroyed colonial buildings around it.

    Tip Genuinely hire a guide here — the layered history is hard to fully appreciate without context.

  3. Shop at San Pedro Market

    A genuinely authentic local experience, browsing stalls of produce, cheese, bread and prepared food alongside locals doing their own shopping.

    Tip Genuinely go with small cash and an appetite — the food stalls are excellent and inexpensive.

  4. Photograph the Twelve-Angled Stone

    A genuinely quick but essential stop, marvelling at the precision of pre-Columbian stonework up close.

    Tip Genuinely combine with a walk down the wider Hatunrumiyoc street to see more surviving Inca walls.

  5. Walk Sacsayhuamán's massive walls

    A genuinely awe-inspiring walk, tracing zigzagging walls built from stones far too massive to imagine moving by hand.

    Tip Genuinely go slowly — the site sits higher than the city centre, and the altitude is noticeable.

  6. See the Cathedral's colonial art

    A genuinely rewarding hour, taking in the cathedral's blend of Spanish and Andean religious art, including its famous Last Supper painting.

    Tip Genuinely photography is restricted inside — take time to look rather than rushing for photos.

Local food

What to eat in Cusco

Cusco's food genuinely blends Andean staples with Peru's wider culinary reputation, at every price point.

  • Lomo saltado

    A genuinely reliable classic found on menus throughout the city, at both simple local spots and finer restaurants.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout the historic centre.

  • Pisco sour

    Genuinely worth trying at altitude, a frothy pisco cocktail that pairs well with Cusco's evening chill.

    Where to try it Bars and restaurants around the Plaza de Armas.

  • Cuy (guinea pig)

    Genuinely a traditional Andean delicacy, roasted whole and considered a special-occasion dish in the region.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout Cusco, especially outside the main tourist strip.

  • Anticuchos

    A genuinely beloved street food, smoky grilled skewers sold from carts around the city in the evening.

    Where to try it Street stalls throughout the historic centre after dark.

  • Coca tea

    Genuinely a common remedy for altitude symptoms, a mild herbal tea served at nearly every hotel and café.

    Where to try it Hotels and cafés throughout Cusco.

  • Ceviche

    Genuinely available even this far from the coast, though best enjoyed once back at sea level in Lima.

    Where to try it Peruvian restaurants throughout the city centre.

Transport

Getting to and around Cusco

Cusco is genuinely reached almost exclusively by domestic flight, given the impractical overland distance from Lima.

Getting there

  • Flight from Lima

    Genuinely the standard way in, a short flight of about an hour and a half.

    Frequent daily flights run between Lima and Cusco.

  • Flight from Arequipa or Puno

    Genuinely a fast link for combining Cusco with the south of Peru.

    A practical alternative to the long overland bus route.

  • Overnight bus from Puno

    Genuinely a workable, if long, overland option for travellers combining Cusco with Lake Titicaca.

    Takes around six to seven hours, with comfortable reclining-seat services available.

Getting around

Cusco's historic centre is genuinely compact enough to explore mostly on foot, altitude permitting.

  • On foot

    Genuinely covers the Plaza de Armas, San Blas and most central sights easily.

    Take it slowly — the altitude makes even gentle inclines feel more strenuous than usual.

  • Taxis

    Genuinely inexpensive and useful for reaching Sacsayhuamán or accommodation outside the centre.

    Widely available throughout the city.

  • Organised tours

    Genuinely the standard way to reach the wider Sacred Valley and Rainbow Mountain from Cusco.

    Both are best done as guided day trips rather than independently.

When to go

Best time to visit Cusco

Cusco's genuinely high altitude means it stays cool year-round, with the dry season offering the most reliable conditions.

  • Dry season (May–September)

    Best time

    Genuinely the most reliable weather, with clear skies and cold nights, also the busiest season.

  • Shoulder months (April, October)

    Good

    Genuinely fewer crowds with still-decent weather odds.

  • Wet season (November–March)

    Good

    Genuinely lush surroundings, though afternoon rain is common and can affect day trips.

Worth planning around: Arriving in Cusco and immediately attempting strenuous activity — genuinely allow at least a day to acclimatise to the altitude first.

Travel tips

Cusco travel tips

Practical notes that make a Cusco stay noticeably smoother.

  • Spend genuinely at least one full day resting before any strenuous activity, given the altitude.
  • Drink genuinely plenty of water and coca tea to help manage altitude symptoms.
  • Buy genuinely the Boleto Turístico if planning to visit multiple sites, including Sacsayhuamán.
  • Wear genuinely comfortable shoes — the historic centre's cobbled streets are steep and uneven.
  • Book Sacred Valley and Rainbow Mountain tours genuinely a day or two ahead through your hotel or a local agency.
  • Pack layers regardless of season — Cusco's evenings genuinely turn cold even in the dry season.

FAQ

Cusco: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Cusco?

Three to four days genuinely allows time to acclimatise and see the main sights at a comfortable pace.

How bad is the altitude in Cusco?

Genuinely noticeable for most visitors, sitting at 3,400 metres — mild symptoms like headache or breathlessness are common in the first day or two.

Is Cusco worth visiting beyond being a Machu Picchu gateway?

Yes, genuinely — its Inca and colonial sites, especially Sacsayhuamán and Qorikancha, are significant destinations in their own right.

Is Cusco safe?

Generally yes for tourists, with genuinely sensible precautions around petty theft in busy tourist areas like the Plaza de Armas.

What is Cusco known for?

Genuinely being the former capital of the Inca Empire, with extraordinary stonework still visible beneath its colonial architecture.

Do I need the Boleto Turístico?

Genuinely useful if visiting Sacsayhuamán and several other sites, as it bundles entry at a better combined price than individual tickets.
