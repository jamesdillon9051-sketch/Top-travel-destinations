Welcome to Lima

Lima is genuinely Peru's sprawling coastal capital, a city where colonial-era grandeur in the historic centre sits alongside a food scene now regularly ranked among the best in the world.

It's also genuinely a city of real archaeological depth — Huaca Pucllana, a pre-Inca clay pyramid predating the Incas by centuries, still stands and is still being excavated in the middle of the modern Miraflores district.

Most visitors genuinely use Lima as their international gateway, spending a couple of days here before or after the Andes, though its food, museums and coastal neighbourhoods easily reward a dedicated stay of their own.

Transport

Getting to and around Lima

Lima is genuinely Peru's main international gateway, with most visitors arriving here first regardless of their final destination.

Getting there

  • International flight to Jorge Chávez Airport

    Genuinely most international routes into Peru land here.

    Well connected to North America, Europe and across South America.

  • Domestic flight onward

    Genuinely the standard way to reach Cusco, Arequipa and other regions.

    Frequent daily flights connect Lima to nearly every major Peru destination.

  • Long-distance bus

    Genuinely a budget option for reaching Ica, Nazca and the southern coast.

    Comfortable overnight services with reclining seats cover longer coastal routes well.

Getting around

Lima is genuinely a sprawling city, and most visitors rely on taxis or rideshare apps rather than walking between districts.

  • Rideshare apps and taxis

    Genuinely the easiest way around, widely available and inexpensive.

    Traffic can be heavy, so budget extra time between districts.

  • On foot within Miraflores or Barranco

    Genuinely easy — both districts are walkable and pleasant on their own.

    Moving between the two, or to the historic centre, is best done by taxi.

  • The Metropolitano bus

    Genuinely a fast, dedicated busway connecting Miraflores to the historic centre.

    A practical, budget-friendly option for avoiding Lima's traffic.

FAQ

Lima: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Lima?

Two to three days genuinely covers the historic centre, Miraflores and Barranco comfortably.

Is Lima worth visiting beyond being a gateway city?

Yes, genuinely — its food scene alone justifies dedicated time, alongside serious pre-Columbian and colonial history.

Is Lima safe?

Generally yes in Miraflores and Barranco, with genuinely sensible precautions elsewhere, especially around petty theft in crowded areas.

What is Lima known for?

Genuinely its world-class food scene, alongside colonial architecture and pre-Inca sites like Huaca Pucllana.

Why is Lima so foggy?

Genuinely a coastal phenomenon called garúa, caused by the cold Humboldt Current, persisting roughly June to September most years.

Should I visit Lima before or after the Andes?

Either works genuinely well — some visitors prefer Lima first to adjust before altitude, others end their trip here to enjoy the food scene at sea level.
