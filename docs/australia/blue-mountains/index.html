Welcome to the Blue Mountains

The Blue Mountains are a plateau of sandstone cut by deep gorges, about 100 km west of Sydney. They are not high, with a maximum elevation of around 1,000 metres, but their cliffs, canyons and forest are dramatic, and the Greater Blue Mountains Area was inscribed by UNESCO in 2000 for its eucalypt diversity. The name comes from the blue haze that hangs over the valleys, caused by fine droplets of eucalyptus oil in the air, combined with the scattering of light.

The best-known sight is the Three Sisters, three sandstone pillars at Echo Point in Katoomba, which Aboriginal Dreaming stories associate with three sisters turned to stone. Beyond them lie the deep Jamison Valley and Megalong Valley, and a network of walking tracks, waterfalls and lookouts that has been used by visitors for more than a century, since the railway arrived in the 1860s.

The mountains are easy to visit from Sydney, by train in two hours, and can be seen in a day, but they deserve a night or two, with a walk, a look at the gardens and villages such as Leura, and a slower pace. They were badly affected by bushfires in 2019–20, but the forest has regenerated and most facilities have reopened.

Why visit

Why visit the Blue Mountains?

The Blue Mountains offer wilderness and spectacular views within easy reach of Sydney.

  • Superb lookouts

    Echo Point and other viewpoints look over huge valleys and cliff faces.

  • Walking tracks

    Clifftop paths, waterfall trails and steps down into the valleys, for all fitness levels.

  • Scenic World

    The world's steepest passenger railway, a cable car and a rainforest boardwalk.

  • Gardens and villages

    Leura and Blackheath have cafés, galleries and beautiful gardens.

  • Easy from Sydney

    A direct train from Central Station takes about two hours.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Blue Mountains

Katoomba and Leura are the hub, with Wentworth Falls and Blackheath close by.

  1. 01

    Echo Point and the Three Sisters

    The classic lookout over the Jamison Valley and the three rock pillars, with an easy path to the Giant Stairway, which descends into the valley.

    Tip Go early or late, when the light is best and crowds are fewer.

  2. 02

    Scenic World

    A family-run attraction with the Scenic Railway, an old coal-mine railway that descends a 52-degree incline, a cable car over the valley and a boardwalk through the rainforest.

    Tip Buy a combined ticket.

  3. 03

    Wentworth Falls

    A waterfall that drops in stages more than 180 metres, with walking tracks including the National Pass, a cliff-side path carved into the rock.

    Tip Check track closures and weather.

  4. 04

    Leura

    A pretty village with a main street of cafés and shops, gardens and the Cliff Drive.

    Tip Stay for lunch.

  5. 05

    Grand Canyon Walk, Blackheath

    A beautiful track through a rainforest gorge, with ferns, moss and waterfalls, about 3 hours.

    Tip Wear good shoes.

  6. 06

    Jenolan Caves

    Some of the oldest open caves in the world, with guided tours into chambers of limestone formations. About 1.5 hours from Katoomba.

    Tip Book a tour in advance.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Blue Mountains

Walking is the highlight, with plenty of options for different abilities.

  1. Walk the National Pass

    A famed track from Wentworth Falls, cut into the cliff in the 1900s.

    Tip Allow 3 hours.

  2. Ride the Scenic Railway

    The steepest railway in the world, with a dramatic descent.

    Tip Go on a weekday.

  3. Take a garden visit

    Leura Gardens, Everglades and the Mount Tomah Botanic Garden.

    Tip Best in spring and autumn.

  4. Try canyoning or abseiling

    Adventure companies run canyoning trips in the slot canyons.

    Tip Book a guided trip.

  5. Watch the sunset at Echo Point

    See the valley change colour.

    Tip Bring a jacket.

  6. Have a Devonshire tea

    A traditional scones and tea in a mountain café.

    Tip Try Leura.

Local food

What to eat in the Blue Mountains

The mountains' food is café-based, with local produce and cosy pubs.

  • Devonshire tea

    Scones with jam and cream, served with tea, a favourite mountain treat.

    Where to try it Cafés in Leura and Katoomba.

  • Local cafés and bakeries

    Good coffee and pastries.

    Where to try it Leura and Blackheath.

  • Pub meals

    Hearty pies, steaks and schnitzels in old pubs.

    Where to try it Mountain hotels.

  • Apples and cider

    Local orchards produce apples and cider.

    Where to try it Bilpin, near Mount Tomah.

  • Modern Australian dining

    Restaurants with local produce and wine.

    Where to try it Leura and Katoomba.

Transport

Getting to and around the Blue Mountains

Katoomba is about 100 km west of Sydney, easily reached by train or car.

Getting there

  • Train from Sydney Central

    About 2 hours to Katoomba, with frequent services.

    Use an Opal card or contactless.

  • Drive

    About 1 hour 30 minutes on the M4 and Great Western Highway.

    Traffic can be heavy on weekends.

  • Tour from Sydney

    Day tours are available.

    Choose a small-group option.

Getting around

The towns are compact, and buses link the sights.

  • Hop-on hop-off bus

    Links Katoomba, Leura and the main lookouts.

    Buy a day ticket.

  • Walking

    Many sights are on foot from Katoomba station.

    Wear good shoes.

  • Car

    Useful for Jenolan Caves and Blackheath.

    Watch for wildlife.

When to go

Best time to visit the Blue Mountains

The mountains are cooler than Sydney, with cold winters and mild summers.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Crisp, clear days and autumn colours.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Cold, with occasional snow and Yulefest in July.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Wildflowers, gardens and mild weather.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Good

    Warm and often humid, with a risk of bushfires.

Worth planning around: Days of extreme fire danger in summer, when tracks close.

Itinerary

Two days in the Blue Mountains

Katoomba's sights first, then a walk and a village.

  1. Day1

    Katoomba

    Views and railway.

    • Morning Echo Point and the Three Sisters.
    • Afternoon Scenic World.
    • Evening Dinner in Katoomba.
  2. Day2

    Walks and Leura

    Falls and gardens.

    • Morning Wentworth Falls and the National Pass.
    • Afternoon Leura village.
    • Evening Train back to Sydney.

Travel tips

The Blue Mountains travel tips

A few tips make the visit smoother.

  • Take an early train to avoid the crowds.
  • Wear sturdy shoes and bring water.
  • Check track closures and fire warnings.
  • Bring a jacket, since it is cooler than Sydney.
  • Stay a night for the sunrise and sunset.
  • Book Jenolan Caves tours in advance.
  • Do not leave the track.

FAQ

The Blue Mountains: frequently asked questions

Can I visit the Blue Mountains as a day trip from Sydney?

Yes. Trains take about 2 hours each way, and it is a popular day trip.

Why are they called the Blue Mountains?

The eucalyptus forest releases fine oil droplets, which scatter blue light and create a haze.

What is the best walk?

The National Pass from Wentworth Falls or the Grand Canyon at Blackheath, depending on fitness and time.

What is the best time to visit?

March to May and September to November.

Were the mountains damaged by fires?

Yes, in 2019–20, but the forest has recovered and most facilities are open.

Do I need a car?

Not for Katoomba and Leura. A car helps for Jenolan Caves and Blackheath.
