Welcome to Uluru

Uluru, a vast sandstone monolith 348 metres high and about 9 km around, rises abruptly from the flat red plains of central Australia. It is sacred to the Anangu, the traditional owners, whose ancestors have lived in the region for tens of thousands of years, and its caves, waterholes and rock art are tied to creation stories called Tjukurpa. The rock, and the nearby domes of Kata Tjuta, are protected in Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park, which was listed by UNESCO for both natural and cultural values.

Climbing Uluru was closed permanently on 26 October 2019, at the wish of the Anangu, who had long asked visitors not to climb. Instead, visitors walk the base of the rock, join guided walks with Anangu guides, watch the colours change at sunrise and sunset and visit the cultural centre. The 10.6-km base walk circles the rock, passing waterholes, caves and sacred sites.

The Red Centre is remote and dramatic. The resort of Yulara, 20 km from the rock, is the only place to stay, while Alice Springs is about 450 km away and Kings Canyon about 300 km. It rewards visitors who arrive with time, respect for the country and a willingness to see the desert in early morning and late afternoon light.

Why visit

Why visit Uluru?

Uluru is one of the most powerful natural and cultural sites in the world, and Australia's iconic image.

  • A landmark unlike any other

    The scale, colour and isolation of the rock are astonishing, particularly at dawn and dusk.

  • Anangu culture

    Guided walks and the cultural centre share stories, art and knowledge of the land.

  • Kata Tjuta

    A group of 36 large domes, with excellent walks in the Valley of the Winds and Walpa Gorge.

  • Desert skies

    Some of the clearest night skies on earth, with installations such as the Field of Light.

  • Kings Canyon

    A dramatic sandstone canyon with a rim walk, about 300 km away.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Uluru

The main sights are within the national park, with Kings Canyon an optional extra.

  1. 01

    Uluru base walk

    A flat 10.6-km walk around the rock, passing waterholes, caves and rock art, with signs describing the stories. Some parts are sacred and photography is restricted.

    Tip Start at sunrise, and bring water and a hat.

  2. 02

    Uluru sunrise and sunset

    The rock changes colour through the day, from dark purple to glowing orange and deep red. There are designated viewing areas for both.

    Tip Arrive early to secure a spot.

  3. 03

    Kata Tjuta and the Valley of the Winds

    Thirty-six rock domes, about 30 km west of Uluru, with walks through the Valley of the Winds (about 7 km) and Walpa Gorge (2.6 km). The Valley of the Winds may close in extreme heat.

    Tip Start early, as the walk is closed later on very hot days.

  4. 04

    The Cultural Centre

    A centre at the base of Uluru, where Anangu artists and guides share Tjukurpa, and where you can buy art directly.

    Tip Visit before you walk.

  5. 05

    Field of Light

    An art installation of about 50,000 slender stems of light spread across the desert at dusk, created by Bruce Munro. Check whether it is still open.

    Tip Book a sunrise or sunset session.

  6. 06

    Kings Canyon (Watarrka)

    A 270-metre-deep canyon with a 6-km rim walk and a green oasis called the Garden of Eden, about 3 to 4 hours' drive from Uluru.

    Tip Start the rim walk early, when it is cool.

Things to do

Best things to do in Uluru

Most experiences are about the light, the rock and the stories.

  1. Watch sunrise at Uluru

    See the rock's colours change, in a peaceful desert dawn.

    Tip Dress warmly, since it is cold before sunrise.

  2. Take an Anangu-guided walk

    Learn about bush foods, rock art and creation stories directly from the traditional owners.

    Tip Book in advance.

  3. Walk the Valley of the Winds

    A rewarding hike among the domes of Kata Tjuta.

    Tip Check for heat closures.

  4. Dine under the stars

    Evening dinners with astronomers, in the open desert, are popular.

    Tip Book early.

  5. Ride a camel at sunset

    Camel tours cross the dunes at Yulara.

    Tip Check the operator's welfare record.

  6. Go on a helicopter flight

    A scenic flight gives a view of the whole landscape.

    Tip Expensive but memorable.

Local food

What to eat in Uluru

Food in the Red Centre is mostly resort dining, with some bush foods on the menus.

  • Bush tucker

    Native ingredients, such as wattleseed, quandong and lemon myrtle, appear in dishes and drinks.

    Where to try it Restaurants at Ayers Rock Resort.

  • Kangaroo and emu

    Lean native meats, grilled or in stews.

    Where to try it Restaurants at Yulara.

  • Barbecue

    Steaks, sausages and seafood cooked on open grills.

    Where to try it Campground and resort barbecues.

  • Damper

    Traditional bush bread baked in the coals.

    Where to try it Outback dinners.

  • Australian wine and beer

    Local wines and craft beers at sunset events.

    Where to try it Sunset viewing areas.

Transport

Getting to and around Uluru

Uluru is in the middle of the continent, far from anywhere.

Getting there

  • Flight to Ayers Rock (AYQ)

    Direct flights from Sydney, Melbourne, Alice Springs and some other cities.

    About 3 hours from Sydney.

  • Flight to Alice Springs and drive

    A 4.5-hour drive of about 450 km from Alice Springs.

    Fill up with fuel and carry water.

  • Tour or train

    Multi-day tours run from Alice Springs, and the Ghan stops at Alice Springs.

    Book ahead.

Getting around

Distances are long, and tours are a common choice.

  • Resort shuttle

    Free shuttles run between Yulara and the rock.

    Check the timetable.

  • Hire car

    Gives freedom for sunrise and sunset.

    Book early.

  • Tour

    Sunrise and sunset tours, and day trips to Kings Canyon.

    Book in advance.

When to go

Best time to visit Uluru

The desert is extremely hot in summer and cold at night in winter. Autumn and spring are best.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm days, cool nights and settled weather.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Best time

    Mild to warm days, but freezing nights.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Warming up, with wildflowers after rain.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Think twice

    Extremely hot, often above 40°C, with flies and closures.

Worth planning around: December to February, when walks may close because of heat.

Travel tips

Uluru travel tips

The desert needs some preparation.

  • Do not climb Uluru. It is closed and the Anangu ask visitors to respect that.
  • Bring a hat, sunscreen, water and a warm layer.
  • Follow the rules about photography at sacred sites.
  • Book Uluru accommodation and tours well in advance.
  • Start walks at dawn to avoid the heat.
  • Buy a park pass in advance.
  • Carry extra water and fuel if driving.

FAQ

Uluru: frequently asked questions

Can I climb Uluru?

No. It was permanently closed to climbers in October 2019, at the request of the Anangu traditional owners.

How many days do you need at Uluru?

Two to three days for the rock, Kata Tjuta and the cultural centre.

What is the best time to visit?

April to September. Summer is very hot.

Do I need a park pass?

Yes, a pass is required to enter Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park. Check the current price and validity.

Is it worth visiting Kings Canyon?

Yes, if you have an extra day or are driving to Alice Springs.

Can I take photos of Uluru?

Yes, in most areas, but some sacred sites are off limits. Follow the signs.
