Welcome to Sydney

Sydney sits on one of the world's great natural harbours, a drowned river valley of coves, headlands and islands that runs deep into the city. The British established their first colony here in 1788, on the land of the Eora people, and it grew from a convict settlement into Australia's largest city, with more than five million people.

Two structures define the skyline. The Sydney Opera House, designed by Danish architect Jørn Utzon and opened in 1973, is one of the most recognisable buildings on earth, with sail-like shells of white tile, and it was inscribed by UNESCO in 2007. The Sydney Harbour Bridge, completed in 1932 and nicknamed the Coathanger, spans the water beside it, and adventurous visitors can climb its arch.

But Sydney is as much about the outdoors as the landmarks. Beaches such as Bondi, Coogee and Manly are a short bus or ferry ride from the centre, cliff-top walks connect them, and ferries are a normal way to commute. It is an expensive city, but a beautiful one, with a relaxed outdoor lifestyle.

Why visit

Why visit Sydney?

Sydney is one of the most beautiful cities in the world, and the easiest introduction to Australia.

  • An incredible harbour

    Ferries, sails and headlands, with the Opera House and Harbour Bridge as the backdrop.

  • City and beach together

    Surf beaches and coastal walks within half an hour of the centre.

  • History and culture

    The Rocks, the Botanic Garden and Aboriginal heritage tours tell layered stories.

  • Food and coffee

    A multicultural food scene, harbourside dining and excellent cafés.

  • A gateway

    The Blue Mountains, the wine regions and the south coast are easy trips.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Sydney

Most sights cluster around Circular Quay and the harbour, with the beaches a short trip away.

  1. 01

    The Sydney Opera House

    The great performing arts centre on Bennelong Point, best seen from the water, from the Botanic Garden and from close up. Tours take you inside, or you can see a performance.

    Tip Book a tour or a show in advance.

  2. 02

    The Sydney Harbour Bridge

    A steel arch bridge that opened in 1932. You can walk across the pedestrian footway for free, or climb the arch with a guide for a panoramic view.

    Tip The BridgeClimb takes about three hours and sells out at weekends.

  3. 03

    The Rocks

    The oldest part of Sydney, where the first settlers landed, with sandstone buildings, pubs, a Saturday and Sunday market and museums.

    Tip Visit early or in the evening.

  4. 04

    The Royal Botanic Garden

    A free, waterfront garden with lawns, palms and fig trees, ending at Mrs Macquarie's Chair with the best view of the Opera House and Bridge together.

    Tip Walk here from the Opera House.

  5. 05

    Bondi Beach and the coastal walk

    The most famous surf beach in Australia, and the start of the 6-km Bondi to Coogee cliff walk, which passes rock pools, coves and sculptures.

    Tip Swim between the flags.

  6. 06

    Manly and the ferry

    A 30-minute ferry from Circular Quay to Manly, a beach suburb with a surf beach, a harbour beach and the Corso, with fine views on the way.

    Tip Sit on the outside deck, on the right side going out.

Things to do

Best things to do in Sydney

Sydney is best enjoyed in the open air, on foot and on the water.

  1. Ride a harbour ferry

    Sydney's ferries are working public transport with the best views in the city, to Manly, Taronga Zoo, Watsons Bay and Parramatta.

    Tip Use an Opal card or contactless payment.

  2. Walk from Bondi to Coogee

    A coastal path of about 6 km past headlands, beaches and rock pools.

    Tip Start early, and bring sunscreen and water.

  3. Climb the Harbour Bridge

    A guided climb of the arch with harnesses, at dawn, day, twilight or night.

    Tip Book weeks ahead for sunset.

  4. See a show at the Opera House

    Opera, ballet, concerts and comedy in an iconic setting.

    Tip Check for last-minute tickets.

  5. Visit Taronga Zoo

    Koalas, kangaroos and a view of the harbour, reached by ferry.

    Tip Go early.

  6. Learn about Aboriginal Sydney

    Walking tours by Aboriginal guides in the Botanic Garden and The Rocks explain the history of the Eora.

    Tip Book in advance.

Local food

What to eat in Sydney

Sydney's dining is diverse, fresh and casual, with seafood, brunch, Asian food and excellent coffee.

  • Seafood

    Sydney rock oysters, prawns, barramundi and fish and chips by the harbour.

    Where to try it Waterfront restaurants and fish markets.

  • Brunch

    Smashed avocado, eggs and coffee, served in cafés across the city.

    Where to try it Surry Hills, Bondi and Paddington.

  • Meat pie

    A traditional pie with a flaky crust.

    Where to try it Bakeries and pubs.

  • Asian food

    Excellent Chinese, Thai, Vietnamese and Japanese food, in Chinatown and the suburbs.

    Where to try it Haymarket and Cabramatta.

  • Flat white and coffee

    Strong, well-made coffee is the norm.

    Where to try it Cafés everywhere.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Sydney

Sydney is expensive, and location matters. Stay near Circular Quay for sights, or in a beach suburb for the coast.

  • Circular Quay and The Rocks

    Hotels close to the Opera House and harbour.

    Best for: Sights and ferries

  • Surry Hills and the CBD

    Boutique hotels and hostels in lively streets.

    Best for: Restaurants and transport

  • Bondi and Manly

    Apartments and guesthouses near the sand.

    Best for: Beach life

Transport

Getting to and around Sydney

Sydney is Australia's main international gateway.

Getting there

  • Flight to Sydney (SYD)

    Direct flights from Asia, North America, Europe via hubs and New Zealand, and domestic flights from every state.

    The airport is connected to the city by train in about 15 minutes.

  • Flight or train from Melbourne

    About 1 hour 30 minutes by air, or a long overnight train or a day's drive of about 9 hours.

    Fly for speed.

  • Road

    Sydney to Melbourne is about 880 km, and to Brisbane about 920 km.

    Allow two days.

Getting around

Public transport is good, and the harbour ferries are part of it.

  • Trains, light rail and buses

    An integrated system, paid with an Opal card or contactless card.

    Tap on and off.

  • Ferries

    Routes to Manly, Taronga Zoo and Watsons Bay.

    The best way to see the harbour.

  • Walking

    The centre and the coastal paths are walkable.

    Wear a hat.

When to go

Best time to visit Sydney

Sydney has a mild, temperate climate, with warm summers and cool winters.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm and sunny, with fewer crowds.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Good

    Hot and humid, the peak beach season. Busy and expensive at Christmas and New Year.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm, dry days and settled weather.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Cool and mostly sunny, with the Vivid light festival in May and June.

Worth planning around: New Year's Eve, unless you want the fireworks and the crowds.

Travel tips

Sydney travel tips

A few tips make Sydney easier and cheaper.

  • Use an Opal card or contactless payment on public transport.
  • Take the ferry for the best views. It is public transport, and cheap.
  • Swim only between the red and yellow flags.
  • Wear sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses, even on cloudy days.
  • Book the Bridge Climb and Opera House shows in advance.
  • Stay near the ferry wharves and stations.
  • Avoid New Year's Eve unless you plan far ahead.

FAQ

Sydney: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Sydney?

Three days for the highlights, four or five with the Blue Mountains.

Is Sydney expensive?

Yes, particularly accommodation and eating out. Free attractions such as the beaches, the garden and the ferries help.

Is it safe to swim at Bondi?

Yes, at the patrolled area between the flags. Rips are common, so follow the lifeguards.

Can I walk across the Harbour Bridge for free?

Yes, the pedestrian footway on the east side is free.

What is the best time to visit?

September to November and March to May.

Is it worth visiting the Blue Mountains?

Yes, as a day trip by train, about two hours each way.
