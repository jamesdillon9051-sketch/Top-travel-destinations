Welcome to the Whitsundays

The Whitsunday Islands lie off the central Queensland coast, between the mainland and the Great Barrier Reef, in a sheltered region of calm water. There are 74 islands, most of them uninhabited national park, with fringing coral reefs, bays and beaches. Captain James Cook sailed through the passage in 1770, and named it on Whitsunday, although the actual date was different, due to the international date line.

The star attraction is Whitehaven Beach, seven kilometres of dazzlingly white sand made of almost pure silica, which stays cool underfoot, and Hill Inlet, at its northern end, where tides swirl white sand into the turquoise water in shifting patterns. Nearby, snorkelling sites, secluded coves and the Great Barrier Reef itself are all within a day's sail.

Most visitors base themselves at Airlie Beach, a lively backpacker and resort town on the mainland, and go out on day trips or multi-day sailing charters. Others stay on the islands, at Hamilton Island, the most developed, or on smaller resort islands. It is a beautiful and relaxing region, best from April to November.

Why visit

Why visit the Whitsundays?

The Whitsundays offer the classic tropical island experience, with good access and a range of budgets.

  • Whitehaven Beach

    One of the most beautiful beaches in the world, with white silica sand and clear water.

  • Sailing

    Sheltered waters and steady winds make it one of the best places to sail in the world.

  • Snorkelling and diving

    Coral reefs on the islands' edges and the outer Great Barrier Reef.

  • Wildlife

    Turtles, rays, dolphins and, from July to September, humpback whales.

  • A gentle pace

    Beaches, boats, sunsets and seafood.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Whitsundays

Most of the highlights are best seen from the water, on a day trip or charter.

  1. 01

    Whitehaven Beach

    A 7-km beach of fine white sand on Whitsunday Island, the region's most famous sight. Sand is so fine it squeaks underfoot, and it stays cool in the sun.

    Tip Visit in the morning, before day-trip boats arrive.

  2. 02

    Hill Inlet lookout

    A short walk from the beach leads to a lookout above Hill Inlet, where tides swirl the sand and water into a natural marbled pattern.

    Tip Best at low tide, around midday.

  3. 03

    Airlie Beach

    The gateway town, with a lagoon, marina, restaurants, bars and tour operators, on the mainland.

    Tip Book tours a few days ahead.

  4. 04

    Heart Reef

    A heart-shaped coral formation on Hardy Reef, best seen from the air by scenic flight. Landing on or swimming at the reef is not permitted.

    Tip Book a scenic flight for the view.

  5. 05

    Hamilton Island

    The largest developed island, with resorts, restaurants, a marina, golf carts as transport and a lookout at Passage Peak.

    Tip Good for a resort-style stay.

  6. 06

    The outer reef

    Hardy Reef and Reefworld pontoons offer snorkelling and diving in the outer Great Barrier Reef, about 2 hours from Airlie Beach.

    Tip Choose a reputable eco-certified operator.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Whitsundays

Sailing, snorkelling and beaches make up the main activities.

  1. Take a sailing trip

    Two-day, three-day or longer charters visit the best beaches and snorkelling spots, with stops at Whitehaven.

    Tip Choose a boat size and vibe to suit you.

  2. Snorkel from a beach

    Coral gardens fringe several islands, easily reached from the sand.

    Tip Bring a stinger suit in season.

  3. Take a scenic flight

    Seaplane and helicopter flights show the islands, Heart Reef and the outer reef.

    Tip Book early on clear days.

  4. Kayak or paddleboard

    Calm bays are ideal for paddling.

    Tip Go early.

  5. Look for whales

    Humpback whales pass through from July to September.

    Tip Join a whale-watching cruise.

  6. Have a drink at sunset

    Bars and restaurants at Airlie Beach and on the islands.

    Tip Try the Airlie Beach lagoon.

Local food

What to eat in the Whitsundays

The Whitsundays' food is seafood, tropical fruit and casual dining.

  • Barramundi and seafood

    Fresh fish, prawns and Moreton Bay bugs, grilled or fried.

    Where to try it Airlie Beach restaurants.

  • Fish and chips

    Takeaway on the beach.

    Where to try it Airlie Beach and Hamilton.

  • Tropical fruit

    Mangoes, pineapples, bananas and passionfruit.

    Where to try it Markets.

  • Australian barbecue

    Steaks and prawns on the barbie.

    Where to try it Boats and resorts.

  • Craft beer and rum

    Local brews and Queensland rum.

    Where to try it Airlie Beach bars.

Transport

Getting to and around the Whitsundays

The Whitsundays are about 1,100 km north of Brisbane.

Getting there

  • Flight to Whitsunday Coast or Hamilton Island

    Direct flights from Brisbane, Sydney and Melbourne.

    Whitsunday Coast is 30 minutes from Airlie Beach.

  • Flight to Proserpine and transfer

    A short shuttle ride from the airport to Airlie Beach.

    Check the transfer.

  • Drive or bus

    About 12 hours by road from Brisbane, or from Cairns.

    Long, so fly if possible.

Getting around

Boats and ferries connect the mainland and islands.

  • Ferry

    Ferries link Airlie Beach with Hamilton and other islands.

    Check timetables.

  • Charter or day trip

    The main way to see the islands.

    Book ahead.

  • Golf cart

    The transport on Hamilton Island.

    Rent at the resort.

When to go

Best time to visit the Whitsundays

The region has a tropical climate, with a dry season from about April to November and a wet season with cyclone risk from December to March.

  • Autumn (April–May)

    Best time

    Clear water, warm weather and few stingers.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Best time

    Dry, sunny and mild, with whales.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm, dry and calm.

  • Summer (December–March)

    Think twice

    Hot, wet and humid, with stingers and cyclones.

Worth planning around: January to March, the peak of the cyclone season.

Travel tips

The Whitsundays travel tips

A few tips make the trip better.

  • Wear a stinger suit from November to May, and swim only in netted areas.
  • Book sailing trips in advance, and choose a boat that suits your style.
  • Go to Whitehaven early, before the crowds.
  • Bring sunscreen, a hat and reef-safe protection.
  • Check the weather and cyclone warnings in the wet season.
  • Do not swim at Heart Reef or take anything from the reef.
  • Take seasickness tablets if you are prone.

FAQ

The Whitsundays: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in the Whitsundays?

Three to four days for the main sights, including a day at Whitehaven.

What is the best time to visit?

April to November. Summer has stingers and cyclones.

Can I swim at Whitehaven Beach?

Yes, but stingers can be present in the wet season, so check advice.

Is it better to stay on the islands or in Airlie Beach?

Airlie Beach is cheaper and more flexible. Islands offer a resort experience.

Can I visit Heart Reef?

You can see it on a scenic flight. Landing and swimming there is not permitted.

Is it a good trip for families?

Yes, with calm waters and easy day trips.
