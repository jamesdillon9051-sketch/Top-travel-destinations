Why go to Sweden

Sweden is a long country in northern Europe, from the Baltic coast and the southern plains to the Arctic mountains of Lapland. It is known for design, clean cities, strong social values and a deep connection to nature, shaped by the right of public access that lets everyone walk, swim and camp in the countryside.

The capital, Stockholm, is spread across islands and has a medieval old town, great museums and an archipelago on its doorstep. Gothenburg and Malmo offer seafood, parks and a relaxed southern atmosphere, while historic towns like Uppsala, Visby and Kalmar show the country's medieval and Viking past.

Further north, Dalarna is the heart of Swedish folk culture, Are is the leading ski resort, and Lapland offers the northern lights, the Midnight Sun and Sami culture. Trains are comfortable and punctual, and English is widely spoken, so Sweden is an easy country to visit.

Food and drink

Sweden foods to try

Swedish food is simple, seasonal and built on fish, potatoes, berries and baking, with a growing New Nordic scene.

  • Meatballs

    Small meatballs with gravy, lingonberries and potatoes.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants.

  • Kanelbulle

    A soft cinnamon bun, the heart of fika.

    Where to try it Cafes and bakeries.

  • Gravlax

    Salmon cured with dill and sugar.

    Where to try it Restaurants.

  • Surstromming

    Fermented Baltic herring, famously pungent.

    Where to try it Eaten in late summer.

  • Prinsesstarta

    A green marzipan cake with cream.

    Where to try it Bakeries.

  • Cloudberries

    Tart orange berries from the north.

    Where to try it Desserts and jam.

Before you go

Practical travel information for Sweden

The essentials, current for 2026. Sweden is safe and straightforward, but check official sources before travelling.

Capital
Stockholm
Language
Swedish is the official language. English is widely spoken.
Currency
Swedish krona (SEK). Roughly 9-11 kronor to the US dollar in recent years.
Cards and cash
Sweden is nearly cashless. Cards and phone payments work everywhere.
Entry and visas
As a Schengen member, Sweden allows visa-free stays of up to 90 days for many nationalities, but check requirements and ETIAS rules.
Power sockets
Type C and F, 230 V, 50 Hz.
Emergency number
112 for all emergencies.
Tipping
Tipping is not required, but rounding up is common in restaurants.
Safety
Sweden is very safe. Take normal precautions in cities and prepare well for cold weather and hiking in the north.
Internet and SIM
Excellent. 5G is common and public Wi-Fi is widespread.
Time zone
Central European Time (UTC+1), with daylight saving in summer.

Getting around

Trains are the best way to travel between cities. Flights suit the north and Gotland, and ferries serve the islands.

  • Ferry

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Sweden

Swedes are polite, reserved and egalitarian, with a love of fairness, punctuality and quiet. The concept of lagom, meaning just enough, shapes life.

  • Be on time

    Punctuality matters.

  • Respect queues

    Take a ticket.

  • Take shoes off

    At homes.

  • Keep it quiet

    Public spaces are calm.

  • Respect personal space

    Not too chatty.

  • Follow allemansratten

    Respect nature.

Do

  • Take your shoes off indoors
  • Join in fika
  • Pay by card
  • Respect quiet hours
  • Follow the right of public access rules
  • Be on time

Don't

  • Do not litter
  • Do not pick protected plants
  • Do not disturb wildlife
  • Do not camp near homes
  • Do not drink and drive
  • Do not expect tips

Travel tips

Sweden travel tips

The things that most often catch visitors out in Sweden.

  • Book long-distance trains early for cheaper fares.
  • Carry a card, not cash.
  • Pack layers year-round.
  • Check daylight hours for winter.
  • Book summer accommodation early.
  • Alcohol is sold in Systembolaget shops only.
  • Cycle helmets are required for children.
  • Midsummer shuts shops for a few days.
