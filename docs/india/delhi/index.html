Welcome to Delhi

Delhi has been the capital of one empire or another for most of the last thousand years, and it has been built and rebuilt many times, by the Delhi sultans, the Mughals and the British. Its ruins, tombs and mosques are scattered across the modern city, and a walk through Delhi is a walk through layers of history.

The city falls roughly into two halves. Old Delhi, or Shahjahanabad, is the walled city founded by Shah Jahan in the 17th century, with the Red Fort, the Jama Masjid and the maze of Chandni Chowk. New Delhi, laid out by Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker and inaugurated as the capital in 1931, has wide avenues, government buildings and India Gate. Beyond these, the south of the city has the Qutb Minar, Humayun's Tomb and a number of other medieval monuments.

Delhi is also a huge, hot, polluted and hectic megacity of more than 30 million people in the metropolitan area. Air quality is a serious problem from about October to January. But its food, its monuments and its energy make it a rewarding start to a trip to India.

Why visit

Why visit Delhi?

Delhi is the arrival point for most visitors, and it deserves more than a night.

  • Monuments of many eras

    Three UNESCO World Heritage Sites, the Red Fort, Humayun's Tomb and the Qutb complex, are all within the city.

  • Old Delhi

    Narrow lanes, spice markets, mosques and street food stalls, in a part of the city that has barely changed in a century.

  • Superb food

    From kebabs and butter chicken to chaat, parathas and sweets, Delhi is one of India's best places to eat.

  • Green spaces and museums

    Lodhi Gardens, the National Museum and many smaller collections give a calmer side.

  • Transport hub

    Trains and flights connect Delhi to almost everywhere, and the metro is clean and efficient.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Delhi

Delhi's sights are spread out, so group them by area: Old Delhi, New Delhi and the south.

  1. 01

    The Red Fort

    The great red sandstone fortress-palace of Shah Jahan, built between 1639 and 1648, from whose ramparts India's prime minister addresses the nation each Independence Day. Inside are audience halls, pavilions and a museum.

    Tip Visit in the morning, and check the days it is closed.

  2. 02

    Jama Masjid

    One of the largest mosques in India, completed in 1656, with a vast courtyard, three domes and two minarets from which you get a view of Old Delhi.

    Tip Cover up, remove shoes and avoid prayer times.

  3. 03

    Chandni Chowk

    The main street of Old Delhi, a chaos of rickshaws, sweet shops, spice markets, jewellers and alleys. Behind the main road are the specialist bazaars and street food lanes.

    Tip Go on foot or by cycle rickshaw, and come hungry.

  4. 04

    Humayun's Tomb

    A garden tomb built in the 1560s for the second Mughal emperor, and an important forerunner of the Taj Mahal. Its symmetrical gardens and red sandstone are beautifully restored.

    Tip Go late in the afternoon for the warm light.

  5. 05

    Qutb Minar complex

    A 72-metre victory tower begun in the late 12th century, the tallest brick minaret in the world, in a complex of early Indo-Islamic ruins, including an iron pillar that has stood for about 1,600 years.

    Tip Go early to avoid crowds.

  6. 06

    India Gate and Kartavya Path

    A war memorial for Indian soldiers of the First World War, at the end of a ceremonial avenue that runs to the presidential palace. Locals picnic and eat ice cream here in the evening.

    Tip Go in the evening when it is lit up.

  7. 07

    Lodhi Gardens

    A park with 15th- and 16th-century tombs of the Sayyid and Lodhi dynasties, popular with joggers and picnickers.

    Tip A calm break from the crowds.

Things to do

Best things to do in Delhi

Delhi is best sampled across a few themes: history, food and quiet places.

  1. Take a food walk in Old Delhi

    Follow a guide through the lanes for parathas, kebabs, chaat, jalebi and lassi in the shops that have been serving them for generations.

    Tip Go with a reputable guide, and eat where locals queue.

  2. Ride the Delhi Metro

    A clean, fast and cheap network that reaches most sights and is far quicker than road traffic.

    Tip Use the women's coaches if you prefer, and avoid rush hour.

  3. Visit Gurudwara Bangla Sahib

    A large Sikh temple with a golden dome and a sacred pool, where a community kitchen serves free meals to all.

    Tip Cover your head and remove shoes.

  4. Explore Mehrauli and Hauz Khas

    Ruined tombs and mosques in the south of the city, and a medieval reservoir with cafés and boutiques.

    Tip Good for a slow afternoon.

  5. See the National Museum

    A large collection of Indian art and archaeology, from Harappan finds to miniature paintings.

    Tip Check opening hours.

  6. Shop at Dilli Haat and Khan Market

    Handicrafts from across India at Dilli Haat, and bookshops, cafés and boutiques at Khan Market.

    Tip Bargain lightly at Dilli Haat.

Local food

What to eat in Delhi

Delhi's food is Punjabi and Mughlai at heart, with street food from across northern India.

  • Butter chicken and kebabs

    Tandoori-cooked chicken in rich tomato and butter sauce, and skewered kebabs, at restaurants in Old Delhi such as Karim's.

    Where to try it Old Delhi near the Jama Masjid.

  • Paranthe

    Stuffed, fried flatbreads with spiced potato, paneer or other fillings, served with curd and pickles, at the famous Paranthe Wali Gali.

    Where to try it Chandni Chowk.

  • Chaat

    Tangy, crunchy snacks such as aloo tikki, dahi bhalla and golgappa.

    Where to try it Street stalls in Old Delhi and Khan Market.

  • Chole bhature

    Spiced chickpeas with puffy fried bread, a hearty Punjabi breakfast or lunch.

    Where to try it Local restaurants across the city.

  • Jalebi and lassi

    Hot, syrupy fried sweets and thick yoghurt drinks.

    Where to try it Old Delhi sweet shops.

  • Daulat ki chaat

    A winter delicacy of frothy sweet milk foam, sold from carts in Old Delhi.

    Where to try it Old Delhi in winter.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Delhi

Delhi is huge, and location matters. Central New Delhi and the areas near the metro are the best bases.

  • Connaught Place and central New Delhi

    Hotels near the metro, with restaurants and shops.

    Best for: Transport and convenience

  • South Delhi (Hauz Khas, Defence Colony)

    Boutique hotels and guesthouses in leafy neighbourhoods.

    Best for: Cafés and a residential feel

  • Old Delhi

    Heritage havelis and small hotels near the Red Fort and Jama Masjid.

    Best for: Atmosphere

  • Aerocity

    International-standard hotels near the airport.

    Best for: Early or late flights

Transport

Getting to and around Delhi

Delhi is India's main international gateway and rail hub.

Getting there

  • Flight to Delhi (DEL)

    Direct flights from Europe, North America, the Middle East and Asia.

    The Airport Express metro line links the airport to the centre in about 20 minutes.

  • Train

    Fast trains from Agra (about 2 hours), Jaipur (about 4 to 5 hours) and Varanasi, and overnight trains from Amritsar and Mumbai.

    Book through IRCTC.

  • Road

    Delhi is about 200 km from Agra and 280 km from Jaipur.

    Expressways make driving easier, but traffic in the city is heavy.

Getting around

Traffic is heavy, and the metro is the quickest way to move.

  • Delhi Metro

    An extensive network of air-conditioned trains that reach the Red Fort, Connaught Place, Qutb Minar and the airport.

    Buy a smart card or use the app.

  • App cabs and auto-rickshaws

    Uber, Ola and Rapido operate, and auto-rickshaws serve short hops.

    Use apps for fixed prices.

  • Cycle rickshaw

    A fun way to see the lanes of Old Delhi.

    Agree the price in advance.

When to go

Best time to visit Delhi

Delhi has an extreme climate, with very hot summers, a monsoon from July to September and cold, foggy, polluted winters.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Best time

    Pleasant days, cold nights. Air pollution can be severe, particularly from November to January.

  • Spring (March–April)

    Best time

    Warm and clear, before the heat builds.

  • Summer (May–June)

    Think twice

    Very hot, often above 40°C.

  • Monsoon (July–September)

    Quiet

    Humid with heavy showers, and clearer air.

Worth planning around: May and June for the heat, and the worst smog weeks from late October to early January if you are sensitive to air quality.

Itinerary

Three days in Delhi

Old Delhi on the first day, New Delhi and the tombs on the second, and the south on the third.

  1. Day1

    Old Delhi

    Fort, mosque and lanes.

    • Morning The Red Fort.
    • Afternoon Jama Masjid and Chandni Chowk.
    • Evening A street food walk.
  2. Day2

    Tombs and avenues

    Mughal and colonial Delhi.

    • Morning Humayun's Tomb.
    • Afternoon Lodhi Gardens and the National Museum.
    • Evening India Gate, and dinner in Khan Market.
  3. Day3

    The south

    Minarets and ruins.

    • Morning The Qutb Minar complex.
    • Afternoon Mehrauli and Hauz Khas.
    • Evening Gurudwara Bangla Sahib, and a last dinner.

Travel tips

Delhi travel tips

Delhi is intense, and a few habits help.

  • Use the metro for speed. Road traffic is heavy.
  • Check the air quality index in winter, and carry a mask if you are sensitive.
  • Use official prepaid taxis or apps from the airport to avoid scams.
  • Avoid touts who say that a monument or station is closed.
  • Carry small notes for rickshaws and tips.
  • Dress modestly, particularly for religious sites.
  • Start early and rest at midday, since the heat and crowds build fast.

FAQ

Delhi: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Delhi?

Two or three days. One day gives you Old Delhi, and another the tombs and New Delhi.

Is Delhi safe?

It is generally safe for visitors, though it has the usual big-city hazards. Women should take particular care at night and use registered taxis.

Is the air pollution really that bad?

In winter, yes. The worst months are November to January. Check daily readings and plan indoor activities on bad days.

What is the best time to visit Delhi?

October to March, though winter smog is a concern. Spring is pleasant.

Should I stay in Old Delhi or New Delhi?

New Delhi or South Delhi is more comfortable, and the metro connects everywhere. Old Delhi is atmospheric but chaotic.

How do I get from the airport to the city?

The Airport Express metro is the quickest and cheapest option. Prepaid taxis and app cabs are alternatives.
