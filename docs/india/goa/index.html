Welcome to Goa

Goa was a Portuguese colony for about 450 years, from 1510 until India annexed it in 1961, which is much longer than the British ruled most of the subcontinent. The legacy is everywhere: in the Catholic churches, the Portuguese-style villas with balconies and tiled roofs, the food, the surnames and the relaxed attitude to alcohol and holidays. About a quarter of the population is Christian.

The coast is 100 km of sand, divided in practice into two. North Goa, around Baga, Calangute, Anjuna, Vagator and Arambol, is livelier, with markets, beach clubs and nightlife. South Goa, around Palolem, Agonda and Colva, is quieter, greener and more relaxed. Inland, rice fields, spice plantations, waterfalls and forest cover the Western Ghats.

Goa is best used as a place to rest, after the intensity of the north or as a beach stop on a longer trip. Its heritage is also worth seeing: the churches of Old Goa are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the old quarter of Panjim, Fontainhas, is a maze of colourful Portuguese houses.

Why visit

Why visit Goa?

Goa offers beaches, heritage and food on a compact coast, and is India's easiest place to unwind.

  • Beaches for all tastes

    From party beaches with music to quiet coves and long open sands.

  • Portuguese heritage

    Old Goa's churches, Fontainhas and colonial mansions.

  • Goan food

    Fish curry rice, vindaloo, sorpotel and bebinca, with Portuguese, Konkani and Indian influences.

  • A relaxed pace

    Beach shacks, yoga, cafés and markets, with less hassle than other parts of India.

  • Nature inland

    Spice plantations, waterfalls and wildlife sanctuaries in the Western Ghats.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Goa

Split your time between the beach and the heritage sites, and choose your side of the coast.

  1. 01

    The churches of Old Goa

    The former Portuguese capital, now a UNESCO site, with the Basilica of Bom Jesus, which holds the remains of St. Francis Xavier, and the huge Sé Cathedral. Most of the city itself has vanished, but the churches remain.

    Tip Go in the morning, and dress modestly.

  2. 02

    Fontainhas, Panjim

    The Latin Quarter of Goa's capital, with narrow lanes of pastel houses, small chapels and cafés, and a preserved Portuguese atmosphere.

    Tip Walk in the late afternoon and stop for a coffee.

  3. 03

    Fort Aguada

    A well-preserved 17th-century Portuguese fort on a headland north of Panjim, with a lighthouse and views along the coast.

    Tip Go at sunset.

  4. 04

    Palolem and South Goa beaches

    A crescent of white sand fringed by palms and fishing boats, with quiet neighbours such as Agonda and Patnem.

    Tip Stay a few nights in South Goa.

  5. 05

    Baga, Calangute and Anjuna

    The lively northern beaches, with shacks, water sports, clubs and the famous Wednesday flea market at Anjuna.

    Tip Go for the atmosphere, but be aware of crowds in the high season.

  6. 06

    A spice plantation

    Working farms inland where you can see pepper, cardamom, vanilla and cashew, and eat a traditional lunch.

    Tip Book a half-day tour.

Things to do

Best things to do in Goa

Goa is a beach destination first, with plenty of options for the days in between.

  1. Eat at a beach shack

    Simple seafood restaurants on the sand, serving grilled fish, prawns and cold beer.

    Tip Choose by the day's catch.

  2. Take a spice plantation tour

    A guided walk through a working farm, followed by a Goan lunch.

    Tip Go in the morning.

  3. Walk through Fontainhas

    Explore the old Portuguese quarter, with its colour and small chapels.

    Tip Try the local bakeries.

  4. Try water sports or a dolphin cruise

    Boat trips from Sinquerim and Palolem spot dolphins in the morning.

    Tip Go with an established operator.

  5. Do a yoga class

    Yoga is popular in Goa, from drop-in classes to retreats.

    Tip Check the teacher's qualifications.

  6. Visit a Saturday night market

    Night markets sell clothes, crafts and food, with live music.

    Tip Check the schedule.

Local food

What to eat in Goa

Goan food mixes Portuguese and Konkani traditions, with coconut, vinegar, chilli and seafood.

  • Goan fish curry rice

    The staple lunch: fish in a tangy, coconut-based curry with rice.

    Where to try it Local restaurants and beach shacks.

  • Pork vindaloo and sorpotel

    Spicy, vinegary pork dishes with Portuguese origins.

    Where to try it Goan restaurants, particularly around Christmas.

  • Prawn balchão and xacuti

    Prawns in a spicy pickle-like sauce, and chicken in a coconut and spice gravy.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Panjim.

  • Bebinca

    A many-layered sweet of egg yolks, coconut milk and sugar.

    Where to try it Bakeries and shops.

  • Poi and pao

    Soft local breads baked in wood ovens, eaten with curries or sausages.

    Where to try it Village bakeries.

  • Feni

    A local spirit distilled from cashew apples or coconut sap.

    Where to try it Bars and shops.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Goa

Choose the coast that matches your mood, and book early for December and January.

  • North Goa (Baga, Anjuna, Vagator)

    Guesthouses, hotels and boutique stays near the beach.

    Best for: Nightlife and activity

  • South Goa (Palolem, Agonda)

    Beach huts, guesthouses and small resorts.

    Best for: Quiet beaches

  • Panjim and Old Goa

    Boutique hotels in restored Portuguese houses.

    Best for: Heritage

Transport

Getting to and around Goa

Goa has two airports and good rail links.

Getting there

  • Flight

    Dabolim (GOI) in the south and Manohar International (GOX) at Mopa in the north, which opened in 2023, connect to Indian cities and some international routes.

    Choose the airport nearest your beach.

  • Train

    The Konkan Railway links Goa with Mumbai (about 10 to 12 hours) and Kerala.

    Scenic and comfortable.

  • Road

    About 9 to 12 hours from Mumbai or Pune, and about 6 to 8 hours from Hampi.

    Roads can be slow.

Getting around

Goa is small but spread out, and public transport is limited.

  • Scooter or motorbike rental

    The most popular way to get around, at a low price.

    Wear a helmet, carry a licence and take care with traffic.

  • Taxi

    Prepaid taxis and app-based services operate.

    Agree the fare in advance.

  • Bus

    Local buses cover the main routes.

    Cheap but slow.

When to go

Best time to visit Goa

Goa has a tropical climate, with a strong monsoon from June to September.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Best time

    Dry, sunny and warm. Peak season, with Christmas and New Year very busy.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Think twice

    Hot and humid, and many places start closing.

  • Monsoon (June–September)

    Quiet

    Heavy rain, rough seas, and most beach shacks closed. The landscape is lush and prices are low.

  • Post-monsoon (October)

    Good

    Green, warm and quiet, as the season begins.

Worth planning around: The monsoon, when the sea is dangerous for swimming, and Christmas and New Year unless you love crowds.

Travel tips

Goa travel tips

Goa is relaxed, and a few tips help.

  • Choose your beach according to your mood: North for activity and South for quiet.
  • Wear a helmet if you rent a scooter, and carry a valid licence.
  • Swim only where lifeguards are present, and avoid the sea in the monsoon.
  • Dress modestly when visiting churches.
  • Book in advance for Christmas and New Year.
  • Watch out for touts and overcharging at beach shacks.
  • Carry cash for small purchases.

FAQ

Goa: frequently asked questions

North or South Goa?

North Goa for nightlife, markets and activity, and South Goa for quieter beaches and a relaxed atmosphere.

How many days do you need in Goa?

Four or five days for the coast and heritage sites.

What is the best time to visit Goa?

November to February. The monsoon closes many beach shacks.

Is Goa safe?

It is a relaxed destination, but take care with scooters, sea currents and petty theft, and keep an eye on your drinks.

Can I drink alcohol in Goa?

Yes, it is widely available and cheaper than in most of India.

Which airport should I use?

Mopa (GOX) is closer to North Goa, and Dabolim (GOI) is closer to Panjim and South Goa.
