India is a subcontinent, and no single trip covers it. This guide covers how the regions divide, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to use trains and flights, when to go, what it costs and three routes that work for a first visit.

How India divides

India is roughly 3,200 km from north to south and east to west, and its regions differ in language, food, religion and climate almost as much as European countries do. Five zones organise most trips.

Delhi and the Golden Triangle. Delhi is the capital and the usual arrival point, with Agra and Jaipur each a few hours away, forming the classic Golden Triangle of Mughal and Rajput monuments.

Rajasthan. Beyond Jaipur, Udaipur is the lake city of the south of the state, with palaces on the water, and the deserts of Jodhpur, Jaisalmer and Bikaner lie further west, though they are not among the ten places here.

The Ganges plain and the Punjab. Varanasi is the holy city of the Ganges, and Amritsar in the north-west is the home of the Golden Temple.

The west coast. Mumbai is the country's largest city and financial capital, and Goa to the south has beaches, Portuguese-era churches and a relaxed pace.

The south. Kerala has backwaters, spice hills and Fort Kochi, and Hampi in Karnataka has the ruins of the Vijayanagara Empire among boulder-strewn hills.

Worth knowing: The practical unit is one or two of these regions per trip, linked by flight or train. Trying to see north and south in a week ends in airports.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If you have one week

    The Golden Triangle: Delhi, Agra and Jaipur, with three or four nights split between them. It is the most efficient first introduction to India.

  • If you want spiritual India

    Varanasi for the Ganges, Amritsar for the Golden Temple and its community kitchen, and Hampi for temples still in use among ruined ones.

  • If you love palaces and forts

    Jaipur and Udaipur for Rajasthan, Agra for the Mughal forts and the Taj, and Delhi for the Red Fort and Humayun's Tomb.

  • If you want a beach or a rest

    Goa for beaches and nightlife, or Kerala for slower days on the water and Ayurveda.

  • If you care about food

    Mumbai for street food and Irani cafés, Amritsar for Punjabi cooking, Kerala for coconut-rich seafood and Old Delhi for kebabs and chaat.

  • If you want something less trodden

    Hampi is quieter than the north, and Amritsar sees fewer foreign visitors than the Golden Triangle.

Moving around a very large country

India's size and traffic mean journeys take longer than the map suggests. Delhi to Mumbai is about 1,400 km, and Delhi to Kochi is about 2,700 km.

Trains. The best way to cover 200–800 km. Book online through IRCTC, choose air-conditioned classes and, for popular routes, book as soon as tickets open. The Vande Bharat and Shatabdi trains are fast and comfortable on main lines.

Flights. Cheap and frequent on domestic routes, and essential for the north–south journey. IndiGo, Air India, Akasa and others compete on price.

Private driver. Excellent for the Golden Triangle and Rajasthan, where the sights are spread out and a driver waits while you explore. Agree the itinerary and the price before you set off.

City transport. Delhi and Mumbai have metros and suburban trains. Auto-rickshaws and app cabs cover the rest, and agreeing the fare or using the meter is important.

Worth knowing: Delays are normal in winter fog, particularly on trains and flights around Delhi. Do not schedule a tight connection on the same day as an international flight.

When to go

India has three seasons that matter: a cool dry winter, a scorching hot spring and a monsoon summer. Where you go changes the answer.

November to February is the best time for almost everywhere, with dry, sunny days. Northern nights are cold, Delhi is often smoggy, and it is the peak season for prices and crowds.

March and April are warm, with Holi in March. Rajasthan and the plains start to get hot, and this is when the hill stations open.

May and June are extremely hot in the plains, often above 40°C, and best avoided apart from the Himalayas.

July to September is monsoon, with heavy rain across most of the country. Kerala's green season is popular for Ayurveda and cheap rates, and Goa closes down. Ladakh is accessible in these months.

Worth knowing: Diwali falls in October or November, and Holi in March, both on lunar dates. Trains and hotels fill up during the week around them.

Money, costs and health

India is one of the best-value countries to travel in. A thali costs a couple of dollars, a good restaurant dinner US$10–25, a comfortable hotel US$50–120 and a private car with driver about US$50–90 per day.

Foreign visitors pay much higher entry fees than Indians at major monuments. The Taj Mahal, Agra Fort and Amber Fort are the priciest, and many can be booked online.

Cash is still important for rickshaws, street food and tips, so withdraw rupees from bank ATMs. Cards work in hotels and larger restaurants.

Health is the main concern. Drink bottled water, choose freshly cooked food, and consult a travel doctor about vaccinations well before you leave. Carry rehydration salts, and travel insurance is essential.

Three routes that work

  1. One week: the Golden Triangle

    Three nights in Delhi, one in Agra, with a stop at Fatehpur Sikri on the way, and two in Jaipur. Use fast trains or a private driver.

    Tip Fly into Delhi and out of Jaipur or Delhi, and book the Taj visit for a day that is not Friday.

  2. Ten days: the north with palaces and the Ganges

    The Golden Triangle plus Udaipur and Varanasi, joined by two internal flights.

    Tip Fly Jaipur or Udaipur to Delhi, then Delhi to Varanasi.

  3. Two weeks: north and south

    The itinerary on the country page: Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, Udaipur, Varanasi and Kerala's backwaters.

    Tip Swap Kerala for Goa if you want a beach, or add Hampi on a longer trip.

FAQ

Planning a trip to India

Can I see the Taj Mahal as a day trip from Delhi?

Yes. Fast trains take about two hours each way, and many visitors do it in a day. Staying a night lets you see the Taj at sunrise and sunset, and is worth it if you have the time.

Is it better to travel by train or fly?

Use trains for shorter hops of up to about six hours, and flights for the long distances between north and south. An overnight sleeper train saves a hotel night, and is an experience in itself.

Should I go north or south first?

Most first-timers start in the north for the monuments, which suits October to March. The south is calmer and easier for those who want a gentler introduction, and it is good in the monsoon months for Kerala.

When should I book?

Trains open for booking about two months ahead and popular ones sell out quickly. Hotels for Diwali, Holi and the winter peak should be booked a month or two ahead. Flights are cheapest a month or more in advance.
