Welcome to Rotterdam

Rotterdam's city centre was almost entirely destroyed in the German bombing of May 1940, and rather than reconstruct a historic replica, the city rebuilt itself as a genuine testing ground for bold modern architecture — a decision that still defines its skyline today, in sharp contrast to Amsterdam's preserved 17th-century canal ring less than an hour away.

That rebuilding produced some of the most photographed buildings in the Netherlands: Piet Blom's tilted Cube Houses, the vast tunnel-vaulted Markthal with its painted ceiling mural, and the cable-stayed Erasmusbrug, nicknamed 'the Swan' for its asymmetric white pylon.

Rotterdam is also genuinely Europe's largest port, and that maritime, working-city character gives it a rougher, more industrial energy than the Netherlands' other major cities — a place that rewards visitors specifically looking for something other than another canal-ring postcard.

Why visit

Why visit Rotterdam?

Rotterdam's case is a genuinely bold, still-evolving modern skyline that no other Dutch city offers, alongside a real working-port energy.

  • The architecture is genuinely unlike anywhere else in the Netherlands

    Rebuilt almost from scratch after 1940, Rotterdam's skyline is a real showcase for experimental design rather than a preserved historic centre.

  • It's genuinely Europe's largest port

    A working maritime city with a rougher, more industrial energy than Amsterdam's tourist-polished centre.

  • The Markthal is genuinely one of Europe's most striking market halls

    A vast tunnel-vaulted structure housing food stalls beneath a painted ceiling mural, with apartments built into the arch itself.

  • It's a genuinely easy contrast to Amsterdam

    Under an hour by train, making a Rotterdam day or two a natural addition to an Amsterdam-based trip.

  • It's genuinely less crowded than the Netherlands' other major cities

    Rotterdam's architecture and museums rarely feel overrun compared to Amsterdam's tourist centre.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Rotterdam

Rotterdam's essential sights are almost entirely 20th- and 21st-century, a genuinely different kind of Dutch city break.

  1. 01

    The Cube Houses (Kubuswoningen)

    Piet Blom's genuinely disorienting 1980s housing complex, dozens of cube-shaped homes tilted at 45 degrees atop concrete pylons, still functioning as ordinary apartments today.

    Tip The Kijk-Kubus show-house is open to visit inside — worth the small entry fee to see how the tilted interior actually works.

  2. 02

    The Markthal

    A genuinely vast tunnel-vaulted market hall, its interior ceiling covered by an enormous painted mural of flowers and produce, with food stalls below and apartments built directly into the arch.

    Tip Look up before you shop — the ceiling mural, 'Horn of Plenty', is genuinely the size of a football pitch.

  3. 03

    The Erasmusbrug

    A genuinely striking cable-stayed bridge across the Nieuwe Maas, its single asymmetric white pylon earning it the nickname 'de Zwaan' (the Swan), connecting the city centre to the redeveloped Kop van Zuid.

    Tip Walk or cycle across for the view back toward the skyline — genuinely one of the best vantage points in the city.

  4. 04

    The Euromast

    A 185-metre observation tower built for a 1960s garden festival, its Space Tower capsule genuinely still rotating up an additional external section for sweeping views over the port and city.

    Tip Go on a clear day — the top-level views genuinely extend well beyond the city on good visibility.

  5. 05

    Rotterdam Centraal station

    A genuinely striking 2014 rebuild of the city's main station, its angular stainless-steel facade functioning as a real architectural statement rather than just a transit hub.

    Tip Even if not catching a train, the station's soaring main hall is worth a quick look on the way past.

  6. 06

    Kunsthal Rotterdam

    A genuinely influential Rem Koolhaas-designed exhibition building with no permanent collection, instead hosting a constantly changing programme of art, design and photography shows.

    Tip Check the current exhibition schedule before visiting — the building itself is worth seeing regardless of what's showing.

  7. 07

    Hotel New York

    The former headquarters of the Holland America Line, whose ships once carried Dutch emigrants to New York, now a genuinely atmospheric hotel and brasserie on the Kop van Zuid waterfront.

    Tip A coffee or meal on the terrace, looking back across the water at the skyline, is a genuinely good way to rest between sights.

Things to do

Best things to do in Rotterdam

Rotterdam's working port and rebuilt skyline reward genuinely active exploration, on the water and on foot.

  1. Take a Spido harbour boat tour

    Boat tours run out from the city centre into Europe's largest port, genuinely giving a sense of scale that the skyline alone doesn't convey.

    Tip Longer tours reach the outer container terminals; shorter ones stick closer to the city centre for a more manageable couple of hours.

  2. Ascend the Euromast's Space Tower

    A genuinely unusual add-on to the standard Euromast visit: a rotating capsule that climbs an additional external section of the tower for even higher views.

    Tip Book the Space Tower ascent as a combined ticket with general Euromast entry — it's cheaper than paying separately.

  3. Wander Delfshaven's historic streets

    One of the only parts of Rotterdam to survive the 1940 bombing largely intact, Delfshaven's narrow canal-side streets genuinely feel like a different, much older city.

    Tip The Pilgrim Fathers Church here marks where the Pilgrims boarded a ship for England before eventually sailing to America.

  4. Explore Witte de Withstraat

    Rotterdam's genuinely liveliest street for bars, galleries and restaurants, a good gauge of the city's creative, design-forward energy after dark.

    Tip Come for dinner and stay for a drink — the street's restaurants and bars flow naturally into each other through the evening.

  5. Day-trip to Kinderdijk

    The UNESCO-listed windmill network at Kinderdijk is genuinely a short bus or ferry ride from central Rotterdam, an easy half-day addition to a city stay.

    Tip The waterbus ferry from central Rotterdam is a genuinely scenic way to reach Kinderdijk without a car.

  6. Walk the Kop van Zuid architecture route

    The redeveloped former dockland south of the Erasmusbrug is genuinely one of the best places to see Rotterdam's contemporary architecture concentrated in one walkable stretch.

    Tip Cross the Erasmusbrug on foot in one direction and take the water taxi back for two different perspectives on the same skyline.

Local food

What to eat in Rotterdam

Rotterdam's food culture draws on its working-port character, with genuinely good, unpretentious Dutch classics alongside a diverse international scene.

  • Stroopwafel

    Two thin waffles sandwiching a genuinely gooey caramel syrup filling, best bought fresh and warm rather than a packaged supermarket version.

    Where to try it Market stalls at the Markthal and around the city centre.

  • Bitterballen

    Deep-fried, breadcrumbed balls of a genuinely rich beef ragout, the classic accompaniment to a beer at any Dutch bar.

    Where to try it Bars along Witte de Withstraat and throughout the city centre.

  • Kibbeling

    Bite-sized pieces of battered, deep-fried white fish, genuinely a working-port-city classic, served with a garlicky sauce and often eaten on the move.

    Where to try it Fish stalls at the Markthal and along the harbour.

  • Haring (raw herring)

    Lightly cured raw herring, traditionally eaten by the tail with chopped onion — a genuine Dutch institution found at stalls across the city.

    Where to try it Herring stalls around the Markthal and the city centre.

  • Poffertjes

    Small, fluffy mini pancakes, genuinely a beloved Dutch treat, traditionally dusted with powdered sugar and a knob of butter.

    Where to try it Dedicated poffertjes stalls at markets and street-food events across the city.

  • Zeeuwse mosselen (Zeeland mussels)

    Fresh mussels from the nearby Zeeland coast, genuinely a Dutch seafood staple, steamed simply in white wine and served with fries on the side.

    Where to try it Seafood restaurants throughout the city, especially good in the autumn mussel season.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Rotterdam

Rotterdam is genuinely compact enough that most central neighbourhoods work well, with the centre and Kop van Zuid the two strongest bases.

  • City centre

    Modern hotels close to the main sights, genuinely convenient though the centre itself is less atmospheric than Amsterdam's canal ring.

    Best for: Walking distance to the Markthal and Cube Houses

  • Kop van Zuid

    The redeveloped former docklands across the Erasmusbrug, home to Hotel New York and genuinely excellent views back at the city centre.

    Best for: Skyline views and a quieter, more residential feel

  • Witte de Withstraat / Cool district

    Rotterdam's most genuinely lively district after dark, with a good concentration of bars, galleries and restaurants.

    Best for: Nightlife and restaurants within walking distance

Transport

Getting to and around Rotterdam

Rotterdam sits directly on the main rail line between Amsterdam and Belgium, making it a genuinely easy stop rather than a detour.

Getting there

  • Train from Amsterdam

    Fast intercity trains connect Amsterdam to Rotterdam Centraal in well under an hour.

    Trains run frequently throughout the day — no need to book far ahead for standard fares.

  • Train from The Hague or Utrecht

    Both The Hague and Utrecht connect to Rotterdam in around 25–40 minutes, making a multi-city Randstad route genuinely easy.

    An OV-chipkaart or contactless bank card covers all NS train travel.

  • Rotterdam The Hague Airport

    A smaller regional airport with a limited number of European routes, otherwise Amsterdam Schiphol is the main gateway.

    A shuttle bus connects the airport to Rotterdam Centraal in about 25 minutes.

Getting around

Rotterdam's centre is genuinely walkable, with a metro and tram network covering the wider city efficiently.

  • Walking

    The Markthal, Cube Houses and Erasmusbrug are all within a compact, walkable central area.

    Rotterdam's wide, modern streets make walking genuinely easy compared to some of the Netherlands' older, narrower city centres.

  • Metro and tram (RET)

    A comprehensive network reaches the Euromast, Kop van Zuid and outer neighbourhoods efficiently.

    An OV-chipkaart or contactless card covers all RET transit — tap in and out on every journey.

  • Water taxi

    A genuinely fun and quick way to cross the Nieuwe Maas between the city centre and Kop van Zuid.

    Water taxis run on demand from marked stops along the waterfront.

  • Cycling

    Rotterdam is flatter and more spread out than Amsterdam, making a bike genuinely useful for covering more ground.

    Rental is widely available near Centraal Station and throughout the city centre.

When to go

Best time to visit Rotterdam

Rotterdam is genuinely workable year-round, since its main appeal is architecture and museums rather than outdoor scenery.

  • Spring (April–May)

    Best time

    Mild temperatures and thinner crowds than Amsterdam sees in the same season.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    The warmest weather, with outdoor terraces along Witte de Withstraat and the harbour in full use.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Mild and quiet, and genuinely a good season for fresh Zeeland mussels.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Cold and often grey, though the Markthal and Kunsthal both work well as indoor options regardless of weather.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Rotterdam's indoor-heavy attractions and lack of Amsterdam-scale crowds make it genuinely workable in any season.

Travel tips

Rotterdam travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Rotterdam trip.

  • Book the Kijk-Kubus show-house and Euromast tickets online ahead in summer to skip the queue.
  • Combine Rotterdam with Kinderdijk via the scenic waterbus ferry rather than a bus, weather permitting.
  • Check the Kunsthal's current exhibition before visiting — the programme changes completely several times a year.
  • Cross the Erasmusbrug on foot at least once — the view back at the skyline is genuinely one of the best in the city.
  • Carry a contactless card — many smaller vendors no longer accept cash at all.
  • Combine Rotterdam easily with Amsterdam, The Hague, Delft or Utrecht — all are under an hour away by train.

FAQ

Rotterdam: frequently asked questions

Is Rotterdam worth visiting alongside Amsterdam?

Yes, genuinely — it offers a completely different, modern-architecture-driven character in under an hour by train, making it one of the easiest and most rewarding additions to an Amsterdam-based trip.

How many days do you need in Rotterdam?

Two days covers the Cube Houses, Markthal, Erasmusbrug and Euromast comfortably, with time for a harbour boat tour. A day trip from Amsterdam is workable but rushed.

Why does Rotterdam look so different from other Dutch cities?

Its historic centre was almost entirely destroyed in the German bombing of May 1940. Rather than rebuild a historic replica, the city chose bold modern architecture, which still defines its skyline today.

What are the Cube Houses?

A 1980s housing complex designed by architect Piet Blom: dozens of cube-shaped homes tilted at 45 degrees atop concrete pylons, still used as ordinary apartments, with one show-house open to visit inside.

Can Rotterdam be visited as a day trip from Amsterdam?

Yes, with trains under an hour each way, though an overnight stay is genuinely worthwhile to see the skyline lit up after dark and fit in a harbour boat tour without rushing.

Is Kinderdijk easy to reach from Rotterdam?

Yes, genuinely easy — a scenic waterbus ferry or a short bus ride connects central Rotterdam to the UNESCO-listed windmills at Kinderdijk in well under an hour.
