Welcome to Delft

Delft is small enough to see thoroughly in a day, yet it produced Johannes Vermeer, one of the most celebrated painters in history, and gave its name to Delftware — the blue-and-white pottery tradition that has been made here continuously since the 17th century.

The city's canals and gabled townhouses are little changed from Vermeer's own lifetime, and the view across the water toward the Nieuwe Kerk's spire still closely resembles the scenes in his paintings, a connection the city takes real, understated pride in rather than over-marketing.

Delft's other defining institution is Royal Delft, the last of the original 17th-century pottery factories still producing hand-painted Delftware today, offering a genuine look at a craft tradition that predates the city's more famous painter by several decades.

Why visit

Why visit Delft?

Delft's case is a compact, well-preserved canal city carrying real artistic and craft history without Amsterdam-scale crowds.

  • Vermeer's Delft is still recognisable

    The canals, gabled houses and the view toward the Nieuwe Kerk closely resemble the city he painted over 350 years ago.

  • Delftware is still made here

    Royal Delft, founded in 1653, remains the last of the city's original pottery factories still hand-painting the famous blue-and-white ware today.

  • The Markt is one of the Netherlands' finest squares

    A grand, harmonious space flanked by the town hall and the Nieuwe Kerk, with William of Orange's tomb inside.

  • It's an easy, worthwhile day trip

    A direct tram from The Hague or a short train from Rotterdam makes Delft simple to add to a Randstad itinerary.

  • It's less crowded than Amsterdam or even Utrecht

    Delft's compact centre rarely feels overrun, even with its genuine historical and artistic significance.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Delft

Delft's essential sights fit comfortably within its compact, canal-lined old town.

  1. 01

    The Markt and the Nieuwe Kerk

    A grand, harmonious market square flanked by the 17th-century town hall and the Nieuwe Kerk, whose tower holds the royal crypt of the House of Orange-Nassau, including William of Orange's tomb.

    Tip Climb the Nieuwe Kerk's tower for sweeping views over the old town's rooftops and canals.

  2. 02

    The Oude Kerk (Old Church)

    Delft's oldest church, its tower leaning about two metres off vertical, and the burial place of Johannes Vermeer.

    Tip The lean is visible to the naked eye from the small square in front of the church.

  3. 03

    Royal Delft (Koninklijke Delft)

    The last of Delft's original 17th-century pottery factories still operating, still hand-painting the famous blue-and-white ware using techniques largely unchanged for centuries.

    Tip The factory tour includes watching painters at work — worth timing your visit for when the studio is actively staffed.

  4. 04

    The Vermeer Centrum Delft

    Housed in the former Guild of St Luke, where Vermeer himself was once a member, the centre explains his life and work through high-quality reproductions rather than originals.

    Tip None of Vermeer's actual paintings hang in Delft — this is the place to understand his life and technique, not see originals.

  5. 05

    Museum Prinsenhof Delft

    A former convent turned royal residence, the site where William of Orange was assassinated in 1584 — bullet holes are still visible in the stairwell wall.

    Tip The museum's exhibits on the Dutch Revolt give useful context for the House of Orange history found throughout the city.

  6. 06

    The Oostpoort (East Gate)

    The only surviving of Delft's original medieval city gates, its twin brick towers a picturesque, quieter corner of the old town away from the main square.

    Tip A short walk from the centre, worth the detour for photos with far fewer visitors than the Markt.

  7. 07

    Delft's old town canals

    Delft's canals are little changed since Vermeer's time, lined with gabled brick townhouses and crossed by small stone bridges throughout the compact old town.

    Tip Wander without a fixed route — Delft is small enough that you can't get lost for long.

Things to do

Best things to do in Delft

Delft rewards an unhurried pace, its pottery tradition and market square the two experiences worth building a visit around.

  1. Browse the Thursday market on the Markt

    A traditional general market fills the square weekly, selling flowers, cheese, fish and household goods against the backdrop of the town hall and church.

    Tip The market runs Thursday mornings — check current days before planning a visit around it.

  2. Paint your own Delftware

    Several workshops in Delft let visitors try their hand at the traditional blue-and-white painting technique, a hands-on complement to the Royal Delft factory tour.

    Tip Book a painting workshop ahead — the popular sessions fill up, especially on weekends.

  3. Find Vermeer's 'View of Delft' viewpoint

    The spot along the Hooikade where Vermeer painted his famous cityscape is still identifiable today, the skyline recognisably similar over three and a half centuries later.

    Tip A small plaque marks the approximate spot — a quietly rewarding stop for anyone who has seen the painting.

  4. Take a canal boat tour

    Short boat tours give a different, slower perspective on the old town's canals and gabled houses than walking alone provides.

    Tip Tours depart regularly from near the Markt in season — no need to book far ahead outside peak summer weekends.

  5. Wander the old town's quiet side streets

    Beyond the Markt, Delft's smaller canal-side lanes are quiet even in high season, rewarding an unhurried wander without a fixed itinerary.

    Tip The streets around the Oostpoort and south of the Oude Kerk are among the least visited in the centre.

  6. Day-trip to The Hague

    The Hague's Mauritshuis and Binnenhof are a short, direct tram ride away, an easy extension to a Delft day.

    Tip Tram 1 runs directly between Delft and The Hague's centre — no train transfer required.

Local food

What to eat in Delft

Delft's food culture runs on the same genuine Dutch classics found throughout the Randstad, enjoyed at an unhurried, small-city pace.

  • Stroopwafel

    Two thin waffles sandwiching a gooey caramel syrup filling, best bought fresh and warm from a market stall.

    Where to try it Stalls at the Thursday market on the Markt square.

  • Bitterballen

    Deep-fried, breadcrumbed balls of a rich beef ragout, the classic accompaniment to a beer on a Markt-side terrace.

    Where to try it Cafés and bars around the Markt square.

  • Gouda cheese

    The Netherlands' most famous export, ranging from mild young wheels to deeply savoury aged versions, widely sold at the weekly market.

    Where to try it Cheese stalls at the Thursday market and shops around the centre.

  • Haring (raw herring)

    Lightly cured raw herring, traditionally eaten by the tail with chopped onion — a genuine Dutch institution found at stalls around the centre.

    Where to try it Fish stalls at the Thursday market.

  • Poffertjes

    Small, fluffy mini pancakes, a beloved Dutch treat, traditionally dusted with powdered sugar and a knob of butter.

    Where to try it Cafés around the Markt square and old town.

  • Patat (Dutch fries)

    Twice-fried potato chips served in a paper cone, defined by the sauce — mayonnaise, not ketchup, is the default order.

    Where to try it Independent friteries around the Markt and old town.

Where to stay in Delft

Delft is small enough that almost anywhere central works well as a base.

Transport

Getting to and around Delft

Delft sits directly on the main rail line between Rotterdam and The Hague, easy to reach from anywhere in the Randstad.

Getting there

  • Train from Rotterdam or The Hague

    Direct trains connect Rotterdam and The Hague to Delft in well under 15 minutes each.

    An OV-chipkaart or contactless bank card covers all NS train travel.

  • Train from Amsterdam

    Direct intercity trains connect Amsterdam to Delft in about an hour.

    An easy day trip, though an overnight stay lets you see the Markt without any day-trip crowds.

  • Tram from The Hague

    Tram 1 runs directly between The Hague's centre and Delft, a scenic alternative to the train.

    Slower than the train but a pleasant, easy option if already using an OV-chipkaart for local transit.

Getting around

Delft's entire old town is tiny and walkable in minutes.

  • Walking

    The Markt, both churches and the main canals are all reachable on foot within a short visit.

    Delft's compact size means a car is never necessary within the old town itself.

  • Cycling

    Flat terrain and good cycle paths make a bike useful for reaching the Royal Delft factory or the TU Delft campus, both slightly outside the centre.

    Rental is available near the train station.

When to go

Best time to visit Delft

Delft is pleasant most of the year, with its small size keeping crowds manageable even in summer.

  • Spring (April–May)

    Best time

    Mild, increasingly warm weather, ideal for walking the canals and Markt square.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Good

    Warm and reliable, with the Thursday market and canal boat tours both at their liveliest.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Comfortable temperatures and thinning crowds, good light for photographing the Markt.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Cool and quiet, with Royal Delft and the museums working well regardless of weather.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Delft's small size means crowds are rarely overwhelming even in peak season.

Travel tips

Delft travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Delft trip.

  • Book a Delftware painting workshop ahead if you want to try it — popular sessions fill up on weekends.
  • Visit on a Thursday if you can, to catch the traditional general market on the Markt square.
  • Remember Delft's own museums hold no original Vermeer paintings — the Vermeer Centrum explains his life through high-quality reproductions instead.
  • Combine Delft easily with The Hague via Tram 1, a scenic and direct alternative to the train.
  • Carry a contactless card — many smaller vendors no longer accept cash at all.
  • Climb the Nieuwe Kerk tower for the best overview of the old town's canals and rooftops.

FAQ

Delft: frequently asked questions

Is Delft worth a day trip?

Yes — it offers a completely walkable old town, real Vermeer and Delftware pottery history, and one of the Netherlands' most beautiful market squares, all reachable in under 15 minutes from Rotterdam or The Hague.

How much time do you need in Delft?

A full day covers the Markt, both churches, the Royal Delft factory and the Vermeer Centrum comfortably, given the town's compact size.

Can you see original Vermeer paintings in Delft?

No — none of Vermeer's original works remain in his home city. The Vermeer Centrum explains his life and technique through high-quality reproductions rather than originals, which hang in museums elsewhere including the Mauritshuis in The Hague.

What is Delftware?

The distinctive blue-and-white pottery tradition that has been made in Delft since the 17th century. Royal Delft, founded in 1653, is the last of the city's original factories still hand-painting it today, and offers factory tours.

Is Delft easy to combine with The Hague or Rotterdam?

Yes, very — both are under 15 minutes away by train, and Tram 1 runs directly between Delft and The Hague, making a combined day or two easy without a car.

Is Delft crowded with tourists?

Less so than Amsterdam or even Utrecht — Delft's genuine historical significance rarely translates into Amsterdam-scale crowds, even at the Markt square in high season.
