Welcome to Madaba and Nebo

Madaba is a small town about 30 km south of Amman, and it is known as the City of Mosaics. It was a prosperous Byzantine bishopric in the 5th and 6th centuries, and it holds one of the densest collections of floor mosaics in the Middle East. The best known is a 6th-century map of the Holy Land on the floor of the Greek Orthodox Church of St George, the oldest surviving map of the region.

Ten kilometres to the north-west is Mount Nebo, a ridge at around 800 m that looks down on the Jordan Valley, the Dead Sea and, on a clear day, the hills around Jerusalem. According to the Book of Deuteronomy, Moses was shown the Promised Land from here before he died. The site is a place of Christian pilgrimage, and a Franciscan monastery and a Byzantine church stand on the summit.

Together, they make a compact visit of religious and archaeological interest. It is easy to combine with the Baptism Site and the Dead Sea for a single long day from Amman, and it sits at the start of the King's Highway.

Why visit

Why visit Madaba and Nebo?

Small, walkable and full of history, the Madaba area is one of the best short stops in Jordan.

  • The Madaba Map

    A floor mosaic of the Holy Land, made in the 6th century AD, showing Jerusalem, the Dead Sea and towns from the Nile Delta to Lebanon. About two million tesserae were used, according to commonly cited estimates.

  • The view from Mount Nebo

    A broad panorama over the Jordan Valley, the Dead Sea and, in clear weather, towards Jerusalem and Jericho. It is a serene place, and its association with Moses gives it a special significance.

  • A collection of mosaics

    Beyond the map, several churches and an archaeological park display Byzantine and Umayyad mosaics of extraordinary quality.

  • A gentle, walkable town

    Madaba is relaxed and small, with a Christian and Muslim community that has coexisted for generations. It is easy to explore on foot.

  • Close to everything

    Amman, the Dead Sea, the Baptism Site and the King's Highway are all within an hour, so it works as a base or a stop.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Madaba and Nebo

Madaba's sights are all within a short walk of each other. Mount Nebo is a 15-minute drive.

  1. 01

    The Madaba Map, St George's Church

    The mosaic map, in the Greek Orthodox Church of St George, is part of a much larger original that once covered the church floor. Jerusalem is the centrepiece, drawn in detail with its walls, gates and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre.

    Tip Go early or late, when there are fewer tour groups. Opening hours vary, and photography rules are posted.

  2. 02

    Mount Nebo and the Moses Memorial

    A Franciscan sanctuary on the summit, with a serpent-shaped cross sculpture that commemorates Moses's bronze serpent. The 4th-century church on the site has been restored and has mosaics of great beauty.

    Tip Go in the late afternoon for the best light over the Jordan Valley. On a very clear day you can see Jerusalem.

  3. 03

    The Madaba Archaeological Park

    A compound of Byzantine and Umayyad ruins, including the Hippolytus Hall, with a mosaic floor of extraordinary quality, and the Burnt Palace. It is a good place to see more mosaics.

    Tip Combine it with the small museum nearby.

  4. 04

    The Church of the Apostles

    A 6th-century church with a floor mosaic showing the personification of the sea, surrounded by fish and mythical creatures. It is small and less crowded than St George's.

    Tip Look at the central medallion, which is the highlight.

  5. 05

    The Madaba Museum

    A small museum in old houses that shows mosaics, traditional costumes and local finds.

    Tip It is a nice, quiet half-hour.

  6. 06

    Umm ar-Rasas

    A UNESCO-listed Roman, Byzantine and early Islamic site about 30 km south-east of Madaba, with a floor mosaic in the Church of Saint Stephen and a tall stylite tower that may have been used by an ascetic monk.

    Tip It is remote and best visited with your own driver.

  7. 07

    Machaerus

    The ruined hilltop fortress of Herod the Great and Herod Antipas, where, according to the historian Josephus, John the Baptist was imprisoned and executed. It has views over the Dead Sea.

    Tip Allow half a day. The site is about 25 km west of Madaba.

Things to do

Best things to do in Madaba and Nebo

The main sights fill half a day, and the rest is for walking and eating.

  1. See the Madaba Map

    The map is best appreciated slowly, with a guidebook or a guide. Look for Jerusalem at the centre, the Dead Sea with ships, and the Nile Delta at the edge.

    Tip There is a small admission fee, and photography is allowed.

  2. Watch the sunset from Mount Nebo

    The sun sinks over the hills of Palestine and the lights come on in the valley. It is an evocative place at dusk.

    Tip Bring a jacket; it is cool and windy at the top.

  3. Visit a mosaic workshop

    Some local workshops and schools produce contemporary mosaics, including Madaba's Institute for Mosaic Art and Restoration. You can see the craft and buy a small piece.

    Tip Check that a piece is handmade before buying.

  4. Walk the old town

    Madaba's centre is compact, with churches, cafés and restaurants. The pace is slow and the mood friendly.

    Tip Walk along the main street and look at the old stone houses.

  5. Combine with the Dead Sea

    The Dead Sea is 40 minutes downhill from Mount Nebo, so the two combine easily. Many visitors also add the Baptism Site.

    Tip Start at Nebo in the morning and finish with a swim.

  6. Take a cooking class

    A few families and guesthouses in Madaba offer cooking classes and home meals.

    Tip Ask at your hotel or a local tourist office.

Local food

What to eat in Madaba and Nebo

Madaba has a distinct food culture, influenced by its Christian community and its position near the desert. It is small enough that a couple of restaurants cover most of what you need.

  • Mansaf

    The national dish of lamb, rice and dried-yoghurt sauce is served in traditional restaurants in the town.

    Where to try it Family-run restaurants on the main street.

  • Maqluba

    An upside-down dish of rice, vegetables and meat, popular as a home-cooked meal.

    Where to try it Home-style restaurants and cooking classes.

  • Local wine

    Madaba's Christian community has a tradition of winemaking, and a few small wineries and restaurants serve local wines.

    Where to try it Restaurants in the old town.

  • Falafel and hummus

    A standard cheap lunch, and good.

    Where to try it Stalls near St George's Church.

  • Fresh bread

    Madaba's bakeries produce round loaves and pastries, often filled with cheese or herbs.

    Where to try it Bakeries in the centre in the early morning.

  • Sweet pastries

    Kunafa, baklava and other pastries are sold in shops around the centre.

    Where to try it Sweet shops near the main square.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Madaba and Nebo

Most people visit as a day trip from Amman, but Madaba is a pleasant place to stay and useful for early access to Mount Nebo and the Dead Sea.

  • Madaba town centre

    A handful of small hotels and guesthouses, some in restored old houses, within walking distance of the churches.

    Best for: Walking and atmosphere

  • Mount Nebo area

    A few lodges and small hotels near the mountain, with views over the valley.

    Best for: Quiet and views

Transport

Getting to and around Madaba and Nebo

Madaba is about 30 km south of Amman, and Mount Nebo is a short drive beyond it.

Getting there

  • Car or taxi from Amman

    About 40 minutes by road. A taxi to Madaba and Mount Nebo with waiting time is good value for a small group.

    Agree the price and the stops in advance.

  • Bus from Amman

    Buses and minibuses run from Amman's Wihdat station to Madaba regularly, taking about 45 minutes.

    For Mount Nebo you will need a taxi from Madaba.

  • Tour

    Many day tours from Amman combine Madaba, Mount Nebo and the Dead Sea.

    Check what is included, including entry tickets.

Getting around

The centre of Madaba is walkable. Mount Nebo and the wider region need a taxi or car.

  • Walking

    Madaba's main sights are within about ten minutes' walk of each other.

    The town is on a gentle hill, and the roads are quiet.

  • Taxi

    Taxis take you to Mount Nebo, the Dead Sea and Machaerus for a fixed price.

    Agree the price and waiting time in advance.

  • Hire car

    Useful if you want to visit Umm ar-Rasas and Machaerus.

    Roads are good but signs can be confusing, so use a map app.

When to go

Best time to visit Madaba and Nebo

Madaba and Nebo are at about 800 m and have a comfortable climate. The best views over the valley come in spring and autumn, when the air is clear.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm, clear days and wildflowers. The best views from Mount Nebo.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Shoulder

    Hot but not extreme, often with haze that reduces the view from Nebo.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Clear and warm, with good light and fewer visitors.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cool and sometimes wet, but on a clear day the views are excellent and the site is quiet.

Worth planning around: Hazy summer afternoons, when the view over the Dead Sea disappears.

Itinerary

One day at Madaba and Mount Nebo

Mount Nebo at opening, Madaba's mosaics at midday and the Dead Sea in the late afternoon. Assumes a driver from Amman.

  1. Day1

    Nebo, Madaba and the sea

    A pilgrimage day with a swim at the end.

    • Morning Mount Nebo when it opens, with time at the memorial and the viewpoint.
    • Afternoon Madaba: St George's Church and the map, the archaeological park and lunch in the old town.
    • Evening Down to the Dead Sea for a swim at sunset, then back to Amman.

Travel tips

Madaba and Nebo travel tips

This is a compact, easy area, and a few suggestions make it better.

  • Go to Mount Nebo first, when it opens. The light is soft, the crowds are small and you have the whole day ahead.
  • Dress modestly for churches, with shoulders and knees covered. Some are active places of worship.
  • Bring a guidebook or hire a guide at St George's to make sense of the map.
  • Check opening hours, particularly on Sundays and holidays, when churches may close to visitors.
  • Buy the Jordan Pass. It includes entry to Mount Nebo and the archaeological park.
  • Take a jacket to Mount Nebo. It is often windy and cool.
  • Combine your visit with the Dead Sea, but do not try to fit in too much in a single day.
  • Use a driver if you plan to visit Umm ar-Rasas or Machaerus, as public transport is limited.

FAQ

Madaba and Nebo: frequently asked questions

What is the Madaba Map?

A floor mosaic from the 6th century AD in the Greek Orthodox Church of St George. It shows the Holy Land from Lebanon to Egypt, with Jerusalem at the centre, and is the oldest surviving map of the region.

Why is Mount Nebo important?

According to the Bible, Moses was shown the Promised Land from Mount Nebo before he died. The site is a place of Christian pilgrimage, with a Franciscan sanctuary and a Byzantine church. Its location is traditional and not proved by archaeology.

How long do you need in Madaba and Mount Nebo?

Half a day covers the map, Nebo and one or two other sites. A full day lets you add the archaeological park, Machaerus or the Dead Sea.

Can I visit as a day trip from Amman?

Yes. It is about 40 minutes each way, and many visitors combine it with the Dead Sea.

Is Madaba safe and welcoming to visitors?

Yes. It is a small, friendly town with a mixed Christian and Muslim population, and it is generally regarded as very safe. Dress modestly and respect the churches.

Can I see Jerusalem from Mount Nebo?

On a very clear day you can see the hills around Jerusalem and Jericho. Haze often reduces the view, so go in spring or autumn and in the morning.
