Welcome to Niagara Falls

Niagara Falls is one of the most powerful waterfalls on Earth, over six thousand cubic metres of water pouring over the edge every second where the Niagara River meets the US-Canada border.

The Canadian side offers the better view, looking directly across at the curved Horseshoe Falls rather than standing on top of it as visitors on the American side do.

Just up the Niagara Parkway, the charming town of Niagara-on-the-Lake anchors one of Canada's best wine regions, known especially for its distinctive ice wine.

Why visit

Why visit Niagara Falls?

Niagara Falls' case rests on raw natural power paired with an easy, well-set-up visitor experience.

  • The falls are as powerful as their reputation suggests

    Six thousand cubic metres of water a second, a sight that photographs undersell.

  • The Canadian side offers the better view

    A direct look at Horseshoe Falls, rather than standing on top of it.

  • The boat tours are a real thrill

    Getting close enough to feel the falls' spray and hear their full roar.

  • Niagara-on-the-Lake is a worthwhile pairing

    A charming wine-country town a short drive up the parkway.

  • It works well as an easy Toronto day trip or overnight stay

    About 1.5 hours by road or a direct train.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Niagara Falls

Niagara Falls' essential sights are centred on the falls themselves, with wine country a short drive away.

  1. 01

    Horseshoe Falls

    The larger and more powerful of Niagara's two main falls, its curved shape giving the site its name.

    Tip The view from Table Rock, right at the edge, is the closest you can get without a boat.

  2. 02

    Niagara Falls illuminated at night

    A spectacular nightly show, coloured floodlights transforming the falls after dark.

    Tip Check for a fireworks schedule, held on select nights through the summer season.

  3. 03

    The Maid of the Mist / Hornblower boat tour

    The classic Niagara experience, a boat sailing right into the falls' base mist.

    Tip Wear the provided poncho — you will get properly wet regardless.

  4. 04

    Niagara-on-the-Lake's historic Old Town

    One of Canada's best-preserved 19th-century towns, its main street lined with heritage buildings and boutiques.

    Tip Combine with a wine-tasting stop — this is the heart of Niagara's wine country.

  5. 05

    Niagara's wine country vineyards

    One of Canada's premier wine regions, its cool climate especially suited to producing distinctive ice wine.

    Tip Book a winery tour ahead in peak summer and harvest season.

  6. 06

    The view from Skylon Tower

    A sweeping overview, taking in both Horseshoe and American Falls from a single elevated vantage point.

    Tip Go near sunset to see the falls in daylight and illuminated within the same visit.

  7. 07

    Rainbows over Horseshoe Falls

    A common, delightful sight, mist and sunlight combining into rainbows over the falls on clear days.

    Tip Most likely in the morning, when the sun angle lines up best with the mist.

Things to do

Best things to do in Niagara Falls

Niagara Falls rewards both classic tourist experiences and a slower afternoon in nearby wine country.

  1. Ride the boat right up to the falls

    An essential Niagara experience, sailing close enough to feel the falls' full force and spray.

    Tip Book online ahead in peak summer to skip the ticket queue.

  2. See the falls illuminated and watch for fireworks

    A lovely evening activity, the falls glowing under coloured lights after dark.

    Tip Check the current fireworks schedule — not every night includes a show.

  3. Drive or cycle the Niagara Parkway

    A scenic route, following the river from the falls up toward Niagara-on-the-Lake through orchards and vineyards.

    Tip A good half-day activity if you have a car or rent a bike for part of the route.

  4. Taste wine in Niagara-on-the-Lake

    A worthwhile change of pace from the falls themselves, sampling the region's distinctive ice wine and other varietals.

    Tip Several wineries offer tastings without a booking, though tours are best arranged ahead.

  5. Explore Niagara-on-the-Lake's Queen Street shops

    A relaxed way to spend an afternoon, browsing boutiques along one of Canada's prettiest main streets.

    Tip Stop for afternoon tea at one of the town's historic inns.

  6. Get soaked behind the falls

    A thrilling experience, tunnels leading to viewing points directly behind the curtain of Horseshoe Falls.

    Tip Wear the provided poncho, and expect to get wet regardless.

Local food

What to eat in Niagara Falls

Food here spans classic Canadian dishes alongside the Niagara region's excellent wine.

  • Niagara ice wine

    A rare, distinctive specialty, sweet dessert wine made from grapes frozen naturally on the vine.

    Where to try it Wineries throughout Niagara-on-the-Lake.

  • Niagara wine

    One of Canada's best wine regions, with excellent whites and increasingly respected reds.

    Where to try it Wineries throughout the Niagara Peninsula.

  • Fresh Ontario peaches

    A real seasonal treat, the Niagara region's orchards producing some of Canada's best stone fruit.

    Where to try it Farm stands and markets throughout the region in late summer.

  • Butter tarts

    A beloved Ontario classic, found at bakeries throughout the region.

    Where to try it Bakeries in Niagara-on-the-Lake and Niagara Falls town.

  • Poutine

    Widely available, a reliable classic between sightseeing stops.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout Niagara Falls town.

  • Ontario craft beer

    A growing local scene, with several breweries scattered throughout the wider region.

    Where to try it Pubs and breweries throughout the region.

Transport

Getting to and around Niagara Falls

Niagara Falls is an easy trip from Toronto, whether as a day trip or overnight stay.

Getting there

  • Train or bus from Toronto

    Straightforward, taking about 1.5 to 2 hours.

    GO Transit trains run direct on a seasonal schedule; buses run year-round.

  • Drive from Toronto

    The most flexible option, especially if continuing on to Niagara-on-the-Lake.

    About 1.5 hours via the Queen Elizabeth Way.

  • Organised day trip

    Convenient for a rushed visit without your own transport.

    Widely available from Toronto, often including wine-country stops.

Getting around

Niagara Falls town is walkable near the falls, with a car or shuttle useful for reaching wine country.

  • On foot near the falls

    Easy for the main falls viewpoints and boat tour docks.

    Table Rock and the surrounding area all sit within a comfortable walk.

  • WEGO shuttle bus

    Useful for covering the wider Niagara Parkway.

    A hop-on hop-off bus connects the falls to nearby attractions.

  • Car or organised tour to wine country

    Necessary for reaching Niagara-on-the-Lake's wineries.

    About a 20-minute drive from the falls themselves.

When to go

Best time to visit Niagara Falls

Niagara Falls is a year-round destination, with each season offering a different character.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    The busiest, warmest season, with the boat tours and fireworks both running in full.

  • Early autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Pleasant weather with wine harvest season underway and noticeably fewer crowds.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Striking with ice formations around the falls, though boat tours typically pause for the season.

  • Spring (April–May)

    Good

    A pleasant shoulder season, with boat tours resuming as the weather warms.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly, though winter closes the boat tours and some outdoor attractions.

Itinerary

Two days at Niagara Falls

Built around the falls themselves, with a second day exploring Niagara-on-the-Lake's wine country.

  1. Day1

    The falls

    The essential Niagara experience.

    • Morning Table Rock and the view over Horseshoe Falls.
    • Afternoon The Maid of the Mist or Hornblower boat tour.
    • Evening See the falls illuminated after dark.
  2. Day2

    Niagara-on-the-Lake

    A slower day of wine and heritage charm.

    • Morning Drive the Niagara Parkway to Niagara-on-the-Lake.
    • Afternoon Wine tasting at a local vineyard.
    • Evening Dinner on Niagara-on-the-Lake's Queen Street.

Travel tips

Niagara Falls travel tips

Practical notes that make a Niagara Falls visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book boat tour tickets online ahead in peak summer.
  • Bring a change of clothes — you will get wet on the boat tour or behind the falls.
  • Visit Table Rock early morning for the clearest view before crowds build.
  • Combine a wine-tasting stop with Niagara-on-the-Lake for a fuller day trip.
  • Check the fireworks schedule if a night visit is part of your plan.
  • Consider staying overnight rather than a rushed day trip to see the falls illuminated.

FAQ

Niagara Falls: frequently asked questions

Is the Canadian side of Niagara Falls better than the American side?

Yes — the Canadian side offers a direct view of Horseshoe Falls, widely considered the better vantage point.

How many days do you need at Niagara Falls?

One day covers the falls themselves; two allows time for Niagara-on-the-Lake's wine country too.

How do you get from Toronto to Niagara Falls?

Easiest by train, bus or car, all taking around 1.5 to 2 hours.

Is the boat tour worth it?

Yes — it's the closest you can get to the falls, and the spray and roar are unforgettable up close.

Can you visit Niagara Falls in winter?

Yes — the ice formations are striking, though the boat tours typically pause for the season.

Is Niagara-on-the-Lake worth visiting?

Yes — it's one of Canada's prettiest towns, and the surrounding wine country produces excellent ice wine.
