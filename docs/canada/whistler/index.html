Welcome to Whistler

Whistler is genuinely North America's largest ski resort, twin mountains rising directly above a purpose-built alpine village about two hours north of Vancouver.

Getting there is genuinely part of the appeal — the Sea to Sky Highway ranks among the continent's most scenic drives, hugging Howe Sound before climbing into the Coast Mountains.

While winter sports genuinely built its reputation, Whistler has become just as much a summer destination, with mountain biking, hiking and lake swimming filling the resort's other half of the year.

Why visit

Why visit Whistler?

Whistler's case rests genuinely on world-class mountain terrain paired with a proper, walkable village at its base.

  • It's genuinely North America's largest ski resort

    Two connected mountains, Whistler and Blackcomb, offering an enormous amount of terrain.

  • The Peak 2 Peak Gondola is genuinely an engineering feat

    The world's longest unsupported cable span, linking the two mountains directly.

  • The Sea to Sky Highway is genuinely one of the best drives in North America

    A dramatic coastal and mountain route between Vancouver and Whistler.

  • It's genuinely a real year-round destination

    World-class mountain biking and hiking take over once the snow melts.

  • The village itself is genuinely well designed

Main attractions

Top things to see in Whistler

Whistler's essential sights are genuinely spread across its village, the mountains above it, and the drive to reach it.

  1. 01

    Whistler Village

    Genuinely a purpose-built, car-free alpine village, its cobbled squares lined with shops, restaurants and bars.

    Tip Genuinely explore both Whistler Village and the smaller, quieter Village North just beyond it.

  2. 02

    The Peak 2 Peak Gondola

    Genuinely the world's longest unsupported cable span, linking Whistler and Blackcomb mountains 436 metres above the valley.

    Tip Genuinely ride it even if you're not skiing — it runs for sightseers too, with glass-bottomed cabins on some cars.

  3. 03

    Whistler Blackcomb's slopes

    Genuinely North America's largest ski resort by terrain, with runs for every level across two connected mountains.

    Tip Genuinely book lift tickets and rentals online ahead in peak ski season.

  4. 04

    Lost Lake

    Genuinely a peaceful escape from the village, a forested lake with swimming, paddling and a network of trails.

    Tip Genuinely an easy walk or bike ride from Whistler Village itself.

  5. 05

    Genuinely worth the stop on the drive up, a gondola near Squamish climbing to sweeping views over Howe Sound.

  6. 06

    Tip Genuinely a popular rock-climbing destination for those with the skill and gear.

  7. 07

    Whistler's mountain backdrop

    Genuinely striking in any season, jagged Coast Mountain peaks rising directly above the village.

    Tip Genuinely the view changes dramatically between summer green and winter snow — worth a visit in both.

Best things to do in Whistler

Whistler genuinely offers something in every season, not just the winter sports it's best known for.

  1. Ride the Peak 2 Peak Gondola between the mountains

    A genuinely essential Whistler experience, gliding between two mountains high above the valley floor.

    Tip Genuinely go on a clear day for the best views — cloud cover can obscure the panorama entirely.

  2. Ski or snowboard Whistler Blackcomb

    A genuinely world-class winter sports experience, with terrain for complete beginners through to experts.

    Tip Genuinely book lessons ahead if you're new — Whistler's ski schools are excellent but do fill up.

  3. Hike or bike around Lost Lake

  4. Ride the Sea to Sky Gondola near Squamish

  5. Explore the Squamish Lil'wat Cultural Centre

    Tip Genuinely check for guided tours, which add real depth to the exhibits.

  6. Relax at an outdoor spa after a day on the mountain

    A genuinely popular way to unwind, alternating between hot pools, saunas and cold plunges in a forest setting.

    Tip Genuinely book ahead — Whistler's outdoor spas are popular and slots do fill up.

Transport

Getting to and around Whistler

Whistler is genuinely reached by road from Vancouver, with no airport of its own.

Getting there

  • Drive the Sea to Sky Highway from Vancouver

    Genuinely the standard route, taking about 2.5 hours.

    One of the most scenic drives in North America, hugging Howe Sound before climbing into the mountains.

  • Shuttle bus from Vancouver or the airport

    Genuinely convenient if you'd rather not drive.

    Several operators run frequent direct services, especially in ski season.

  • Private transfer or rental car

    Genuinely the most flexible option for stopping along the way.

    Allows stops at the Sea to Sky Gondola and other viewpoints en route.

Getting around

Whistler Village is genuinely car-free and entirely walkable, with lifts and shuttles covering the mountains.

  • On foot

    Genuinely the only way to move through the village itself.

    Cars are largely restricted from the pedestrian core.

  • Lifts and gondolas

    Genuinely the way up the mountains, whether skiing or sightseeing.

    A single pass typically covers both Whistler and Blackcomb.

  • Local shuttle bus

    Genuinely useful for reaching Creekside or other outlying areas.

    A free or low-cost shuttle connects the village's different zones.

When to go

Best time to visit Whistler

Whistler is genuinely a true year-round resort, with two distinct high seasons depending on what you're after.

  • Winter (December–March)

    Best time

    Genuinely prime ski season, with the most reliable snow and the resort at its liveliest.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Genuinely excellent for mountain biking, hiking and lake swimming, with warm, sunny weather.

  • Early autumn (September)

    Good

    Genuinely a quieter shoulder season, still pleasant for hiking before the snow returns.

  • Spring and late autumn

    Good

    Genuinely the quietest, muddiest months, with limited activities as the resort transitions between seasons.

Worth planning around: Genuinely late spring (April–May) and late autumn (November), when neither skiing nor summer activities are fully available.

No, genuinely not — summer brings excellent mountain biking, hiking and lake activities, making it a true year-round destination.

Genuinely one of Canada's pricier destinations, especially in peak ski season, though summer offers noticeably better value.

No, genuinely not — the resort has extensive terrain and lessons for complete beginners through to experts.

Yes, genuinely easily — the Peak 2 Peak Gondola, Lost Lake and the village itself are all worthwhile without touching a ski.
