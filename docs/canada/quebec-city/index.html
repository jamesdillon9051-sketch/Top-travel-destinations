Main attractions

Top things to see in Quebec City

Quebec City's essential sights are genuinely concentrated within and just outside its historic walled core.

  1. 01

    Château Frontenac

    Genuinely said to be the most photographed hotel in the world, a turreted 1893 railway hotel dominating the skyline.

    Tip Genuinely worth a coffee inside even without staying — the lobby and terrace are open to visitors.

  2. 02

    Dufferin Terrace

    Genuinely the city's grandest promenade, a boardwalk running along the clifftop beside Château Frontenac.

    Tip Genuinely visit at sunset for the best light over the St Lawrence River.

  3. 03

    The Petit-Champlain quarter

    Genuinely one of the oldest commercial streets in North America, steep and narrow, lined with boutiques and cafés.

    Tip Genuinely take the funicular down from Dufferin Terrace rather than the steep stairs alone.

  4. 04

    Montmorency Falls

    Genuinely taller than Niagara Falls, a dramatic 83-metre waterfall just outside the city.

    Tip Genuinely cross the suspension bridge at the top for a view straight down the falls.

  5. 05

    Old Quebec's colonial streets

    Genuinely a near-complete French colonial townscape, stone buildings and cobblestones dating back to the 17th century.

    Tip Genuinely visit early morning before day-trippers from cruise ships and tour buses arrive.

  6. 06

    Château Frontenac illuminated at night

    Genuinely a striking sight after dark, the hotel's copper roofs and turrets lit up above the city.

    Tip Genuinely walk down to the Lower Town for a view looking back up at the illuminated hotel.

  7. 07

    The House of Chevalier

    Genuinely one of the Old Town's best-preserved merchant houses, a glimpse into 18th-century Quebec commerce.

    Tip Genuinely just one of many historic facades worth pausing for throughout the Lower Town.

Things to do

Best things to do in Quebec City

Quebec City genuinely rewards unhurried wandering, split between the clifftop Upper Town and the riverside Lower Town.

  1. Walk Dufferin Terrace at sunset

    A genuinely essential evening ritual, watching the light change over the St Lawrence River from the boardwalk.

    Tip Genuinely a lovely spot to simply sit on a bench and take it all in.

  2. Explore Petit-Champlain's boutiques

    A genuinely relaxed way to spend a couple of hours, browsing shops in one of North America's oldest commercial streets.

    Tip Genuinely check the funicular's operating hours if you'd rather not walk back up the hill.

  3. Day-trip to Montmorency Falls

    A genuinely worthwhile half-day trip, a dramatic waterfall taller than Niagara just outside the city.

    Tip Genuinely take the cable car up rather than the stairs if you'd like an easier visit.

  4. Wander Old Quebec's walled streets

    A genuinely rewarding way to spend an afternoon, tracing centuries of French colonial history through narrow lanes.

    Tip Genuinely walk a stretch of the city's fortified walls for a different perspective on the Old Town.

  5. Listen to a street pianist on Dufferin Terrace

    A genuinely charming, low-key pleasure, live music often drifting along the terrace in warmer months.

    Tip Genuinely a nice reason to linger longer than you'd planned.

  6. Walk the Plains of Abraham

    A genuinely significant historic site, the battlefield park where a pivotal 1759 battle shaped Canada's future.

    Tip Genuinely a peaceful green space today, popular for picnics and jogging as much as history.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout Old Quebec and beyond.

  • Tourtière (meat pie)

    Genuinely a traditional Quebec comfort food, a spiced meat pie especially popular around the winter holidays.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout Old Quebec.

  • Maple products

    Genuinely a real point of pride, with Quebec producing most of the world's maple syrup.

    Where to try it Markets and specialty shops throughout the city.

  • Quebec cheese

    Genuinely an underrated part of the province's food scene, with a genuine artisanal cheese-making tradition.

    Where to try it Specialty cheese shops throughout Old Quebec.

  • Caribou (traditional winter drink)

  • Fresh game and river fish

    Where to try it Traditional Quebec restaurants throughout Old Quebec.

Transport

Getting to and around Quebec City

Quebec City is genuinely well connected by train and road from Montreal, with its own smaller international airport.

Getting there

  • Train from Montreal

    Genuinely a comfortable, frequent option, no car needed.

    Takes about three hours via Via Rail's Central Canada corridor.

  • Bus or drive from Montreal

    Genuinely a budget-friendly alternative to the train.

    Similar journey time, with more frequent departures than rail.

  • Direct flights

    Genuinely available from a handful of Canadian and US cities.

    Québec City Jean Lesage International Airport sits about 20 minutes from downtown.

Getting around

Quebec City's genuinely compact walled core is entirely walkable, with a funicular connecting its two levels.

  • On foot

    Genuinely the only way to properly explore Old Quebec.

    The Upper and Lower Towns are both compact and best experienced walking.

  • The Old Quebec Funicular

    Genuinely the easy way to connect the Upper and Lower Towns.

    A quick, fun alternative to the steep stairs between the two levels.

  • Bus or car to Montmorency Falls

    Genuinely necessary for reaching the falls outside the city.

    A local bus or short drive covers the distance easily.

Travel tips

Quebec City travel tips

Practical notes that make a Quebec City visit noticeably smoother.

  • Wear genuinely comfortable shoes — Old Quebec's streets are cobbled and often steep.
  • Use genuinely the funicular to save the climb between the Upper and Lower Towns.
  • Learn genuinely a few basic French phrases — French is even more dominant here than in Montreal.
  • Visit genuinely Old Quebec early morning to avoid cruise-ship and tour-bus crowds.
  • Pack genuinely serious cold-weather gear for a winter visit.
  • Book genuinely accommodation well ahead for the Quebec Winter Carnival in February.

Two to three days genuinely covers Old Quebec and a trip to Montmorency Falls comfortably.

No, genuinely not, though French is even more dominant here than in Montreal, and a few basic phrases are genuinely appreciated.

Genuinely easiest by train, taking about three hours via Via Rail's Central Canada corridor.
