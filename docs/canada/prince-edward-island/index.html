Welcome to Prince Edward Island

Prince Edward Island is genuinely Canada's smallest province, a gentle landscape of red sand beaches, rolling farmland and small fishing harbours that feels a world away from the Rockies or big city life.

It's genuinely also the setting for Anne of Green Gables, the beloved novel that still draws visitors to the farmhouse and landscape that inspired it.

The island's real character genuinely comes through in its seafood — lobster suppers, fresh mussels and PEI's own celebrated potatoes fill menus across the province.

Transport

Getting to and around Prince Edward Island

Prince Edward Island is genuinely reached by bridge, ferry or air, with the Confederation Bridge the most common route.

Getting there

  • Drive across the Confederation Bridge

    Genuinely the standard route from mainland New Brunswick.

    A toll applies when leaving the island, not on arrival.

  • Ferry from Nova Scotia

    Genuinely a scenic seasonal alternative.

    Runs between Wood Islands, PEI, and Caribou, Nova Scotia, spring through autumn.

  • Direct flight to Charlottetown

    Genuinely available from several Canadian cities.

    Charlottetown Airport sits about ten minutes from downtown.

Getting around

A car is genuinely essential for exploring Prince Edward Island beyond Charlottetown itself.

  • Car

    Genuinely necessary for reaching the island's beaches, lighthouses and small towns.

    Distances are short, but public transport is genuinely limited outside Charlottetown.

  • On foot in Charlottetown

    Genuinely easy for the compact downtown core.

    The waterfront and historic streets sit within a comfortable walk.

  • Bicycle on the Confederation Trail

    Genuinely a lovely way to see the island slowly.

    A former rail line converted into a cross-island cycling and walking trail.
