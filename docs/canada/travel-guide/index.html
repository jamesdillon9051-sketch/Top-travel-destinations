How to plan a Canada trip: which region to choose, when to go for the best weather and open roads, what it costs, and a practical twelve-day Western Canada itinerary.

How Canada divides

Canada divides into distinct regions separated by real distance, not just a line on a map — a first trip realistically covers one.

Western Canada. Vancouver, Whistler, Banff and Lake Louise and Jasper — mountains, glacial lakes and the country's most photographed scenery.

Central Canada. Toronto, Niagara Falls, Montreal and Quebec City — the country's biggest cities and its French colonial heart, all connected by a manageable train corridor.

The Maritimes. Prince Edward Island — a slower, seafood-and-lighthouses side of Canada, distinct from either coast.

The North. Churchill — polar bears, beluga whales and the northern lights, reached only by plane or a long train ride.

Worth knowing: Most first-time visitors pick Western Canada's mountains or Central Canada's cities — both work as complete trips on their own and don't require internal flights within the region itself.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If this is your first trip

    Vancouver, Banff and Lake Louise and Jasper, the classic Rockies route.

  • If you want walkable historic cities

    Montreal and Quebec City, Canada's most European-feeling corner.

  • If you're travelling with kids or want an easy city break

    Toronto paired with Niagara Falls, an easy, low-effort combination.

  • If you want skiing or mountain adventure

    Whistler, one of North America's best ski resorts.

  • If you want a slower, seafood-focused pace

    Prince Edward Island, a world away from the mountain parks.

  • If you want a singular wildlife experience

    Churchill, the polar bear capital of the world.

Getting around: flights, driving, and one common mistake

Canada's continental scale means flying between regions and driving within them, rather than one mode covering the whole trip.

Fly between regions, don't drive. Vancouver to Toronto takes over 40 hours by road versus 4.5 hours by air.

Rent a car for the Rockies. Banff and Lake Louise and Jasper have limited public transport between viewpoints and trailheads.

Use the train corridor in Central Canada. Toronto, Montreal and Quebec City are well connected by frequent Via Rail service, no car needed.

Book Churchill flights well ahead. Churchill has no road access — flights from Winnipeg are the only fast way in.

Worth knowing: Book flights and Rockies lodging months ahead for a July or August trip — Banff and Jasper's peak season sells out early.

When to go

Canada's vast range means 'the best time' depends heavily on region, though June to September works well almost everywhere.

Summer brings the most reliable weather nationwide, with every mountain road and trail open.

Winter is superb for skiing in Whistler and Banff, and for aurora and polar bear viewing in Churchill, though many mountain routes close.

Shoulder seasons (spring and fall) are workable in the cities, with fall offering excellent colour in the Rockies and Quebec alike.

Worth knowing: Book Banff, Jasper and Whistler accommodation several months ahead for peak summer or ski season — both sell out their best options early.

Four routes that work

  1. Six days: the Rockies highlights

    Banff and Lake Louise and Jasper, the most popular short Canada itinerary, flying into Calgary.

    Tip Fly into Calgary rather than Vancouver — it's much closer to the Rockies.

  2. Twelve days: the classic Western Canada loop

    The itinerary on the Canada country page: Vancouver, Whistler, Banff and Lake Louise and Jasper. A complete first look at Western Canada.

    Tip Book Moraine Lake's shuttle or arrive before 6am — the access road often closes to regular cars by mid-morning in peak season.

  3. A week focused on Central Canada's cities

    Toronto, Niagara Falls, Montreal and Quebec City, connected by train.

    Tip No car needed — Via Rail's corridor covers this entire route comfortably.

  4. A Maritimes or Churchill add-on for a second trip

    Prince Edward Island or Churchill, the parts of Canada most first-time visitors never see.

    Tip Book Churchill's polar bear season (October–November) flights and lodges up to a year ahead — capacity is limited.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Canada

How many days do you need in Canada?

Ten to fourteen days covers one region properly, such as the Western Canada loop in this guide. Six days works for a focused Rockies-only trip.

Should I fly into Vancouver or Calgary for the Rockies?

Calgary sits much closer to Banff and Jasper; Vancouver adds a worthwhile city and the Sea to Sky Highway if you have the extra days.

Do you need a car in Canada?

Not in Toronto, Montreal or Quebec City, which are well connected by train and transit, but yes for the Rockies and Prince Edward Island.

Is one trip enough to see Canada?

No, not — the country's regions differ enough that most visitors return for a second, entirely different trip rather than trying to add more to one.
