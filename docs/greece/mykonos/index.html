Welcome to Mykonos

Mykonos built its international reputation on beach clubs and nightlife starting in the 1960s, when the island became a genuine magnet for a jet-set crowd seeking sun and freedom that mainland Greece's more conservative culture didn't offer at the time. That reputation persists today, and the island remains genuinely one of the Mediterranean's most famous party destinations.

What that reputation tends to obscure is Mykonos Town itself, a genuinely gorgeous labyrinth of whitewashed cube houses, narrow marble-paved lanes designed centuries ago to confuse pirates, and the iconic row of windmills overlooking Little Venice's waterfront homes.

The island rewards visitors willing to look past the beach-club scene toward its genuine architectural beauty, its quieter northern beaches and Ano Mera's more traditional inland village, alongside the easy day trip to Delos, one of the ancient world's most sacred and significant islands.

Why visit

Why visit Mykonos?

Mykonos' case is that no other Greek island pairs this famous a nightlife scene with this much genuine architectural beauty and ancient history alongside it.

  • Mykonos Town is genuinely one of the Cyclades' most beautiful old towns

    Its labyrinthine whitewashed lanes and Little Venice waterfront rival any island's old town, beach clubs aside.

  • The nightlife is genuinely world famous

    From sunset beach bars to late-night clubs, Mykonos' reputation as a party island is well earned for those seeking it.

  • Delos adds genuine ancient significance

    A short boat trip reaches one of the ancient Greek world's most sacred islands, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

  • The beaches are genuinely excellent

    From organised beach clubs to quieter northern coves, Mykonos' coastline offers real range beyond the famous party beaches.

  • It pairs genuinely well with Santorini

    A short ferry or flight connects the two most famous Cyclades islands into an easy combined itinerary.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Mykonos

Mykonos' essential sights, from its famous windmills to the ancient island of Delos just offshore.

  1. 01

    Mykonos Town (Chora)

    A genuinely gorgeous maze of whitewashed cube houses and narrow marble lanes, deliberately laid out centuries ago to confuse raiding pirates.

    Tip Getting genuinely lost in the old town's lanes is part of the experience — most paths eventually lead back to the waterfront.

  2. 02

    The windmills (Kato Mili)

    A row of white stone windmills overlooking the town and Little Venice, once used to mill wheat and now the island's single most photographed landmark.

    Tip The area around the windmills offers genuinely excellent sunset views over Little Venice below.

  3. 03

    Little Venice

    A row of colourful waterfront houses with balconies hanging directly over the sea, genuinely reminiscent of Venice's own canal-side architecture.

    Tip Several of the waterfront buildings are now bars, offering a genuinely good excuse to enjoy the view with a drink.

  4. 04

    Panagia Paraportiani

    A genuinely striking cluster of five whitewashed churches fused into one asymmetrical structure, among the most photographed churches in Greece.

    Tip Early morning light gives the church's white curves a genuinely soft, dramatic quality for photographs.

  5. 05

    Delos archaeological site

    A short boat trip reaches this uninhabited island, considered one of the ancient Greek world's most sacred sites and a genuinely significant UNESCO World Heritage Site.

    Tip Bring water and sun protection — the island is genuinely exposed with very little shade across its ruins.

  6. 06

    Ano Mera village

    A genuinely quieter inland village away from the coast, home to the historic Panagia Tourliani Monastery and a real sense of pre-tourism Mykonos life.

    Tip The village's central square offers genuinely good, less touristy taverna options than the coast.

Things to do

Best things to do in Mykonos

Beyond the old town, Mykonos rewards its beach club culture, its nightlife and the ancient island just offshore.

  1. Spend a day at Paradise or Super Paradise beach

    The island's most famous beach clubs, genuinely central to Mykonos' party reputation, with music, sunbeds and beachside bars running throughout the day.

    Tip Book sunbeds ahead in peak season at the most popular clubs, which genuinely fill by mid-morning.

  2. Watch the sunset from Little Venice

    A genuinely essential Mykonos evening, watching the sun set over the water from one of the waterfront bars beneath the windmills.

    Tip Arrive with enough time to find a good seat — the best-positioned bars fill quickly as sunset approaches.

  3. Day trip to Delos

    A genuinely essential piece of ancient history a short boat ride away, with ruins spanning temples, mosaics and an entire ancient sacred precinct.

    Tip Boats run a limited schedule — check return times carefully to avoid being stranded past the last crossing.

  4. Get lost in Mykonos Town's lanes

    The old town's genuinely labyrinthine streets reward simply wandering without a fixed destination, discovering small shops and quiet corners.

    Tip The area known as 'Little Venice' and the streets just behind it hold some of the town's most photogenic corners.

  5. Experience Mykonos' nightlife

    From sunset cocktail bars to late-night clubs, Mykonos' nightlife scene is genuinely central to the island's international reputation.

    Tip Many of the island's famous clubs don't get busy until well after midnight — pace the evening accordingly.

  6. Try windsurfing or watersports

    Mykonos' reliable winds make it a genuinely well-regarded windsurfing and kitesurfing destination, with several beaches offering lessons and rentals.

    Tip Kalafatis beach on the island's eastern side is genuinely the most established watersports hub.

Local food

What to eat in Mykonos

Mykonos' food culture centres on genuine Cycladic specialities alongside the island's own cured meats and cheese.

  • Louza

    Cured, spiced pork loin, a genuine Mykonos specialty traditionally air-dried and thinly sliced, similar in spirit to Italian cured meats.

    Where to try it Traditional tavernas and delicatessens throughout the island, often served as part of a meze spread.

  • Kopanisti

    A spicy, tangy whipped cheese made from fermented local cheese, a genuine Mykonos and wider Cyclades specialty distinct from milder Greek cheeses.

    Where to try it Traditional tavernas across the island, often served as a dip with bread.

  • Mykonian sausage (loukaniko)

    A genuinely distinctive local sausage, seasoned with orange peel and local spices, grilled and served as a taverna staple.

    Where to try it Traditional tavernas throughout Mykonos Town and Ano Mera.

  • Fresh seafood

    Grilled octopus, fresh fish and shellfish, genuinely excellent around the island's harbourside restaurants, typically priced by weight.

    Where to try it Harbourside tavernas in Mykonos Town and the smaller fishing village of Ornos.

  • Amygdalota

    Soft almond-based sweets, genuinely popular across the Cyclades, often dusted with powdered sugar and shaped by hand.

    Where to try it Traditional bakeries and sweet shops throughout Mykonos Town.

  • Local Cycladic wine

    Mykonos' own small wine production, alongside wines from neighbouring islands, genuinely worth trying at a taverna's house selection.

    Where to try it Tavernas across the island, many offering a genuinely good regional wine list.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Mykonos

Mykonos' accommodation spans the old town's central energy to quieter beachside resort areas.

  • Mykonos Town

    The most central, atmospheric base, putting Little Venice, the windmills and old town restaurants within easy walking distance.

    Best for: Walking to the sights and nightlife

  • Platis Gialos or Ornos

    Established beach resort areas with good bus connections to Mykonos Town.

    Best for: Beach access with easier transport

  • Near Paradise or Super Paradise

    Puts the island's most famous party beaches directly on your doorstep, at a genuine premium for peak season.

    Best for: A beach-club-focused stay

Transport

Getting to and around Mykonos

Mykonos is reached by short flight or ferry from Athens, both genuinely well served in season.

Getting there

  • Flight from Athens

    A short flight from Athens reaches Mykonos in about 40 minutes, considerably faster than the ferry alternative.

    Book ahead in peak summer, when popular flight times can sell out or spike in price.

  • Ferry from Piraeus

    High-speed ferries from Athens' port reach Mykonos in about 3 hours, with slower conventional ferries also available.

    Book high-speed ferries ahead in July and August, when popular Cyclades routes can sell out.

  • Ferry from Santorini or Crete

    Direct ferries connect Mykonos to Santorini in about 2–3 hours and to Crete with a longer connection.

    Routes and frequency vary by season — check current schedules before finalising an island-hopping itinerary.

Getting around

Mykonos Town is walkable, with buses and taxis covering the wider island's beaches.

  • Local buses

    A reasonably frequent network connects Mykonos Town to the island's main beaches, including Paradise and Platis Gialos.

    Buses can be genuinely crowded in peak season and at popular beach departure times.

  • Rental car or ATV

    Offers genuine flexibility for reaching quieter northern beaches and Ano Mera at your own pace.

    Mykonos Town's narrow lanes are largely pedestrian — park at the edge of town rather than attempting to drive in.

  • Water taxis

    Seasonal boat services connect several of the island's southern beaches directly by sea.

    A genuinely scenic alternative to the bus, particularly between the beach-club beaches.

When to go

Best time to visit Mykonos

Mykonos' summer nightlife and beach club scene peak hard in July and August, and shoulder season offers a genuinely quieter, more affordable version of the island.

  • Spring (April–June)

    Best time

    Mild, increasingly warm weather with beach clubs reopening and crowds well below the summer peak.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Hot, lively and genuinely at the height of its party reputation, but also the island's most crowded and expensive months.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Warm sea temperatures and a winding-down nightlife scene, with meaningfully thinner crowds than August.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Shoulder

    Genuinely quiet, with most beach clubs, bars and many hotels closed for the season.

Worth planning around: Peak July and August without accommodation booked many months ahead — Mykonos' rooms fill early and prices spike considerably during its busiest party season.

Itinerary

Three days in Mykonos

The old town and windmills first, then Delos, then the island's famous beaches.

  1. Day1

    Mykonos Town and Little Venice

    The island's iconic old town.

    • Morning Wander the old town's lanes and Panagia Paraportiani.
    • Afternoon The windmills and Little Venice.
    • Evening Sunset drinks in Little Venice, then dinner in town.
  2. Day2

    Delos day trip

    Ancient history a short boat ride away.

    • Morning Boat to Delos and explore the ancient ruins.
    • Afternoon Return to Mykonos and rest.
    • Evening Dinner and Mykonos' nightlife scene.
  3. Day3

    Beach day

    Mykonos' famous beach club culture.

    • Morning Travel to Paradise or Super Paradise beach.
    • Afternoon A full beach club day.
    • Evening A final dinner in Ano Mera or back in town.

Travel tips

Mykonos travel tips

Practical notes on timing, booking and etiquette that make a real difference to a Mykonos trip.

  • Book accommodation many months ahead for July and August — Mykonos' rooms sell out and prices spike early.
  • Book beach club sunbeds ahead at the most popular spots, which genuinely fill by mid-morning in peak season.
  • Check Delos boat schedules carefully — services are limited and missing the last return crossing is a genuine inconvenience.
  • Wear comfortable shoes for Mykonos Town's marble-paved lanes, which can be genuinely slippery when wet.
  • Consider Platis Gialos or Ornos over the very centre of town for a quieter, better-value stay with easy bus access.
  • Bring cash for smaller tavernas and beach vendors, some of which remain cash-preferring even on this well-touristed island.
  • Pack for genuine wind — the strong meltemi winds common in summer can affect beach comfort and boat schedules alike.

FAQ

Mykonos: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Mykonos?

Three works well — enough for the old town, a Delos day trip and at least one full beach day without feeling rushed.

Is Mykonos only about nightlife and beach clubs?

No, genuinely — the old town's architecture, Delos' ancient history and the island's quieter northern beaches all stand on their own beyond the party reputation.

Is Mykonos expensive?

Yes, genuinely among Greece's most expensive islands, particularly for beach club sunbeds and accommodation in July and August.

What is the best time of year to visit?

May to June and September offer warm weather with meaningfully lower crowds and prices than July and August, which are genuinely the island's busiest, most expensive party months.

Is Mykonos suitable for a quieter, non-party trip?

Yes, genuinely — basing away from the main party beaches, visiting in shoulder season, and focusing on the old town, Delos and quieter beaches all make for a considerably calmer visit.
