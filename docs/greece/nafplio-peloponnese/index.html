Welcome to Nafplio and the Peloponnese

Nafplio was modern Greece's first capital after independence, and its genuinely elegant old town still reflects that brief prominence — Venetian and neoclassical architecture, a hilltop fortress and the small island castle of Bourtzi sitting directly in the harbour, widely considered one of the most attractive towns in the Peloponnese.

The wider Peloponnese peninsula holds one of Greece's densest concentrations of major ancient sites within easy driving distance of Nafplio, including Mycenae, the legendary seat of Agamemnon, and Epidaurus, home to the ancient world's most acoustically celebrated theatre.

Together, Nafplio and its surrounding region offer a genuinely rewarding road trip through Greek history spanning the Bronze Age to the Venetian era, without the crowds or costs of the country's more famous island destinations.

Why visit

Why visit Nafplio and the Peloponnese?

Nafplio's case is that no other Peloponnese base pairs this elegant a town with this dense a concentration of major ancient sites nearby.

  • The old town is genuinely one of Greece's most elegant

    Venetian and neoclassical architecture, narrow lanes and a harbourside setting rank among the Peloponnese's most attractive towns.

  • Mycenae is genuinely one of Greece's great Bronze Age sites

    The legendary seat of Agamemnon offers real depth into Greece's history well before the classical era most visitors know.

  • Epidaurus's theatre is genuinely remarkable

    Its acoustics remain celebrated today, still used for performances more than two thousand years after construction.

  • It rewards a genuine road trip

    The wider Peloponnese offers real range beyond Nafplio itself, from mountain villages to further ancient sites.

  • It's genuinely less crowded than Greece's islands

    Comparable historic and scenic significance without the peak-season crowds or prices of Santorini or Mykonos.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Nafplio and the Peloponnese

Nafplio and the wider Peloponnese's essential sights, from its Venetian old town to two of Greece's most significant ancient sites.

  1. 01

    Nafplio's old town

    A genuinely elegant network of Venetian and neoclassical streets, its architecture reflecting the town's brief period as modern Greece's first capital.

    Tip The old town is genuinely at its best in early evening, when the day-trip crowds thin and the streets take on their own atmosphere.

  2. 02

    The Bourtzi fortress

    A small Venetian castle on its own island directly in Nafplio's harbour, genuinely among the most photographed sights in the Peloponnese.

    Tip A short boat ride from the harbour reaches the fortress itself — genuinely worth it for the view back toward the town.

  3. 03

    Palamidi fortress

    An imposing hilltop Venetian fortress above Nafplio, reached by 857 steps, with genuinely sweeping views across the town and Argolic Gulf.

    Tip Climb early morning to avoid both the heat and the crowds — the steps are genuinely demanding in midday summer sun.

  4. 04

    Ancient Mycenae

    The legendary seat of Agamemnon, home to the Lion Gate and beehive tombs, genuinely one of Greece's most significant Bronze Age sites.

    Tip The on-site museum genuinely rewards a visit alongside the ruins — the gold artefacts found here rank among Greece's finest.

  5. 05

    The Theatre of Epidaurus

    The ancient world's most acoustically celebrated theatre, genuinely still used for performances today more than two thousand years after construction.

    Tip Test the famous acoustics yourself — even a coin dropped at the centre stage is genuinely audible from the topmost seats.

  6. 06

    The Sanctuary of Asklepios at Epidaurus

    An ancient healing sanctuary surrounding the theatre, genuinely significant as one of the classical world's most important medical centres.

    Tip The sanctuary's ruins are genuinely worth the extra time beyond the theatre alone — most visitors rush past them.

Things to do

Best things to do in Nafplio and the Peloponnese

Beyond Nafplio's old town and the region's ancient sites, the Peloponnese rewards its coastline, its mountain villages and its genuinely serious food culture.

  1. Walk up to the Palamidi fortress

    A genuinely demanding but rewarding climb, its 857 steps leading to some of the best views in the entire Peloponnese.

    Tip Bring water — the climb offers genuinely little shade, particularly through its middle stretch.

  2. Take a boat out to the Bourtzi fortress

    A genuinely short, worthwhile excursion from Nafplio's harbour, offering a different perspective on the town from the water.

    Tip Boats depart regularly from the harbour promenade — no advance booking is genuinely necessary outside peak season.

  3. See a performance at Epidaurus, if timing allows

    The ancient theatre genuinely still hosts a summer festival of performances, an extraordinary way to experience the site beyond a daytime visit.

    Tip Check the festival schedule well ahead — performances genuinely sell out for popular productions.

  4. Explore the Argolic coastline

    The coast around Nafplio holds genuinely pleasant beach towns and swimming spots, a quieter complement to the region's historic sites.

    Tip Karathona beach, a short walk from Nafplio's old town, offers a genuinely convenient swim without needing a car.

  5. Visit a Peloponnese mountain village

    The region's interior holds genuinely traditional villages, offering a rural counterpoint to Nafplio's Venetian elegance and the coast's beach towns.

    Tip Villages around the wider Argolis region genuinely reward a slow drive without a fixed itinerary.

  6. Try the region's olive oil and wine

    The Peloponnese is genuinely one of Greece's most productive olive oil and wine regions, with real depth beyond the more famous Santorini or Naoussa labels.

    Tip Several small producers around Nafplio and the wider Argolis offer genuinely worthwhile tastings.

Local food

What to eat in Nafplio and the Peloponnese

The Peloponnese's food culture draws on genuinely some of Greece's best olive oil, citrus and produce, alongside Nafplio's own specialities.

  • Peloponnese olive oil

    Among Greece's most productive olive oil regions, genuinely central to the area's cuisine and among the country's most respected.

    Where to try it Local producers across the region, several offering direct tastings and sales.

  • Argolis citrus

    The Argolis plain's orange and citrus groves are genuinely significant to the region's produce, visible along much of the drive into Nafplio.

    Where to try it Roadside stalls and markets across the Argolis plain, particularly in season.

  • Nafplio's amygdalota

    Almond sweets genuinely associated with Nafplio's confectionery tradition, often sold in the old town's small sweet shops.

    Where to try it Traditional sweet shops throughout Nafplio's old town.

  • Grilled octopus and fresh seafood

    A genuine Nafplio harbourside staple, given the town's coastal setting on the Argolic Gulf.

    Where to try it Harbourside tavernas in Nafplio, particularly along the waterfront promenade.

  • Peloponnese wine

    A genuinely significant Greek wine region, particularly Nemea's reds, made from the local Agiorgitiko grape.

    Where to try it Wine lists throughout Nafplio, and wineries directly in the Nemea region.

  • Loukoumades

    Warm, honey-soaked fried dough puffs, a genuinely popular Greek dessert served across Nafplio's cafes and old town restaurants.

    Where to try it Cafes throughout Nafplio's old town, particularly around the main squares.

Transport

Getting to and around Nafplio and the Peloponnese

Nafplio sits within easy driving distance of Athens, without its own airport.

Getting there

  • Car from Athens

    A road trip of around 2 hours from Athens, genuinely the most flexible way to explore Nafplio and the wider Peloponnese together.

    The modern toll motorway makes this a genuinely straightforward, scenic drive.

  • Bus from Athens

    Direct KTEL buses from Athens reach Nafplio in around 2.5 hours, a genuinely straightforward option without a car.

    Reaching Mycenae or Epidaurus without a car afterward is genuinely more limited — consider a local tour or taxi.

  • Organised day tour

    Popular day tours from Athens combine Nafplio, Mycenae and Epidaurus, typically as a long single day.

    A day tour suits visitors who want the region's highlights covered without renting a car.

Getting around

Nafplio's old town is genuinely walkable, with a rental car considerably improving access to Mycenae, Epidaurus and the wider region.

  • Walking

    Nafplio's old town, harbour and the base of the Palamidi climb are all within genuinely easy walking distance of one another.

    The Palamidi climb itself involves real elevation change — comfortable shoes matter.

  • Rental car

    The most flexible way to reach Mycenae, Epidaurus and the wider Peloponnese's mountain villages and coastline.

    Roads between sites are generally in genuinely good condition, with clear signage to major attractions.

  • Local taxi or organised tour

    A genuinely convenient option for Mycenae and Epidaurus without a rental car, with local operators offering half-day circuits.

    Taxis can usually be arranged through your accommodation for a set half-day rate.

When to go

Best time to visit Nafplio and the Peloponnese

The Peloponnese's ancient sites offer genuinely little shade, and shoulder season provides the best balance of comfortable walking weather and thinner crowds.

  • Spring (April–June)

    Best time

    Mild temperatures and green countryside, genuinely the most comfortable season for visiting Mycenae and Epidaurus.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Hot and reliably sunny, with the Epidaurus Festival running, but also genuinely the region's most crowded, hottest months.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Comfortable temperatures and noticeably thinner crowds than summer, with clear light well suited to photography.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Shoulder

    Mild by northern European standards, genuinely quiet, though some coastal businesses reduce hours outside peak season.

Worth planning around: Peak midsummer midday heat at Mycenae and Epidaurus — both sites offer genuinely little shade across their exposed ruins.

Travel tips

Nafplio and the Peloponnese travel tips

Practical notes on timing, transport and etiquette that make a real difference to a Nafplio and Peloponnese trip.

  • Rent a car for at least part of the trip — Mycenae, Epidaurus and the wider region genuinely reward the flexibility.
  • Climb the Palamidi fortress early morning to avoid both the heat and the steepest crowds.
  • Check the Epidaurus Festival schedule ahead if a performance interests you — popular shows genuinely sell out.
  • Wear proper shoes for Mycenae and Epidaurus — both involve real elevation change and uneven ancient stonework.
  • Book Nafplio accommodation ahead for summer weekends, when Athenians themselves flock to the town.
  • Combine Mycenae and Epidaurus in one day if time is limited — both sit within a similar driving radius of Nafplio.
  • Allow real time for the Bourtzi boat trip and Karathona beach — Nafplio rewards more than a rushed history-only visit.

FAQ

Nafplio and the Peloponnese: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Nafplio and the Peloponnese?

Four works well — enough for Nafplio's old town, Mycenae, Epidaurus and at least one slower day on the coast or in the countryside.

Can Nafplio be visited as a day trip from Athens?

Yes, though it makes for a genuinely long day — an overnight stay allows Mycenae and Epidaurus to be properly explored without rushing.

Do I need a car in the Peloponnese?

Highly recommended — Nafplio itself is walkable, but reaching Mycenae, Epidaurus and the wider region genuinely benefits from a rental car.

What is the best time of year to visit?

April to June and September to October offer mild weather and noticeably thinner crowds than the hot, busier July-August peak.

Is Nafplio worth visiting beyond its ancient sites?

Genuinely, yes — the old town's Venetian architecture, harbour and Palamidi fortress rank among the Peloponnese's best sights in their own right.
