Welcome to Athens

Athens gave the world democracy, theatre, and much of the philosophical foundation Western thought still stands on, and the city's ancient monuments remain genuinely central to daily life rather than roped off as museum pieces. The Acropolis rises directly above the modern city's rooftops, visible from countless rooftop bars and apartment balconies going about ordinary business beneath it.

The city's more recent history is genuinely more layered than the ancient postcard suggests — Ottoman rule, a 1834 rebuilding as the capital of a newly independent Greek state, and a 20th century of rapid, sometimes chaotic growth have all left their mark on a city that can feel simultaneously ancient and unpolished.

That mix is precisely what visitors consistently find compelling: genuinely world-class ancient sites within walking distance of graffiti-covered streets, excellent contemporary restaurants and a nightlife scene that runs considerably later than most European capitals.

Why visit

Why visit Athens?

Athens' case is that no other capital holds this much ancient history standing directly within a genuinely lived-in modern city.

  • The Acropolis is genuinely one of the world's great monuments

    Standing among the Parthenon's columns delivers on the reputation in a way photographs alone never quite capture.

  • The ancient sites are genuinely woven into daily life

    Ruins sit directly among residential streets and metro stations rather than sealed away from the modern city.

  • The food scene is genuinely excellent

    From neighbourhood tavernas to an increasingly serious modern restaurant scene, Athens rewards eating well at every budget.

  • It's genuinely well connected for onward travel

    Piraeus port and Athens' airport make the city the natural gateway to virtually every Greek island.

  • It works as both a destination and a base

    A genuinely complete trip on its own, and the practical starting point for island-hopping or mainland day trips.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Athens

Athens' essential sights, spanning the Acropolis and its surrounding ancient city.

  1. 01

    The Acropolis and Parthenon

    The ancient citadel crowning the city, the Parthenon's columns standing since the 5th century BC as one of history's most significant surviving temples.

    Tip Book timed-entry tickets online well ahead in summer, and arrive at opening time to beat both heat and crowds.

  2. 02

    The Acropolis Museum

    A genuinely excellent modern museum built directly over active excavations, housing artefacts from the Acropolis with a glass floor revealing ruins below.

    Tip The top floor's Parthenon Gallery, aligned with the actual temple's orientation, is genuinely worth lingering in.

  3. 03

    The Ancient Agora

    The genuine marketplace and civic heart of ancient Athens, where democracy itself was practiced, with the well-preserved Temple of Hephaestus at its edge.

    Tip The Agora is genuinely less crowded than the Acropolis and rewards an unhurried wander among its scattered ruins.

  4. 04

    Plaka neighbourhood

    Athens' oldest surviving neighbourhood, its narrow lanes and neoclassical buildings genuinely charming despite sitting directly beneath the tourist-heavy Acropolis slopes.

    Tip Anafiotika, a tiny Cycladic-style enclave tucked within Plaka, is genuinely worth seeking out for its whitewashed, island-like lanes.

  5. 05

    The National Archaeological Museum

    Greece's largest archaeological museum, its collection of ancient artefacts, including the Mask of Agamemnon, genuinely rivalling any museum in the world for this period.

    Tip Allow at least two hours — the collection is genuinely vast and easy to underestimate.

  6. 06

    Mount Lycabettus

    Athens' highest hill, reachable by funicular or a genuinely rewarding hike, offering sweeping views over the entire city and the Acropolis from a distance.

    Tip Sunset from the summit is genuinely spectacular, with the Acropolis visible across the city as the light fades.

Things to do

Best things to do in Athens

Beyond the ancient sites, Athens rewards its neighbourhood contrasts and its genuinely serious food and nightlife culture.

  1. Wander Monastiraki and Psiri

    A genuinely lively flea market district and adjacent nightlife neighbourhood, packed with small bars, street food and vintage shops.

    Tip The Sunday flea market around Avissynias Square is genuinely worth timing a visit around, if your dates allow it.

  2. Eat at a neighbourhood taverna

    A long, unhurried meal of shared mezze and grilled dishes at a genuine local taverna is essential to understanding Athenian food culture.

    Tip Koukaki and Exarchia both hold genuinely excellent, less touristy taverna options than central Plaka.

  3. Take a day trip to Cape Sounion

    The Temple of Poseidon's dramatic clifftop ruins, a genuinely worthwhile sunset day trip about an hour from central Athens.

    Tip Time the drive to arrive for sunset — the temple's columns silhouetted against the sea is a genuinely memorable sight.

  4. Explore Athens' street art scene

    Exarchia and parts of Psiri hold genuinely striking large-scale murals and street art, a distinctly modern counterpoint to the ancient sites.

    Tip A dedicated walking tour offers genuine context on the political and artistic movements behind the city's street art.

  5. Visit Piraeus for a genuine port-city evening

    Athens' historic port, a short metro ride away, offers genuinely good seafood tavernas around Mikrolimano's small harbour.

    Tip Mikrolimano's harbourside restaurants are genuinely worth the short trip out from central Athens for a relaxed dinner.

  6. Experience Athens' nightlife

    Rooftop bars with Acropolis views, late-night tavernas and a genuinely serious cocktail bar scene keep Athens' evenings running considerably later than most capitals.

    Tip Many rooftop bars require no reservation for a drink, only for dinner — a good way to enjoy the view without a full meal commitment.

Local food

What to eat in Athens

Athens' food scene spans genuine neighbourhood tavernas to an increasingly serious modern Greek restaurant movement.

  • Souvlaki and gyros

    Athens' defining fast food, grilled skewered meat or a rotisserie wrap, genuinely excellent and cheap throughout the city.

    Where to try it Long-running souvlaki shops around Monastiraki and Psiri, several open late into the night.

  • Mezze at a traditional taverna

    A shared spread of small dishes — dips, saganaki cheese, dolmades — genuinely central to how Athenians actually eat out.

    Where to try it Neighbourhood tavernas in Koukaki, Exarchia or Psiri, away from the most tourist-heavy Plaka strips.

  • Fresh fish in Piraeus or Mikrolimano

    Athens' port district offers genuinely excellent fresh seafood, priced by weight and grilled simply.

    Where to try it Mikrolimano's harbourside tavernas, a short metro ride from central Athens.

  • Loukoumades

    Small honey-soaked doughnuts, genuinely one of Athens' most beloved street desserts, often topped with cinnamon or crushed nuts.

    Where to try it Dedicated loukoumades shops around Monastiraki, several with decades of history.

  • Modern Greek cuisine

    A genuinely serious contemporary restaurant scene has developed in Athens, reinterpreting traditional Greek ingredients and dishes with real ambition.

    Where to try it A growing cluster of modern Greek restaurants in Koukaki and central Athens.

  • Greek coffee and frappé

    Traditional slow-brewed Greek coffee alongside the genuinely beloved cold frappé, an Athenian café culture institution regardless of season.

    Where to try it Neighbourhood kafeneio cafés throughout the city, a genuine daily ritual for locals.

Transport

Getting to and around Athens

Athens International Airport is Greece's main gateway, with a direct metro line into the city centre.

Getting there

  • Athens International Airport (ATH)

    The metro's Blue Line reaches central Athens in about 40 minutes, a genuinely reliable and affordable option.

    A dedicated airport express bus offers an alternative if your accommodation sits away from a metro stop.

  • Ferries from Piraeus

    Athens' port connects directly to Santorini, Mykonos and Crete, with high-speed and conventional options.

    Piraeus is reachable from central Athens by metro in about 25 minutes.

  • Intercity buses (KTEL)

    Reliable bus connections reach Delphi, Meteora and Nafplio from central Athens.

    Book ahead for popular routes in peak season rather than relying on walk-up availability.

Getting around

Central Athens is genuinely walkable, with an efficient metro covering the wider city and airport.

  • Walking

    The Acropolis, Plaka, Monastiraki and the Ancient Agora all sit within a genuinely compact, walkable central area.

    Comfortable shoes matter for the Acropolis's ancient marble paths, which can be genuinely slippery when worn smooth.

  • Metro

    A clean, efficient three-line system covers the city centre, Piraeus port and the airport directly.

    Several metro stations double as small archaeological displays, built around ruins uncovered during construction.

  • Taxis

    Affordable by European capital standards and useful late at night or for reaching Piraeus with luggage.

    Confirm the meter is running, or agree a fare in advance for longer or airport journeys.

When to go

Best time to visit Athens

Athens' summer heat runs genuinely intense, and spring and autumn offer the most comfortable balance of weather and crowds for exploring the ancient sites.

  • Spring (April–June)

    Best time

    Mild, increasingly warm weather, ideal for walking the ancient sites without the peak summer heat.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Shoulder

    Genuinely intense heat, often exceeding 35°C, alongside the year's biggest crowds at major sites.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Comfortable temperatures and thinning crowds, a genuinely excellent window for sightseeing.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Mild by northern European standards, with genuinely quiet major sites and lower accommodation prices.

Worth planning around: Midday hours at the Acropolis in July and August, when heat and crowds both peak — an early morning or late afternoon visit is genuinely more comfortable.

Travel tips

Athens travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to an Athens trip.

  • Book Acropolis tickets online well ahead in summer — walk-up queues can mean significant waiting in the heat.
  • Visit major ancient sites at opening time or late afternoon to avoid both the worst heat and the biggest crowds.
  • Wear comfortable, sturdy shoes — the Acropolis's ancient marble paths are genuinely slippery when worn smooth.
  • Carry water and sun protection in summer — shade is genuinely limited at most ancient sites.
  • Use the metro rather than driving — central Athens' traffic and parking are genuinely difficult.
  • Book ferries from Piraeus ahead if island-hopping onward in July or August, when popular routes sell out.
  • Explore beyond Plaka — Koukaki and Exarchia offer genuinely better value and a more local atmosphere.

FAQ

Athens: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Athens?

Three to four. Two covers the Acropolis and central ancient sites at a rushed pace; four allows Cape Sounion and deeper neighbourhood time without feeling hurried.

Is Athens a good base for island-hopping?

Yes, genuinely — Piraeus port and the airport both connect easily to Santorini, Mykonos, Crete and beyond, making Athens the natural start or end point for an island-hopping trip.

Is Athens safe for tourists?

Yes, very — violent crime against visitors is rare. The practical risk is pickpocketing in crowded areas like Monastiraki and on the metro, manageable with ordinary vigilance.

What is the best time of year to visit?

April to June and September to October offer the most comfortable weather for exploring ancient sites on foot. Summer is genuinely hot, often exceeding 35°C, and considerably more crowded.

Is Athens worth visiting beyond the Acropolis?

Yes, genuinely — the food scene, neighbourhood culture, museums and nightlife all stand on their own, and many visitors find the city's less touristy corners just as rewarding as the ancient monuments.
