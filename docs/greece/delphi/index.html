Welcome to Delphi

Delphi was the ancient Greek world's most important oracle site, home to the Pythia, whose pronouncements at the Temple of Apollo were sought by kings, generals and ordinary citizens alike for centuries, and were believed to come directly from the god Apollo himself.

The ancient Greeks considered Delphi the centre of the earth, marked by the omphalos stone still displayed at the site's museum, and the sanctuary's dramatic mountainside setting, spread across the slopes of Mount Parnassus above the Pleistos valley, genuinely reinforces why it held such mythological significance.

A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1987, Delphi's ruins today include the Temple of Apollo, a well-preserved ancient theatre and stadium, and an archaeological museum holding some of Greece's most significant ancient sculpture, all within a genuinely spectacular natural setting.

Why visit

Why visit Delphi?

Delphi's case is that no other ancient Greek site combines this much genuine mythological significance with this dramatic a natural setting.

  • It was genuinely the ancient world's most important oracle

    Kings and city-states across the Mediterranean sought the Pythia's pronouncements before major decisions for centuries.

  • The setting is genuinely spectacular

    The sanctuary spreads dramatically across Mount Parnassus's slopes above the Pleistos valley, a setting that reinforces its ancient reputation.

  • The museum holds genuinely significant sculpture

    The Charioteer of Delphi and other major ancient Greek artworks rank among the country's most important archaeological finds.

  • It's genuinely manageable as a focused visit

    Unlike Greece's larger sites, Delphi rewards a one- or two-day visit without demanding an extended stay.

  • It pairs naturally with wider central Greece

    Delphi combines genuinely well with Meteora or Athens on a central Greece itinerary.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Delphi

Delphi's essential sights, from its famous oracle temple to its well-preserved theatre and significant archaeological museum.

  1. 01

    The Temple of Apollo

    The sanctuary's centrepiece, where the Pythia delivered her oracular pronouncements, its standing columns still genuinely evocative of the site's ancient significance.

    Tip Visit early morning for both softer light and considerably thinner crowds around the temple itself.

  2. 02

    The Ancient Theatre of Delphi

    A genuinely well-preserved 4th-century BC theatre above the Temple of Apollo, offering sweeping views back down across the entire sanctuary.

    Tip The climb up to the theatre is genuinely worth the effort for the view alone, even without knowledge of its performance history.

  3. 03

    The Delphi Archaeological Museum

    Home to the Charioteer of Delphi and other genuinely significant ancient Greek sculpture recovered from the sanctuary over more than a century of excavation.

    Tip Visit the museum before the ruins themselves — the context genuinely enriches what you'll see walking through the site.

  4. 04

    The Ancient Stadium

    A remarkably intact stadium above the theatre, once host to the Pythian Games, one of the four great Panhellenic athletic festivals of ancient Greece.

    Tip It's a genuinely further climb above the theatre — allow extra time and water, particularly in warmer months.

  5. 05

    The Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia

    A separate, genuinely picturesque sanctuary a short walk from the main site, home to the circular Tholos, one of Delphi's most photographed structures.

    Tip The Tholos's function remains genuinely uncertain to archaeologists — part of its appeal is the enduring mystery.

  6. 06

    The Castalian Spring

    A natural spring where ancient visitors and the Pythia herself were said to ritually purify before approaching the sanctuary, still marked along the roadside today.

    Tip The spring sits directly along the road between the two sanctuary areas — genuinely easy to combine with either visit.

Things to do

Best things to do in Delphi

Beyond the ruins themselves, Delphi rewards its mountain setting, its museum and the wider region's mountain villages.

  1. Walk the full sanctuary from bottom to top

    A genuinely rewarding route following the ancient Sacred Way up through the Temple of Apollo to the theatre and stadium above.

    Tip Wear proper shoes — the ancient paving and considerable elevation change are genuinely demanding in places.

  2. Spend real time in the archaeological museum

    The museum's collection is genuinely significant enough to reward an hour or more, not just a quick pass-through before the ruins.

    Tip The Charioteer of Delphi, in its own dedicated room, is genuinely worth lingering over rather than rushing past.

  3. Take in the view from the Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia

    A genuinely quieter, less crowded corner of the site, its circular Tholos ranking among the most photographed structures in Greece.

    Tip Late afternoon light genuinely flatters the Tholos's warm stone columns for photography.

  4. Visit Arachova, a mountain village nearby

    A genuinely charming stone-built mountain village a short drive from Delphi, known for its own distinctive cheese and winter tourism.

    Tip Arachova gets genuinely busy on winter weekends with skiers heading to nearby Mount Parnassus — visit midweek for a quieter experience.

  5. Drive or hike part of Mount Parnassus

    The mountain that gave Delphi its mythological weight offers genuinely rewarding hiking and, in winter, skiing above the ancient site.

    Tip Mountain weather changes genuinely quickly — check conditions before setting out on any significant hike.

  6. Watch sunset over the Pleistos valley

    The view from Delphi's modern town down across the valley and toward the Gulf of Corinth is genuinely spectacular at sunset.

    Tip Several tavernas in Delphi town have terraces genuinely positioned to catch this view with dinner.

Local food

What to eat in Delphi

Delphi's mountain setting in Central Greece brings its own distinct food traditions, from Arachova's cheese to hearty mountain cooking.

  • Formaella cheese

    A protected, distinctive semi-hard cheese from nearby Arachova, genuinely one of Greece's most respected regional cheeses, often served fried.

    Where to try it Tavernas in both Delphi and Arachova, and cheese shops in Arachova itself.

  • Wild greens (horta)

    Foraged mountain greens, boiled and dressed simply with olive oil and lemon, a genuine staple of Central Greek mountain cooking.

    Where to try it Traditional tavernas throughout Delphi and the surrounding villages.

  • Grilled mountain lamb

    Lamb from the surrounding mountain pastures, genuinely central to the region's traditional cooking, often served simply grilled or slow-roasted.

    Where to try it Traditional tavernas in Delphi and Arachova, particularly for a special meal.

  • Local mountain wine

    Wines from vineyards on the slopes around Mount Parnassus, genuinely distinct from the better-known wine regions further south.

    Where to try it Tavernas throughout Delphi, several offering wine from small regional producers.

  • Trahana with formaella

    A hearty mountain dish combining trahana grain with Arachova's own formaella cheese, genuinely representative of the region's cold-weather cooking.

    Where to try it Traditional tavernas in Arachova, particularly through the cooler months.

  • Honey and mountain herbs

    Central Greece's mountain slopes produce genuinely excellent honey and wild herbs, both widely sold and used across local cooking.

    Where to try it Small shops in Delphi and Arachova, and traditional tavernas across the region.

Transport

Getting to and around Delphi

Delphi sits in Central Greece, most commonly reached by car or organised tour from Athens without its own airport.

Getting there

  • Car from Athens

    A road trip of around 2.5 hours from Athens, genuinely the most flexible way to combine Delphi with Arachova or the wider region.

    The final approach through the mountains offers genuinely dramatic views before arriving at the site.

  • Bus from Athens

    Direct KTEL buses from Athens reach Delphi in around 3 hours, a genuinely straightforward option without a car.

    Check current timetables ahead, as service frequency can be genuinely limited outside peak season.

  • Organised day tour

    Popular day tours run from Athens, typically covering the main site and museum in a long single day.

    A day tour suits visitors who want Delphi covered without renting a car or managing bus timetables.

Getting around

Delphi's ancient site and modern town are both genuinely walkable, with a car or taxi useful for Arachova and the wider region.

  • Walking

    The ancient site, museum and Delphi town are all within genuinely easy walking distance of one another.

    The site itself involves real elevation change — comfortable shoes matter more than distance.

  • Rental car

    The most flexible way to reach Arachova, the Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia and wider Mount Parnassus.

    Mountain roads are genuinely narrow in places — allow more time than GPS estimates suggest.

  • Local taxi

    A genuinely convenient option for the short hop to Arachova or the Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia without a rental car.

    Taxis can usually be arranged through your accommodation in Delphi town.

When to go

Best time to visit Delphi

Delphi's exposed mountainside site can be genuinely uncomfortable in peak summer heat, and shoulder season offers the best balance of weather and thinner crowds.

  • Spring (April–June)

    Best time

    Mild temperatures and green mountain slopes, genuinely the most comfortable season for walking the full site.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Hot and reliably sunny, but also the site's most crowded months, with midday heat genuinely intense on the exposed hillside.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Comfortable temperatures and noticeably thinner crowds than summer, with clear mountain light well suited to photography.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Shoulder

    Cold and occasionally snowy, though Arachova genuinely livens up considerably as a ski-season base for nearby Mount Parnassus.

Worth planning around: Peak midsummer midday heat when walking the full site — the exposed hillside paths offer genuinely little shade.

Travel tips

Delphi travel tips

Practical notes on timing, transport and etiquette that make a real difference to a Delphi visit.

  • Visit the museum before the ruins — the context genuinely improves what you'll understand walking through the site.
  • Wear proper shoes — the ancient paths and stadium climb involve real elevation change and uneven stone.
  • Visit early morning in peak summer to avoid both the heat and the busiest tour bus hours.
  • Combine Delphi with Arachova for a genuinely fuller sense of the region beyond the ancient site alone.
  • Book a rental car if visiting independently — bus schedules to Arachova and beyond are genuinely limited.
  • Check Mount Parnassus weather before any significant hike — mountain conditions change genuinely quickly.
  • Consider a one-day trip from Athens only if time is genuinely tight — an overnight stay allows a considerably fuller visit.

FAQ

Delphi: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Delphi?

One focused day covers the essentials, but two days allow a genuinely fuller visit including Arachova and the Sanctuary of Athena Pronaia.

Can Delphi be visited as a day trip from Athens?

Yes, and it's a genuinely popular option, though it makes for a long day — an overnight stay allows more time at the site itself.

Is Delphi worth visiting if I'm not especially interested in ancient history?

Genuinely, yes — the mountainside setting and views alone are spectacular, even setting aside the site's historic significance.

Do I need a car to visit Delphi?

Helpful but not essential — buses and organised tours from Athens cover the main site well, though a car helps considerably for Arachova and the wider region.

What is the best time of year to visit?

April to June and September to October offer mild weather and noticeably thinner crowds than the hot, busier summer months.
