Welcome to Thessaloniki

Thessaloniki is Greece's second-largest city and, for many Greeks themselves, its most genuinely underrated — a layered Byzantine, Ottoman and Sephardic Jewish crossroads that receives a fraction of the international attention paid to Athens, despite comparable historic depth and, by local consensus, a stronger food and nightlife scene.

The city's UNESCO-listed Byzantine monuments, including the Rotunda and the Church of Saint Demetrios, reflect its centuries as one of the Byzantine Empire's most important cities, second only to Constantinople itself, while its Ottoman and Sephardic Jewish history added further genuine depth before the population exchanges and Holocaust of the 20th century reshaped the city.

Today, Thessaloniki is genuinely known among Greeks as a food and student city, its waterfront promenade, Ano Poli old town and youthful energy giving it a noticeably different character from the more monument-focused, tourist-heavy atmosphere of Athens.

Why visit

Why visit Thessaloniki?

Thessaloniki's case is that it offers genuinely comparable historic depth to Athens with a fraction of the crowds and, by wide local consensus, better food.

  • The Byzantine heritage is genuinely significant

    UNESCO-listed churches and monuments reflect centuries as the Byzantine Empire's second city after Constantinople.

  • The food scene is genuinely exceptional

    Many Greeks themselves rate Thessaloniki's food culture above Athens's, shaped by Ottoman, Sephardic and Balkan influence.

  • It's genuinely less crowded with tourists

    Comparable historic significance to Athens without the same volume of international visitors, particularly outside summer.

  • The nightlife and student energy are genuinely real

    A large student population gives Thessaloniki a youthful, lively atmosphere distinct from Athens's own.

  • It makes a genuinely good base for northern Greece

    Meteora, Halkidiki's beaches and the wider Macedonia region all sit within comfortable reach.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Thessaloniki

Thessaloniki's essential sights, from its Byzantine churches to its Ottoman-era landmarks and waterfront promenade.

  1. 01

    The White Tower

    The city's defining landmark, a genuinely striking waterfront tower now housing a museum on Thessaloniki's history, anchoring the seafront promenade.

    Tip Climb to the top for genuinely excellent views back across the city and the Thermaic Gulf.

  2. 02

    The Rotunda

    A genuinely remarkable Roman-era rotunda, later converted into a Byzantine church and Ottoman mosque, its layered history reflected in its surviving mosaics.

    Tip The interior mosaics are genuinely among the finest surviving examples of early Byzantine art — worth real time inside.

  3. 03

    The Church of Saint Demetrios

    Thessaloniki's patron saint's basilica, genuinely one of the largest churches in Greece, holding significant early Byzantine mosaics and a crypt beneath.

    Tip The crypt below the main church is genuinely worth the extra visit, holding archaeological remains from earlier structures on the site.

  4. 04

    Ano Poli, the old town

    Thessaloniki's genuinely atmospheric upper town, its Ottoman-era houses and narrow lanes surviving the fires and redevelopment that reshaped the lower city.

    Tip Ano Poli's Byzantine walls offer genuinely excellent sunset views back down across the city and gulf.

  5. 05

    The Arch of Galerius and Rotunda complex

    A genuinely significant Roman triumphal arch marking the entrance to the imperial palace complex of Emperor Galerius.

    Tip The arch sits directly on a busy modern street — a genuinely striking juxtaposition of ancient and contemporary Thessaloniki.

  6. 06

    The Archaeological Museum of Thessaloniki

    A genuinely significant collection spanning prehistoric to Roman-era Macedonia, including major finds from the royal tombs at nearby Vergina.

    Tip The Vergina gold artefacts are genuinely a highlight — among Greece's most significant archaeological finds of the 20th century.

Things to do

Best things to do in Thessaloniki

Beyond the Byzantine churches and museums, Thessaloniki rewards its waterfront, its old town and its genuinely serious food and coffee culture.

  1. Walk the waterfront promenade

    A genuinely pleasant seafront walk connecting the White Tower to the wider city, popular with locals for an evening stroll.

    Tip Early evening genuinely draws the biggest local crowds, walking, cycling and gathering along the water.

  2. Explore Ano Poli's lanes

    A genuinely rewarding wander through Thessaloniki's old town, its Ottoman-era houses and Byzantine walls offering a different pace from the busy lower city.

    Tip Several tavernas in Ano Poli offer genuinely excellent views alongside good, unpretentious food.

  3. Go café-hopping in the city centre

    Thessaloniki's genuinely serious coffee culture rivals any Greek city, its student population supporting a real density of cafes.

    Tip Ladadika and the area around Aristotelous Square hold a genuinely high concentration of well-regarded cafes.

  4. Eat your way through the Modiano and Kapani markets

    Thessaloniki's genuinely vibrant covered markets offer real insight into the city's food culture, from produce to prepared street food.

    Tip Visit hungry — the markets are genuinely full of small stalls worth sampling rather than a single sit-down meal.

  5. Learn about the city's Sephardic Jewish history

    Thessaloniki was once genuinely one of the world's great Sephardic Jewish centres — the Jewish Museum of Thessaloniki tells this significant, largely lost history.

    Tip The museum genuinely rewards real time — the city's pre-war Jewish population and its devastating loss are essential context for understanding modern Thessaloniki.

  6. Experience Thessaloniki's nightlife

    A genuinely large student population gives the city a livelier, later nightlife scene than much of Greece, particularly around Ladadika and Valaoritou.

    Tip Thessaloniki nights genuinely start later than you might expect — venues fill up well after midnight on weekends.

Local food

What to eat in Thessaloniki

Thessaloniki's food culture is genuinely considered among Greece's best, shaped by Ottoman, Sephardic and Balkan influence alongside its own specialities.

  • Bougatsa

    A genuine Thessaloniki breakfast institution, a filo pastry filled with sweet custard, cheese or minced meat, best eaten fresh and warm.

    Where to try it Dedicated bougatsa shops across the city, several open since early morning specifically for this purpose.

  • Trigona Panoramatos

    Triangular pastries filled with custard, a genuine Thessaloniki specialty named for the suburb of Panorama where they originated.

    Where to try it Pastry shops throughout the city, particularly those specialising in the original Panorama recipe.

  • Thessaloniki-style souvlaki

    A genuinely distinct local style, typically wrapped with fries directly inside the pita, differing from souvlaki traditions elsewhere in Greece.

    Where to try it Souvlaki spots throughout the city centre, a genuine local institution at any hour.

  • Tsipouro with mezedes

    Thessaloniki's own strong grape spirit culture, genuinely central to the city's social eating, traditionally served alongside a spread of small dishes.

    Where to try it Traditional ouzeri and tsipouradika throughout the city, particularly in Ladadika.

  • Sephardic Jewish-influenced dishes

    Thessaloniki's Sephardic Jewish heritage genuinely shaped several local specialities, reflecting the city's once-significant Jewish population.

    Where to try it A small number of restaurants and bakeries preserving these traditions, worth seeking out for their historic significance.

  • Thessaloniki-style koulouri

    Sesame-crusted bread rings sold from street carts across the city, a genuinely ubiquitous, beloved Thessaloniki breakfast or snack.

    Where to try it Street carts throughout the city centre, sold fresh from early morning.

Transport

Getting to and around Thessaloniki

Thessaloniki has its own international airport, with train and bus connections to Athens and the wider region.

Getting there

  • Flight from Athens

    A short flight from Athens reaches Thessaloniki in about an hour, the fastest option from the Greek capital.

    Book ahead in peak season, when popular flight times can sell out or spike in price.

  • Train from Athens

    Direct trains from Athens reach Thessaloniki in around 4 hours, a genuinely comfortable, scenic alternative to flying.

    Book ahead for the fastest direct services, which can sell out on popular travel dates.

  • Car from Meteora or Athens

    A road trip of around 2.5 hours from Meteora or 5 hours from Athens, offering genuine flexibility for combining destinations.

    The motorway connection to Athens is genuinely fast and well maintained for a long single-day drive if needed.

Getting around

Thessaloniki's city centre is genuinely walkable, with buses and a metro covering the wider city.

  • Walking

    The waterfront, White Tower, Aristotelous Square and most major sights sit within genuinely easy walking distance of one another.

    Ano Poli involves real elevation change from the lower city — comfortable shoes help for the climb.

  • Metro

    Thessaloniki's newly opened metro connects the city centre to outer districts, a genuinely welcome addition to the city's transport.

    The metro network remains genuinely limited in coverage compared to Athens's — walking still suits most central sightseeing.

  • Local buses

    A reasonably comprehensive network covers areas the metro doesn't yet reach, including access to the airport.

    Buy tickets ahead at kiosks or via the local transport app — validating on board is genuinely required.

When to go

Best time to visit Thessaloniki

Thessaloniki's summer heat runs genuinely hot and humid, and shoulder season offers the best balance of comfortable walking weather and thinner crowds.

  • Spring (April–June)

    Best time

    Mild, increasingly warm weather, genuinely comfortable for walking the waterfront and Ano Poli's hills.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Hot and humid, with the waterfront and outdoor cafes genuinely still lively, but midday heat considerably intense.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Comfortable temperatures and a genuinely pleasant return of the city's student energy as the academic year begins.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Shoulder

    Cold and occasionally rainy by Greek standards, though the city's cafe and restaurant culture genuinely continues year-round.

Worth planning around: Peak midsummer humidity if walking extensively — Thessaloniki's coastal position makes summer heat genuinely more humid than much of inland Greece.

Itinerary

Three days in Thessaloniki

The waterfront and Byzantine landmarks first, then Ano Poli and the markets, then a slower day of food and museums.

  1. Day1

    The waterfront and city centre

    Thessaloniki's defining landmarks.

    • Morning The White Tower and the waterfront promenade.
    • Afternoon The Rotunda and the Arch of Galerius.
    • Evening Dinner and drinks in Ladadika.
  2. Day2

    Ano Poli and the markets

    The old town and the city's food culture.

    • Morning Explore Ano Poli's lanes and Byzantine walls.
    • Afternoon The Modiano and Kapani markets.
    • Evening Souvlaki and tsipouro with mezedes.
  3. Day3

    Museums and Byzantine history

    A slower day of deeper history.

    • Morning The Archaeological Museum of Thessaloniki.
    • Afternoon The Church of Saint Demetrios and the Jewish Museum of Thessaloniki.
    • Evening A final dinner in the city centre, saving bougatsa for one last breakfast the next morning.

Travel tips

Thessaloniki travel tips

Practical notes on timing, transport and etiquette that make a real difference to a Thessaloniki trip.

  • Eat bougatsa fresh in the morning — it's genuinely a breakfast institution, not an all-day snack.
  • Visit the Jewish Museum of Thessaloniki for genuinely essential context on the city's pre-war history.
  • Wear comfortable shoes for Ano Poli — the old town's hills involve real elevation change from the lower city.
  • Go out later than you might expect — Thessaloniki's nightlife genuinely starts well after most Greek cities.
  • Visit the markets hungry — Modiano and Kapani genuinely reward grazing across several stalls.
  • Book train tickets from Athens ahead on popular dates — fast direct services can genuinely sell out.
  • Consider Thessaloniki as a genuine base for Meteora or Halkidiki, both within comfortable reach.

FAQ

Thessaloniki: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Thessaloniki?

Three works well — enough for the waterfront, Ano Poli, the major Byzantine sites and a real taste of the city's food scene.

Is Thessaloniki worth visiting instead of, or alongside, Athens?

Genuinely both are worth it if time allows — Thessaloniki offers comparable historic depth with fewer crowds and, by wide local consensus, better food.

Do I need a car in Thessaloniki?

Not for the city itself — the centre is genuinely walkable, though a car helps for day trips to Halkidiki or further into Macedonia.

What is the best time of year to visit?

April to June and September to October offer mild weather and comfortable walking conditions without summer's heat and humidity.

What is Thessaloniki known for?

Genuinely its food scene above all, alongside significant Byzantine monuments and a layered Ottoman and Sephardic Jewish history.
