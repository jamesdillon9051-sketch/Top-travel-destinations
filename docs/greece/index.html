Why go to Greece

Greece holds a genuinely disproportionate share of the ideas and monuments that shaped Western civilization — democracy, philosophy, theatre and the Olympic Games all trace directly back here, and the country's ancient sites remain standing, walkable and genuinely central to a visit rather than curated behind glass alone. The Acropolis, Delphi and Olympia are not just ruins but the actual physical settings where these ideas took shape.

That ancient inheritance sits alongside a genuinely different Greece scattered across more than 200 inhabited islands, each with its own distinct character — Santorini's volcanic caldera views, Mykonos' nightlife, Crete's scale and Corfu's Venetian-influenced architecture all coexist within ferry or short-flight reach of each other and the mainland.

The practical spine connecting all of this is a well-developed network of ferries and short domestic flights, alongside a food and hospitality culture — genuine philoxenia, the traditional obligation of welcoming strangers — that consistently exceeds what visitors expect even after reading about Greek hospitality in advance.

Experiences

Best things to do in Greece

The experiences that recur across the country and that make a Greece trip feel like more than a checklist of ruins and beaches.

  1. Island-hop by ferry

    Greece's ferry network genuinely makes combining several islands in one trip straightforward, from high-speed catamarans to slower, more scenic conventional ferries.

    Tip Book high-speed ferries ahead in peak summer season, when popular routes between the Cyclades islands can sell out.

  2. Eat a proper taverna meal

    A long, unhurried meal of shared mezze plates, grilled fish or meat and local wine, eaten at a genuine neighbourhood taverna rather than a tourist-strip restaurant.

    Tip A taverna filled with Greek families rather than tour groups is a reliable sign of quality and fair pricing.

  3. Watch the sunset from a caldera or hilltop

    Santorini's caldera is the most famous version, but genuinely spectacular sunset viewpoints exist across the islands and mainland alike.

    Tip Arrive at least 30 minutes ahead of sunset at Santorini's most famous viewpoints, which fill with crowds well before the sun actually sets.

  4. Visit an ancient site with real context

    Hiring a local guide or reading ahead genuinely transforms a visit to the Acropolis, Delphi or Olympia from a photo stop into something considerably more meaningful.

    Tip Visit major ancient sites at opening time in summer, both for the light and to beat the heat and the crowds.

  5. Swim in genuinely clear water

    Greece's beaches and coves offer some of the Mediterranean's clearest, bluest water, from organised beach clubs to quiet, undeveloped coves reachable only by boat or a short hike.

    Tip Sperlonga-style beach umbrella rental fees are common at organised beaches — bring your own gear if you'd rather avoid the charge.

  6. Try a Greek coffee or ouzo, properly

    Greek coffee, brewed slowly and served with the grounds settled at the bottom, and ouzo, sipped slowly with water and mezze, are both genuine social rituals rather than quick drinks.

    Tip Order Greek coffee 'metrio' for medium sweetness if you don't have a strong preference — it's the most commonly served default.

Food and drink

Greece foods to try

Greek food's international reputation as gyros and Greek salad undersells it badly. Genuine regional variety, fresh seafood and a deep olive oil and wine culture make the case on their own.

  • Moussaka

    Layered aubergine, spiced minced meat and a genuine béchamel top, a defining Greek comfort dish found on menus nationwide with real regional variation.

    Where to try it Traditional tavernas across the country, ideally made fresh rather than reheated from a steam tray.

  • Souvlaki and gyros

    Grilled skewered meat or a rotisserie wrap, genuinely Greece's most beloved everyday fast food, eaten standing up from a paper wrap or on a plate with fries.

    Where to try it Dedicated souvlaki shops nationwide — a queue of locals is a reliable sign of quality.

  • Fresh Greek seafood

    Grilled octopus, whole fish and shellfish, genuinely excellent around the islands and coastal towns, typically priced by weight.

    Where to try it Harbourside tavernas in fishing towns, where the day's catch is often on genuine display.

  • Greek salad (horiatiki)

    Tomatoes, cucumber, olives and a genuine slab of feta, dressed simply in olive oil, a dish that tastes considerably better in Greece than its export version ever suggests.

    Where to try it Any taverna nationwide, though tomato quality genuinely peaks in summer.

  • Greek wine

    A winemaking tradition dating back millennia, with genuinely distinct regional grape varieties like Assyrtiko from Santorini increasingly recognised internationally.

    Where to try it Island and mainland wineries offering tastings, and any taverna's house wine, often a genuinely good value option.

  • Baklava and Greek sweets

    Honey-soaked filo pastry desserts, alongside loukoumades (honey doughnuts) and other genuinely rich, syrup-soaked sweets shaped by centuries of Ottoman and Byzantine influence.

    Where to try it Traditional zaharoplasteio (sweet shops) nationwide, particularly strong in Thessaloniki.

  • Olive oil, seriously

    Greece is among the world's largest olive oil producers, and the genuine quality difference between a proper Greek olive oil and an export supermarket bottle is considerable.

    Where to try it Family-run producers across the Peloponnese and Crete, several offering tastings and direct sales.

When to go

Best time to visit Greece

Greece's summer heat and crowds peak hard in July and August, and late spring and early autumn offer the best balance of warm weather, open facilities and manageable crowds.

  • Spring (April–June)

    Best time

    Mild, increasingly warm weather with wildflowers across the countryside and crowds well below the July-August peak.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Hot, dry and the most reliable weather for swimming, but also genuinely the most crowded and expensive months, especially on the islands.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Warm sea temperatures lingering from summer, comfortable air temperatures and noticeably thinner crowds than August.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Shoulder

    Mild on the mainland but genuinely quiet on the islands, where many hotels, restaurants and ferry routes reduce service or close entirely.

Worth planning around: Mid-August without accommodation and ferry tickets booked far ahead — the Greek domestic August holiday period pushes the islands to their busiest, most expensive point of the year.

Trip length

How long to spend in Greece

Greece's ferry and flight network makes an ambitious island-and-mainland itinerary genuinely workable, though the Cyclades and Crete each reward more time than a rushed loop allows.

  • 4–5 days

    Athens alone fills four or five days, or Athens plus a single island like Santorini or Mykonos reached by short flight or ferry.

    A short Greece trip is better spent deeply in one or two places than spread thin across the islands.

  • 8–10 days

    Athens, then island-hop between Santorini and Mykonos — the classic first-trip route connecting ancient history and the Cyclades.

    Add a day in Delphi for a genuinely well-rounded route without extending the core itinerary much.

  • 12–16 days

    The itinerary on this page: Athens, Delphi, Meteora, then island-hopping through Santorini, Mykonos and Crete, covering both the mainland's ancient history and the islands' range.

    Enough time to add Nafplio and the Peloponnese or Thessaloniki to the classic Athens-and-islands route.

  • 3 weeks or more

    Add Rhodes, Corfu or a slower, multi-week island-hopping route through the Cyclades and Dodecanese.

    The outer islands and a genuinely unhurried ferry-hopping pace are where a second Greece trip belongs.

Getting around

Transportation in Greece

Greece's ferry network connects the islands in this guide, while domestic flights and a growing rail network cover the mainland.

  • Ferries

    Connect Athens' port of Piraeus to Santorini, Mykonos and Crete, with high-speed and conventional options on most routes.

    Book high-speed ferries ahead in July and August, when popular Cyclades routes can sell out days in advance.

  • Domestic flights

    Short flights from Athens reach Santorini, Mykonos, Crete, Rhodes and Corfu in under an hour, considerably faster than the ferry alternative.

    Book budget carrier fares a little ahead for genuinely low prices, particularly outside peak summer weekends.

  • Intercity buses (KTEL)

    A comprehensive network connects Athens to Delphi, Meteora, Nafplio and Thessaloniki reliably and affordably.

    Buy tickets a day ahead for popular routes in peak season rather than relying on walk-up availability.

  • Rental car

    Useful for exploring Crete&#39;s scale and the wider Peloponnese at a flexible pace beyond the main towns.

    An International Driving Permit is recommended, and mountain roads can be narrow and winding.

  • Athens Metro and local transport

    A clean, efficient system covering central Athens well, including a direct airport line.

    A combined ticket covers metro, bus and tram within the same fare period.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Greece

Greek culture varies genuinely between the mainland and the islands, but a handful of broadly shared norms — hospitality, a relaxed relationship with time, and a strong family and community focus — hold nationwide.

  • Philoxenia, hospitality to strangers, is genuinely taken seriously

    Greeks have a real cultural tradition of welcoming visitors warmly, and hospitality offered is generally meant sincerely rather than as a transaction.

  • Time runs more relaxed than in northern Europe

    Greek time ('siga siga', slowly slowly) means schedules and reservations are treated with more flexibility than visitors from punctuality-focused cultures may expect.

  • Meals are a genuine social occasion

    Dinner runs late by many countries' standards, often starting after 9pm, and is meant to be lingered over rather than rushed.

  • Church and tradition still hold real weight

    Greek Orthodox Christianity shapes daily life, holidays and etiquette even for visitors who aren't religious themselves — dress modestly when visiting churches and monasteries.

  • Directness and warmth coexist

    Greeks are often genuinely direct in conversation, which is not meant as rudeness — it typically comes paired with real warmth once you get past first impressions.

  • August is a genuine national holiday month

    Many Greek businesses close for part of August as owners take their own holidays — this is a real cultural institution, not poor planning.

Do

  • Dress modestly when visiting churches and monasteries — cover shoulders and knees
  • Greet with a warm hello and accept hospitality graciously when offered
  • Linger over meals rather than rushing — it's part of the social occasion
  • Learn a few basic Greek phrases — the effort is genuinely appreciated
  • Book ferries and island accommodation well ahead for July and August travel
  • Carry some cash for smaller island tavernas, kiosks and taxis

Don't

  • Don't rush a taverna meal — service is deliberately unhurried, not slow
  • Don't wear beach clothes into churches or monasteries
  • Don't assume every small island business takes cards — some remain cash-only
  • Don't be surprised if some businesses close for part of August
  • Don't skip travel insurance for ferry-dependent island itineraries — weather can genuinely delay crossings
  • Don't rent a moped without genuine experience and a proper licence — road conditions vary considerably by island

Travel tips

Greece travel tips

The things that most often go wrong on a Greece trip, in rough order of how much they cost people.

  • Book inter-island ferries and flights as early as possible for July and August travel — popular routes genuinely sell out.
  • Build a buffer day into ferry-dependent itineraries — weather can delay or cancel crossings, especially in shoulder season.
  • Carry some cash — smaller island tavernas, kiosks and taxis can be genuinely cash-preferring.
  • Book Santorini and Mykonos accommodation many months ahead for summer — both islands' rooms fill and prices spike early.
  • Dress modestly for churches and monasteries — cover shoulders and knees, particularly at Meteora.
  • Avoid renting a moped without real experience — road conditions and traffic vary considerably by island.
  • Pack for real heat in July and August, particularly on the Cyclades islands, and plan around the midday sun for sightseeing.
  • Reserve Acropolis tickets online well ahead in summer, when walk-up queues can mean significant waiting in the heat.

FAQ

Visiting Greece: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Greece?

Ten to fourteen for a first trip. Eight to ten covers Athens and two islands with reasonable pacing; twelve to sixteen lets you add Delphi and Meteora to the classic Athens-and-islands route, which is the itinerary on this page. Four or five days is enough for Athens alone, and Athens alone genuinely justifies a trip.

What is the best time of year to visit Greece?

May to June and September are the sweet spots — warm weather, manageable crowds and lower prices than peak summer. July and August bring the most reliable sunshine at the cost of genuine crowds and heat; winter is quiet but many island businesses close.

Do I need a car in Greece?

Only for specific regions. Ferries and flights cover the islands well, and Athens' metro covers the capital. A car becomes genuinely useful for exploring Crete's scale and the wider Peloponnese at a flexible pace.

Is Greece expensive?

Mid-range travel runs about €100–170 per person per day, with Santorini and Mykonos adding a genuine premium over that figure. Booking ferries and flights well ahead and eating at neighbourhood tavernas rather than tourist-strip restaurants are the biggest levers on cost.

Is Greece safe for tourists?

Very. Violent crime against visitors is rare, and most areas are safe to walk in the evening. The practical risk is pickpocketing in crowded tourist areas and on Athens' public transport, manageable with ordinary vigilance around bags and phones.

How does island-hopping actually work?

Book ferries or flights between your chosen islands ahead of time, particularly in summer, and build a buffer day or two into the itinerary in case of weather delays — most visitors combine two to four islands in a single trip rather than attempting more.
