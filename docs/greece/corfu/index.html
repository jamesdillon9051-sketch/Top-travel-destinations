Welcome to Corfu

Corfu's history sets it apart from most of Greece — centuries of Venetian rule, followed by periods of French and British administration, left the island with a genuinely distinct architectural and cultural character, never having fallen under direct Ottoman control the way much of the mainland and other islands did.

That history is most visible in Corfu's Old Town, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2007, its Venetian fortifications, Italianate architecture and the genuinely elegant Liston esplanade giving the island's capital an atmosphere unlike anywhere else in Greece.

Beyond the Old Town, Corfu is genuinely one of Greece's greenest islands, its interior covered in olive groves and cypress trees, with a coastline offering real range from dramatic cliffs to some of the country's most photographed beaches.

Why visit

Why visit Corfu?

Corfu's case is that no other Greek island combines this distinct a Venetian architectural heritage with this much genuine natural lushness.

  • The Old Town is genuinely unlike anywhere else in Greece

    Centuries of Venetian, French and British influence produced a UNESCO-listed old town with a genuinely distinct character.

  • The island is genuinely one of Greece's greenest

    Dense olive groves and cypress-covered hills set Corfu apart from the more arid landscapes of many other Greek islands.

  • The coastline offers real range

    Dramatic cliffs, quiet coves and some of Greece's most photographed beaches all sit within a single island.

  • The food culture is genuinely distinctive

    Venetian and Italian influence gives Corfu dishes genuinely found nowhere else in Greece, from pastitsada to sofrito.

  • It connects genuinely well to Italy and the wider Ionian

    Ferries reach Italy directly, alongside easy connections to Paxos and other Ionian islands.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Corfu

Corfu's essential sights, from its UNESCO-listed Venetian old town to its dramatic clifftop monastery and striking coastline.

  1. 01

    Corfu Old Town

    A genuinely well-preserved UNESCO World Heritage Site, its narrow Venetian lanes, Italianate buildings and elegant Liston esplanade unlike anywhere else in Greece.

    Tip Get genuinely lost in the old town's narrow kantounia lanes — much of its charm lies in unplanned wandering rather than a fixed route.

  2. 02

    The Old Fortress

    A genuinely imposing Venetian, and later British, fortress on its own promontory, offering sweeping views across the old town and Ionian Sea.

    Tip Visit late afternoon for genuinely softer light across the fortress walls and the sea beyond.

  3. 03

    The New Fortress

    A second, genuinely less-visited Venetian fortress above the old town, offering a quieter alternative view with real historic depth of its own.

    Tip The New Fortress draws genuinely far fewer visitors than the Old Fortress — a good choice if you prefer avoiding crowds.

  4. 04

    Achilleion Palace

    A genuinely striking 19th-century palace built for Empress Elisabeth of Austria, its gardens and classical statuary reflecting her fascination with Greek mythology.

    Tip The palace's gardens genuinely reward as much time as the interior — the statue of the dying Achilles is a particular highlight.

  5. 05

    Paleokastritsa

    A genuinely dramatic stretch of coastline on Corfu's west side, its turquoise coves backed by a clifftop monastery among the island's most photographed views.

    Tip Boat trips from Paleokastritsa's beach reach genuinely secluded coves inaccessible by road.

  6. 06

    Angelokastro

    A ruined Byzantine castle on a dramatic clifftop above Paleokastritsa, genuinely one of the most important Byzantine fortifications in the Ionian Islands.

    Tip The climb up is genuinely steep — reward yourself with the panoramic coastal views from the top.

Things to do

Best things to do in Corfu

Beyond the Old Town and its fortresses, Corfu rewards its beaches, its lush interior and its genuinely distinctive food culture.

  1. Wander the Old Town's kantounia lanes

    A genuinely rewarding way to experience Corfu's Venetian character, its narrow lanes hung with laundry lines opening onto small squares.

    Tip Morning light genuinely suits photography in the narrow lanes, before the day's heat and crowds build.

  2. Have a coffee on the Liston

    Corfu Old Town's genuinely elegant arcaded esplanade, modelled on Paris's Rue de Rivoli, remains the island's classic spot to sit and watch the world go by.

    Tip The Liston genuinely gets busy in the evening — an earlier coffee offers a quieter, equally atmospheric experience.

  3. Swim at Paleokastritsa or a west coast beach

    Corfu's west coast holds genuinely some of Greece's most striking turquoise coves, Paleokastritsa foremost among them.

    Tip Arrive early in peak season — Paleokastritsa's beaches genuinely fill quickly given their popularity.

  4. Take a boat trip to Paxos

    A genuinely worthwhile day trip to a smaller, quieter Ionian island, known for its own turquoise coves and traditional villages.

    Tip Boat trips typically include Antipaxos too — a genuinely beautiful smaller island with some of the Ionian's best beaches.

  5. Explore Corfu's olive groves and interior villages

    The island's genuinely lush interior, covered in centuries-old olive groves, holds traditional villages well away from the coastal resorts.

    Tip A rental car genuinely rewards exploring the interior at your own pace, away from fixed tour routes.

  6. Try Corfu's distinctive Venetian-influenced cuisine

    Corfu's food culture is genuinely unlike the rest of Greece, its Venetian influence producing dishes found nowhere else in the country.

    Tip Look specifically for pastitsada or sofrito on taverna menus — both are genuinely distinctive to the island.

Local food

What to eat in Corfu

Corfu's centuries of Venetian rule left the island with genuinely the most distinctive food culture of any Greek island.

  • Pastitsada

    A rich tomato and spice-based stew of meat and pasta, genuinely Corfu's signature dish and unlike anything found on the mainland.

    Where to try it Traditional tavernas throughout the Old Town and across the island.

  • Sofrito

    Veal slow-cooked in a garlic and white wine sauce, a genuinely distinctive Corfiot dish reflecting the island's Venetian culinary heritage.

    Where to try it Traditional tavernas across Corfu, particularly in the Old Town.

  • Bourdeto

    A spicy fish stew in a rich red pepper sauce, genuinely one of Corfu's most distinctive seafood dishes.

    Where to try it Taverns along the coast and in the Old Town, particularly those specialising in seafood.

  • Corfu's kumquat products

    Kumquats, introduced to Corfu in the 19th century, are genuinely now a local specialty, turned into liqueur, spoon sweets and marmalade found nowhere else in Greece.

    Where to try it Shops throughout the Old Town, many offering tastings of kumquat liqueur and preserves.

  • Corfu olive oil

    Among Greece's most respected, genuinely reflecting the island's centuries-old, exceptionally dense olive groves.

    Where to try it Local producers across the island, several offering direct tastings and sales.

  • Tsitsibira ginger beer

    A traditional Corfiot ginger beer, genuinely a legacy of the island's period of British administration, distinct from anywhere else in Greece.

    Where to try it Cafes and shops throughout the Old Town, particularly around the Liston.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Corfu

Corfu's right base depends on the balance you want between Old Town atmosphere and beach access.

  • Corfu Old Town

    The island's most atmospheric base, putting the fortresses, Liston and Old Town restaurants all within easy reach.

    Best for: Venetian atmosphere and walkable convenience

  • Paleokastritsa

    A genuinely striking coastal base, trading Old Town convenience for direct access to Corfu's most photographed coves.

    Best for: West coast beach access

  • Kassiopi or the northeast coast

    Genuinely calmer resort towns offering direct beach access away from the Old Town's crowds.

    Best for: A quieter beach-focused stay

Transport

Getting to and around Corfu

Corfu has its own international airport, with ferries also connecting the island to Italy and the mainland.

Getting there

  • Flight from Athens

    A short flight from Athens reaches Corfu in about an hour, the fastest option from the Greek mainland.

    Book ahead in peak summer, when popular flight times can sell out or spike in price.

  • Ferry from Italy

    Direct ferries connect Corfu to Italian ports including Brindisi, Bari and Ancona, a genuinely popular route for travellers combining Greece and Italy.

    Overnight sailings from Italy offer a genuinely comfortable way to arrive without flying.

  • Ferry from Igoumenitsa

    A short ferry from the mainland port of Igoumenitsa reaches Corfu in under an hour, useful for those driving from mainland Greece.

    Frequent daily crossings make this a genuinely flexible option without advance booking in most seasons.

Getting around

Corfu Old Town is genuinely walkable, with buses and rental vehicles covering the wider island.

  • Rental car

    The most practical way to reach Paleokastritsa, Angelokastro and the island's interior villages at your own pace.

    Coastal and mountain roads can be genuinely narrow and winding — allow more time than GPS estimates suggest.

  • Local buses

    A reasonably reliable network connects Corfu Town to the island's main beach towns and villages.

    Service to more remote interior villages is genuinely limited — a car helps for these areas.

  • Organised tours or boat trips

    A genuinely convenient option for Paxos and Antipaxos, which involve logistics easier left to a boat operator.

    Boat trips typically depart from Corfu Town or Paleokastritsa each morning in season.

When to go

Best time to visit Corfu

Corfu's summer crowds and prices peak in July and August, and shoulder season offers genuinely comparable weather with real relief from both.

  • Spring (April–June)

    Best time

    Mild, increasingly warm weather with the island's famous lush greenery at its most vivid.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Hot and reliably sunny, genuinely the best swimming weather, but also the island's most crowded and expensive months.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Warm sea temperatures lingering from summer, comfortable air temperatures and noticeably thinner crowds.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Shoulder

    Genuinely wetter than much of Greece given the Ionian's climate, but mild and quiet, with the Old Town's architecture still rewarding a visit.

Worth planning around: Peak July and August without accommodation booked ahead — Corfu's popular beach towns fill considerably and prices spike.

Itinerary

Four days in Corfu

The Old Town first, then the island's dramatic west coast, then its interior and a nearby island day trip.

  1. Day1

    Arrive in Corfu Town

    The island's Venetian heart.

    • Morning Land and settle into Corfu Old Town.
    • Afternoon The Old Fortress and the Liston.
    • Evening Dinner featuring pastitsada in the Old Town.
  2. Day2

    Paleokastritsa and Angelokastro

    The island's most dramatic coastline.

    • Morning Swim at Paleokastritsa's coves.
    • Afternoon Climb to the Byzantine castle of Angelokastro.
    • Evening Return to Corfu Town for dinner.
  3. Day3

    Achilleion Palace and the interior

    Imperial history and the island's lush countryside.

    • Morning Achilleion Palace and its gardens.
    • Afternoon Explore an interior village and Corfu's olive groves.
    • Evening A final Corfiot dinner featuring sofrito or bourdeto.
  4. Day4

    Paxos

    A day trip to a smaller, quieter island.

    • Morning Boat trip to Paxos.
    • Afternoon Explore Paxos and, if included, Antipaxos's beaches.
    • Evening Return to Corfu Town for a final evening on the Liston.

Travel tips

Corfu travel tips

Practical notes on timing, transport and etiquette that make a real difference to a Corfu trip.

  • Get genuinely lost in the Old Town's kantounia lanes — much of the charm lies in unplanned wandering.
  • Rent a car for at least part of the trip — Paleokastritsa, Angelokastro and the interior genuinely reward the flexibility.
  • Book accommodation ahead for peak summer, when Corfu's popular coastal towns fill up considerably.
  • Try pastitsada or sofrito at least once — both are genuinely distinctive to Corfu and found nowhere else in Greece.
  • Visit Paleokastritsa early in the day during peak season, before its beaches genuinely fill up.
  • Consider a ferry from Italy if combining Corfu with an Italian trip — the direct route is genuinely convenient.
  • Pack for genuinely more rain than other Greek islands if visiting in winter — Corfu's climate is noticeably wetter.

FAQ

Corfu: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Corfu?

Four to five for a first visit, enough to cover the Old Town, Paleokastritsa, Achilleion Palace and a Paxos day trip without feeling rushed.

Is Corfu different from other Greek islands?

Genuinely, yes — centuries of Venetian rather than Ottoman rule gave Corfu a distinct architecture, food culture and overall atmosphere.

Do I need a car in Corfu?

Helpful but not essential — buses cover the Old Town and main beach towns reasonably well, though a car helps considerably for the interior and Angelokastro.

What is the best time of year to visit?

May to June and September offer warm weather with crowds and prices well below the July-August peak, which is genuinely the island's busiest season.

Can I visit Italy from Corfu?

Yes — direct ferries connect Corfu to Brindisi, Bari and Ancona, a genuinely popular route for travellers combining Greece and Italy.
