Welcome to Boracay

Boracay is a small island, about 7 km long and less than 1 km wide, off the north-west tip of Panay island. It is the Philippines' most famous beach destination, thanks to White Beach, a 4-km stretch of fine, powdery sand that is among the best in Asia. In 2018, the government closed the island for six months to tackle sewage and overdevelopment, and today it operates with stricter rules on waste, building and beach use.

White Beach is divided into three stations. Station 1 is the quietest and most upscale, Station 2 the busiest, with D'Mall shops and restaurants, and Station 3 the most laid-back. In the evening, the beach fills with fire dancers, beach bars and dining tables in the sand. On the other side of the island, Bulabog Beach has strong winds for kitesurfing and windsurfing from November to April.

Other highlights include Puka Shell Beach on the north tip, a quieter beach of coarse sand and shells, island-hopping tours, the sunset paraw sailing boats that glide on the water, and diving off the island. The island is small enough to see in three days, and it is best in the dry season.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Boracay

Boracay is small, and everything is accessible by tricycle or on foot.

  1. 01

    White Beach

    A 4-km strip of powder-soft sand divided into three stations, with the calmest water in the dry season.

    Tip Stay at Station 1 for quiet.

  2. 02

    Paraw sailing at sunset

    Traditional outrigger sailboats that carry passengers along the coast as the sun goes down.

    Tip Book a slot before 4 pm.

  3. 03

    Puka Shell Beach

    A less developed beach on the north end, named for its puka shells.

    Tip Take a tricycle.

  4. 04

    Bulabog Beach

    The windy east side for kitesurfing and windsurfing.

    Tip Best from November to April.

  5. 05

    Mount Luho

    The island's highest point, with a view.

    Tip Go at sunrise.

  6. 06

    D'Mall

    Shops, cafés and restaurants at Station 2.

    Tip Good for evenings.
