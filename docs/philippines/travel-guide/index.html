The Complete Philippines Travel Guide

How the archipelago divides, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to plan flights and ferries around the weather, and what two weeks can realistically hold.

The Philippines offers limestone lagoons, reefs, rice terraces and heritage towns across thousands of islands. This guide covers how the regions divide, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to travel between islands, when to go, what it costs and three routes that work.

How the Philippines divides

The Philippines has three main island groups, Luzon in the north, the Visayas in the centre and Mindanao in the south, and for a visitor it falls into five zones.

Luzon. Manila is the gateway. North of it, Banaue and Batad have rice terraces and Vigan has Spanish streets.

Palawan. El Nido, Coron and Puerto Princesa hold limestone lagoons and the Underground River.

The Visayas. Cebu, Bohol and Boracay are the beach, diving and hills heartland.

Mindanao's north-east. Siargao is the surf island.

Worth knowing: The practical unit is two or three of these, linked by flights from Manila or Cebu. Palawan and the Visayas together make a classic two-week trip.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If you have one week

    El Nido and Coron in Palawan, or Cebu and Bohol in the Visayas.

  • If you love beaches and islands

    El Nido, Boracay and Coron have the best sand and lagoons.

  • If you love diving and snorkelling

    Coron for wrecks, Cebu for sardines and whale sharks, and Panglao for reefs.

  • If you love culture and history

    Manila&#39;s Intramuros, Vigan and the Ifugao terraces.

  • If you love surfing

    Siargao is the place, especially from August to November.

  • If you love nature

    The Underground River, the Chocolate Hills and tarsiers and Batad.

Getting around

Inter-island travel is the main challenge of the Philippines, and requires flexibility.

Flights. Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines and AirAsia link Manila with most destinations. Book early, and keep a buffer day before an international flight.

Ferries. Fast ferries link Cebu and Bohol, and El Nido and Coron. Book with reputable operators.

Local transport. Jeepneys, tricycles, vans and Grab in cities.

Vans. The road from Puerto Princesa to El Nido takes about 5 to 6 hours in a shared van.

Worth knowing: Cancellations happen. Build slack into your schedule.

When to go

The Philippines has a dry season and a wet season, and typhoons matter.

December to February is cool and dry, the most popular window.

March to May is hot and dry, ideal for beaches, diving and island hopping.

June to August is the start of the rains, cheaper and quieter, and the surf season starts at Siargao.

September to November is peak typhoon season, with cancelled flights and ferries, particularly in the north.

Worth knowing: The southern islands are less affected by typhoons than Luzon and the Visayas.

Money, costs and safety

The Philippines is good value. A meal costs US$3–10, a mid-range hotel US$40–90, and an island-hopping tour US$15–30. Diving and domestic flights are the main costs.

Carry cash on islands, since ATMs can be scarce or empty.

Petty crime and scams occur in Manila. Government advisories apply to parts of Mindanao.

Always wear a life jacket on boats, and avoid overloaded vessels in bad weather.

Three routes that work

  1. One week: Palawan

    Fly to Puerto Princesa, van to El Nido for four nights, then ferry to Coron.

    Tip Book the El Nido ferry ahead.

  2. Ten days: the Visayas

    Three nights in Cebu and Moalboal, then ferry to Bohol for three nights.

    Tip Add Boracay for beach days.

  3. Two weeks: the classic

    The itinerary on the country page: Manila, Palawan, Cebu and Bohol.

    Tip Swap Coron for Banaue if you love hiking.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Philippines

How many islands can I realistically visit?

In two weeks, two or three island groups. Inter-island travel takes time, and it is better to see fewer places well.

Should I book flights and ferries in advance?

Book flights early for good prices. Ferries can usually be booked a day or two ahead, but leave buffer days for weather.

Are island tours worth booking?

Yes. Small-group boat tours are the way to see lagoons and beaches in El Nido and Coron. Compare operators and read recent reviews.

Is it safe to travel during typhoon season?

It is risky and disruptive. September to November is peak season for storms. If you go, buy flexible tickets and travel insurance.
