Welcome to Vigan

Vigan, on the north-west coast of Luzon, is the best-preserved example of a planned Spanish colonial town in Asia. It was established in the 16th century as a trading post, where Chinese, Filipino and Spanish cultures met, and its surviving grid of streets and mansions combines Philippine, Chinese and European building traditions. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and in 2015 it was named one of the New 7 Wonders Cities.

The heart of the historic district is Calle Crisologo, a cobbled street lined with ancestral houses whose ground floors are stone or brick and whose upper floors are wooden with sliding capiz-shell windows. Horse-drawn calesas clatter along the street, and lamps light it in the evening. Traffic is closed at times. The nearby Plaza Salcedo, Vigan Cathedral and Plaza Burgos are the social heart.

Vigan is also a craft and food town. The Pagburnayan workshops produce large burnay clay jars, and the Ilocos region is famous for its empanadas, longganisa sausage and bagnet, crispy pork belly. A day trip can take in nearby Paoay Church and the sand dunes of Laoag. Two days is a good stay.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Vigan

The historic district is compact and walkable.

  1. 01

    Calle Crisologo

    The famous cobbled street of ancestral houses and shops, magical in the evening.

    Tip Go at dusk.

  2. 02

    Vigan Cathedral

    A baroque cathedral of the 16th century, with a striking facade.

    Tip Visit in the morning.

  3. 03

    Plaza Salcedo

    A park with a monument and a dancing fountain at night.

    Tip Go in the evening.

  4. 04

    Pagburnayan

    Pottery workshops where burnay jars are made.

    Tip Try the potter's wheel.

  5. 05

    Syquia Mansion

    A museum in the home of a president's family.

    Tip Allow an hour.

  6. 06

    Baluarte Zoo

    A zoo with animals and a view.

    Tip Good for families.
