Welcome to Phu Quoc

Phu Quoc is Vietnam's largest island, about 45 km long, lying in the Gulf of Thailand, closer to Cambodia than to the Vietnamese mainland. Its interior is covered with forest, much of it a national park, and its western coast has long sandy beaches facing the sunset. For decades it was a quiet place of fishing villages and plantations, and it has become the country's leading beach resort, with big resorts, a new international airport and a growing list of attractions.

The island is still known for its traditional industries. Its fish sauce, made by fermenting anchovies in large wooden barrels, is prized across Vietnam and protected by an EU geographical indication. Black pepper farms, pearl farms and a local breed of dog, the Phu Quoc ridgeback, are other symbols. Night markets in Duong Dong sell seafood, and the south of the island has fishing communities and offshore islands.

Development has been rapid, and some of the best-known beaches now have resorts and traffic, but there are still quieter corners, particularly in the north and the south-east. It is a good place for a few days of rest at the end of a Vietnam trip, particularly in the dry season.

Why visit

Why visit Phu Quoc?

Phu Quoc is the easiest place in Vietnam for a beach holiday, with a mix of resorts and rustic corners.

  • Beautiful beaches

    Sao Beach, Long Beach, Ong Lang and others offer fine sand and calm water.

  • Snorkelling and boat trips

    The An Thoi islands in the south have coral reefs and clear water.

  • Seafood and night markets

    Fresh fish, squid and crab at low prices.

  • Local products

    Fish sauce, pepper, pearls and honey.

  • Nature

    A national park with forest trails and waterfalls.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Phu Quoc

The island is spread out, and the sights are best combined by area.

  1. 01

    Sao Beach

    The island's most beautiful beach, on the south-east coast, with soft white sand and calm, clear water.

    Tip Go on a weekday, and arrive early.

  2. 02

    Long Beach and Duong Dong

    A long strip of sand on the west coast, with resorts, restaurants and the main town, and the Dinh Cau night market.

    Tip Stay for sunset.

  3. 03

    Dinh Cau Night Market

    A nightly market with seafood stalls, souvenirs and local snacks, beside the Dinh Cau temple.

    Tip Check prices before you order.

  4. 04

    Hon Thom Cable Car

    One of the longest sea-crossing cable cars in the world, from An Thoi to Hon Thom island, with a water park and views.

    Tip Go in the morning.

  5. 05

    The southern islands

    The An Thoi archipelago, with snorkelling and diving among coral reefs, reached by boat tours.

    Tip Book a tour with a good operator.

  6. 06

    Fish sauce factories and pepper farms

    Visit the barrels of fermenting fish sauce and the peppercorn plantations in the north.

    Tip Buy directly from producers.

Things to do

Best things to do in Phu Quoc

Phu Quoc is for the beach first, and for day trips second.

  1. Snorkel or dive

    Coral reefs and clear water off the An Thoi islands.

    Tip Best from November to April.

  2. Eat seafood at the night market

    Grilled squid, prawns and fish.

    Tip Check prices and weights.

  3. Watch the sunset

    The west coast is ideal, with beach bars and restaurants.

    Tip Bring a jacket for the breeze.

  4. Visit a fish sauce factory

    See how the sauce is made, and taste it.

    Tip Buy a bottle to take home.

  5. Explore the national park

    Forest trails, waterfalls and viewpoints in the north.

    Tip Wear good shoes.

  6. Take a squid-fishing trip

    An evening trip to fish for squid with lights.

    Tip Book through your hotel.

Local food

What to eat in Phu Quoc

Phu Quoc's food is seafood and fish sauce, with a range of local specialities.

  • Grilled seafood

    Squid, crab, prawns, scallops and fish, cooked to order.

    Where to try it The night market and beach restaurants.

  • Bun quay

    Fresh noodles in a broth with fish, a Phu Quoc speciality.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Duong Dong.

  • Herring salad (goi ca trich)

    Raw herring with coconut, herbs and peanuts.

    Where to try it Local restaurants.

  • Sea urchin and sea snails

    Fresh shellfish grilled with spring onions.

    Where to try it Night market.

  • Sim wine and pepper

    Wine made from rose myrtle, and local black pepper.

    Where to try it Shops and farms.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Phu Quoc

Choose the coast that suits you. The west has the most choice, and the south-east is quieter.

  • Long Beach

    Hotels, resorts and beach bars, near Duong Dong.

    Best for: Restaurants and sunsets

  • Ong Lang and the north-west

    Boutique resorts and bungalows.

    Best for: Quiet beaches

  • South-east (Sao Beach and Bai Khem)

    Luxury resorts and quieter stays.

    Best for: The best beaches

Transport

Getting to and around Phu Quoc

Phu Quoc is reached by air, or by ferry from the mainland.

Getting there

  • Flight to Phu Quoc (PQC)

    Direct flights from Ho Chi Minh City (about 1 hour), Hanoi and other cities, and some international routes.

    The airport is close to Duong Dong.

  • Ferry from Rach Gia or Ha Tien

    About 2 to 3 hours by ferry from mainland ports in the Mekong Delta.

    Combine with a delta trip.

  • From Cambodia

    Via Ha Tien, near the Cambodian border, and then by ferry.

    Check visa requirements.

Getting around

The island is large, and a scooter or taxi is needed.

  • Scooter

    The most popular way to explore.

    Wear a helmet, and check insurance.

  • Taxi and Grab

    Available around the main areas.

    Agree the fare.

  • Tours and resort shuttles

    Most resorts arrange transfers and day trips.

    Ask your hotel.

When to go

Best time to visit Phu Quoc

Phu Quoc has a tropical climate, with a dry season from November to April and a wet season with rough seas from May to October.

  • Dry season (November–February)

    Best time

    Sunny, dry, calm seas and clear water.

  • Hot season (March–April)

    Best time

    Hot and dry, with warm seas.

  • Wet season (May–October)

    Think twice

    Heavy rain, rougher seas and some closures. Green and cheap.

  • Tet (January or February)

    Quiet

    Busy with domestic tourists.

Worth planning around: August to October, the wettest months, when the sea is rough and murky.

Travel tips

Phu Quoc travel tips

A few tips help you plan the island.

  • Visit in the dry season, from November to April.
  • Rent a scooter only if you have experience, a licence and insurance.
  • Choose the beach area carefully, as they differ a lot.
  • Check prices at seafood stalls before ordering.
  • Book snorkelling tours with reputable operators.
  • Bring reef-safe sunscreen.
  • Buy fish sauce and pepper from producers.

FAQ

Phu Quoc: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Phu Quoc?

Three or four days for beaches and the main attractions.

What is the best time to visit?

November to April. The wet season has rougher seas.

Which beach is best?

Sao Beach for beauty, Long Beach for services, and Ong Lang for quiet.

Is Phu Quoc worth visiting?

Yes, for a beach break, though it is more developed than it used to be.

Can I visit from Ho Chi Minh City?

Yes, about one hour by air.

Is it safe to ride a scooter?

Roads are quiet outside the main areas, but accidents are common. Take care and wear a helmet.
