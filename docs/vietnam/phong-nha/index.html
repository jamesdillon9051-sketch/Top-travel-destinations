Welcome to Phong Nha

Phong Nha–Ke Bang National Park in central Vietnam holds one of the world's finest karst landscapes, with a system of caves and underground rivers cut into limestone that is about 400 million years old. UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 2003, and expanded it in 2015. Its caves range from short boat trips to some of the longest and largest in the world.

The best known is Son Doong, discovered by a local man in 1991 and first properly explored by a British caving team in 2009. It is considered the largest cave in the world by volume, with chambers so large that, it is often said, a city block of skyscrapers could fit inside, and its own jungle and river. Access is limited to organised expeditions with a strictly limited number of permits each year, and they are expensive. Other caves, such as Paradise, Phong Nha and the Dark Cave, are easily visited.

The park's other side is quiet. It is a district of small villages, rice fields, rivers and jungle, still far less visited than the coastal towns. The war has left its mark, as the Ho Chi Minh Trail passed through the region and many bombs were dropped here, and unexploded ordnance remains, so it is important to stay on marked paths.

Why visit

Why visit Phong Nha?

Phong Nha is the best place in Vietnam for cave and jungle adventure, and a good place to slow down.

  • Huge caves

    Paradise Cave and Son Doong are among the most spectacular caves anywhere.

  • Accessible adventure

    Boat trips, ziplines, kayaking and short treks suit most visitors.

  • Real jungle

    A wilderness of limestone forest, with rare wildlife and birds.

  • A quiet base

    Village life, rice fields and small guesthouses.

  • Wartime history

    Sites on the Ho Chi Minh Trail and the story of the region.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Phong Nha

There are dozens of caves, but a few stand out, with different experiences.

  1. 01

    Paradise Cave (Thien Duong)

    A vast, dry cave with a wooden walkway through a huge chamber of stalactites and stalagmites. About 31 km long in total, though only the first kilometre or so is open to casual visitors.

    Tip Go early to avoid tour groups.

  2. 02

    Phong Nha Cave

    The first cave of the region to be opened to visitors, entered by boat along an underground river, with impressive formations.

    Tip Combine with Paradise Cave.

  3. 03

    Dark Cave

    An adventure cave where you zipline in, swim through the river and mud-bathe in a chamber, popular with active visitors.

    Tip Not for those who dislike water or mud.

  4. 04

    Son Doong and Hang En expeditions

    Son Doong is visited on multi-day guided expeditions with a limited number of permits and a high price, and requires good fitness. Hang En, the third-largest cave, is a cheaper three-day trek.

    Tip Book many months ahead, and check the season, as caves close in the wet months.

  5. 05

    The Botanic Garden and Bong Lai Valley

    A nature area with trails and a wildlife rescue centre, and a valley of paddies and streams with swimming spots.

    Tip Good for a relaxed day.

Things to do

Best things to do in Phong Nha

Most activities focus on caves and the outdoors.

  1. Explore Paradise Cave

    Walk the wooden path through the main chamber, or add a longer guided trek.

    Tip Bring a light jacket.

  2. Take a boat into Phong Nha Cave

    Row along the underground river, and see formations by the boat's light.

    Tip Go early.

  3. Visit the Dark Cave

    Zipline into the entrance, swim in the river and bathe in mud.

    Tip Wear old clothes.

  4. Cycle or ride through the countryside

    Quiet roads pass paddies and limestone hills.

    Tip Rent a bicycle or scooter.

  5. Go on a jungle trek

    Guided treks visit smaller caves and jungle.

    Tip Use a licensed guide.

  6. Visit wartime sites

    Sites along the Ho Chi Minh Trail and wartime shelters.

    Tip Stay on marked paths, since unexploded ordnance remains.

Local food

What to eat in Phong Nha

Phong Nha's food is simple, rural and central Vietnamese, with specialities such as pancakes and river fish.

  • Banh xeo

    Crisp savoury pancakes with pork and shrimp, wrapped in herbs.

    Where to try it Local restaurants in Son Trach.

  • Fresh river fish

    Fish from the Son River, steamed or grilled.

    Where to try it Riverside restaurants.

  • Grilled pork skewers

    Nem lui, lemongrass pork skewers served with rice paper and herbs.

    Where to try it Street stalls.

  • Pig with local herbs

    Local pork cooked in jungle herbs.

    Where to try it Farm restaurants.

  • Coffee and fresh fruit

    Locally grown coffee and fruit.

    Where to try it Cafés in Son Trach.

Transport

Getting to and around Phong Nha

Phong Nha is in central Vietnam, about halfway between Hue and Vinh.

Getting there

  • Flight to Dong Hoi (VDH)

    Direct flights from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. Then a 45-minute drive.

    Arrange a transfer.

  • Train to Dong Hoi

    The Reunification Express stops in Dong Hoi, about 3 to 4 hours from Hue.

    Continue by taxi or bus.

  • Road from Hue

    About 4 to 5 hours by car or bus.

    Book a private car or a bus.

Getting around

The area is spread out, with tours, scooters and bicycles as the main options.

  • Scooter or bicycle

    Popular for reaching caves and countryside.

    Wear a helmet.

  • Tour vehicles

    Most cave tours include transport.

    Book in advance.

  • Taxi

    Available for longer trips.

    Agree the fare.

When to go

Best time to visit Phong Nha

Phong Nha is dry in spring and summer, and wet from September to December, when caves close and floods occur.

  • Spring (February–April)

    Best time

    Dry, mild and pleasant.

  • Summer (May–August)

    Good

    Hot and dry. Good for caves and swimming.

  • Autumn (September–December)

    Think twice

    Wet, with flooding and closed caves.

  • Winter (January)

    Quiet

    Cool and drizzly.

Worth planning around: The flood season from September to December, when Son Doong and many caves are closed.

Travel tips

Phong Nha travel tips

A few tips help you plan.

  • Book Son Doong expeditions many months ahead through the official operator, and check the season.
  • Stay on marked paths. Unexploded ordnance is still present.
  • Bring a light jacket for the caves, and waterproof shoes for the Dark Cave.
  • Avoid the flood season, when caves can close.
  • Go early to Paradise Cave.
  • Use licensed guides.
  • Carry water and sun protection.

FAQ

Phong Nha: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Phong Nha?

Two to three days for the main caves and countryside. Expeditions take three to five days.

Can anyone visit Son Doong?

It requires a permit, a guided expedition, good fitness and a significant budget, and is only offered in the dry season.

What is the best time to visit?

February to August. Autumn brings floods and closed caves.

Is Phong Nha safe?

Yes, but stay on marked paths because of unexploded ordnance from the war.

How do I get there from Hue?

By car or bus, about 4 to 5 hours, or by train to Dong Hoi.

Is Paradise Cave worth visiting?

Yes. It is one of the most spectacular caves in Vietnam and easy to reach.
