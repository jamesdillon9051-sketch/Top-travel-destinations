Welcome to Ha Long Bay

Ha Long Bay, in the Gulf of Tonkin, is a seascape of nearly 2,000 limestone islands and islets rising steeply from calm green water, many topped with jungle and pierced by caves and lagoons. The name means Bay of the Descending Dragon, from a legend in which a dragon family came down to defend the Vietnamese from invaders and their spitting jewels became the islands. UNESCO inscribed the bay in 1994.

It is the most visited natural attraction in Vietnam, and the standard way to see it is an overnight cruise on a wooden-style junk, with kayaking, a cave visit, a swim and a seafood dinner. The classic route, from Bai Chay near Ha Long City, is popular and busy, and many travellers prefer the quieter waters of Lan Ha Bay, south of Cat Ba Island, or Bai Tu Long Bay to the north-east.

Cruise quality varies widely, and it is worth choosing carefully. The bay is at its best in the early morning and evening, when the tour boats thin out and the mist gives the islands a painterly look.

Why visit

Why visit Ha Long Bay?

Ha Long Bay is one of the natural wonders of Southeast Asia, and worth the trip from Hanoi.

  • Extraordinary scenery

    Thousands of limestone towers in a sheltered bay, unlike anywhere else.

  • A night on the water

    Sleeping on a junk, watching the sunset and sunrise, is the highlight.

  • Kayaks and caves

    Paddle into hidden lagoons and explore caves such as Sung Sot.

  • Floating villages

    See fishing communities living on the water, though many have been resettled.

  • Quieter alternatives

    Lan Ha and Bai Tu Long bays offer similar scenery with fewer boats.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Ha Long Bay

The main experience is the bay itself, with a few key stops.

  1. 01

    The karst seascape

    The islands and channels that form the bay, best seen from a boat at sunrise or sunset, when the light and the mist are at their best.

    Tip Spend at least one night on board.

  2. 02

    Sung Sot (Surprise) Cave

    One of the largest caves in the bay, with three huge chambers and stalactites, on Bo Hon Island. It is a standard stop on the route.

    Tip Go early to avoid crowds.

  3. 03

    Kayaking in the lagoons

    Paddle through low arches into hidden lagoons and around floating villages, such as at Luon Cave and Vung Vieng.

    Tip Choose small groups.

  4. 04

    Cat Ba Island and Lan Ha Bay

    The largest island in the bay, with a national park and beaches, and the calmer, less crowded Lan Ha Bay on its south side, with scenery as good as Ha Long.

    Tip Book a cruise starting from Cat Ba or Hai Phong.

  5. 05

    Ti Top Island

    A small island with a beach and a viewpoint accessed by a short climb, offering a panorama over the bay.

    Tip The climb takes about 15 minutes.

Things to do

Best things to do in Ha Long Bay

Most activities are organised as part of a cruise.

  1. Take an overnight cruise

    A junk or a modern ship takes you through the bay, with meals, activities and a cabin.

    Tip Read recent reviews on safety and cabin quality.

  2. Kayak among the islands

    See lagoons and arches close-up, in a quiet, personal way.

    Tip Wear sun protection.

  3. Do a tai chi class at sunrise

    Many cruises offer a morning session on the deck.

    Tip Bring a warm layer.

  4. Visit a cave

    See the huge chambers of Sung Sot, or smaller caves off the main route.

    Tip Go early.

  5. Swim from a beach

    Some cruises stop at Ti Top or small beaches for a swim.

    Tip Check water quality.

  6. Explore Cat Ba National Park

    Hiking trails, viewpoints and wildlife, including the rare golden-headed langur.

    Tip Add a night on the island.

Local food

What to eat in Ha Long Bay

Cruise meals are typically fresh seafood and Vietnamese dishes, and the region has its own specialities.

  • Fresh seafood

    Squid, prawns, crab and fish, often served as a set dinner on board.

    Where to try it Cruises and floating restaurants.

  • Cha muc (squid cake)

    Pounded squid fried into bouncy patties, a Ha Long speciality.

    Where to try it Ha Long City markets and restaurants.

  • Sa sung

    Sipunculid worms, dried or fried, a local delicacy.

    Where to try it Local restaurants.

  • Spring rolls

    Fried or fresh rolls, served at many cruise dinners.

    Where to try it Cruise boats.

  • Fruit and tea

    Fresh fruit and green tea for breakfast on deck.

    Where to try it Cruises.

Transport

Getting to and around Ha Long Bay

Ha Long Bay is about 160 km east of Hanoi.

Getting there

  • Cruise transfer from Hanoi

    Most cruise operators include a bus or limousine transfer from Hanoi, about 2.5 to 3 hours on the expressway.

    Confirm what is included.

  • Private car or bus

    Buses and cars go from Hanoi to Ha Long City.

    Book reputable companies.

  • Via Hai Phong to Cat Ba

    About 2 hours from Hanoi to Hai Phong, then a ferry or speedboat to Cat Ba.

    A good option for Lan Ha Bay.

Getting around

Once in the bay, everything is by boat.

  • Cruise boat

    Your cruise follows a set route.

    Ask about the route in advance.

  • Tender and kayak

    Small boats and kayaks reach lagoons and caves.

    Life jackets are provided.

  • Ferry

    Ferries link Cat Ba with Hai Phong and the mainland.

    Check schedules.

When to go

Best time to visit Ha Long Bay

The bay is misty in spring and can be cold in winter, and typhoons in summer occasionally cancel cruises.

  • Spring (March–April)

    Good

    Warm, often misty and drizzly, with atmospheric scenery.

  • Summer (May–August)

    Think twice

    Hot and humid, with storms and typhoons.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Clear skies, warm days and calm seas.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Good

    Cool and misty, with fewer visitors.

Worth planning around: July to September, when typhoons can disrupt cruises.

Travel tips

Ha Long Bay travel tips

A good cruise makes a great trip, so it pays to choose with care.

  • Read recent reviews and check the safety record of the operator.
  • Compare itineraries. Some cruises spend most of the time on the water, and others on tourist stops.
  • Consider Lan Ha or Bai Tu Long Bay for fewer crowds.
  • Bring layers. It can be chilly on deck at night and in the morning.
  • Avoid the cheapest cruises, since quality is often poor.
  • Do not litter or feed wildlife.
  • Book ahead in October and April.

FAQ

Ha Long Bay: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need for Ha Long Bay?

One night on a cruise is the standard. Two nights allows a slower pace and quieter areas.

Which is better, Ha Long or Lan Ha Bay?

Lan Ha has similar scenery with fewer boats, and is often preferred. Ha Long has the best-known sights.

Can I visit Ha Long Bay on a day trip?

Yes, but the transfer takes 5 to 7 hours in all. An overnight cruise is much better.

Is it safe?

Cruises are generally safe, but standards vary. Choose reputable operators, and check the weather.

What is the best time to visit?

October to April. Summer typhoons can disrupt cruises.

Can I swim?

Yes, at certain stops, though water quality varies.
