Sri Lanka packs ancient kingdoms, tea hills, safaris and beaches into a small island. This guide covers how the regions divide, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to travel, when to go, what it costs and three routes that work.

How Sri Lanka divides

Sri Lanka is about 435 km from north to south, and for a visitor it falls into five zones.

The west and Colombo. Colombo is the gateway city, with an international airport 35 km north.

The Cultural Triangle. Sigiriya, Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa hold the ancient kingdoms, with Kandy at the southern edge.

The hill country. Nuwara Eliya and Ella are cool, green and famous for tea and trains.

The south. Galle, Mirissa and Yala offer beaches, whales and wildlife.

Worth knowing: The practical route is a loop: Colombo, the Cultural Triangle, Kandy, the hills and the south coast.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If you have one week

    Sigiriya, Kandy and Ella, with a train ride between the last two.

  • If you love history

    Anuradhapura, Polonnaruwa, Sigiriya and Galle&#39;s fort.

  • If you love wildlife

    Yala for leopards, Minneriya near Polonnaruwa for elephants, and Mirissa for whales.

  • If you love hiking and scenery

    Ella, Horton Plains and the tea country.

  • If you love beaches

    Mirissa and the south coast, best from December to March.

  • If you love city life

    Colombo for food and shopping, Galle for atmosphere and Kandy for culture.

Getting around

Roads are slow and winding, so distances that look short on a map can take hours.

Car with driver. Popular, comfortable and affordable. Your driver often knows the best stops.

Trains. The Kandy–Ella line is a highlight. Reserve seats early.

Buses. Very cheap and frequent, but crowded and fast.

Tuk-tuks. For short hops. Use PickMe or agree fares.

Worth knowing: The expressways from Colombo to Galle and Matara save hours on the south coast.

When to go

Sri Lanka's two monsoons decide the best place to be.

December to March: the south-west coast, hills and Yala are dry and sunny. This is the peak season.

April to May: hot and humid, with thunderstorms. A quiet time.

June to September: the south-west coast is rough and wet, but the Cultural Triangle, Kandy and the east coast are dry. Minneriya elephant gatherings peak.

October and November: heavy rain nearly everywhere.

Worth knowing: Whales off Mirissa are best seen from December to April.

Money, costs and safety

Sri Lanka is good value. A local meal costs US$2–6, a mid-range hotel US$40–90, and a driver with car US$50–90 per day.

Entrance fees to major sites such as Sigiriya are charged in US dollars and are high for foreigners.

Beware of scams and inflated prices from touts. Use reputable operators for safaris and whale watching.

Take care in the sea, since currents are strong.

Three routes that work

  1. One week: culture and hills

    Two nights near Sigiriya, two in Kandy, and three in Ella.

    Tip Take the train from Kandy to Ella.

  2. Ten days: adding the south

    The one-week route, plus Yala and Mirissa.

    Tip Hire a driver.

  3. Two weeks: the classic

    The itinerary on the country page, covering all ten destinations from Colombo to Galle.

    Tip Skip Anuradhapura if you are short of time.

A 17th-century Dutch fort that is still a living community, with ramparts, a lighthouse, mosques and churches, boutiques, cafés and the beaches of the south coast nearby.

A small hill-country village with Little Adam's Peak, Ella Rock, the Nine Arch Bridge, Ravana Falls and cafés overlooking the Ella Gap.

Central Province, hill country

Sri Lanka's tea capital at 1,900 metres, with tea estate tours, colonial architecture, Gregory Lake and the Horton Plains' World's End and Baker's Falls.

Southern and Uva Provinces

Sri Lanka's most famous safari park, with one of the highest densities of leopards in the world, elephants, buffalo, crocodiles and birds, explored in jeep safaris.

North Central Province

FAQ

Planning a trip to Sri Lanka

Should I hire a driver or use public transport?

A car with a driver is the easiest and most flexible, and it is affordable. Trains are the best experience for the hill country. Buses are cheap but crowded.

Which coast should I choose?

The south and west from December to March, and the east from April to September. The cultural triangle and hills are good most of the year.

How far ahead should I book the Ella train?

Reserved seats on the Kandy–Ella line sell out weeks ahead in season. Book early or use a second-class ticket.

Is Sri Lanka easy for first-timers to Asia?

Yes. English is widely understood, people are friendly, and distances are short.
