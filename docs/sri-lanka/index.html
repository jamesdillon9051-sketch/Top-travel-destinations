Why go to Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka is an island about the size of Ireland or West Virginia, off the southern tip of India, with roughly 22 million people. Its history is long: the Buddhist kingdom of Anuradhapura was founded in the 4th century BCE, the rock fortress of Sigiriya was built in the 5th century CE, and successive Portuguese, Dutch and British colonisers left forts, churches and tea plantations. Sinhalese Buddhists form the majority, alongside Tamil Hindus, Muslims and Christians.

For visitors, the main appeal is variety in a small space. In a single trip you can climb a rock fortress, see ruins of ancient cities, watch elephants gather at a reservoir, take a slow train through misty tea country, go on a leopard safari, and finish on a palm-lined beach where blue whales pass offshore. The distances are short, though roads are slow, so two weeks covers much of the island.

Sri Lanka went through a severe economic crisis in 2022, and the situation has stabilised, with tourism growing again. Visitors are warmly welcomed, and prices are low. The island has two monsoons, so the best region depends on the season, and a flexible plan lets you follow the sun.

Experiences

Best things to do in Sri Lanka

The experiences that recur across the country and make a Sri Lankan trip feel like one.

  1. Ride a scenic train

    The line from Kandy through Nuwara Eliya to Ella is one of the world's most beautiful rail journeys, through tea plantations and cloud forest. See Ella and Nuwara Eliya.

    Tip Book reserved seats well ahead, or take a second-class carriage for the open doors.

  2. Climb Sigiriya

    The 200-metre rock fortress with frescoes and a mirror wall is Sri Lanka's most famous landmark. See Sigiriya.

    Tip Go at opening time to avoid the heat and crowds.

  3. Go on safari

    Yala has one of the world's highest densities of leopards, and Minneriya has huge seasonal gatherings of elephants. See Yala.

    Tip Book early morning jeeps.

  4. Visit ancient cities

    Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa were royal capitals with huge stupas and carved Buddhas. See Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa.

    Tip Hire a bicycle.

  5. Watch whales

    Mirissa is one of the best places in the world to see blue whales. See Mirissa.

    Tip Choose a responsible operator.

  6. Tour a tea estate

    See how Ceylon tea is picked and processed in the hill country. See Nuwara Eliya.

    Tip Try a fresh cup on the estate.

  7. Stroll a Dutch fort

    Galle Fort's ramparts, cafés and shops are a delight at sunset. See Galle.

    Tip Walk the ramparts at sunset.

Food and drink

Sri Lanka foods to try

Sri Lankan food is fiery, coconut-rich and full of spices, based on rice and curry, with hoppers, string hoppers, sambols and seafood.

  • Rice and curry

    A plate of rice with several small curries: dhal, vegetables, fish or chicken, sambols and papadum. Meals vary by region.

    Where to try it Local restaurants and guesthouses, especially at lunch.

  • Kottu roti

    Chopped flatbread stir-fried on a hot griddle with vegetables, egg, spices and meat, noisily prepared with two metal blades.

    Where to try it Street stalls and cafés at night.

  • Hoppers and string hoppers

    Bowl-shaped fermented rice-flour pancakes (appa), often with an egg, and steamed noodle nests, served with curries and sambol.

    Where to try it Breakfast and dinner cafés.

  • Fish curry and seafood

    Spicy fish curries, crab and prawn dishes on the coast, especially in Galle and Mirissa.

    Where to try it Coastal restaurants, such as in Mirissa and Galle.

  • Ceylon tea

    Black tea grown in the highlands, served with milk and sugar or plain. The best comes from the Nuwara Eliya region.

    Where to try it Tea estates in Nuwara Eliya.

  • Watalappan and sweets

    A coconut milk and jaggery custard, served at celebrations, alongside treacle-drenched curd.

    Where to try it Restaurants and sweet shops.

Before you go

Practical travel information for Sri Lanka

The essentials, current for 2026. Sri Lanka is easy and friendly, though roads are slow and rules change often.

Capital
Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte (administrative), Colombo (commercial)
Language
Sinhala and Tamil are official. English is widely understood in tourism and cities.
Currency
Sri Lankan rupee (LKR). Roughly 300–330 rupees to the US dollar in recent years, following a sharp fall in 2022.
Cards and cash
Cards are accepted in hotels and larger restaurants, and cash is needed for tuk-tuks, markets and small guesthouses. ATMs are common in towns, and tourist-price trades in US dollars occur.
Entry and visas
Almost all visitors need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA), applied for online before travel. A visa-free scheme has been trialled for some nationalities, and fees and rules change often, so check the official ETA site.
Power sockets
Type D, M and G, 230 V, 50 Hz. UK plugs (type G) fit many sockets, but a universal adaptor is safest.
Emergency number
119 for police, 110 for ambulance, 111 for fire, and 1912 for the Tourist Police.
Tipping
A 10% service charge is often added in restaurants. Small tips for drivers, guides and hotel staff are appreciated.
Safety
Sri Lanka is generally safe for visitors. Watch for scams and overcharging by tuk-tuk drivers and touts, and be careful in the sea, as rip currents are strong. Women travelling alone sometimes face harassment. Check government travel advice, and note that political and economic conditions can change quickly.
Internet and SIM
Good in towns, with cheap SIM cards at the airport. Coverage is patchy in the hills and national parks.
Time zone
Sri Lanka Standard Time, UTC+5:30 all year, with no daylight saving.

When to go

Best time to visit Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka has two monsoons. The south-west coast and hills are best from December to March, and the east coast from April to September. The cultural triangle is best from May to September, and warm all year.

  • December–March

    Best time

    The best time for the south and west coasts, Yala and the hills. The peak season, with high prices around Christmas and New Year.

  • April–May

    Quiet

    Inter-monsoon, hot and humid, with afternoon thunderstorms. The east coast begins to warm up.

  • June–September

    Good

    The south-west monsoon brings rain and rough seas to the south and west, but the cultural triangle, east coast and Kandy are dry. Elephant gatherings at Minneriya peak from July to September.

  • October–November

    Think twice

    The second inter-monsoon, with heavy rain and storms across much of the island.

Worth planning around: October and November for the wettest conditions almost everywhere.

Trip length

How long to spend in Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka is small, but roads and trains are slow. A week covers the cultural triangle and hill country, and two weeks allows the south coast and Yala.

  • 7–8 days

Getting around

Transportation in Sri Lanka

Trains are scenic and cheap, buses are packed and fast, tuk-tuks are everywhere, and hiring a car with a driver is the most popular way to see the island.

  • Car with driver

    The most popular option for visitors, giving flexibility. Rates are around US$50–90 per day including fuel and accommodation for the driver.

    Book through your guesthouse or a reputable agency.

  • Trains

    The Kandy–Ella line is world-famous. Book first-class or reserved seats early on popular routes.

    Use the official railway booking system or an agent.

  • Buses

    Cheap and frequent, though crowded and fast.

    Avoid with large luggage.

  • Tuk-tuks and PickMe

    Three-wheeled taxis are everywhere. PickMe and Uber operate in Colombo.

    Agree the fare or use the app.

  • Domestic flights

    Limited, but small seaplane and helicopter services exist.

    Rarely needed.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Sri Lanka

Sri Lankans are warm, hospitable and curious about visitors. Religion matters: Buddhism is the majority faith, with strong Hindu, Muslim and Christian communities, and temples are active places of worship. Modesty and respect are expected.

  • Dress modestly at temples

    Cover shoulders and knees, and remove shoes and hats before entering.

  • Never pose with your back to a Buddha

    Turning your back to a statue for photos is seen as disrespectful and has led to visitors being detained or deported.

  • Greet with 'Ayubowan'

    Say hello with palms together, or shake hands with men.

  • Use your right hand

    Eat, give and receive with the right hand.

  • Respect monks

    Women should not touch monks, and everyone should give them seats.

  • Dress modestly on beaches

    Bikinis are fine at resort beaches, but cover up in villages.

Do

  • Learn a few words: sthuthi (thank you, Sinhala), nandri (thank you, Tamil)
  • Carry a scarf for temples
  • Book trains early
  • Negotiate tuk-tuk fares or use PickMe
  • Use bottled or filtered water
  • Bargain politely in markets

Don't

  • Don't pose with your back to Buddha statues
  • Don't wear shoes in temples
  • Don't touch monks (women)
  • Don't feed wild animals
  • Don't swim in strong surf
  • Don't take drones without a permit

Travel tips

Sri Lanka travel tips

The things that most often catch visitors out in Sri Lanka, several of which are specific to how the country works.

  • Apply for your ETA online before you fly, using the official site.
  • Book trains between Kandy and Ella weeks ahead, and consider a second-class ticket if reserved seats sell out.
  • Budget more travel time than the map suggests, since roads are slow.
  • Book safari jeeps for dawn and choose reputable operators.
  • Respect temple dress codes, carrying a sarong or scarf.
  • Agree the tuk-tuk fare first, or use the PickMe app.
  • Keep an eye on the weather, since monsoons decide which coast to visit.
  • Carry cash for small purchases.

FAQ

Visiting Sri Lanka: frequently asked questions

Is Sri Lanka safe for tourists?

Generally yes. Watch for scams and rip currents, and check government advice.

How many days do you need in Sri Lanka?

Ten to fourteen days for a first trip, covering the cultural triangle, hill country and south coast.

When is the best time to visit Sri Lanka?

December to March for the south and hills, and May to September for the cultural triangle and east coast.

Do I need a visa for Sri Lanka?

Most visitors need an ETA. Check the official site, since fees and eligible nationalities change.

Is Sri Lanka cheap?

Yes. Food, transport and accommodation are inexpensive, though safaris and entrance fees add up.

Do I need a driver?

It is the easiest way to see the island, but trains and buses work well for many routes.
