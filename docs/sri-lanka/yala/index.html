Welcome to Yala

Yala National Park in south-eastern Sri Lanka covers almost 1,000 square kilometres of scrub, forest, rocky outcrops and lagoons along the Indian Ocean. It is the country's most visited park and one of the world's best places to see wild leopards, the Sri Lankan subspecies, with a density among the highest recorded. The park is divided into blocks, and the accessible Block 1 draws the most safari jeeps.

Leopards are the main quarry, but the park also has Asian elephants, water buffalo, sloth bears, crocodiles, jackals, mongoose and more than 200 bird species, including peacocks, painted storks and eagles. Tours run at dawn and in the afternoon, in open jeeps with local trackers, and take three to four hours. Sightings are never guaranteed, though leopards are seen on many drives.

Yala can be busy, with many jeeps converging on a single sighting, so choose an operator that behaves responsibly and consider quieter parks such as Udawalawe for elephants, or Wilpattu in the north-west for leopards. Yala is about five to six hours from Colombo, so it is often combined with Ella and the south coast.

When to go

Best time to visit Yala

Yala is best in the dry season, when animals gather at waterholes.

  • February–July

    Best time

    Dry season with good sightings.

  • August–September

    Good

    Very dry, hot.

  • October–November

    Think twice

    Rains, and Block 1 sometimes closes.

  • December–January

    Good

    Wetter but green.

Worth planning around: The park may close for a period in September or October.

Travel tips

Yala travel tips
