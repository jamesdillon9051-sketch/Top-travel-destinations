Welcome to Galle

Galle is a city on the south-west coast, and its historic heart, the Galle Fort, is one of the best-preserved colonial sea fortresses in Asia. The Portuguese built the first fort in 1588, and the Dutch took the town in 1640 and constructed the massive stone ramparts and bastions visible today. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and unlike many such sites, it is a living town, with shops, schools, mosques, churches and families inside the walls.

The fort covers about 52 hectares, and a walk along the ramparts, about 2.5 km, takes in the lighthouse, the Old Gate, the Clock Tower and sea views. Inside, streets are lined with Dutch-colonial houses, some converted into boutique hotels, antique shops, galleries and cafés. Landmarks include the Dutch Reformed Church of 1755, the Meeran Mosque and the National Museum.

The 2004 tsunami hit Galle hard, but the fort's thick walls protected the old town. Beyond the walls, the south coast has beaches such as Unawatuna and Jungle Beach, and a stilt fishermen coastline at Koggala. Two days is a good stay, ideally with sunset on the ramparts.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Galle

The fort is small and walkable.

  1. 01

    Galle Fort ramparts

    A 2.5-km walk along the top of the walls, with views of the sea and town.

    Tip Go at sunset.

  2. 02

    The Lighthouse

    A white lighthouse at the southern point of the fort, a photo favourite.

    Tip Best in late afternoon.

  3. 03

    The Old Clock Tower

    A clock tower built in 1883 in the fort.

    Tip Photograph in the morning.

  4. 04

    Dutch Reformed Church

    A church built in 1755 with tombstones on the floor.

    Tip Free to enter.

  5. 05

    Meeran Mosque

    A white mosque near the lighthouse.

    Tip Dress modestly.

  6. 06

    Unawatuna Beach

    A curved beach 5 km from the fort.

    Tip Take a tuk-tuk.
