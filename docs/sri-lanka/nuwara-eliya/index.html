Welcome to Nuwara Eliya

Nuwara Eliya sits at about 1,900 metres in the central highlands, and its climate is cool and often misty, a relief after the heat of the plains. British planters founded a hill station here in the 19th century, and it still feels like an English village transplanted to the tropics, with Tudor-style cottages, a golf club, a racecourse and a post office in red brick. The area is the heart of Sri Lanka's tea country, and the highest-grown Ceylon teas are among the most delicate.

The surrounding hills are covered in tea bushes, tended by Tamil pickers, and estates such as Pedro, Mackwoods and Blue Field offer tours of the factory and tastings. Gregory Lake is popular for boat rides and picnics, and there are waterfalls close to the road. The town itself can be busy with weekend visitors and in April, when the season brings horse races and parties.

About 32 km away, Horton Plains National Park is a high grassland plateau of cloud forest, with a 9.5-km circular trail to World's End, a cliff dropping nearly 900 metres, and Baker's Falls. The walk should start at dawn, before the mist. The train from Kandy to Nuwara Eliya (via Nanu Oya) is a scenic journey through tea country.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Nuwara Eliya

Tea estates are near town; Horton Plains is a half-day trip.

  1. 01

    Tea estates

    Estates with factories where you can watch leaf being withered, rolled and dried.

    Tip Visit in the morning.

  2. 02

    Gregory Lake

    An artificial lake for boating, picnics and horse riding.

    Tip Go on weekdays.

  3. 03

    World's End, Horton Plains

    A precipice with a sheer drop of about 870 metres, best seen at dawn before the mist.

    Tip Start at 6 am.

  4. 04

    Baker's Falls

    A waterfall along the World's End loop.

    Tip Walk the circuit.

  5. 05

    Victoria Park

    A garden with birds and flowers.

    Tip Good in April.

  6. 06

    Colonial buildings

    The Grand Hotel, Hill Club and post office.

    Tip Take afternoon tea.
