Welcome to Guadalajara and Tequila

Guadalajara is the capital of Jalisco and the second-largest city in Mexico, and the home of the country's most iconic traditions: mariachi music, charrería (rodeo), the Mexican hat dance and tequila. It was founded in 1542, and its centre has a huge twin-spired cathedral, four large plazas and grand public buildings, and it remains a lively and prosperous, if less touristy, alternative to Mexico City.

Its greatest artistic monument is the Hospicio Cabañas, a neoclassical orphanage and hospital built in the early 1800s, whose chapel dome and walls are covered in murals by José Clemente Orozco. UNESCO listed it in 1997. Nearby, Tlaquepaque and Tonalá are craft villages of glass, ceramics and furniture, now absorbed into the metropolitan area.

About an hour north-west, the town of Tequila is surrounded by fields of blue agave, and the agave landscape and its distilleries were inscribed by UNESCO in 2006. A day there, with a distillery tour and a lunch, is one of the classic Mexican excursions.

Why visit

Why visit Guadalajara and Tequila?

Guadalajara is a great city for food, music and crafts, and a gateway to tequila country.

  • The Hospicio Cabañas

    Orozco's murals, especially the Man of Fire in the dome, are among the masterpieces of 20th-century Mexican art.

  • Mariachi

    Guadalajara is the birthplace of mariachi, and Plaza de los Mariachis and Tlaquepaque are the best places to hear it.

  • Food

    Birria, tortas ahogadas and carne en su jugo are Guadalajara's specialities.

  • Tequila country

    Agave fields, distilleries and a UNESCO landscape, with tastings at sources such as Jose Cuervo and small producers.

  • Crafts

    Tlaquepaque and Tonalá sell glass, pottery and furniture.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Guadalajara and Tequila

The centre takes a day, Tlaquepaque and Tonalá a half-day, and Tequila a full day.

  1. 01

    The Cathedral and the four plazas

    The Metropolitan Cathedral, with its yellow spires, stands in the middle of four connected plazas, including the Plaza de la Liberación and the Rotonda de los Jaliscienses Ilustres.

    Tip Walk the plazas in the evening.

  2. 02

    The Hospicio Cabañas

    A vast neoclassical complex of 23 courtyards, now a cultural centre, whose chapel is covered in Orozco's murals, including the Man of Fire.

    Tip Lie on the benches to see the dome.

  3. 03

    Government Palace

    A baroque palace with an Orozco mural of Miguel Hidalgo, the priest who started the independence struggle.

    Tip Free to enter.

  4. 04

    Mercado San Juan de Dios

    One of the largest indoor markets in Latin America, with food, crafts and goods on multiple floors.

    Tip Eat on the first floor.

  5. 05

    Tlaquepaque and Tonalá

    Craft villages with blown glass, ceramics, furniture and jewellery, with restaurants and mariachi.

    Tip Tonalá's markets are on Thursdays and Sundays.

  6. 06

    Tequila town and the agave landscape

    A town of about 40,000 people surrounded by blue agave fields, with distilleries such as Jose Cuervo and Casa Herradura, and a tequila museum.

    Tip Take a distillery tour and try a range.

  7. 07

    Lake Chapala and Ajijic

    Mexico's largest lake, about 50 km south, with lakeside towns that are popular with expats.

    Tip Good for a relaxed day.

Things to do

Best things to do in Guadalajara and Tequila

Guadalajara is for eating, listening and drinking well.

  1. Take the Tequila Express

    A tourist train from Guadalajara to the distillery at Amatitán, with tastings and mariachi on board.

    Tip Book ahead.

  2. Eat birria

    Try spiced goat or beef stew, served with tortillas and consommé.

    Tip Try it at a local birrieria.

  3. Hear mariachi

    Plaza de los Mariachis, Tlaquepaque and Garibaldi have bands playing for tips.

    Tip Agree the price per song.

  4. Watch a charreada

    Mexican rodeo shows at charro arenas on Sundays.

    Tip Ask your hotel.

  5. Shop in Tlaquepaque

    Browse galleries and craft shops, and have lunch.

    Tip Go on a weekday.

  6. Visit a distillery

    See agave harvested by jimadores, baked and distilled.

    Tip Choose a small producer.

Local food

What to eat in Guadalajara and Tequila

Jalisco's food is robust and meat-heavy, with drinks to match.

  • Birria

    Spiced goat or beef stew from Jalisco, served in a bowl or in tacos with consommé.

    Where to try it Birrierias across the city.

  • Torta ahogada

    A pork sandwich in a crusty roll, 'drowned' in a spicy tomato sauce.

    Where to try it Stalls and markets.

  • Carne en su jugo

    Beef stew with beans and bacon, served in its own broth.

    Where to try it Local restaurants.

  • Tequila and tejuino

    Tequila is the region's spirit, and tejuino is a fermented corn drink served with lime and ice.

    Where to try it Bars and street stands.

  • Jericalla and sweets

    A custard-like dessert, and cajeta.

    Where to try it Markets and bakeries.

  • Tacos and street food

    Tacos, tostadas and elotes.

    Where to try it Street stalls.

Transport

Getting to and around Guadalajara and Tequila

Guadalajara is about 550 km west of Mexico City.

Getting there

  • Flight

    Direct flights from Mexico City (1 hour 15 minutes), US cities and Europe.

    Take a taxi or Uber from the airport.

  • Bus from Mexico City

    About 7–8 hours by first-class bus.

    Prefer flights.

  • To Tequila

    About 1 hour by bus or car, or by the Tequila Express train.

    Buses leave from the Old Bus Station.

Getting around

The centre is walkable, and Uber and light rail cover the rest.

  • Uber and taxis

    Cheap and convenient.

    Agree fares.

  • Light rail and buses

    Line 1 and the Macrobús serve the centre.

    Buy a card.

  • Walking

    The historic centre is compact.

    Watch the traffic.

Itinerary

Three days in Guadalajara and Tequila

The centre and murals on the first day, Tequila on the second, Tlaquepaque on the third.

  1. Day1

    The centre

    Murals and markets.

    • Morning The Cathedral and the Hospicio Cabañas.
    • Afternoon The San Juan de Dios market.
    • Evening Mariachi and birria.
  2. Day2

    Tequila

    Agave country.

    • Morning The Tequila Express or a bus to Tequila.
    • Afternoon A distillery tour and lunch.
    • Evening Return to Guadalajara.
  3. Day3

    Tlaquepaque

    Crafts and food.

    • Morning Tlaquepaque's workshops.
    • Afternoon Lunch and Tonalá.
    • Evening Last dinner.

Travel tips

Guadalajara and Tequila travel tips

Guadalajara is easy, and a few tips help.

  • Book distillery tours in advance.
  • Eat birria for breakfast or lunch.
  • Do not drink and drive after tastings.
  • Use Uber at night.
  • Check advice for Jalisco state, which has areas of concern.
  • Bring cash for markets.
  • Go to Tlaquepaque on a weekday.

FAQ

Guadalajara and Tequila: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Guadalajara?

Three, including a day in Tequila.

Is Guadalajara safe?

Tourist areas are generally safe, but Jalisco state has seen serious cartel violence in some areas. Check your government's advice and use normal precautions.

What is the difference between tequila and mezcal?

Tequila is made only from blue agave and mainly in Jalisco. Mezcal can be made from many agave types and comes mostly from Oaxaca.

How far is Tequila from Guadalajara?

About 60 km, or one hour.

What is the best time to visit?

October to April for dry weather.

Is the Hospicio Cabañas worth visiting?

Yes, for Orozco's murals, one of the great sights in Mexico.
