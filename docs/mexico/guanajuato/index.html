Welcome to Guanajuato

Guanajuato sits in a narrow ravine in the Mexican highlands, and it grew rich in the 18th century on silver. At its peak, its mines produced a large share of the world's silver, and the wealth paid for churches, theatres and mansions that still stand. It was also the cradle of the Mexican independence movement: the first major battle of 1810 took place at the Alhóndiga de Granaditas.

The city's oddities come from its geography. Streets are too narrow and steep for cars, so many of the main roads run underground through old mining tunnels. Houses in bright colours climb the hills, and tiny plazas and alleys, such as the Callejón del Beso, where balconies almost touch, fill the centre. UNESCO listed the city and its mines in 1988.

It is also a young city, with a large university. Students in medieval costume lead evening serenades called callejoneadas, cafés and bars are busy, and the annual Cervantino arts festival in October fills the streets with music and theatre.

Why visit

Why visit Guanajuato?

Guanajuato is one of the most charming and unusual cities in Mexico, and it is compact and easy on foot.

  • Colour and shape

    Pastel houses climb steep hillsides in a way that recalls a stage set, and the maze of alleys rewards wandering.

  • Underground streets

    Former mine tunnels carry traffic beneath the centre, an unusual sight and a sensible solution.

  • A young, musical city

    Students, buskers and callejoneada troupes give the evenings a festive air.

  • Historic sites

    The Alhóndiga, Basilica, Teatro Juárez and Universidad de Guanajuato are all within a short walk.

  • Quirky museums

    The Museo de las Momias displays naturally mummified bodies, and the Diego Rivera birthplace holds early works.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Guanajuato

The centre is compact, and two days is enough for the main sights.

  1. 01

    The Callejón del Beso

    A tiny alley where the balconies of two houses are so close that neighbours could kiss across them, and according to legend two lovers did. It is a local ritual to kiss on the third step.

    Tip Go early to avoid the crowds.

  2. 02

    The Pípila monument and viewpoint

    A statue of a folk hero of the independence war on a hill above the centre, with a panorama of the city. A funicular runs up from the Teatro Juárez.

    Tip Go at sunset.

  3. 03

    The Alhóndiga de Granaditas

    A former granary turned fortress, where the first great battle of the independence war took place in 1810. It is now a regional museum with murals by José Chávez Morado.

    Tip Read the plaques for the story.

  4. 04

    The Basilica and Plaza de la Paz

    A yellow baroque basilica housing the Virgin of Guanajuato, surrounded by mansions and cafés.

    Tip Have a coffee on the plaza.

  5. 05

    The Teatro Juárez and Jardín de la Unión

    A grand neoclassical theatre with a colonnade and lions, next to the leafy garden that is the city's living room.

    Tip Visit the theatre's interior.

  6. 06

    The Museo de las Momias

    A museum of naturally mummified bodies from the local cemetery, a macabre and famous attraction.

    Tip Not for the squeamish or for small children.

  7. 07

    The Diego Rivera Museum

    The house where the muralist was born, now a museum with a collection of his early works and family furniture.

    Tip Check opening hours.

Things to do

Best things to do in Guanajuato

Guanajuato is for slow walking, and for evenings out.

  1. Get lost in the alleys

    Follow the stairs and lanes uphill, and stop for coffee in the tiny plazas.

    Tip Wear comfortable shoes.

  2. Join a callejoneada

    Students in traditional costume lead a singing, drinking, storytelling walk through the alleys at night.

    Tip Book a ticket the same day.

  3. Ride the funicular to Pípila

    A short ride with views over the whole city.

    Tip Stay for dusk.

  4. Attend the Cervantino festival

    A month-long arts festival in October, with performers from around the world.

    Tip Book accommodation months ahead.

  5. Visit a silver mine

    The Valenciana mine and its church are just outside the centre.

    Tip Combine with a taxi tour.

  6. Explore the tunnels

    Walk or drive through the underground streets, some of which follow old river beds.

    Tip Watch for traffic.

Local food

What to eat in Guanajuato

Guanajuato's cooking is central Mexican, with enchiladas mineras, miners' style, as its signature.

  • Enchiladas mineras

    Tortillas dipped in chile sauce, filled with cheese and served with potatoes, carrots and chicken, the miners' dish.

    Where to try it Restaurants in the centre.

  • Guacamayas

    Crusty bolillo rolls stuffed with pork crackling and a hot salsa.

    Where to try it Street stalls.

  • Nieves and paletas

    Sorbets and ice pops of local fruit.

    Where to try it Ice-cream shops.

  • Cajeta and sweets

    Goat's milk caramel and candied fruit, sold in the markets.

    Where to try it Mercado Hidalgo.

  • Tacos and carnitas

    Simple tacos at busy stands and in the market.

    Where to try it Mercado Hidalgo.

  • Local wine and mezcal

    Wine from the surrounding vineyards, and small-batch mezcal.

    Where to try it Bars in the centre.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Guanajuato

Stay in the historic centre, in a restored mansion or guesthouse, to walk everywhere.

Transport

Getting to and around Guanajuato

Guanajuato is about 350 km north-west of Mexico City.

Getting there

  • Bus from Mexico City

    About 5 hours from the Terminal Norte, with several daily services.

    Buy tickets online.

  • Flight to Del Bajío (BJX)

    Flights from Mexico City and US cities, then a 45-minute transfer.

    Book a shuttle.

  • From San Miguel de Allende

    About 1 hour 30 minutes by road.

    Buses run regularly.

Getting around

The centre is walkable, and taxis and buses cover the rest.

  • Walking

    Most sights are within a short walk, with steep climbs.

    Wear good shoes.

  • Taxis

    Cheap and plentiful, useful for hilltop sites.

    Agree the fare.

  • Funicular

    Runs from the Teatro Juárez to Pípila.

    Cheap and quick.

When to go

Best time to visit Guanajuato

At 2,000 m, Guanajuato has a mild climate, with a rainy season from June to September.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Best time

    Dry, sunny and cool in the evening.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm and dry.

  • Summer (June–September)

    Quiet

    Afternoon rain, green hills, fewer visitors.

  • Autumn (October–November)

    Best time

    Pleasant, with the Cervantino festival in October.

Worth planning around: The Cervantino weeks if you want quiet, and Semana Santa.

Travel tips

Guanajuato travel tips

Guanajuato is small and walkable, and a few tips help.

  • Wear comfortable shoes. The streets are steep and cobbled.
  • Book a hotel with a central location.
  • Go early to the Callejón del Beso.
  • Join a callejoneada for a memorable evening.
  • Watch for traffic in the tunnels, which are also used by pedestrians.
  • Book well ahead for the Cervantino festival.
  • Bring a jacket for the evenings.

FAQ

Guanajuato: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Guanajuato?

Two days is enough for the sights. Add a day for the mines or a trip to San Miguel de Allende.

Is Guanajuato safe?

The city's tourist centre is generally regarded as safe, though the state has seen violence elsewhere. Check your government's advice and use normal precautions.

What is a callejoneada?

A night-time walking musical parade through the alleys, led by students in traditional costume, with songs and stories.

What is the best time to visit?

October to April. The Cervantino festival in October is lively, and busy.

Can I visit as a day trip from San Miguel de Allende?

Yes, but it is a long day. An overnight stay is better.

Why are there tunnels?

Because the ravine is too narrow for roads, engineers built roads in old mine tunnels and river channels to relieve traffic in the 20th century.
