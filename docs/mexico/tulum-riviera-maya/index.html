Welcome to Tulum and the Riviera Maya

The Caribbean coast of the Yucatán, known as the Riviera Maya, runs about 130 km from Cancún south to Tulum, a string of beaches, coves, lagoons and small resort towns backed by jungle. Offshore lies the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the second-largest in the world. Before the Spanish arrived, the coast was a busy Maya trading route, and its ruins are dotted along the shore.

Tulum, the Maya walled city, is the most photographed: a small but beautifully sited fortress of temples on a limestone cliff, with a white beach below. It was an important port between about 1200 and 1500 CE and was still occupied when the Spanish arrived. Nearby, Cobá is a much larger city in the jungle with a pyramid you can climb, and the Sian Ka'an biosphere reserve protects mangroves, lagoons and reef.

Development has been intense, and Tulum in particular has changed from a hippie hideaway to a fashionable, expensive resort strip. It still offers wonderful swimming, cenotes and easy access to the Maya world, and quieter alternatives exist in Akumal, Bacalar and Sian Ka'an. Sargassum seaweed can affect the beaches in some seasons.

Why visit

Why visit Tulum and the Riviera Maya?

Tulum and the Riviera Maya combine beaches, snorkelling, cenotes and ruins within a short drive.

  • Ruins above the sea

    Tulum's temples stand on a cliff, with the Caribbean turquoise behind, one of the most striking settings for any ancient site.

  • Cenotes

    Dozens of sinkholes, from open pools to caves with stalactites, are within a short drive.

  • Snorkelling and diving

    Sea turtles graze at Akumal, and the reef offers snorkelling, scuba and freediving.

  • Cobá

    A large jungle city with sacbé roads, stelae and the tallest pyramid in the northern Yucatán.

  • Nature reserves

    Sian Ka'an and the lagoon of Bacalar offer wildlife, kayaking and quiet.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Tulum and the Riviera Maya

The ruins take half a day, and the beaches, cenotes and reserve fill the rest.

  1. 01

    The Tulum ruins

    A walled Maya city of about 60 buildings on a cliff, including El Castillo, the Temple of the Frescoes and the Temple of the Descending God. A small beach lies below.

    Tip Go at opening or late afternoon, and swim from the beach.

  2. 02

    Cobá

    A jungle city spread over lakes, linked by raised stone roads, with the 42-metre Nohoch Mul pyramid, the highest in the region. Bikes and pedicabs are available inside.

    Tip Rent a bike at the entrance. Check whether climbing is permitted.

  3. 03

    Gran Cenote and Dos Ojos

    Two of the most popular cenotes, with crystal-clear water, stalactites and cave diving and snorkelling routes.

    Tip Go early, before the tour groups.

  4. 04

    Akumal Bay

    A sheltered bay where green sea turtles graze on seagrass, easily seen while snorkelling. Regulations require a guide and a life jacket.

    Tip Follow the rules and do not touch the turtles.

  5. 05

    Sian Ka'an Biosphere Reserve

    A UNESCO reserve of about 5,000 km² of mangroves, lagoons, jungle and reef south of Tulum, with dolphins, manatees, crocodiles and over 300 bird species.

    Tip Book a guided boat trip.

  6. 06

    Playa del Carmen and Cozumel

    A lively resort town with a pedestrian Fifth Avenue, and a ferry to Cozumel, an island with excellent diving.

    Tip Use Playa as a transport hub.

Things to do

Best things to do in Tulum and the Riviera Maya

The Riviera Maya is built around sun, sea and short excursions.

  1. Visit Tulum at opening

    Explore the ruins in cool light, then swim at the small beach below.

    Tip Arrive before 09:00.

  2. Swim in a cenote

    Try the Gran Cenote, Dos Ojos, Cenote Calavera or Casa Cenote.

    Tip Bring reef-safe sunscreen and rinse first.

  3. Snorkel with turtles at Akumal

    Green turtles swim in the shallows of the bay.

    Tip Go in the morning.

  4. Rent a bike at Cobá

    Ride through the jungle between temples, and climb the pyramid if allowed.

    Tip Go early to avoid heat.

  5. Kayak at Bacalar

    The Lagoon of Seven Colours, two hours south, has clear water in many blues, stromatolites and kayaks.

    Tip Stay a night.

  6. Relax on a beach

    Beaches from Playa del Carmen to Tulum offer white sand and clear water, though sargassum can affect them in spring and summer.

    Tip Check current sargassum reports.

Local food

What to eat in Tulum and the Riviera Maya

The coast serves Yucatecan classics and fresh seafood, along with a growing international food scene.

  • Ceviche and fresh seafood

    Raw fish cured in lime with onion, tomato and coriander, and grilled catch of the day.

    Where to try it Beach shacks and restaurants.

  • Cochinita pibil and tacos

    Yucatecan pork and grilled fish tacos, sold in taquerías in Tulum town.

    Where to try it Taquerías off the beach road.

  • Fresh coconut and fruit

    Coconut water and sliced mango or papaya with chile and lime.

    Where to try it Beach vendors.

  • Lionfish

    An invasive species that is now hunted and served on menus. It has a mild, sweet flavour.

    Where to try it Seafood restaurants.

  • Craft cocktails and mezcal

    Cocktail bars and beach clubs pour mezcal margaritas.

    Where to try it Beach clubs in Tulum.

  • Guacamole and tortillas

    Made to order at the table.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Tulum and the Riviera Maya

Choose between Tulum town (cheaper, walkable), the beach road (pricey, atmospheric) and the quieter smaller towns.

  • Tulum town

    Guesthouses, hostels and small hotels with restaurants and tour operators.

    Best for: Value and restaurants

  • The Tulum beach road

    Boutique hotels and eco-lodges along the sand, expensive and often lacking mains power and water.

    Best for: Beach access

  • Akumal, Bacalar and Sian Ka'an

    Smaller places away from the crowds.

    Best for: Quiet

Transport

Getting to and around Tulum and the Riviera Maya

Tulum is about 130 km south of Cancún.

Getting there

  • Cancún airport (CUN)

    The main gateway, with buses and shuttles running to Tulum in about 1 hour 30 minutes to 2 hours.

    Pre-book a transfer.

  • Tulum International Airport (TQO)

    A new airport near Tulum with a growing number of flights.

    Check current routes.

  • Bus or Tren Maya

    ADO buses run along the coast, and the Tren Maya connects Cancún with Tulum.

    Check the timetable.

Getting around

Colectivos, taxis and hire cars link the towns, and bicycles are common in Tulum.

  • Colectivos

    Shared vans run along Highway 307 between Playa del Carmen and Tulum, cheaply.

    Flag them from the road.

  • Taxis

    Taxis in Tulum are expensive, particularly along the beach road. Agree the fare.

    Uber is restricted in some places.

  • Hire car or bike

    A car gives freedom to reach cenotes and Cobá, and bikes are useful in town and on the beach road.

    Watch for police checkpoints and speed bumps.

When to go

Best time to visit Tulum and the Riviera Maya

The dry winter and spring are the best time. Summer is hot and rainy, sargassum is worse from April to August, and hurricanes can occur from June to November.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Best time

    Warm, sunny and dry, with clear water. The high season.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Shoulder

    Hot, with increasing sargassum on some beaches.

  • Summer (June–September)

    Think twice

    Hot, humid and rainy, with hurricane risk and sargassum. Low prices.

  • Autumn (October–November)

    Shoulder

    Peak hurricane risk in October. Improving by late November.

Worth planning around: Hurricane season, and Christmas and Easter for crowds.

Travel tips

Tulum and the Riviera Maya travel tips

Tulum is popular, and a few tips help.

  • Go to Tulum and Cobá early, before the heat and the tour groups.
  • Use reef-safe sunscreen, and rinse off before entering cenotes.
  • Check sargassum reports and hurricane forecasts before you book.
  • Carry pesos. Many restaurants in Tulum quote in dollars at poor rates.
  • Book beach hotels and restaurants ahead in winter.
  • Follow rules on turtles, reefs and protected areas.
  • Do not drive at night.

FAQ

Tulum and the Riviera Maya: frequently asked questions

Is Tulum worth visiting?

Yes, for the ruins, beaches and cenotes, though it has become expensive and crowded. Quieter alternatives include Akumal, Bacalar and Sian Ka'an.

Is sargassum a problem?

It can be from April to August, with some beaches covered in seaweed. Conditions vary by week and beach, so check reports.

Can I climb the pyramid at Cobá?

Climbing has been restricted in recent years. Check the current rules at the entrance.

How many days do you need?

Three or four for Tulum, Cobá and cenotes. A week lets you add Bacalar and Sian Ka'an.

How far is Tulum from Cancún?

About 130 km, or 1.5 to 2 hours by road.

Is the Riviera Maya safe?

It is heavily visited, and most visits are trouble-free. Petty crime and occasional violence occur, so check your government's advice and use common sense.
