Welcome to San Miguel de Allende

San Miguel de Allende sits at 1,900 metres in the highlands of central Mexico, founded by the Spanish in 1542 and enriched by silver and the road that carried it. It was almost abandoned in the early 20th century, and that neglect preserved its colonial centre. In the 1930s American artists arrived, including Stirling Dickinson, who founded an art school, and the town has been a magnet for creative visitors and retirees ever since.

Its symbol is the Parroquia de San Miguel Arcángel, whose pink neo-Gothic spires were designed in the 1880s by a self-taught indigenous builder who reportedly used postcards of European cathedrals as a guide. Around it, the Jardín, the central square, hosts musicians, vendors and families every evening, and the surrounding streets are lined with pastel houses, arched doorways and hidden courtyards. UNESCO listed the centre, and the nearby Sanctuary of Atotonilco, in 2008.

It is polished and relatively expensive, with a large foreign community, and some visitors find it too gentrified. Others enjoy it for that reason: good restaurants, boutique hotels, galleries and rooftop bars, with hot springs, vineyards and hiking a few minutes away.

Why visit

Why visit San Miguel de Allende?

San Miguel is an easy, beautiful town for a relaxed few days, and a comfortable introduction to colonial Mexico.

  • A perfectly preserved centre

    Cobbled streets, ochre and pink façades and courtyards make a compact and photogenic centre.

  • Art and crafts

    Galleries, artisan markets and the Fábrica La Aurora arts complex reflect the town's artistic tradition.

  • Food and drink

    Some of the best restaurants outside Mexico City, and rooftop terraces with views.

  • Hot springs and countryside

    Natural thermal pools, wineries and hills lie close to the town.

  • A base for the region

    Guanajuato and Querétaro are both within a couple of hours.

Main attractions

Top things to see in San Miguel de Allende

The centre is compact, and the sights are all within walking distance.

  1. 01

    The Parroquia and the Jardín

    The town's main church and its central square, shaded by laurel trees and busy on weekends. The pink stone glows at sunset.

    Tip Sit on a bench and watch the evening promenade.

  2. 02

    The Bellas Artes Cultural Centre

    A former convent with a courtyard, murals by David Alfaro Siqueiros and a rotating programme of exhibitions and concerts.

    Tip Free to enter.

  3. 03

    Fábrica La Aurora

    A former textile factory turned into a complex of galleries, design shops and cafés.

    Tip Good for an afternoon.

  4. 04

    Mercado de Artesanías

    A large craft market with tin, glass, ceramics and textiles.

    Tip Bargain politely.

  5. 05

    The Santuario de Atotonilco

    An 18th-century sanctuary with vividly painted walls and ceilings, called the Sistine Chapel of Mexico, and a key site for pilgrims.

    Tip About 15 minutes' drive from town.

  6. 06

    Cañada de la Virgen

    A pre-Hispanic pyramid complex 30 minutes from town, with astronomical alignments.

    Tip Visit with a guide.

  7. 07

    The Mirador

    A hilltop viewpoint with a panorama of the town and its spires.

    Tip Go at sunset.

Things to do

Best things to do in San Miguel de Allende

San Miguel is for wandering, eating and unwinding.

  1. Watch the sunset from a rooftop

    Several rooftop bars overlook the Parroquia, with mezcal cocktails.

    Tip Arrive before sunset to get a table.

  2. Soak in hot springs

    Thermal pools at La Gruta, Escondido Place and Xote, a short drive outside town.

    Tip Book in advance.

  3. Take a cooking class

    Learn to make salsas, tortillas and mole from local cooks.

    Tip Combine with a market visit.

  4. Visit galleries and studios

    Dozens of galleries show contemporary Mexican art.

    Tip Many close on Mondays.

  5. Go horse-riding or hiking

    Trails run through the hills and along the Cañada de la Virgen.

    Tip Book a guide.

  6. Sample local wine

    Vineyards in the Guanajuato wine region offer tastings.

    Tip Hire a driver.

Local food

What to eat in San Miguel de Allende

San Miguel's restaurants range from Mexican classics to international cuisine, and the town has a strong farm-to-table scene.

  • Modern Mexican

    Chefs reinterpret regional dishes with local ingredients.

    Where to try it Restaurants around the centre.

  • Tacos and street food

    Carnitas, al pastor and birria at stalls and taquerías.

    Where to try it Ignacio Ramírez market.

  • Churros and hot chocolate

    Fresh churros with thick chocolate.

    Where to try it Churrerías near the Jardín.

  • Guanajuato wine

    Reds and whites from nearby vineyards.

    Where to try it Wine bars and restaurants.

  • Coffee and pastries

    Excellent cafés serve local coffee and baked goods.

    Where to try it Cafés in the centre.

  • Mezcal cocktails

    Cocktail bars pour mezcal-based drinks.

    Where to try it Rooftop bars.

Accommodation

Where to stay in San Miguel de Allende

Boutique hotels in restored mansions dominate, with a range of prices.

  • Centro

    Boutique hotels and guesthouses within a few blocks of the Jardín.

    Best for: Walking

  • Guadiana and Ojo de Agua

    Residential neighbourhoods with guesthouses, a 15-minute walk from the centre.

    Best for: Quiet and value

Transport

Getting to and around San Miguel de Allende

San Miguel is about 3.5 hours from Mexico City by road.

Getting there

  • Bus from Mexico City

    About 4 hours from the Terminal Norte.

    Buy tickets in advance.

  • Flight to Del Bajío or Querétaro

    Then a transfer of 1 to 1 hour 15 minutes.

    Book a shuttle.

  • From Guanajuato

    About 1 hour 30 minutes by bus or car.

    Buses run regularly.

Getting around

The centre is small and walkable.

  • Walking

    Sights are within 15 minutes of each other, on cobbled, sloping streets.

    Wear flat shoes.

  • Taxis

    Cheap and plentiful.

    Agree the fare.

  • Tours

    For hot springs, wineries and Atotonilco.

    Book through your hotel.

When to go

Best time to visit San Miguel de Allende

The climate is mild at 1,900 m, with a rainy season from June to September.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Best time

    Dry, sunny and cool in the evenings.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm and dry.

  • Summer (June–September)

    Quiet

    Afternoon rain, green hills and lower prices.

  • Autumn (October–November)

    Best time

    Clear and pleasant, with Día de Muertos.

Worth planning around: Semana Santa and Christmas for crowds.

Travel tips

San Miguel de Allende travel tips

San Miguel is easy, and a few tips help.

  • Book weekends and holidays ahead.
  • Wear flat shoes for the cobbles.
  • Visit the Jardín on a Sunday evening.
  • Prices are higher than elsewhere in Mexico.
  • Bring a jacket for the evenings.
  • Book hot springs in advance.
  • Use taxis at night.

FAQ

San Miguel de Allende: frequently asked questions

Is San Miguel de Allende worth visiting?

Yes, for its architecture, food and atmosphere. It is touristy and expensive but very pretty.

How many days do you need?

Two or three, longer if you want to relax.

Is it safe?

The town centre is generally safe, and popular with expats. Check your government's advice for Guanajuato state.

How far is it from Mexico City?

About 280 km, or 3.5 to 4 hours by road.

What is Atotonilco?

A pilgrimage sanctuary with painted walls, a UNESCO-listed site, near San Miguel.

What is the best time to visit?

October to April for dry weather.
