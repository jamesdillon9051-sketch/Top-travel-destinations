The Complete Colombia Travel Guide

Colombia combines Caribbean beaches, Andean cities, coffee hills and colonial villages with warm people and good value. This guide covers how the regions divide, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to get around safely, when to go, what it costs and three routes that work.

How Colombia divides

Colombia is about 1,600 km from north to south, and it has roughly five zones for a visitor.

The Caribbean coast. Cartagena is the main gateway, with Tayrona and Santa Marta to the east and San Andrés out at sea.

The Andes around Bogotá. Bogotá is the capital, with Villa de Leyva and Barichara as colonial villages.

Antioquia. Medellín and Guatapé have spring-like weather and lakes.

The Coffee Axis. Salento and Cocora are the centre of coffee country.

The Llanos and Amazon. Caño Cristales is a remote river of colours.

Worth knowing: The practical unit is two or three of these, linked by cheap flights. Bogotá, Medellín and Pereira are all one hour apart.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If you have one week

    Cartagena for the coast and Bogotá for the capital, or Medellín with the Coffee Region.

  • If you love colonial towns

    Cartagena, Villa de Leyva and Barichara are among the best preserved in the Americas.

  • If you love hiking and nature

    Cocora Valley, Tayrona and Caño Cristales are the highlights.

  • If you love cities

    Bogotá for culture and Medellín for innovation and nightlife.

  • If you love beaches

    Tayrona, the Rosario Islands from Cartagena and San Andrés.

  • If you love coffee

    The Coffee Region has farms and tours, and Medellín has excellent specialty cafés.

Getting around safely

Colombia is easy to navigate by air, and slower by land.

Flights. Fly between Bogotá, Medellín, Cartagena, Santa Marta and Pereira. Fares are low if booked early.

Buses. Comfortable but slow in the mountains. Travel by day.

Ride-hailing. Uber, InDrive and Cabify are safer and cheaper than street taxis in cities.

Local transport. Colectivos serve towns such as Salento, and jeeps take you to trailheads.

Worth knowing: Do not use your phone in the street, and keep bags close on buses.

When to go

Colombia's climate depends on altitude and region, and there are two rainy seasons in the Andes.

December to March is the main dry season in the Andes and Caribbean, and the best window for most trips.

April and May are wetter, with afternoon showers in the Andes.

June to August is a second drier spell in the Andes, and the best months for Villa de Leyva and Caño Cristales (from late June).

September to November is the wettest period in much of the country.

Worth knowing: The Caribbean coast is hot year-round, and Bogotá is cool year-round.

Money, costs and safety

Colombia is good value. A restaurant meal costs US$5–15, a mid-range hotel US$50–100, and an internal flight US$40–100.

Cards are accepted in cities. Carry cash for small towns and colectivos.

Safety takes common sense. Use ride-hailing apps, avoid displaying valuables, and never leave drinks unattended.

Check current government advice for remote regions and border areas.

Three routes that work

  1. One week: Bogotá and Cartagena

    Three nights in Bogotá with a day at Villa de Leyva, then four in Cartagena.

    Tip Fly between them, about 1.5 hours.

  2. Ten days: adding coffee country

    Three nights in Bogotá, three in Medellín with Guatapé, and three in Salento.

    Tip Fly from Medellín to Pereira.

  3. Two weeks: the classic

    The itinerary on the country page: Bogotá, Villa de Leyva, Medellín, Guatapé, the Coffee Region, Cartagena and Tayrona.

    Tip Swap Tayrona for San Andrés if you prefer a Caribbean island.

A perfectly preserved colonial town three hours from Bogotá, with the huge Plaza Mayor, whitewashed streets, Iguaque hikes, waterfalls, fossils and wineries.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Colombia

Should I fly or take buses?

Fly between regions, since domestic flights are cheap and roads are winding. Use buses for shorter hops such as Bogotá to Villa de Leyva or Medellín to Guatapé.

Is it safe to travel independently?

Yes on the main tourist routes, with precautions. Use ride-hailing apps, avoid the street at night with valuables, and follow local advice.

How do I handle the altitude?

Bogotá is at 2,640 metres. Drink water, go easy on the first day and limit alcohol. Medellín and the coast are lower.

When should I book?

Book Tayrona and Ciudad Perdida several weeks ahead, and Cartagena hotels far ahead for December and January.
