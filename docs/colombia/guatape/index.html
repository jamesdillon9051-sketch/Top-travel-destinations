Welcome to Guatapé

Guatapé is a small town about two hours east of Medellín, set on a large artificial reservoir created in the 1970s to feed a hydroelectric plant. The flooded valley left a landscape of green islands and inlets, and a giant rock, La Piedra del Peñol, a 200-metre granite monolith rising from the shore.

The town is known for its zócalos, colourful bas-relief panels along the base of the houses, which show scenes of local life, animals and family names in bright colours. The Plazoleta de los Zócalos and the side streets are a pleasure to walk, with bars, shops and boats on the waterfront.

El Peñol has a zigzag staircase of about 740 steps, built into a crack in the rock, which takes 20 to 30 minutes to climb and rewards you with a huge view over the reservoir. The trip is a classic day out from Medellín, though staying overnight allows quieter time in the evening.
