Welcome to Cartagena

Cartagena de Indias was founded by the Spanish in 1533, and for two centuries it was the main port through which the wealth of the Americas, including gold and emeralds, was shipped to Spain. Pirates and privateers, including Sir Francis Drake, attacked it repeatedly, and in response the Spanish built miles of stone walls and forts that survive today. The walled city and its fortresses are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Inside the walls, the old town is a maze of cobbled streets, plazas and coloured houses with wooden balconies draped in bougainvillea. Sidewalk cafés, fruit vendors, palenqueras in bright dresses and horse-drawn carriages give it a theatrical air, and in the evening the plazas fill with music. Just outside the walls, Getsemaní, once a working-class quarter, is now a lively neighbourhood of street art, hostels and bars.

Cartagena is hot and humid, so days are best spent early or on the water. The Castillo San Felipe de Barajas fortress is the largest built by the Spanish in the Americas, and boats take visitors to the Rosario Islands for snorkelling and beaches. Three or four days is a good stay, and the city is a natural start or end to a Colombian trip.

  • A walled colonial city

  • Getsemaní

  • Music and nightlife

    Salsa, champeta and rooftop bars.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Cartagena

The old city is compact and best on foot, with the fortress and islands as excursions.

  1. 01

    The Clock Tower and the walled city

    The Torre del Reloj is the main gate into the walled city, opening onto the Plaza de los Coches and a maze of colonial streets.

    Tip Enter at dusk.

  2. 02

    Castillo San Felipe de Barajas

    The largest Spanish fortress in the Americas, built in the 17th century on a hill with tunnels and cannon.

    Tip Go early, since it is hot.

  3. 03

    The walls and sunset

    The ramparts around the old city make a lovely walk, with a sunset drinking spot at Café del Mar.

    Tip Arrive before sunset.

  4. 04

    Rosario Islands

    A coral archipelago about an hour by boat from the city, with clear water, snorkelling and beach clubs.

    Tip Book a boat for a full day.

  5. 05

    Getsemaní

    A colourful neighbourhood of murals, plazas, hostels and bars, centred on Plaza de la Trinidad.

    Tip Come in the evening.

  6. 06

    Convent of La Popa

    A hilltop convent with views over the city.

    Tip Take a taxi.

Things to do

Best things to do in Cartagena

Cartagena rewards wandering, eating and a day on the water.

  1. Walk the old town

    Early morning or evening.

    Tip Bring water.

  2. Take a boat to the islands

    Snorkel and swim.

    Tip Book with a reputable operator.

  3. Visit the fortress

    Explore the tunnels.

    Tip Go at opening time.

  4. Dance salsa

    In Café Havana or Getsemaní bars.

    Tip Go with friends.

  5. Try a street art tour

    Learn about Getsemaní's murals.

    Tip Book a guide.

  6. Take a mud volcano trip

    Float in El Totumo's mud.

    Tip Half-day trip.

When to go

Best time to visit Cartagena

Cartagena is hot year-round, with a dry season from December to April.

  • December–April

    Best time

    Dry and breezy.

  • May–August

    Good

    Hot and humid, with some rain.

  • September–November

    Think twice

    The wettest months, with flooding possible.

  • Christmas and New Year

    Quiet

    Crowded and expensive.

Worth planning around: October and November rains.

Travel tips

Cartagena travel tips

A few tips help you plan.

  • Go out early and in the evening.
  • Bring sun protection.
  • Use Uber or official taxis.
  • Agree prices before boat trips.
  • Ignore touts on the beach.
  • Keep valuables safe.
  • Book hotels early for December.
