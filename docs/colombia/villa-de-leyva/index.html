Welcome to Villa de Leyva

Villa de Leyva was founded in 1572, and it has been preserved as a national monument since 1954, so building styles, roof tiles and whitewash follow the colonial pattern. It sits at 2,150 metres in a high, dry valley in Boyacá, with a mild, sunny climate that contrasts with cloudy Bogotá.

The heart of the town is the Plaza Mayor, one of the largest cobbled plazas in the Americas at around 14,000 square metres, surrounded by whitewashed buildings and a church. Narrow streets lead off in a grid, past courtyards, museums and cafés. Weekends fill with visitors from Bogotá, but weekdays are quiet.

The surroundings offer plenty to do. There are fossils of a 110-million-year-old kronosaurus at El Fósil, blue pools called Pozos Azules, the Iguaque sanctuary with a sacred lake, waterfalls, wineries and the pottery village of Ráquira. Cycling is popular. It is about three to four hours by bus from Bogotá, and two days is a good stay.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Villa de Leyva

The town is small, and the sights are around it.

  1. 01

    Plaza Mayor

    The enormous cobbled square, with a church and colonial buildings all around.

    Tip Visit early or at dusk.

  2. 02

    Cerro de Iguaque

    A nearby mountain reserve with cloud forest and a lake sacred to the Muisca.

    Tip Start hikes early.

  3. 03

    El Fósil

    A museum around a 110-million-year-old fossil of a marine reptile.

    Tip A short visit.

  4. 04

    Pozos Azules

    Blue and green pools in the desert.

    Tip Go in the morning.

  5. 05

    Ráquira

    A village of colourful handicraft shops and pottery.

    Tip Visit for gifts.

  6. 06

    Casa Terracota

    A house made entirely of clay.

    Tip Unusual architecture.
