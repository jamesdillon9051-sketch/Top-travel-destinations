Welcome to Tayrona

On Colombia's Caribbean coast, the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta rises straight from the sea to snow-capped peaks of more than 5,700 metres, the highest coastal range in the world. At its foot, Tayrona National Park protects a stretch of coast with white-sand coves, granite boulders and tropical dry forest, once home to the Tayrona people. Their descendants, the Kogi, Wiwa and Arhuaco, still live in the mountains.

Tayrona's beaches are reached by hiking trails from the entrance at El Zaino, through forest with monkeys, iguanas and butterflies, to coves such as Arrecifes, La Piscina and Cabo San Juan. Swimming is unsafe on some beaches because of strong currents, so heed the signs. Accommodation ranges from camping and hammocks to eco-lodges inside the park.

Santa Marta, founded in 1525, is South America's oldest surviving Spanish city, and its lively historic centre and waterfront make a base. In the hills above, Minca is a cloud-forest village with coffee farms, waterfalls and birdwatching. For the ambitious, the four- to six-day Ciudad Perdida trek reaches an ancient Tayrona city that predates Machu Picchu by about 650 years.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Tayrona

Tayrona is the star, with Minca and Santa Marta around it.

  1. 01

    Cabo San Juan

    The park's most famous beach, on a rocky cape with a hammock hut on a boulder, reached by a walk of around 2 hours.

    Tip Book hammocks in advance.

  2. 02

    Minca

    A mountain village at 600 metres, with coffee farms, waterfalls, birdwatching and cooler air.

    Tip Take a moto-taxi up.

  3. 03

    Ciudad Perdida

    The Lost City, Teyuna, built around 800 CE, reached by a 4 to 6-day trek through jungle with stays in camps.

    Tip Book with an authorised operator.

  4. 04

    Santa Marta bay

    The waterfront and historic centre of the oldest Spanish city in South America.

    Tip Visit the Gold Museum.

  5. 05

    Playa Cristal

    A snorkelling beach with clear water.

    Tip Reserve a boat trip.

  6. 06

    Quinta de San Pedro Alejandrino

    The hacienda where Simón Bolívar died in 1830.

    Tip A quick visit.

When to go

Best time to visit Tayrona

The coast has a dry season and a wet season.

  • December–March

    Best time

    Dry.

  • April–May

    Quiet

    Hotter and starting to rain.

  • June–August

    Good

    Some rain.

  • September–November

    Think twice

    Wettest, and Tayrona closes for some weeks for maintenance.

Worth planning around: Rainy months and park closure periods.

Travel tips

Tayrona travel tips

A few tips help you plan.

  • Book Tayrona hammocks ahead.
  • Check park closures.
  • Bring cash.
  • Do not swim where signs warn of currents.
  • Pack light.
  • Use sunscreen and insect repellent.
  • Book Ciudad Perdida with an authorised operator.
