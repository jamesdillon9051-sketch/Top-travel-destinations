Welcome to the Westfjords

The Westfjords are genuinely Iceland's least-visited major region, a mountainous arm of fjords in the far northwest that most Ring Road itineraries skip entirely — which is exactly why the travellers who do make the detour tend to talk about it as their favourite part of the country.

The scenery here is genuinely on the same scale as the rest of Iceland — towering bird cliffs, a tiered waterfall that rivals anything on the south coast, a red sand beach — but with a fraction of the traffic, since reaching it means either a long, winding fjord drive or a short domestic flight.

It's genuinely not a place to rush: distances between sights look short on a map but take far longer on narrow, curving fjord roads, so most visitors give the Westfjords three or four unhurried days rather than a single loop.

Why visit

Why visit the Westfjords?

The Westfjords' case rests on genuine remoteness and scenery that rivals anywhere else in Iceland.

  • Látrabjarg is genuinely one of Europe's great bird cliffs

    Iceland's westernmost point, home to hundreds of thousands of nesting puffins and other seabirds in summer.

  • Dynjandi genuinely rivals the south coast's famous waterfalls

    A dramatic tiered cascade widening as it drops, with almost none of Skógafoss's crowds.

  • It's genuinely Iceland's least-visited major region

    The same dramatic fjord and mountain scenery as elsewhere, without the tour buses.

  • Rauðasandur is a genuinely unique red-golden sand beach

    A striking colour shift from Iceland's usual black sand, backed by dramatic mountains.

  • Ísafjörður is genuinely a proper, characterful small town

    Colourful old wooden buildings and real local life at the head of a dramatic fjord.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Westfjords

The Westfjords' sights are genuinely spread far apart, so most visitors pick a loop rather than trying to cover everything.

  1. 01

    Dynjandi

    Genuinely the Westfjords' signature sight, a series of cascades widening as they drop nearly 100 metres, often called the region's Gullfoss.

    Tip The genuinely short but steep trail to the top tier is well worth the climb for the view back down.

  2. 02

    Látrabjarg Bird Cliffs

    Iceland's genuine westernmost point and one of Europe's largest bird cliffs, home to huge numbers of nesting puffins from May to August.

    Tip Approach the genuinely unfenced cliff edge slowly and stay well back — puffins are surprisingly unbothered by quiet visitors.

  3. 03

    Rauðasandur

    A genuinely striking red-golden sand beach, the longest in the Westfjords, in sharp contrast to Iceland's usual black sand coastline.

    Tip The colour genuinely shifts with the light and tide — late afternoon light is especially good.

  4. 04

    Ísafjörður

    The Westfjords' genuine capital, a charming, colourful old fishing town squeezed onto a sandy spit at the head of a dramatic fjord.

    Tip The genuinely well-preserved old harbour district has some of the town's oldest wooden buildings.

  5. 05

    Hornstrandir Nature Reserve

    A genuinely roadless wilderness reachable only by boat or on foot, home to Iceland's healthiest population of curious, unafraid Arctic foxes.

    Tip Multi-day hikers genuinely need to be fully self-sufficient — there are no shops or roads inside the reserve.

  6. 06

    Vigur Island

    A genuinely tiny, car-free farming island in Ísafjarðardjúp fjord, home to Iceland's last working windmill and dense seabird colonies.

    Tip Guided boat tours genuinely include a walk around the whole island and its farm.

  7. 07

    Hólmavík

    A genuinely small fishing town home to the Museum of Icelandic Sorcery and Witchcraft, telling the region's dark 17th-century witch-trial history.

    Tip The genuinely infamous necropants exhibit is stranger than any description does justice to.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Westfjords

The Westfjords genuinely reward slow travel — long drives between a handful of unhurried, uncrowded stops.

  1. Hike into Hornstrandir

    A genuinely serious multi-day wilderness hike, or a shorter guided day trip by boat for a taste of Iceland's remotest terrain.

    Tip Book a genuinely reliable boat transfer in advance — services are limited and weather-dependent.

  2. Spot puffins at Látrabjarg

    Genuinely one of the best, easiest puffin-watching spots in Iceland, with birds often just metres from the cliff-top path in summer.

    Tip Visit genuinely in June or July for the highest concentration of nesting birds.

  3. Photograph Dynjandi's tiers

    A genuinely photogenic climb past several smaller falls before reaching the main cascade at the top.

    Tip Midday light genuinely works well here, unlike many waterfalls that favour early or late light.

  4. Take the boat to Vigur Island

    A genuinely relaxed half-day trip from Ísafjörður, mixing farm life, seabird colonies and Iceland's last standing windmill.

    Tip Tours genuinely run only in summer and need booking ahead.

  5. Learn Iceland's witchcraft history in Hólmavík

    A genuinely fascinating, slightly macabre look at 17th-century Icelandic witch trials and folk magic, including infamous necropants.

    Tip Allow genuinely about an hour, and read the English translations for the full context.

  6. Wander Ísafjörður's old harbour

    A genuinely relaxed wander among some of Iceland's oldest wooden buildings, with good cafés and a lively small-town feel.

    Tip The genuinely small maritime museum here gives useful context on the region's fishing history.

Local food

What to eat in the Westfjords

The Westfjords are genuinely one of Iceland's great fishing regions, and the seafood here reflects it.

  • Fresh Icelandic seafood

    Genuinely as fresh as it gets — the Westfjords remain one of Iceland's most important fishing regions.

    Where to try it Harbourside restaurants in Ísafjörður.

  • Harðfiskur (dried fish)

    A genuinely defining regional product — the Westfjords' cold, windy climate is ideal for traditional fish drying.

    Where to try it Fish shops and markets in Ísafjörður and Bíldudalur.

  • Icelandic lamb

    Genuinely common at guesthouses across the region, often raised on the same fjordside slopes you've been driving past.

    Where to try it Guesthouses and restaurants in Ísafjörður.

  • Skyr

    A genuinely reliable breakfast option at guesthouses throughout the Westfjords.

    Where to try it Guesthouses and the few supermarkets in Ísafjörður.

  • Rúgbrauð

    A genuinely dense, traditional dark rye bread, often served alongside dried or smoked fish.

    Where to try it Cafés and guesthouses in Ísafjörður.

  • Brennivín

    Iceland's genuinely famous caraway schnapps, a fitting way to end a long day of fjord driving in Ísafjörður.

    Where to try it Bars and restaurants in Ísafjörður.

Accommodation

Where to stay in the Westfjords

Most visitors genuinely base themselves in Ísafjörður, with a guesthouse night further south near Látrabjarg.

  • Ísafjörður

    The largest town, with the widest choice of hotels, restaurants and onward tours.

    Best for: The genuine regional hub, with the best services

  • Patreksfjörður area

    Small guesthouses put you within easy reach of the region's southwestern sights.

    Best for: Being genuinely close to Látrabjarg and Rauðasandur

Transport

Getting to and around the Westfjords

The Westfjords are genuinely reached either by a long fjord-road drive or a short domestic flight to Ísafjörður.

Getting there

  • Domestic flight to Ísafjörður

    A genuinely quick 40-minute hop from Reykjavik, the fastest way in and out.

    Flights run several times a week, weather permitting.

  • Self-drive from Reykjavik

    About genuinely 5-7 hours, mostly on narrow, winding fjord roads.

    Route 60 is the main road into and through the region.

  • Ferry from Snæfellsnes

    The genuinely scenic Baldur ferry links Snæfellsnes to the southern Westfjords, cutting out some driving.

    Runs year-round, though less frequently in winter.

Getting around

The Westfjords genuinely demand a car — public transport barely reaches this far.

  • Rental car (4x4 recommended)

    Genuinely essential — distances are long and some roads are gravel, especially in the south.

    Route 60 and its side roads connect all the main sights.

  • Guided day tours from Ísafjörður

    Genuinely cover Vigur Island and some nearby sights for visitors without a car.

    Options are more limited than elsewhere in Iceland — book ahead.

  • Boat to Hornstrandir

    The genuinely only way into the roadless nature reserve.

    Boats run from Ísafjörður in summer only.

When to go

Best time to visit the Westfjords

The Westfjords are genuinely a summer destination for most visitors, since several key roads and the Hornstrandir boats only run from June to August.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Genuinely all roads open, puffins nesting at Látrabjarg, and Hornstrandir accessible by boat.

  • Early autumn (September)

    Good

    Genuinely still accessible with fewer visitors, though some services start winding down.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Genuinely challenging — many roads close, and daylight is very short this far north.

  • Late spring (May)

    Good

    Genuinely quiet, with roads reopening but before the puffins fully arrive.

Worth planning around: Avoid genuinely relying on a self-drive winter visit — several key mountain roads close for the season.

Built around a loop from the south of the region up to Ísafjörður.

  1. Day1

    Dynjandi and the south

    The drive in, via the region's signature waterfall.

    • Afternoon Dynjandi waterfall.
    • Evening Overnight near Patreksfjörður.
  2. Day2

    Látrabjarg and Rauðasandur

    Puffin cliffs and a red sand beach.

    • Morning Látrabjarg's bird cliffs.
    • Afternoon Rauðasandur beach.
    • Evening Drive north to Ísafjörður.
  3. Day3

    Ísafjörður and Vigur Island

    The region's main town and a farming island.

    • Morning Boat tour to Vigur Island.
    • Afternoon Wander Ísafjörður's old harbour district.
    • Evening Dinner in Ísafjörður before departing.

Travel tips

The Westfjords travel tips

The Westfjords reward extra planning — services and roads here are far sparser than elsewhere in Iceland.

  • Book a genuinely reliable rental car with good gravel-road experience.
  • Fill up on genuinely fuel whenever you pass a station — they're far apart here.
  • Check road.is genuinely before setting out, even in summer.
  • Book Vigur Island and Hornstrandir boats genuinely well ahead — capacity is limited.
  • Allow genuinely more driving time than the map suggests — fjord roads are slow and winding.
  • Bring genuinely more cash and supplies than you think you'll need — shops are sparse.

FAQ

The Westfjords: frequently asked questions

Are the Westfjords worth visiting?

Yes, genuinely, for travellers with the extra time — it's Iceland's least-crowded major region, with scenery to match anywhere else in the country.

How do you get to the Westfjords?

Either a genuinely long fjord-road drive from Reykjavik, a short domestic flight to Ísafjörður, or the Baldur ferry from Snæfellsnes.

Is Látrabjarg the best place to see puffins in Iceland?

Yes, genuinely widely considered so — its cliff-top paths bring you remarkably close to huge nesting colonies in summer.

Do you need a 4x4 in the Westfjords?

Not always, but it's genuinely recommended — several roads are gravel and conditions change quickly.

How many days do you need in the Westfjords?

Three to four days genuinely lets you cover the main sights without rushing the long fjord drives.

When do the Westfjords' key roads close?

Several mountain passes genuinely close from around October to May, making summer the most reliable season to visit.
