Welcome to Landmannalaugar & the Highlands

The Icelandic Highlands are a different country from the coastal ring most visitors see — a vast, roadless volcanic interior of black sand deserts, glaciers and mountains streaked improbably with orange, pink and green rhyolite, accessible only by rugged F-roads and only in summer.

Landmannalaugar is the highlands' most accessible gateway: a natural hot spring set among those rainbow mountains, and the starting point of the Laugavegur trail, widely considered one of the world's great multi-day treks.

It's a different kind of Iceland trip — fewer paved roads, fewer facilities, and a real sense of remoteness — which is exactly why the travellers who make it out here tend to rank it among the best few days of their whole visit.

Why visit

Why visit Landmannalaugar & the Highlands?

The Highlands' case rests on scenery and solitude that Iceland's more accessible regions simply can't match.

  • Landmannalaugar's rhyolite mountains are unlike anywhere else

    Streaked in shades of orange, pink, green and black, unlike Iceland's more familiar black-and-green palette.

  • The Laugavegur trail is one of the world's great treks

    Four days through glaciers, deserts and river crossings, ending in the birch valley of Þórsmörk.

  • It's one of the last accessible wildernesses in Europe

    No permanent settlements, no paved roads, and vast stretches without another vehicle in sight.

  • The natural hot spring is free and unspoiled

    A warm stream fed by geothermal water and glacial meltwater, with none of the Blue Lagoon's infrastructure.

  • It's a chance to see Iceland's active volcanoes up close

    Hekla, one of Iceland's most active volcanoes, looms over the highland's northern approach.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Landmannalaugar & the Highlands

The Highlands' sights are spread across a vast area, most reachable only by 4x4 on rugged F-roads.

  1. 01

    Landmannalaugar

    The Highlands' most iconic spot, a natural warm stream set among rhyolite mountains in shades of orange, pink and green.

    Tip Bring your own towel and be ready to change outdoors — facilities are basic.

  2. 02

    The Laugavegur Trail

    A renowned four-day, 55km trek from Landmannalaugar to Þórsmörk, crossing glaciers, deserts and rivers along the way.

    Tip Book mountain huts along the route months ahead in peak summer.

  3. 03

    Hekla

    One of Iceland's most active and historically feared volcanoes, its long ridge visible across much of the southern highlands.

    Tip Climbing requires a guide and current volcanic activity clearance — check before planning.

  4. 04

    Eldgjá Canyon

    One of the largest volcanic canyons on Earth, formed by an enormous 10th-century eruption, with the Ófærufoss waterfall inside it.

    Tip The rough F-road access means only high-clearance 4x4s should attempt the drive.

  5. 05

    Þórsmörk

    A sheltered, surprisingly green valley ringed by three glaciers, and the traditional endpoint of the Laugavegur trail.

    Tip River crossings on the approach road mean only specially equipped highland buses or serious 4x4s get in.

  6. 06

    Kerlingarfjöll

    A striking geothermal mountain range of colourful rhyolite peaks and steaming valleys, quieter than Landmannalaugar.

    Tip The on-site mountain hut makes a good overnight base for exploring the valley.

  7. 07

    Askja & Víti Crater

    A remote volcanic caldera in the northern highlands, with a stark blue lake and the smaller, swimmable Víti crater beside it.

    Tip The long, rough drive means most visitors join a guided highland tour rather than self-driving.

Things to do

Best things to do in Landmannalaugar & the Highlands

The Highlands reward a mix of relaxing at the hot spring and pushing further into the wilderness.

  1. Soak in Landmannalaugar's natural hot spring

    An unspoiled, free alternative to Iceland's commercial spas, warmed by geothermal water flowing into a cold mountain stream.

    Tip Look for the warmer pockets near where the geothermal water enters the stream.

  2. Hike a stage of the Laugavegur trail

    Even a single day-hike stage gives a real taste of the highland scenery, without committing to the full four-day trek.

    Tip The first stage to Hrafntinnusker is a popular, manageable day-hike option.

  3. Cross highland rivers by super jeep

    A thrilling way to reach the more remote corners of the highlands, fording unbridged glacial rivers in a modified 4x4.

    Tip Book with an experienced local operator rather than attempting river crossings in a standard rental.

  4. Swim in the Víti crater at Askja

    A surreal swim in a volcanic crater's warm, milky water, reached by a steep path down from the rim.

    Tip The steep, slippery path down needs sturdy footwear and real care.

  5. Photograph Kerlingarfjöll's rhyolite peaks

    A quieter alternative to Landmannalaugar for the same striking colour palette of volcanic rock.

    Tip Morning light brings out the colour contrasts best.

  6. Camp under the midnight sun

    An unforgettable way to experience the highlands, with campsites at Landmannalaugar and Þórsmörk open through summer.

    Tip Bring a proper four-season tent — highland nights stay cold even in July.

Local food

What to eat in Landmannalaugar & the Highlands

The Highlands are sparse on dining — most food here comes from mountain huts, campsites or what you carry in yourself.

  • Icelandic lamb

    Served at the handful of highland mountain huts that offer cooked dinners to trekkers.

    Where to try it Mountain huts along the Laugavegur trail.

  • Skyr

    A practical, protein-rich trail food, light to carry and sold at highland huts and campsite shops.

    Where to try it Campsite shops at Landmannalaugar and Þórsmörk.

  • Rúgbrauð

    A dense, long-lasting bread that holds up well as trail food over several trekking days.

    Where to try it Packed in from Reykjavik before setting out.

  • Harðfiskur (dried fish)

    A traditional, high-protein trail snack that needs no refrigeration, popular with Laugavegur trekkers.

    Where to try it Packed in from supermarkets before the trek.

  • Hut dinners and trail food

    Simple, hearty meals at the highland huts, from soup to pasta, are a welcome end to a long trekking day.

    Where to try it Booked mountain huts along the Laugavegur trail.

  • Brennivín

    Iceland's famous caraway schnapps, occasionally shared around a mountain hut table after a long day's hike.

    Where to try it Carried in — the huts themselves rarely sell alcohol.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Landmannalaugar & the Highlands

Highland accommodation is limited to mountain huts and campsites, both needing advance booking in summer.

  • Landmannalaugar hut and campsite

    Basic but well-located, with the natural hot spring right beside it.

    Best for: The genuine gateway to the hot spring and trail

  • Þórsmörk huts

    Sheltered valley huts, popular with trekkers finishing the trail.

    Best for: The genuine end of the Laugavegur trek

Transport

Getting to and around Landmannalaugar & the Highlands

The Highlands are reached only by rugged F-roads, open for a short summer season only.

Getting there

  • Highland bus from Reykjavik

    The most common option for hikers, running directly to Landmannalaugar in summer.

    Specially adapted buses handle the unbridged river crossings on the way.

  • Self-drive (serious 4x4 only)

    Possible but demanding — F-roads require a proper high-clearance 4x4 and river-crossing experience.

    Standard rental cars are not permitted or insured on F-roads.

  • Guided highland tour

    The easiest way to see Askja and other remote sights without self-driving.

    Multi-day and day tours run from Reykjavik in summer.

Getting around

Once inside the highlands, getting around means hiking, highland buses, or a well-equipped 4x4.

  • On foot

    The only way to properly experience the Laugavegur trail and the terrain around each hut.

    Trails are well-marked but require full trekking self-sufficiency.

  • Highland bus network

    Links the main huts and trailheads for hikers not doing the full point-to-point trek.

    Services run only from around late June to early September.

  • 4x4 with river-crossing experience

    Necessary for reaching Þórsmörk, Eldgjá or Askja by road.

    Check current river and road conditions before setting out.

When to go

Best time to visit Landmannalaugar & the Highlands

The Highlands are a short-season destination, with F-roads and huts only open for about ten weeks each summer.

  • Mid-summer (July–early August)

    Best time

    The safest window, with roads open, huts staffed and rivers at their most manageable levels.

  • Late June

    Good

    Quieter, though some snow can still linger on higher passes.

  • Late August–early September

    Good

    Fewer crowds, though huts and roads start closing for the season.

  • Rest of the year

    Good

    Inaccessible — F-roads, huts and highland buses all close outside the short summer season.

Worth planning around: Avoid planning a highland trip outside late June to early September — the interior is simply closed.

Itinerary

Two days at Landmannalaugar

Built around the hot spring and a taste of the Laugavegur trail, without committing to the full multi-day trek.

  1. Day1

    Arrival and the hot spring

    Travelling in and settling by the natural hot spring.

    • Morning Highland bus or 4x4 drive in to Landmannalaugar.
    • Afternoon Explore the surrounding rhyolite mountains on a short loop trail.
    • Evening Soak in the natural hot spring as the light softens.
  2. Day2

    A taste of the Laugavegur trail

    A day-hike into the highlands before heading back.

    • Morning Hike the first stage of the Laugavegur trail toward Hrafntinnusker.
    • Afternoon Return to Landmannalaugar for a final soak.
    • Evening Highland bus back toward Reykjavik or the south coast.

Travel tips

Landmannalaugar & the Highlands travel tips

The Highlands demand more preparation than anywhere else in Iceland — plan accordingly.

  • Book mountain huts months ahead if hiking the Laugavegur trail in July or August.
  • Never attempt F-road river crossings in a standard rental car.
  • Check safetravel.is for current highland road and weather conditions before setting out.
  • Pack full trekking gear, even for a single day hike — weather changes fast up here.
  • Bring more cash and supplies than expected — huts have very limited shops.
  • Confirm the highland bus season dates before planning your trip, since they're short and weather-dependent.

FAQ

Landmannalaugar & the Highlands: frequently asked questions

Is Landmannalaugar worth visiting?

Yes, for travellers with the time — it's one of the most distinctive landscapes in Iceland, with a free natural hot spring to match.

How do you get to Landmannalaugar?

By highland bus from Reykjavik in summer, or by a properly equipped 4x4 capable of river crossings.

How long is the Laugavegur trail?

About 55km over four days, from Landmannalaugar to Þórsmörk, with mountain huts along the way.

When are the Highlands open?

Roughly late June to early September — F-roads, huts and highland buses are all closed the rest of the year.

Do you need a 4x4 to visit the Highlands?

For self-driving, yes — standard rental cars aren't permitted on F-roads. Highland buses are the alternative.

Is the Laugavegur trail difficult?

It's a moderate, well-marked trek, but river crossings, weather and altitude changes mean real trekking fitness and gear are needed.
