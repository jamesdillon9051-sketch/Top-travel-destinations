Welcome to the Westman Islands

The Westman Islands are genuinely one of Iceland's most dramatic volcanic stories made physical: in 1973, a new volcano, Eldfell, tore open on the edge of the main island's only town and buried a third of it in lava and ash over several months, in an eruption residents fought back against by pumping seawater onto the advancing flow.

Today Heimaey, the only inhabited island in the archipelago, is genuinely a thriving fishing town that has rebuilt itself around that eruption rather than away from it — the Eldheimar museum sits atop an excavated buried house, and Eldfell itself is now a short, popular climb.

The islands are genuinely just as famous for wildlife as for volcanism: they hold one of the world's largest Atlantic puffin colonies, and the nearby island of Surtsey, formed entirely by an eruption in the 1960s, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site studied as a living laboratory for how life colonises new land.

Why visit

Why visit the Westman Islands?

The Westman Islands' case rests on a genuinely rare combination of recent volcanic drama and world-class wildlife.

  • Eldfell is genuinely a volcano within living memory

    It didn't exist before 1973, and residents who lived through the eruption still tell their stories on the island.

  • The Eldheimar museum is genuinely unlike any other in Iceland

    Built directly over an excavated house buried by the eruption, giving it a real claim to being Iceland's 'Pompeii of the North.'

  • The islands genuinely hold one of the world's largest puffin colonies

    Hundreds of thousands of pairs nest on the sea cliffs each summer.

  • Surtsey is genuinely a UNESCO World Heritage natural laboratory

    An island born from the sea in the 1960s, visible from boat tours and still scientifically off-limits to casual visitors.

  • It's genuinely an easy, self-contained escape

    A short ferry or flight from the south coast, but with a distinct island character all its own.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Westman Islands

Heimaey's compact size means its genuinely essential sights are all within a short drive or walk of each other.

  1. 01

    Heimaey Harbour & Town

    A genuinely busy working fishing harbour, still one of Iceland's most important, with the town rebuilt around and over the 1973 lava field.

    Tip Walk genuinely up to the harbour's northern breakwater for the best view back over the town.

  2. 02

    Eldfell Volcano

    A genuinely young volcano, born in a 1973 eruption that buried much of the town below, now a popular hour-long climb with warm ground still detectable underfoot.

    Tip You can genuinely still feel residual heat by pushing a stick into the ground near the summit.

  3. 03

    Eldheimar Museum

    A genuinely striking museum built directly over one of the houses excavated from the ash, telling the story of the 1973 eruption and evacuation.

    Tip The genuinely included audio guide, narrated partly by survivors, adds real emotional weight.

  4. 04

    Stórhöfði Puffin Colony

    One of Iceland's genuinely most reliable puffin-watching spots, a windswept headland at Heimaey's southern tip with a colony numbering in the hundreds of thousands.

    Tip Visit genuinely in the evening, when birds return to their burrows in the greatest numbers.

  5. 05

    Beluga Whale Sanctuary

    A genuinely pioneering open-water sanctuary in Klettsvík bay, home to two beluga whales relocated from a Chinese aquarium in 2019.

    Tip The genuinely viewing platform lets you watch the belugas without a boat trip.

  6. 06

    Herjólfsdalur

    A genuinely striking natural amphitheatre valley, believed to be the archipelago's first Norse settlement site and now home to Iceland's biggest summer festival.

    Tip The genuinely short walking loop around the valley rim gives good views over the town.

  7. 07

    Surtsey

    A genuinely brand-new island, formed entirely by an underwater eruption between 1963 and 1967, now a UNESCO World Heritage site studied for how life colonises new land.

    Tip Landing is genuinely restricted to scientists — most visitors see it only from a boat tour or scenic flight.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Westman Islands

Heimaey genuinely rewards a mix of volcanic history, wildlife watching and its own laid-back island pace.

  1. Climb Eldfell

    A genuinely accessible hour-long round trip to the summit, with sweeping views over the town, harbour and surrounding islands.

    Tip Wear genuinely sturdy shoes — the loose volcanic gravel underfoot can be slippery.

  2. Step inside the Eldheimar Museum

    A genuinely moving hour spent among the artefacts and excavated remains of a house buried in the 1973 eruption.

    Tip Allow genuinely at least an hour, longer if you want the full audio guide experience.

  3. Watch puffins return to their burrows

    A genuinely magical evening ritual in summer, as thousands of puffins wheel in from the sea to their cliffside nests.

    Tip Stórhöfði's genuinely viewing area is a short, easy drive from town.

  4. See the rescued belugas

    A genuinely rare chance to see beluga whales up close in a real bay sanctuary, rather than a traditional aquarium tank.

    Tip The genuinely visitor centre explains the belugas' full rescue and relocation story.

  5. Take a boat tour past the sea stacks and Surtsey

    A genuinely dramatic way to see the archipelago's sea stacks, sea caves and birdlife up close, with Surtsey visible in the distance.

    Tip Boats genuinely run only in summer and can be cancelled in rough weather — book flexibly.

  6. Wander Herjólfsdalur

    A genuinely peaceful spot most of the year, exploding into Iceland's biggest festival crowd for one weekend each August.

    Tip Ask genuinely locally about the þjóðhátíð festival if visiting in early August — book accommodation well ahead.

Local food

What to eat in the Westman Islands

As a genuine working fishing community, Heimaey's food leans heavily on the daily catch alongside Iceland's usual staples.

  • Fresh Icelandic seafood

    Genuinely as fresh as it comes — Heimaey remains one of Iceland's most important fishing ports.

    Where to try it Harbourside restaurants in the town centre.

  • Icelandic lamb

    Genuinely available at most restaurants in town, often from sheep grazed on the surrounding islands.

    Where to try it Restaurants around Heimaey's town centre.

  • Skyr

    A genuinely easy breakfast or snack option at the town's cafés and small supermarket.

    Where to try it Cafés in Heimaey town.

  • Puffin

    A genuinely traditional local delicacy, historically an important food source on the islands, though increasingly debated as puffin numbers decline.

    Where to try it A small number of traditional restaurants in Heimaey town.

  • Rúgbrauð

    A genuinely traditional dark rye bread, often served with the day's fish catch at breakfast.

    Where to try it Guesthouses and cafés in Heimaey town.

  • Brennivín

    Iceland's genuinely famous caraway schnapps, a fitting way to end an evening of puffin watching in town.

    Where to try it Bars in Heimaey town.

Transport

Getting to and around the Westman Islands

The Westman Islands are genuinely reached by a short ferry crossing or a quick domestic flight.

Getting there

  • Ferry from Landeyjahöfn

    A genuinely quick 30-40 minute crossing, the most common way over, taking cars as well as foot passengers.

    Landeyjahöfn is about 2.5 hours from Reykjavik by car.

  • Domestic flight

    A genuinely fast option from Reykjavik, useful if you'd rather skip the drive to the ferry terminal.

    Flights are limited and weather-dependent.

  • Organised day tour

    Genuinely bundles the ferry crossing and island sights for visitors without a car.

    Day tours typically depart from Reykjavik or the south coast.

Getting around

Heimaey is genuinely small enough to see most of on foot, with a car useful only for Stórhöfði.

  • On foot

    Genuinely covers the town, harbour, museum and Eldfell easily within a day.

    Nothing in town is more than a 20-minute walk from anything else.

  • Rental car or taxi

    Genuinely useful for the short drive out to Stórhöfði at the island's southern tip.

    The whole island's road network covers only a few kilometres.

  • Boat tours

    Genuinely the only way to see the sea stacks, caves and Surtsey up close.

    Tours depart from Heimaey's harbour in summer.

When to go

Best time to visit the Westman Islands

Summer genuinely brings the puffins and reliable boat tours, though Eldfell and the Eldheimar museum are worth visiting in any season.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Genuinely peak puffin season and the only reliable window for boat tours around the islands.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Genuinely quieter, though puffins have mostly left for the winter by late September.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Genuinely atmospheric for the museum and Eldfell, though ferries can face weather delays.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Good

    Genuinely quiet, with puffins beginning to return toward the end of the season.

Worth planning around: Avoid genuinely counting on boat tours or flights in winter — rough weather regularly disrupts both.

Travel tips

The Westman Islands travel tips

A handful of practical notes make a Westman Islands day trip run smoothly.

  • Book your genuinely ferry crossing ahead in summer — car spaces sell out.
  • Check genuinely ferry schedules carefully if doing this as a day trip from the south coast.
  • Visit Stórhöfði genuinely in the evening for the best puffin activity.
  • Allow genuinely a full day rather than rushing it between ferry times.
  • Book accommodation genuinely months ahead if visiting during the early August festival.
  • Bring genuinely a windproof layer — the islands are notably exposed even by Icelandic standards.

FAQ

The Westman Islands: frequently asked questions

Are the Westman Islands worth a day trip?

Yes, genuinely — the combination of the 1973 eruption story, Eldfell and the puffin colonies makes for a genuinely distinctive day away from the south coast.

How do you get to the Westman Islands?

By a genuinely short ferry from Landeyjahöfn, about 2.5 hours from Reykjavik, or by a quick domestic flight.

When is the best time to see puffins on Heimaey?

Genuinely from around May to August, with evening visits to Stórhöfði giving the best activity.

Can you visit Surtsey?

Not genuinely on land — access is restricted to scientists, but boat tours and scenic flights pass close by.

What happened on Heimaey in 1973?

A new volcano, Eldfell, erupted genuinely without warning at the edge of town, eventually burying about a third of it in lava and ash over several months.

How long do you need in the Westman Islands?

A genuinely full day covers the main sights comfortably; an overnight stay allows a more relaxed pace and an evening puffin visit.
