Welcome to Lake Mývatn & North Iceland

Lake Mývatn is genuinely North Iceland's answer to the Golden Circle: a compact area packed with geothermal fields, lava formations and a genuinely otherworldly volcanic lake, but with a fraction of the crowds since it sits a full day's drive from Reykjavik.

The region genuinely rewards a slower pace than a day trip — Hverir's roaring steam vents, Dimmuborgir's lava pillars and Goðafoss and Dettifoss waterfalls are all close together, while Akureyri, Iceland's genuine second city, and Húsavík, the country's best whale-watching town, are both under an hour away.

It's also genuinely one of the best bases for exploring the north as a whole, whether you're driving the full Ring Road or flying directly into Akureyri to skip the long drive from Reykjavik.

Why visit

Why visit Lake Mývatn & North Iceland?

North Iceland's case rests on genuine geological drama with far fewer crowds than the south.

  • Hverir is genuinely one of Iceland's most visceral geothermal fields

    Roaring steam vents and bubbling mudpots on a stark, colourful, almost lunar landscape.

  • It's genuinely far quieter than the south coast or Golden Circle

    The same volcanic drama, without anywhere near the same tour bus traffic.

  • Húsavík is genuinely Iceland's best whale-watching town

    Calm bay waters and a high density of humpback and other whale species through the summer.

  • Akureyri is genuinely a real, charming small city

    A proper working town with good restaurants, unlike some of Iceland's more purely scenic stops.

  • It's genuinely rich in waterfalls beyond the famous ones

    Goðafoss and Dettifoss both rival the south coast's headline falls for sheer drama.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Lake Mývatn & North Iceland

North Iceland's core sights cluster tightly around Lake Mývatn, with two of the country's most powerful waterfalls a short drive further.

  1. 01

    Lake Mývatn

    A genuinely shallow volcanic lake dotted with pseudocraters and rich in birdlife, formed by a massive basalt lava eruption 2,300 years ago.

    Tip The genuinely short Höfði peninsula walk gives some of the lake's best photo angles.

  2. 02

    Hverir Geothermal Area

    A genuinely stark, almost lunar geothermal field of roaring steam vents, bubbling mudpots and mineral-stained orange earth beneath Námafjall mountain.

    Tip Stay genuinely on the marked paths — the ground here is scalding and unstable underfoot.

  3. 03

    Dimmuborgir

    A genuinely eerie labyrinth of twisted lava pillars, caves and arches, formed when a lava lake drained and left its crust collapsed and standing.

    Tip The genuinely longer loop trail gets you well away from the crowds near the car park.

  4. 04

    Goðafoss

    A genuinely striking horseshoe-shaped waterfall on the Skjálfandafljót river, its name meaning 'waterfall of the gods.'

    Tip Trails on genuinely both sides of the river offer different, equally good angles.

  5. 05

    Dettifoss

    Genuinely one of Europe's most powerful waterfalls by volume, thundering into Jökulsárgljúfur canyon with a spray visible from a distance.

    Tip The genuinely rougher east-side road gives closer views, but the west side has an easier, paved approach.

  6. 06

    Akureyri

    Iceland's genuine second city, a compact, colourful town at the head of Eyjafjörður fjord with good restaurants and a striking modernist church.

    Tip Climb the steps to genuinely Akureyrarkirkja for the best view down the town's main street.

  7. 07

    Húsavík

    A genuinely picturesque fishing town on Skjálfandi Bay, widely considered Iceland's best base for whale watching.

    Tip Book a genuinely early morning tour for the calmest water and best sighting odds.

Things to do

Best things to do in Lake Mývatn & North Iceland

North Iceland genuinely rewards mixing geothermal walks with wildlife and a proper town stop in Akureyri.

  1. Go whale watching from Húsavík

    Genuinely Iceland's best whale-watching odds, with humpback, minke and sometimes blue whales spotted through the summer months.

    Tip Dress genuinely far warmer than you think you need to — the boat deck is cold even in summer.

  2. Soak in the Mývatn Nature Baths

    A genuinely quieter, cheaper alternative to the Blue Lagoon, with the same warm, mineral-rich water and far fewer crowds.

    Tip Sunset genuinely makes for the best light over the surrounding landscape.

  3. Climb Hverfjall crater

    A genuinely straightforward climb up the rim of this near-perfectly symmetrical explosion crater, with sweeping views over Mývatn.

    Tip The full rim loop takes genuinely about an hour, though the short climb alone is worth it too.

  4. Wander Dimmuborgir's lava labyrinth

    Genuinely several marked trails wind through the pillars and arches, ranging from a quick 20-minute loop to a longer hour-plus circuit.

    Tip Local folklore genuinely holds these formations are home to Iceland's Yule Lads — ask locally for the stories.

  5. Feel Dettifoss's power

    Genuinely one of the most visceral waterfall experiences in Iceland — the ground itself seems to shake near the viewing platform.

    Tip A waterproof layer is genuinely essential — the spray carries a long way on a windy day.

  6. Wander Akureyri's town centre

    A genuinely relaxed afternoon of cafés, a good bookshop and the excellent botanical garden, a rare treat this far north.

    Tip The genuinely free botanical garden is worth an hour even outside peak bloom season.

Local food

What to eat in Lake Mývatn & North Iceland

North Iceland genuinely leans on traditional preserved foods alongside fresh fish from Húsavík and Akureyri's harbours.

  • Hangikjöt (smoked lamb)

    A genuinely traditional smoked lamb, historically cured over smouldering birch or dried sheep dung, served thinly sliced.

    Where to try it Restaurants and guesthouses around Akureyri and Mývatn.

  • Harðfiskur (dried fish)

    A genuinely traditional wind-dried fish snack, often eaten with a thick smear of butter, sold at petrol stations and markets alike.

    Where to try it Supermarkets and fish shops in Húsavík and Akureyri.

  • Skyr

    A genuinely reliable breakfast staple across the north, often served with local berries in season.

    Where to try it Cafés in Akureyri and guesthouses around Mývatn.

  • Fresh Icelandic seafood

    Genuinely excellent in Húsavík especially, a working fishing harbour where the catch barely travels before it's served.

    Where to try it Harbourside restaurants in Húsavík and Akureyri.

  • Rúgbrauð

    A genuinely dense, dark rye bread, often served alongside smoked fish or lamb at breakfast buffets.

    Where to try it Guesthouses and cafés around Mývatn.

  • Brennivín

    Iceland's genuinely famous caraway schnapps, a fitting way to end a day of geothermal walking in Akureyri's bars.

    Where to try it Bars and restaurants in Akureyri.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Lake Mývatn & North Iceland

Most visitors genuinely split their nights between Akureyri and a guesthouse near Mývatn itself.

  • Akureyri

    Genuinely the north's main hub, about an hour from Mývatn and close to Húsavík.

    Best for: Restaurants, services and a real small-city base

  • Reykjahlíð (Mývatn village)

    Small, but puts you within minutes of Hverir, Dimmuborgir and the Nature Baths.

    Best for: Being genuinely right at the lake itself

  • Húsavík

    A charming small harbour town, useful if whale watching is your main priority.

    Best for: Prioritising genuinely early whale-watching tours

Transport

Getting to and around Lake Mývatn & North Iceland

North Iceland is genuinely reached either by a long Ring Road drive or a short domestic flight.

Getting there

  • Domestic flight to Akureyri

    A genuinely quick 45-minute hop from Reykjavik, the fastest way to reach the north.

    Multiple daily flights run year-round, weather permitting.

  • Self-drive via the Ring Road

    About genuinely 5-6 hours from Reykjavik, often broken up with stops along the way.

    Route 1 runs directly through Akureyri and past Mývatn.

  • Organised multi-day tour

    Genuinely bundles transport, guiding and accommodation for visitors who'd rather not self-drive the whole Ring Road.

    Multi-day North Iceland tours typically depart from Reykjavik.

Getting around

The region's sights are genuinely spread along Route 1 and a few short side roads.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely the most practical way to link Mývatn, Akureyri, Húsavík and the waterfalls.

    Route 1 and 87 connect all the main stops directly.

  • Local tours from Akureyri or Mývatn

    Genuinely cover Dettifoss, Goðafoss and whale watching for visitors without a car.

    Day tours run regularly from both Akureyri and Mývatn village.

  • On foot at each stop

    Genuinely all the individual sights are short, well-marked walks from parking areas.

    None require more than an hour or two on foot.

When to go

Best time to visit Lake Mývatn & North Iceland

Summer genuinely gives the easiest driving and long daylight, while winter offers a real shot at the aurora away from Reykjavik's light pollution.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Genuinely easy driving, long daylight and the best whale-watching season.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Genuinely excellent aurora viewing, though some roads and the airport can face weather delays.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Genuinely quiet, with good early-season aurora chances and mild weather.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Good

    Genuinely increasing daylight, though some minor roads may still be closed.

Worth planning around: Avoid Hverir on genuinely still, windless days if you're sensitive to sulphur smell — it lingers close to the vents.

Itinerary

Two days in North Iceland

Built around Mývatn's geothermal core, with Húsavík's whale watching and Akureyri's town centre either side.

  1. Day1

    Mývatn's geothermal core

    Hverir, Dimmuborgir and Goðafoss.

    • Morning Hverir Geothermal Area and Hverfjall crater.
    • Afternoon Dimmuborgir's lava labyrinth, then Goðafoss.
    • Evening Soak at the Mývatn Nature Baths, staying near the lake.
  2. Day2

    Dettifoss, Húsavík and Akureyri

    Iceland's mightiest waterfall, whale watching and a real town.

    • Morning Dettifoss, Europe's most powerful waterfall by volume.
    • Afternoon Whale watching from Húsavík harbour.
    • Evening Dinner and a wander around Akureyri's town centre.

Travel tips

Lake Mývatn & North Iceland travel tips

A handful of practical notes make North Iceland far easier to plan around.

  • Book whale-watching tours genuinely a day or two ahead in peak summer.
  • Fly into Akureyri genuinely if you want to skip the long Ring Road drive from Reykjavik.
  • Bring genuinely warm, windproof layers for the whale-watching boat deck, even in July.
  • Check road.is genuinely for the Dettifoss access roads, which can close in bad weather.
  • Base yourself near Mývatn genuinely village rather than driving in daily from Akureyri.
  • Allow genuinely a full two to three days rather than a rushed day trip from Akureyri.

FAQ

Lake Mývatn & North Iceland: frequently asked questions

Is Lake Mývatn worth visiting?

Yes, genuinely — it's North Iceland's geothermal and volcanic highlight, with far fewer crowds than the south coast or Golden Circle.

Is Húsavík the best place for whale watching in Iceland?

Yes, genuinely widely considered so — its calm bay waters and whale density are hard to match elsewhere in the country.

How far is Mývatn from Reykjavik?

About genuinely 5-6 hours by car, or a 45-minute flight to Akureyri followed by a short drive.

Can you visit North Iceland without a car?

Yes — local tours from Akureyri and Mývatn village genuinely cover the main sights and whale watching.

How does Dettifoss compare to Gullfoss?

Dettifoss is genuinely more powerful by volume and rawer in feel, while Gullfoss is more accessible and better set up for visitors.

How many days do you need for North Iceland?

Two to three days genuinely covers Mývatn's geothermal sights, the waterfalls, Húsavík and Akureyri at a relaxed pace.
