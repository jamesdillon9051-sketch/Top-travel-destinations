Welcome to Reykjavik

Reykjavik is genuinely the world's northernmost capital of a sovereign state, and it wears that fact lightly — a small, colourful, low-rise city of corrugated-metal houses in bright paint, built around a working harbour that still lands fish daily alongside the whale-watching boats.

It holds well over half of Iceland's entire population within its wider metropolitan area, yet the centre itself is genuinely compact enough to cover on foot in a single afternoon, with Hallgrímskirkja's distinctive tower serving as a landmark visible from almost anywhere in the old town.

For nearly every visitor, Reykjavik is also genuinely the start and end point of a wider Iceland trip — the base for the Golden Circle, the launch point for whale watching and northern lights tours, and the place to eat well and soak in a geothermal pool before or after days on the Ring Road.

Why visit

Why visit Reykjavik?

Reykjavik's case rests on being a genuinely charming, walkable capital that also happens to be the natural gateway to the rest of Iceland.

  • It's genuinely small enough to explore entirely on foot

    The old town, harbour and main shopping street all sit within a compact, easily walkable area.

  • The geothermal pool culture is genuinely a real local institution

    Neighbourhood pools are a daily ritual for locals, not just a tourist activity.

  • Whale watching from the city itself is genuinely excellent

    Boats depart directly from the old harbour, with genuinely good sighting odds much of the year.

  • The food scene is genuinely disproportionate to the city's size

    From fine dining to the city's famous hot dog stand, Reykjavik's food culture punches well above its population.

  • It's genuinely the natural base for the rest of the country

    The Golden Circle, South Coast and northern lights tours all operate conveniently from here.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Reykjavik

Reykjavik's essential sights span its harbourfront, its landmark church and its compact, walkable old town.

  1. 01

    Hallgrímskirkja

    Iceland's genuinely largest church and tallest building for decades, its facade inspired by the basalt columns found across the country's volcanic landscape.

    Tip Take the lift up the 74-metre tower for genuinely the best view over the city's coloured rooftops.

  2. 02

    The Old Harbour

    A genuinely working harbour that doubles as the departure point for whale-watching and puffin tours, lined with seafood restaurants and the Maritime Museum.

    Tip Book a whale-watching trip for genuinely the best chance of sightings, departing directly from here.

  3. 03

    Harpa Concert Hall

    A genuinely striking geometric glass facade inspired by Iceland's basalt coastline, home to the Icelandic Symphony Orchestra and Opera.

    Tip The facade is genuinely spectacular lit up after dark — walk past even without a ticket to a performance.

  4. 04

    The Sun Voyager

    A genuinely striking steel sculpture resembling a Viking longship, framing the mountains across the bay — an ode to the sun, not a literal ship.

    Tip Sunset or a clear night with the northern lights genuinely make this the most photogenic spot in the city.

  5. 05

    Laugavegur

    The genuine heart of Reykjavik's shopping and café culture, its side street Skólavörðustígur running straight up toward Hallgrímskirkja.

    Tip Skólavörðustígur's colourful facades are genuinely one of the most photographed streets in the city.

  6. 06

    Perlan

    A genuinely distinctive glass-domed building atop the city's hot water tanks, its observation deck and ice cave exhibit offering sweeping views.

    Tip The indoor ice cave exhibit is a genuinely good preview if you won't make it to a real glacier.

  7. 07

    Tjörnin and the City Pond

    A genuinely peaceful pond right at the centre of the city, home to a wide variety of birdlife and bordered by City Hall and the National Gallery.

    Tip A genuinely pleasant, quiet loop walk between the old town's main sights.

Things to do

Best things to do in Reykjavik

Reykjavik genuinely rewards a slow, walkable pace, with the harbour and geothermal pools as its defining local rituals.

  1. Go whale watching from the old harbour

    Boats depart directly from the city centre, with genuinely good odds of minke whales, dolphins and, in season, humpbacks.

    Tip Dress genuinely warmer than you think necessary — the boat deck is cold even in summer.

  2. Soak in a local geothermal pool

    Laugardalslaug and Sundhöllin are genuinely where locals actually go, far cheaper and just as authentic as the tourist-focused lagoons outside town.

    Tip A proper pre-swim shower is genuinely mandatory and strictly enforced — don't skip it.

  3. Climb Hallgrímskirkja's tower

    A genuinely quick lift ride up delivers the best view in the city, the coloured rooftops spreading out toward the bay and mountains.

    Tip Genuinely quieter first thing in the morning, before tour groups arrive.

  4. Explore Laugavegur's shops and cafés

    A genuinely easy afternoon of independent shops, design stores and coffee, running the length of the old town.

    Tip Duck up Skólavörðustígur genuinely partway along for the best photo of the colourful side street.

  5. See the Sun Voyager at sunset

    A genuinely short, worthwhile walk along the waterfront path, particularly rewarding around sunset or under the northern lights.

    Tip The walk continues genuinely pleasantly further along the shore toward Harpa.

  6. Visit Perlan's observation deck

    A genuinely quick way to get your bearings over the whole city, plus an indoor ice cave and planetarium show.

    Tip Genuinely worth combining with a coffee at the revolving restaurant if the weather's poor for outdoor sightseeing.

Local food

What to eat in Reykjavik

Reykjavik's food scene is genuinely disproportionate to the city's size, from a famous hot dog stand to serious New Nordic dining.

  • Icelandic hot dog

    Bæjarins Beztu Pylsur, genuinely the country's most famous hot dog stand, has served the same lamb-and-pork sausage since 1937.

    Where to try it Bæjarins Beztu Pylsur, downtown near the old harbour.

  • Fresh seafood

    Genuinely landed daily at the old harbour, and served everywhere from casual plokkfiskur stew to serious tasting menus.

    Where to try it Harbour-side restaurants around the old harbour and Grandi district.

  • Skyr

    A genuinely daily staple for locals, thick and protein-rich, sold everywhere from supermarkets to café breakfast menus.

    Where to try it Cafés and supermarkets citywide.

  • Icelandic lamb

    Free-range and grass-fed, genuinely a staple of Reykjavik's restaurant menus at every price point.

    Where to try it Restaurants citywide, from casual bistros to fine dining.

  • Rúgbrauð

    A genuinely dense, faintly sweet dark rye bread, traditionally geothermally baked, sold at bakeries across the city.

    Where to try it Bakeries citywide, particularly good with smoked fish.

  • Brennivín

    A genuinely potent caraway schnapps nicknamed 'Black Death,' worth trying at least once in a proper Reykjavik bar.

    Where to try it Bars citywide, and the Vínbúðin liquor store to take a bottle home.

Central Reykjavik is genuinely small enough that almost any downtown base works well on foot.

Transport

Getting to and around Reykjavik

Reykjavik is genuinely reached via Keflavík International Airport, about 45 minutes from the city centre.

Getting there

  • Airport transfer bus

    Flybus and similar services run frequently from Keflavík, genuinely the standard way to reach the city without a car.

    Booking ahead online is genuinely cheaper than paying on arrival.

  • Rental car

    Available at Keflavík Airport, genuinely useful if starting a Ring Road trip immediately.

    Not necessary for the city itself, which is genuinely walkable.

  • Domestic flights

    Reykjavik Domestic Airport, in the city itself, connects to Akureyri and other regional towns.

    A genuinely quick way to reach North Iceland without the full drive.

Getting around

Central Reykjavik is genuinely walkable, with a car needed mainly for day trips beyond the city.

  • Walking

    The old town, harbour and Laugavegur are all genuinely within a compact, easily walkable area.

    Most visitors genuinely need no other transport within the city itself.

  • Strætó city buses

    A reliable network covering the wider city, genuinely useful for reaching Laugardalslaug pool or the suburbs.

    A Klapp card covers fares across the network.

  • Rental car or organised tours

    Genuinely necessary for day trips to the Golden Circle or South Coast from the city.

    Numerous tour operators run day trips for visitors without a car.

When to go

Best time to visit Reykjavik

Reykjavik genuinely works year-round, since most of its sights are indoors or short outdoor walks.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Near-endless daylight and the mildest weather, also genuinely the busiest season.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Genuinely quieter, with the aurora becoming visible again as nights darken.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Short days but genuinely good aurora odds, with a cosy indoor café culture to retreat to.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Good

    Increasingly long days and genuinely thinner crowds than summer.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Reykjavik genuinely works in any season, with the choice mainly about daylight and northern lights odds rather than comfort.

Itinerary

Two days in Reykjavik

  1. Day1

    • Afternoon Laugavegur's shops and cafés, then the Sun Voyager.

  2. Day2

    The harbour and beyond

    Whale watching and a wider city view.

    • Evening A soak at a local geothermal pool.

Travel tips

Reykjavik travel tips

Practical notes on booking, weather and pools that make a real difference to a Reykjavik visit.

  • Book whale watching and northern lights tours ahead, especially in peak summer or winter weeks.
  • Shower thoroughly before any pool — it's genuinely mandatory, not optional, and staff do check.
  • Pack proper layers even in summer — Reykjavik's weather genuinely changes fast.
  • Use Reykjavik as a base for Golden Circle and South Coast day trips if you don't want to drive.
  • Check the aurora forecast on clear winter nights — you can genuinely see it from the city outskirts.
  • Book restaurants ahead for dinner in peak season — Reykjavik's best kitchens genuinely fill up.

FAQ

Reykjavik: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Reykjavik?

Two days covers the old town, harbour and a whale-watching trip comfortably. Most visitors then use the city as a base for the Golden Circle and South Coast.

Is Reykjavik worth visiting on its own?

Yes, genuinely, for two or three days — but it's also the natural base for the rest of Iceland, and few visitors stop at the city alone.

Can you see the northern lights from Reykjavik?

Yes, genuinely, on clear nights roughly September to April, though moving slightly outside the city centre improves visibility away from streetlights.

Do you need a car in Reykjavik?

Not for the city itself, which is genuinely walkable. A car or organised tour becomes necessary for day trips like the Golden Circle.

Is whale watching worth it from Reykjavik?

Yes, genuinely good odds much of the year, with minke whales and dolphins common and humpbacks frequent in summer.

What is the best geothermal pool in Reykjavik?

Laugardalslaug is genuinely the largest and most popular with locals; Sundhöllin, the city's oldest pool, is smaller and more central.
