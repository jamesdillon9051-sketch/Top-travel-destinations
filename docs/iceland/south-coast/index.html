Welcome to the South Coast

The South Coast is genuinely Iceland's most photographed stretch of road, a single route running from just past the Golden Circle out toward the glacier lagoon, past two of the country's most famous waterfalls and a beach of black sand and sea stacks that looks genuinely unlike anywhere else in Europe.

It's also a genuinely young, actively volcanic landscape — Eyjafjallajökull's 2010 eruption grounded European air travel for days, and the whole coastline sits in the shadow of glacier-capped volcanoes that have shaped it for millennia.

Unlike much of Iceland's interior, the South Coast is genuinely accessible on a standard rental car along paved Route 1, which is exactly why it forms the backbone of almost every Iceland road trip, whether as a long day trip or the first leg of a full Ring Road loop.

Why visit

Why visit the South Coast?

The South Coast's case rests on packing an exceptional density of genuinely world-class natural sights into a single accessible road.

  • Seljalandsfoss is genuinely one of very few waterfalls you can walk behind

    A full path circles behind the falling water, framing the valley through the curtain of spray.

  • Skógafoss is genuinely one of Iceland's most powerful single-drop waterfalls

    A wide, thunderous 60-metre curtain, with a staircase climbing to a viewpoint directly above it.

  • Reynisfjara is genuinely unlike any beach in Europe

    Black volcanic sand, hexagonal basalt columns and dramatic sea stacks rising from the surf.

  • The whole route is genuinely accessible on a standard rental car

    Unlike the Highlands, the South Coast needs no 4x4 or special equipment.

  • It's genuinely still an active volcanic landscape

    Eyjafjallajökull's 2010 eruption is a real, recent reminder that this coastline is still being shaped today.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the South Coast

The South Coast's essential sights run in a genuine line along Route 1, easy to visit in the order you reach them.

  1. 01

    Seljalandsfoss

    A genuinely rare waterfall you can walk fully behind, a 60-metre drop with a circular path framing the valley through the falling water.

    Tip Bring a genuinely proper raincoat — the path behind the falls gets seriously wet in any wind.

  2. 02

    Skógafoss

    A genuinely thunderous, wide single-drop waterfall, with a staircase of over 400 steps climbing to a viewpoint directly above it.

    Tip Rainbows are genuinely almost guaranteed in the spray on any sunny afternoon.

  3. 03

    Reynisfjara and Reynisdrangar

    A genuinely striking black sand beach backed by hexagonal basalt columns, facing the dramatic Reynisdrangar sea stacks offshore.

    Tip Stay genuinely well back from the waterline — sneaker waves here are a real, serious hazard, not a formality.

  4. 04

    Dyrhólaey

    A genuinely dramatic promontory with a natural arch large enough for boats to pass through, and a puffin colony in early summer.

    Tip The upper viewpoint road genuinely closes during nesting season — check current access before visiting.

  5. 05

    Vík í Mýrdal

    Iceland's genuinely southernmost village, its small white church looking out over the black sand coastline and Reynisdrangar beyond.

    Tip A genuinely convenient overnight base and fuel stop between the waterfalls and the glacier lagoon further east.

  6. 06

    Fjaðrárgljúfur Canyon

    A genuinely dramatic, deep canyon with sheer, moss-covered walls, carved by glacial meltwater over roughly two million years.

    Tip Stay genuinely on marked paths — the canyon edge is fragile and has been damaged by off-path foot traffic.

  7. 07

    Eyjafjallajökull

    The genuinely infamous glacier-capped volcano whose 2010 eruption grounded European air travel for days, now quiet and visible from the coast road.

    Tip The Lava Centre and Eyjafjallajökull visitor centres genuinely add real context to what you're driving past.

Things to do

Best things to do in the South Coast

The South Coast genuinely rewards stopping often — this is a route where the journey between sights is as much the point as the sights themselves.

  1. Walk behind Seljalandsfoss

    A genuinely short, easy walk that circles fully behind the falling water, one of the only waterfalls in the world where this is possible.

    Tip Waterproof your camera and phone genuinely properly — the spray is relentless on the path.

  2. Climb the steps beside Skógafoss

    Over 400 steps genuinely climb alongside the falls to a viewpoint at the top, with the Fimmvörðuháls hiking trail continuing beyond it.

    Tip The climb is genuinely worth it even if you only go partway up for the view.

  3. Photograph the basalt columns at Reynisfjara

    A genuinely striking combination of hexagonal basalt columns, a sea cave and offshore sea stacks, best photographed in the low midday winter light.

    Tip Never turn your back on the surf here — this is genuinely one of the few spots in Iceland where visitors have died to sneaker waves.

  4. Spot puffins at Dyrhólaey

    A genuinely reliable puffin colony nests on the cliffs here from around May to August, alongside other seabirds.

    Tip Bring binoculars genuinely — puffins nest on inaccessible cliff ledges, not at close range.

  5. Hike the rim of Fjaðrárgljúfur

    A genuinely easy, well-marked trail follows the canyon rim, with viewpoints down into its winding, moss-green walls.

    Tip The full out-and-back walk takes genuinely about an hour at an easy pace.

  6. Visit the Lava Centre

    A genuinely well-made interactive museum in Hvolsvöllur explaining Iceland's volcanic and seismic history, including the 2010 eruption.

    Tip A genuinely good rainy-day stop, or a useful primer before continuing along the coast.

Local food

What to eat in the South Coast

The South Coast's food genuinely centres on farm restaurants and small-town cafés along Route 1, with standard Icelandic staples throughout.

  • Icelandic lamb

    Genuinely widely available at farm restaurants along the route, often raised in the surrounding hills.

    Where to try it Farm restaurants and guesthouses around Vík and Hvolsvöllur.

  • Fresh Icelandic seafood

    Genuinely fresh, particularly good in the small fishing town of Vík, right on the coast.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Vík í Mýrdal.

  • Skyr

    A genuinely reliable stop at any café along the route, often made at local dairy farms.

    Where to try it Farm shops and cafés along Route 1.

  • Rúgbrauð

    A genuinely traditional dark rye bread, found at bakeries and farm cafés throughout the region.

    Where to try it Bakeries in Hvolsvöllur and Vík.

  • Icelandic hot dog

    A genuinely reliable, quick stop at petrol stations along the route between waterfalls.

    Where to try it Service stations along Route 1.

  • Brennivín

    Genuinely worth trying at a guesthouse bar after a long day of driving and waterfall stops.

    Where to try it Guesthouse bars in Vík and along the coast.

Where to stay in the South Coast

Vík í Mýrdal and Hvolsvöllur are genuinely the two main bases along the South Coast route.

    Genuinely the South Coast's main village, with the widest range of accommodation along the route.

Transport

Getting to and around the South Coast

The South Coast is genuinely a single road trip east from Reykjavik along Route 1.

Getting there

  • Rental car from Reykjavik

    About 2 hours to Seljalandsfoss, genuinely the most flexible way to stop at every sight along the way.

    The full route to Vík takes about 2h30 without stops.

  • Organised day or multi-day tour

    Widely available from Reykjavik, genuinely useful for visitors without a car.

    Multi-day tours typically continue on to Jökulsárlón.

Getting around

The South Coast's sights are genuinely all reached directly from paved Route 1, needing no 4x4.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely the standard way to explore, with all major sights visible from or a short walk off Route 1.

    A standard car is genuinely sufficient outside winter conditions.

  • Organised tours

    Genuinely handle transport for visitors without a car, typically as a long day trip or multi-day tour.

    Small-group options allow more stopping time than large coach tours.

When to go

Best time to visit the South Coast

The South Coast genuinely works year-round on Route 1, though winter driving requires real caution.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Long daylight, puffins nesting at Dyrhólaey, and the easiest, most reliable driving conditions.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Genuinely quieter, with a real chance of aurora sightings on clear nights.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Genuinely dramatic with ice and snow, though short days and winter driving conditions demand real caution.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Good

    Increasingly long days, with waterfalls genuinely running at full force from snowmelt.

Worth planning around: Driving the coast road in a winter storm — check road.is and be genuinely willing to delay plans when conditions turn severe.

Travel tips

The South Coast travel tips

Practical notes on safety and timing that make a real difference to a South Coast trip.

  • Never turn your back on the surf at Reynisfjara — sneaker waves are a genuine, serious hazard.
  • Bring proper waterproofs for Seljalandsfoss and Skógafoss regardless of the forecast.
  • Start genuinely early to beat tour bus crowds at the most famous stops.
  • Check road.is before winter driving — conditions genuinely change fast along this stretch.
  • Fill up on fuel in Vík — options thin out considerably further east.
  • Book accommodation in Vík well ahead for summer — it's a small village with real capacity limits.

FAQ

The South Coast: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need on the South Coast?

Two days covers the main waterfalls and black sand beaches comfortably. It's also doable as a very long single day trip from Reykjavik.

Can you walk behind Seljalandsfoss?

Yes, genuinely — a full path circles behind the falls, one of very few waterfalls anywhere where this is possible.

Is Reynisfjara dangerous?

It can be, genuinely — sneaker waves have caused real injuries and deaths. Stay well back from the waterline and never turn your back on the sea.

Do you need a 4x4 for the South Coast?

No, the main route is entirely on paved Route 1, suitable for a standard rental car outside severe winter weather.

Can you see puffins on the South Coast?

Yes, genuinely, at Dyrhólaey roughly May to August, though access to the upper cliffs sometimes closes during nesting season.

Should I combine the South Coast with Jökulsárlón?

Yes, genuinely — most visitors continue east to the glacier lagoon, extending the South Coast into a longer loop or one-way Ring Road leg.
