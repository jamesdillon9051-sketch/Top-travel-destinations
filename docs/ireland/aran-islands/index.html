Welcome to the Aran Islands

The Aran Islands are genuinely a step back in time, three small islands off Galway Bay where Irish remains the everyday language and a dense network of stone walls still divides the fields, built by hand over centuries from cleared limestone.

Inis Mór, the largest, is genuinely home to Dún Aengus, a dramatic Iron Age stone fort perched right on the edge of a sheer 100-metre cliff, with no barrier between visitors and the drop.

Most visitors genuinely take the ferry from Galway or Doolin as a day trip, though staying overnight allows real time to cycle the island's quiet lanes at an unhurried pace, well after the day-trippers have left.

Why visit

Why visit the Aran Islands?

The Aran Islands' case rests on genuinely real remoteness and a living connection to old Irish culture found nowhere else.

  • Dún Aengus is genuinely one of Ireland's most dramatic ancient sites

    A 2,000-year-old fort on a sheer cliff edge, with no barrier between visitors and the Atlantic below.

  • Irish is genuinely still the everyday language here

    One of Ireland's strongest Gaeltacht regions, not a tourist performance of the language.

  • The stone walls are genuinely a landscape in themselves

    Thousands of kilometres of hand-built walls dividing tiny fields cleared from bare limestone.

  • It's genuinely quiet once the day-trip ferries leave

    An overnight stay reveals a much slower, quieter island than the daytime crowds suggest.

  • The traditional Aran sweater genuinely originates here

    A distinctive hand-knit style with patterns once said to identify individual island families.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Aran Islands

Inis Mór's essential sights combine ancient forts with a genuinely still-living traditional culture.

  1. 01

    Dún Aengus

    Genuinely one of Ireland's most dramatic ancient sites, a 2,000-year-old fort on the edge of a sheer 100-metre cliff, with the semicircular walls open to the drop.

    Tip Stay genuinely well back from the unguarded cliff edge — there is no barrier at all.

  2. 02

    The cliffs at Dún Aonghasa

    A genuinely vertigo-inducing view straight down the cliff face from the fort's edge, among Ireland's most striking natural drops.

    Tip Genuinely lie flat and crawl to the very edge if you want the full drop in view — many visitors do.

  3. 03

    Kilronan village

    Genuinely the islands' main village and ferry port, a small, easy base with shops, pubs and bike rental right at the harbour.

    Tip Genuinely rent a bike here on arrival — it's the best way to see the rest of the island.

  4. 04

    Inis Mór's stone walls

    Genuinely a landscape in themselves, thousands of kilometres of hand-built dry-stone walls dividing tiny fields cleared from bare limestone.

    Tip Genuinely look closely at the wall construction — many are loosely stacked on purpose, to let storm winds pass through.

  5. 05

    The Seven Churches

    Genuinely an atmospheric early Christian monastic site, despite the name actually comprising two churches and several other ruined structures.

    Tip Genuinely quieter than Dún Aengus — a peaceful stop away from the main crowds.

  6. 06

    Aran sweater shops

    Genuinely the origin of the famous hand-knit style, with distinctive stitch patterns and a history tied closely to the islands.

    Tip Genuinely look for a hand-knit label if authenticity matters to you — many cheaper versions are machine-made elsewhere.

  7. 07

    Traditional currach boats

    Genuinely still used by some island fishermen, lightweight canvas-and-tar boats built to a design centuries old.

    Tip Genuinely look for currachs drawn up near Kilronan harbour or smaller landing spots around the island.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Aran Islands

Inis Mór genuinely rewards a full day on a bike, covering the island at an unhurried, human pace.

  1. Cycle Inis Mór's quiet lanes

    Genuinely the best way to see the island, pedalling between stone-walled fields with barely any traffic to worry about.

    Tip Genuinely rent a bike at Kilronan harbour on arrival — no booking needed outside peak summer.

  2. Walk up to Dún Aengus

    A genuinely moderate uphill walk from the visitor centre, rewarded by one of Ireland's most dramatic ancient sites.

    Tip Genuinely wear sturdy shoes — the final stretch crosses uneven limestone.

  3. Explore the Seven Churches

    A genuinely quieter, atmospheric stop away from the main Dún Aengus crowds, easily reached by bike.

    Tip Genuinely combine with a stop at the nearby beach for a full, relaxed afternoon.

  4. Shop for a genuine Aran sweater

    A genuinely worthwhile souvenir with real history, distinctive stitch patterns once said to identify individual families.

    Tip Genuinely ask about hand-knit versus machine-made if authenticity and craft matter to your purchase.

  5. Photograph the island's abandoned cottages

    A genuinely evocative, if slightly melancholy, part of cycling the island, passing stone cottages left empty by past emigration.

    Tip Genuinely respect that these are on private land — photograph from the road rather than entering.

  6. Watch a traditional currach on the water

    A genuinely rare, living link to the islands' fishing heritage, still occasionally seen worked by local rowers.

    Tip Genuinely ask locally about any currach races timed around your visit, held some summer weekends.

Transport

Getting to and around the Aran Islands

The Aran Islands are genuinely reachable only by ferry or small plane, never by road.

Getting there

  • Ferry from Galway or Rossaveal

    Genuinely the most common route, with regular sailings, especially in summer.

    Crossing takes around 40 minutes to an hour and a half depending on departure point.

  • Ferry from Doolin

    Genuinely a popular option for visitors coming from the Cliffs of Moher direction.

    A seasonal route, weather-dependent like all island sailings.

  • Small plane from Connemara Airport

    Genuinely a fast, scenic alternative, taking only a few minutes in the air.

    A memorable, if pricier, way to reach the islands.

Getting around

Inis Mór is genuinely best explored by bike, with a few alternatives for those who prefer not to cycle.

  • Bike rental

    Genuinely the most popular and rewarding way to see the island, available right at Kilronan harbour.

    Covers Dún Aengus, the Seven Churches and the island's main sights comfortably in a day.

  • Minibus tours

    Genuinely a practical option for those unable or unwilling to cycle.

    Widely available at the harbour, covering the main sights with a local driver-guide.

  • On foot

    Genuinely fine for exploring Kilronan itself.

    The island's spread-out sights are better covered by bike or minibus beyond the village.

When to go

Best time to visit the Aran Islands

The islands are genuinely visitable through the warmer months, with far fewer sailings and services outside the main season.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely mild weather with noticeably thinner crowds than midsummer.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    The busiest season, with the widest choice of ferry sailings and the most day-trippers.

  • Early autumn (September)

    Good

    Genuinely a quiet, often mild window with fewer visitors than summer.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Genuinely limited ferry services and a real chance of sailings being cancelled for weather.

Worth planning around: Booking a winter day trip without a flexible schedule — genuinely check ferry status closely, since sailings are weather-dependent.

Travel tips

The Aran Islands travel tips

Practical notes that make an Aran Islands visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book ferry tickets genuinely ahead in summer, and check the weather — sailings depend on conditions.
  • Rent a bike genuinely right at Kilronan harbour on arrival, no advance booking usually needed.
  • Stay genuinely well back from Dún Aengus's unguarded cliff edge.
  • Consider genuinely an overnight stay to see the island once the day-trip crowds leave.

FAQ

The Aran Islands: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need on the Aran Islands?

A single day genuinely covers Kilronan and Dún Aengus, though an overnight allows for a much quieter, more relaxed visit.

Can you visit the Aran Islands as a day trip?

Yes, genuinely — it's a popular day trip from Galway or Doolin, with regular ferries in season.

Is Dún Aengus safe to visit?

Yes, genuinely, with sensible caution — the cliff edge itself is unguarded, so visitors need to stay well back and supervise children closely.

Do people still speak Irish on the Aran Islands?

Yes, genuinely — the islands are one of Ireland's strongest Gaeltacht, or Irish-speaking, regions, with the language used in daily life.

What is the best way to get around Inis Mór?

Genuinely by bike, rented right at Kilronan harbour — it covers the island's main sights comfortably in a day.

Where does the Aran sweater come from?

Genuinely from these islands — the distinctive hand-knit style originates here, with stitch patterns historically tied to individual island families.
