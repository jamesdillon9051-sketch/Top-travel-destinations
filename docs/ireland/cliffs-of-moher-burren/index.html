Welcome to the Cliffs of Moher & the Burren

The Cliffs of Moher are genuinely Ireland's most photographed natural landmark, rising up to 214 metres straight out of the Atlantic along an eight-kilometre stretch of County Clare's coastline.

Just inland lies the Burren, a genuinely strange and beautiful limestone karst landscape unlike anywhere else in Ireland, its bare grey pavement hiding rare Arctic and Mediterranean wildflowers in its cracks alongside one of Europe's densest concentrations of ancient tombs.

Most visitors genuinely combine the two as a single day trip or overnight from Galway or the Dingle Peninsula, basing themselves in the small music-pub village of Doolin at the region's edge.

Why visit

Why visit the Cliffs of Moher & the Burren?

The Cliffs of Moher and the Burren's case rests on genuinely dramatic, easily accessible natural scenery unlike anywhere else on the island.

  • The cliffs are genuinely as dramatic as the photos suggest

    A sheer 214-metre drop to the Atlantic, with a well-built cliff-edge path running the full length.

  • The Burren is genuinely unlike anywhere else in Ireland

    A bare limestone karst landscape hiding rare wildflowers and one of Europe's densest concentrations of ancient tombs.

  • Poulnabrone Dolmen is genuinely older than Stonehenge

    A Neolithic portal tomb dating back roughly 5,800 years, standing alone in the open karst.

  • Doolin's pub sessions are genuinely among Ireland's best

    A tiny village with an outsized reputation for authentic, nightly traditional music.

  • It works genuinely well as a day trip or a slower overnight

    Reachable from Galway or the Dingle Peninsula in either direction.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Cliffs of Moher & the Burren

The essential sights split between the Atlantic drama of the cliffs and the quiet strangeness of the Burren just inland.

  1. 01

    The Cliffs of Moher

    Genuinely Ireland's best-known natural landmark, rising up to 214 metres above the Atlantic along an eight-kilometre stretch of coastline.

    Tip Visit genuinely early morning or late afternoon for softer light and noticeably thinner crowds.

  2. 02

    O'Brien's Tower

    A genuinely photogenic 19th-century viewing tower at the cliffs' highest point, built in 1835 for Victorian-era tourists.

    Tip Climb genuinely to the top for the single best panoramic view of the full cliff line.

  3. 03

    The Burren

    A genuinely strange, beautiful limestone karst landscape found almost nowhere else in Europe, its cracks sheltering rare Arctic and Mediterranean plants side by side.

    Tip Genuinely look closely at the limestone cracks (grikes) — the rare wildflowers hide in their sheltered microclimate.

  4. 04

    Poulnabrone Dolmen

    A genuinely striking Neolithic portal tomb standing alone in the open karst, dating back roughly 5,800 years, older than Stonehenge.

    Tip Genuinely free to visit, with a small roadside car park and a short walk across the limestone.

  5. 05

    Doolin village

    A genuinely tiny village with an outsized reputation as one of Ireland's best spots for authentic traditional music sessions.

    Tip Genuinely book accommodation well ahead in summer — Doolin's small size means it fills fast.

  6. 06

    Aillwee Cave

    A genuinely accessible show cave running deep into the Burren's limestone, with guided tours past underground waterfalls and ancient bear pits.

    Tip Genuinely bring a light jacket — the cave stays a constant, cool temperature year-round.

  7. 07

    Kilfenora & the Burren Centre

    A genuinely small village anchoring the Burren, home to a cathedral with High Crosses and an interpretive centre explaining the region's unique geology.

    Tip Genuinely worth a stop to understand the Burren's landscape before exploring it further.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Cliffs of Moher & the Burren

The region genuinely rewards a mix of cliff-edge walking and slower time in the Burren's quieter landscape.

  1. Walk the Cliffs of Moher coastal path

    A genuinely well-maintained path runs the length of the cliffs, with the quieter southern stretch toward Hag's Head often less crowded.

    Tip Stay genuinely behind the safety fencing — the cliff edges are a real, serious hazard in wind.

  2. Explore Poulnabrone Dolmen up close

    A genuinely short, easy walk across open limestone to one of Ireland's most photographed ancient monuments.

    Tip Genuinely visit early or late in the day to have the site largely to yourself.

  3. Go underground at Aillwee Cave

    A genuinely accessible guided tour into the Burren's underground world, good for a rainy-day alternative to the cliffs.

    Tip Genuinely combine with the on-site farm shop's cheese, made from the surrounding Burren pasture.

  4. Spot rare Burren wildflowers

    A genuinely unusual botanical mix of Arctic, alpine and Mediterranean species growing side by side, found almost nowhere else on Earth.

    Tip Genuinely late spring brings the widest variety of Burren wildflowers into bloom.

  5. Hear trad music in a Doolin pub

    A genuinely essential Doolin evening, with several small pubs hosting some of Ireland's most respected nightly sessions.

    Tip Genuinely arrive early for a seat near the musicians — Doolin's sessions draw a loyal crowd.

  6. Drive the Burren's open karst plateau

    A genuinely otherworldly drive across bare grey limestone terraces, unlike any other landscape in Ireland.

    Tip Genuinely stop often — the Burren's scale and strangeness are best appreciated slowly, not from a moving car.

Transport

Getting to and around the Cliffs of Moher & the Burren

The Cliffs of Moher and the Burren sit genuinely within easy reach of Shannon, Galway and the wider west coast.

Getting there

  • Flight to Shannon Airport

    Genuinely the closest airport, about an hour's drive from the cliffs.

    Well connected to the UK, mainland Europe and North America.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely the most flexible way to combine the cliffs, the Burren and Doolin in one visit.

    Roads here are relatively straightforward compared to the Ring of Kerry or Dingle.

  • Day tour from Galway

    Genuinely a popular no-driving option, widely available as a full-day coach trip.

    Covers the cliffs and often a stop in the Burren, returning to Galway by evening.

Getting around

A car is genuinely the most practical way to see both the cliffs and the Burren in the same visit.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely essential for covering the cliffs, Poulnabrone Dolmen and the Burren's wider sights at a flexible pace.

    Distances are short but the sights are spread across open countryside.

  • On foot in Doolin

    Genuinely easy — the village itself is small and walkable.

    The Cliffs of Moher are also reachable on foot via a scenic coastal trail from Doolin.

  • Organised day tours

    Genuinely a relaxed option from Galway, Limerick or the Dingle Peninsula.

    A practical choice for visitors without a rental car.

When to go

Best time to visit the Cliffs of Moher & the Burren

The cliffs are genuinely visitable year-round, though clear days for the best views are far more likely outside midsummer.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely good odds of clear visibility and the Burren's wildflowers in full bloom.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    The busiest season at the cliffs, with the visitor centre car park often at capacity by midday.

  • Early autumn (September)

    Good

    Genuinely a quieter window with still-decent weather odds.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Genuinely dramatic but often wet and windy, with real risk of the cliff path partially closing in storms.

Worth planning around: Expecting clear visibility on any given day — genuinely check the forecast, since low cloud can hide the cliffs entirely.

Travel tips

The Cliffs of Moher & the Burren travel tips

Practical notes that make a Cliffs of Moher and Burren visit noticeably smoother.

  • Visit genuinely early morning or late afternoon to avoid the busiest midday tour bus crowds.
  • Check the genuinely weather forecast before going — low cloud can hide the cliffs completely.
  • Stay genuinely behind the safety fencing at all times — the cliff edges are a real, serious hazard.
  • Book Doolin accommodation genuinely well ahead in summer given the village's small size.
  • Wear genuinely sturdy shoes and a windproof layer, even on an apparently calm day.
  • Combine genuinely the cliffs with the Burren rather than choosing one — they're a short drive apart.

FAQ

The Cliffs of Moher & the Burren: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need for the Cliffs of Moher and the Burren?

One to two days genuinely covers both comfortably, and the cliffs alone are an easy half-day visit.

Can you visit the Cliffs of Moher as a day trip from Galway?

Yes, genuinely — it's about a ninety-minute drive each way, and organised day tours run regularly.

Is the Cliffs of Moher walk difficult?

No, genuinely accessible — the main path is well maintained and mostly flat, though the full length is a few hours round trip.

What is the Burren known for?

Genuinely its unusual limestone karst landscape, hiding rare Arctic and Mediterranean wildflowers alongside one of Europe's densest concentrations of ancient tombs.

Is Doolin worth staying in?

Yes, genuinely — it's small, but its traditional music pubs are among Ireland's most respected, and it sits close to both the cliffs and the Burren.

How old is Poulnabrone Dolmen?

Genuinely around 5,800 years old, a Neolithic portal tomb older than Stonehenge and England's other famous prehistoric monuments.
