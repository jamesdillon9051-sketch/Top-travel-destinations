Welcome to Kilkenny

Kilkenny is genuinely Ireland's best-preserved medieval city, its narrow streets still following a layout laid out under Norman rule nearly a thousand years ago.

The city's skyline is genuinely anchored by two landmarks from very different eras — the Anglo-Norman Kilkenny Castle overlooking the River Nore, and St Canice's 6th-century round tower rising above the cathedral quarter, one of the few in Ireland still open for visitors to climb.

Most visitors genuinely explore it as an easy day trip or overnight from Dublin, walking the signposted Medieval Mile between the city's historic sites at an unhurried pace.

Why visit

Why visit Kilkenny?

Kilkenny's case rests on genuinely dense, walkable medieval history rarely matched elsewhere in Ireland.

  • It's genuinely Ireland's best-preserved medieval city

    Streets and buildings still follow a layout largely unchanged since Norman times.

  • St Canice's Round Tower is genuinely climbable

    A rare 6th-century round tower where visitors can still climb to the top for city views.

  • Kilkenny Castle is genuinely a full afternoon on its own

    Restored state rooms and formal gardens right in the city centre.

  • It's genuinely a real brewing town

    Home to Smithwick's, Ireland's oldest operating brewery, with a genuine history to match.

  • It works genuinely well as an easy Dublin day trip

    A little over an hour by train, making it accessible even on a short Ireland itinerary.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Kilkenny

Kilkenny's essential sights are genuinely dense enough to cover comfortably on foot along the signposted Medieval Mile.

  1. 01

    Kilkenny Castle

    Genuinely the city's dominant landmark, a Norman-founded castle rebuilt over centuries, with restored state rooms open to visitors.

    Tip Book genuinely the state apartments tour ahead in summer — the castle's grounds themselves are free to explore.

  2. 02

    St Canice's Cathedral & Round Tower

    A genuinely striking medieval cathedral beside a rare 6th-century round tower, one of the few in Ireland still open for visitors to climb.

    Tip Climb genuinely the round tower's steep internal ladders for a rewarding view over the whole city.

  3. 03

    The Medieval Mile Museum

    Genuinely housed in a beautifully restored medieval church, telling the city's history through artefacts found along the Medieval Mile itself.

    Tip Genuinely a good first stop to get context before walking the rest of the Mile.

  4. 04

    Rothe House

    Genuinely Ireland's most complete surviving Tudor merchant's house, built around three courtyards for a wealthy trading family.

    Tip The genuinely walled garden behind the house has been recreated in a 17th-century style.

  5. 05

    Kilkenny Castle's Rose Garden

    Genuinely formal gardens within the castle's parkland, a peaceful, free-to-enter space right in the city centre.

    Tip Genuinely a good picnic spot on a fine day, with wide lawns beyond the formal beds.

  6. 06

    The Smithwick's Experience

    Genuinely tells the story of Ireland's oldest operating brewery, founded on the site of a medieval abbey in 1710.

    Tip The genuinely tour ends with a tasting, included in the ticket price.

  7. 07

    Kilkenny's medieval streets

    Genuinely narrow lanes like the Butter Slip and High Street still trace the city's original Norman-era layout.

    Tip Genuinely look up — many buildings hide medieval features above modern shopfronts.

Things to do

Best things to do in Kilkenny

Kilkenny genuinely rewards a slow walk along the Medieval Mile, mixing museums with its brewing heritage.

  1. Climb St Canice's Round Tower

    A genuinely rare chance to climb inside a 6th-century round tower, one of only a handful in Ireland still open to visitors.

    Tip Genuinely not for the claustrophobic — the internal ladders are steep and narrow.

  2. Explore Kilkenny Castle's gardens

    A genuinely relaxed way to spend an hour, walking the castle's formal gardens and wider parkland.

    Tip Genuinely free to enter, even without a ticket for the castle's state apartments.

  3. Learn brewing history at Smithwick's

    A genuinely engaging tour through Ireland's oldest brewery, built on the site of a medieval Franciscan abbey.

    Tip Genuinely book ahead in summer — tour slots can fill up on busy days.

  4. Browse the Medieval Mile Museum

    A genuinely worthwhile hour, exploring artefacts and stories from along the city's historic medieval core.

    Tip Genuinely a good first stop before walking the rest of the Mile yourself.

  5. Walk the full Medieval Mile

    A genuinely signposted walking route linking the castle, cathedral and the city's best-preserved medieval buildings.

    Tip Genuinely allow a full afternoon to do the route justice with stops along the way.

  6. Have a pint in a medieval pub

    A genuinely atmospheric way to end the day, several of Kilkenny's pubs occupy buildings centuries old.

    Tip Genuinely ask locally for the oldest pub in town — several make a genuine claim to the title.

Transport

Getting to and around Kilkenny

Kilkenny is genuinely one of the easiest Irish towns to reach from Dublin, well suited to a day trip or short stay.

Getting there

  • Intercity train from Dublin

    Genuinely direct and comfortable, taking just over an hour and a half.

    Trains arrive at MacDonagh Station, a short walk from the city centre.

  • Intercity bus

    Genuinely frequent and affordable, running from Dublin, Cork and Waterford.

    A practical, if slightly slower, alternative to the train.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely convenient if combining Kilkenny with a wider southeast Ireland loop.

    About an hour and a half from Dublin via the M9 motorway.

Getting around

Kilkenny is genuinely compact enough to explore entirely on foot.

  • On foot

    Genuinely covers the entire Medieval Mile easily, from the castle to St Canice's Cathedral.

    The whole city centre is walkable in well under an hour, end to end.

  • Local taxis

    Genuinely useful for reaching accommodation slightly outside the centre.

    Rarely needed for sightseeing, given the compact city centre.

When to go

Best time to visit Kilkenny

Kilkenny's genuinely museum-heavy, walkable character makes it a viable day trip or short stay in any season.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely mild weather and thinner crowds than midsummer.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    The warmest and busiest season, including the popular Kilkenny Arts Festival in August.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Genuinely mild with thinner crowds than summer.

Kilkenny travel tips

Practical notes that make a Kilkenny visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book Kilkenny Castle's state apartments tour genuinely ahead in summer.
  • Follow the genuinely signposted Medieval Mile trail markers set into the pavement to stay on route.

FAQ

Kilkenny: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Kilkenny?

One day genuinely covers the Medieval Mile's main sights comfortably; an overnight allows a more relaxed pace.

Can you visit Kilkenny as a day trip from Dublin?

Yes, genuinely easily — direct trains take just over ninety minutes each way.

Is Kilkenny Castle worth visiting?

Yes, genuinely — the restored state apartments and free gardens make it worth a couple of hours on their own.

What is Kilkenny known for?

Genuinely its exceptionally well-preserved medieval streets, its castle, and its brewing heritage at Smithwick's.

Can you climb St Canice's Round Tower?

Yes, genuinely — it's one of the few round towers in Ireland still open to visitors, via a steep internal ladder.

Is Kilkenny worth visiting if you're only doing Dublin and one other stop?

Yes, genuinely — its combination of density and easy access from Dublin makes it one of the best single-day additions to a short Ireland trip.
