Welcome to Dublin

Dublin is genuinely one of Europe's most compact capital cities, small enough to cross on foot in under an hour, yet packed with a literary and musical reputation far larger than its size would suggest.

It's also genuinely a city of contrasts — a working medieval castle and a Georgian university sit blocks from buzzing Temple Bar pubs, while the Guinness Storehouse and Kilmainham Gaol tell two very different stories about the same small island.

Most visitors genuinely use Dublin as the start or end of a wider Ireland trip, and a couple of days here earns that role, offering real context — historical, literary and culinary — for everywhere else on the itinerary.

Why visit

Why visit Dublin?

Dublin's case rests on genuinely world-class museums and history packed into a small, easy-to-navigate city.

  • Trinity College holds a genuinely extraordinary manuscript

    The 9th-century Book of Kells and the Old Library's Long Room are among Europe's most atmospheric sights.

  • The Guinness Storehouse is genuinely a full sensory experience

    Seven floors built around a single pint, ending with panoramic city views from the rooftop Gravity Bar.

  • Kilmainham Gaol tells genuinely essential Irish history

    The site of the 1916 Rising leaders' executions, and one of the country's most moving museum experiences.

  • It's genuinely compact and walkable

    Most major sights sit within a comfortable walk of the city centre, either side of the River Liffey.

  • The pub and music culture is genuinely the real thing

    Live traditional sessions happen nightly, not just as a tourist performance.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Dublin

Dublin's essential sights combine genuine medieval and Georgian history with world-class museums.

  1. 01

    Trinity College & the Book of Kells

    Genuinely Ireland's oldest university, home to the illuminated 9th-century Book of Kells and the barrel-vaulted Long Room lined with ancient volumes.

    Tip Book genuinely timed tickets online ahead — the Long Room sells out, especially in summer.

  2. 02

    Guinness Storehouse

    A genuinely immersive seven-floor experience built around Ireland's most famous export, ending with a pint and a view from the rooftop Gravity Bar.

    Tip The genuinely rooftop bar offers one of Dublin's best panoramic views, included with entry.

  3. 03

    Dublin Castle

    A genuinely still-functioning seat of Irish state ceremony, its state apartments and Chapel Royal open to visitors between official events.

    Tip Check genuinely ahead — the state apartments occasionally close for official functions.

  4. 04

    Kilmainham Gaol

    A genuinely powerful former prison where leaders of the 1916 Easter Rising were executed, now a museum central to modern Irish history.

    Tip Book genuinely well ahead — tours are guided-only and regularly sell out days in advance.

  5. 05

    St Patrick's Cathedral

    Genuinely Ireland's largest church, founded in 1191, with connections to Jonathan Swift, who served as dean here for over 30 years.

    Tip The genuinely choral evensong, held most weekdays, is free and atmospheric.

  6. 06

    Phoenix Park

    A genuinely vast city park, more than twice the size of New York's Central Park, home to a herd of wild fallow deer and the President's residence.

    Tip Rent genuinely a bike to cover the park's full size — it's larger than it first appears.

  7. 07

    Temple Bar

    Genuinely Dublin's best-known nightlife quarter, cobbled lanes packed with pubs, live music and a lively, touristy energy after dark.

    Tip Prices genuinely run higher here than elsewhere — a few streets over often means a quieter, cheaper pint.

Things to do

Best things to do in Dublin

Dublin genuinely rewards mixing its major museums with a wander through its riverside and parkland.

  1. Cross the Ha'penny Bridge

    A genuinely essential Dublin moment, crossing the elegant cast-iron footbridge that has linked the city's two halves since 1816.

    Tip The genuinely bridge and riverbanks are especially atmospheric lit up after dark.

  2. Shop and stroll Grafton Street

    A genuinely lively pedestrianised street of shops, cafés and buskers, running from Trinity College to St Stephen's Green.

    Tip Genuinely street performers here are often excellent — worth stopping for a few minutes.

  3. Relax in St Stephen's Green

    A genuinely peaceful Victorian park right in the city centre, a good spot to pause between museums and shopping streets.

    Tip Genuinely free and open year-round, with a bandstand hosting occasional summer concerts.

  4. Day-trip to Howth

    A genuinely easy DART train ride to a working fishing village with cliff walks, seafood and views back across Dublin Bay.

    Tip The genuinely cliff path loop takes about two hours and needs no special gear.

  5. Catch a live trad session

    A genuinely nightly occurrence in pubs across the city, informal sessions of fiddle, bodhrán and tin whistle open to anyone who wanders in.

    Tip Genuinely arrive early for a seat near the musicians in the more popular venues.

  6. Explore Dublin's literary history

    A genuinely rich trail connecting Joyce, Wilde, Beckett and Yeats, from statues and plaques to the Dublin Writers Museum.

    Tip Genuinely a literary pub crawl combines the theme with a proper night out.

Local food

What to eat in Dublin

Dublin's food genuinely spans serious modern Irish dining and simple, hearty pub classics at every price point.

  • A pint of Guinness

    Genuinely tastes different at the source, poured fresh with the traditional two-part method across the city's pubs.

    Where to try it Traditional pubs citywide, plus the Guinness Storehouse itself.

  • Irish stew

    Genuinely worth seeking out at a traditional pub, a simple, slow-cooked stew of lamb, potatoes and root vegetables.

    Where to try it Traditional pubs and restaurants throughout the city centre.

  • Full Irish breakfast

    A genuinely substantial start to the day, found on nearly every café menu across the city.

    Where to try it Cafés and hotel breakfasts throughout Dublin.

  • Fish and chips

    A genuinely reliable, hearty classic, sold at chippers across the city, especially popular after a night out.

    Where to try it Chip shops citywide, particularly around Temple Bar and the north city centre.

  • Irish soda bread

    A genuinely daily staple, dense and slightly sweet, served alongside soup, stew or a simple breakfast.

    Where to try it Cafés and bakeries throughout the city.

  • Boxty

    A genuinely traditional potato pancake, crisp outside and soft within, often served wrapped around a savoury filling.

    Where to try it Traditional Irish restaurants around Temple Bar.

Where to stay in Dublin

Dublin genuinely offers accommodation across every budget, concentrated within a compact, walkable city centre.

  • Docklands and north city centre

    A short walk or tram ride from the centre, with good access to Phoenix Park and the main train stations.

Transport

Getting to and around Dublin

Dublin is genuinely Ireland's main international gateway, well connected by air and sea.

Getting there

  • Flight to Dublin Airport

    Genuinely most international routes into Ireland land here, about 30 minutes from the city centre.

    Frequent express buses connect the airport to central Dublin around the clock.

  • Ferry from the UK

    A genuinely popular option for visitors bringing a car from Britain, docking at the nearby port of Dublin.

    Routes run from Holyhead and other UK ports.

  • Intercity train

    Genuinely fast and direct from Cork, Galway, Kilkenny and other major towns.

    Trains arrive at Heuston or Connolly station, both close to the city centre.

Getting around

Dublin is genuinely compact enough to explore mostly on foot, with good public transport for the rest.

  • On foot

    Genuinely covers Trinity College, Temple Bar, Dublin Castle and most of the city centre easily.

    Central Dublin's main sights sit within a comfortable walking radius of each other.

  • The Luas tram and buses

    Genuinely reliable for reaching Phoenix Park, the docklands and other outer neighbourhoods.

    A single Leap Card covers trams, buses and the DART train.

  • The DART train

    Genuinely the easiest way to reach Howth and the coastal suburbs.

    Runs frequently along the coast, both north and south of the city centre.

When to go

Best time to visit Dublin

Dublin's genuinely museum-heavy, walkable character makes it a viable destination in any season, though summer brings the mildest outdoor conditions.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely mild weather and long daylight hours, with fewer crowds than midsummer.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    The warmest and busiest season, with festivals and outdoor events, also genuinely the priciest time to visit.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Genuinely mild with thinner crowds than summer, a good balance for museum-heavy sightseeing.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Short days and frequent rain, though genuinely atmospheric, with cosy pubs and lower prices.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Dublin's genuinely museum-rich, compact character makes it a workable destination in any season.

Travel tips

Dublin travel tips

Practical notes that make a Dublin stay noticeably smoother.

  • Book Guinness Storehouse and Kilmainham Gaol tickets genuinely well ahead — both regularly sell out.
  • Get a Leap Card for genuinely easy, discounted travel on trams, buses and the DART.
  • Budget for genuinely high restaurant and pub prices, especially around Temple Bar.
  • Wear genuinely comfortable shoes — Dublin's compact centre still adds up to real walking distances.
  • Pack a proper waterproof layer regardless of season — Dublin's weather genuinely shifts fast.
  • Walk a few streets beyond Temple Bar for a genuinely quieter, cheaper, and often better pint.

FAQ

Dublin: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Dublin?

Two to three days genuinely covers the major museums and sights at a comfortable, unrushed pace.

Is Dublin worth visiting before heading to the rest of Ireland?

Yes, genuinely — its museums and history add real context, and it's the natural gateway for trains and buses onward.

Is Dublin expensive?

Yes, genuinely one of the more expensive parts of Ireland — self-catering and a guesthouse help manage costs.

Is Dublin walkable?

Yes, genuinely — most major sights sit within a comfortable walk of the city centre, with good public transport for the rest.

What is Dublin famous for?

Genuinely its literary history, Trinity College and the Book of Kells, the Guinness Storehouse, and a legendary pub and live music culture.

Do I need to book the Book of Kells in advance?

Yes, genuinely strongly recommended — timed tickets for the Long Room and Book of Kells sell out, especially in summer.
