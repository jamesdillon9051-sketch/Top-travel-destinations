Welcome to Donegal

Donegal is genuinely Ireland's wild northwestern edge, a county of sea cliffs, empty beaches and mountains that sees a fraction of the visitors drawn to the southwest's Ring of Kerry or Dingle.

Its centrepiece is genuinely Slieve League, among the highest sea cliffs in Europe at nearly 600 metres, reached by a road and trail with barely a fraction of the Cliffs of Moher's crowds.

Most visitors genuinely combine a couple of days here with Glenveagh National Park's mountains and lakes further north, rewarding those willing to travel further off Ireland's classic first-timer route.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Donegal

Donegal's essential sights are genuinely spread across a large, sparsely populated county best explored by car.

  1. 01

    Slieve League Cliffs

    Genuinely among the highest sea cliffs in Europe, rising nearly 600 metres straight from the Atlantic, with a fraction of the Cliffs of Moher's crowds.

    Tip Genuinely park at Bunglass viewpoint for the best views without a long hike.

  2. 02

    Donegal Castle

    A genuinely well-restored 15th-century Gaelic chieftain's castle right in Donegal town, combining Irish and later Jacobean architecture.

    Tip The genuinely guided tour is worth the extra time for the castle's layered history.

  3. 03

    Glenveagh National Park

    A genuinely remote, wild valley of lakes and mountains, one of Ireland's largest national parks and home to a reintroduced golden eagle population.

    Tip Genuinely the shuttle bus from the visitor centre to the castle saves a long walk in.

  4. 04

    Glenveagh Castle

    A genuinely romantic 19th-century castle on the shore of Lough Beagh, with elaborate gardens sheltered within the wild valley.

    Tip Genuinely the walled garden is a surprising, sheltered contrast to the open mountains around it.

  5. 05

    Malin Head

    Genuinely Ireland's most northerly point, a dramatic, windswept headland with real end-of-the-world character.

    Tip Genuinely look for Banba's Crown, the old signal tower at the headland's tip.

  6. 06

    Fanad Head Lighthouse

    Genuinely regarded as one of the world's most beautiful lighthouses, perched dramatically above Lough Swilly's entrance.

    Tip Genuinely book ahead to stay overnight in the lighthouse keeper's cottages for a truly remote experience.

  7. 07

    Glencolmcille

    A genuinely remote Irish-speaking valley, home to a folk village museum recreating centuries of rural Donegal life.

    Tip Genuinely one of the further-flung stops in the county, rewarding those who make the drive out.

Transport

Getting to and around Donegal

Donegal genuinely requires more travel time than the southwest, given its remote northwestern position.

Getting there

  • Rental car from Dublin or Galway

    Genuinely the most practical way in, though a real time commitment given the distance.

    About three and a half hours from Dublin, or two and a half from Galway.

  • Flight to Donegal Airport

    Genuinely a scenic small regional airport with limited routes, mainly to Dublin.

  • Intercity bus

    Genuinely a workable, if slow, option from Dublin, Galway and other major towns.

    A practical budget alternative given limited flight and no direct train options.

Getting around

A car is genuinely essential for exploring Donegal's spread-out, sparsely populated landscape.

FAQ

Donegal: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Donegal?

Two days genuinely covers Slieve League, Donegal town and Glenveagh National Park comfortably; more time allows a trip to Malin Head or Fanad Head.

Is Slieve League better than the Cliffs of Moher?

Genuinely different rather than better — Slieve League is taller and far less crowded, though its viewpoints and facilities are more basic than the Cliffs of Moher's visitor centre.

Is Donegal worth the extra travel time?

Yes, genuinely, for visitors with time for a longer trip — its scenery rivals the southwest with a fraction of the visitors.

What is Donegal known for?

Genuinely its dramatic, sparsely visited coastline, including Slieve League's cliffs and Malin Head, Ireland's most northerly point.

Do you need a car in Donegal?

Yes, genuinely essential — the county's sights are spread across a large, sparsely populated area with limited public transport.

Is Glenveagh National Park worth visiting?

Yes, genuinely — it's one of Ireland's wildest national parks, with a genuinely romantic castle and real chances to spot golden eagles.
