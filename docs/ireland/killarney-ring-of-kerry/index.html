Welcome to Killarney & the Ring of Kerry

Killarney is genuinely the gateway to one of Ireland's most beloved landscapes, a town of lakes and mountains that has drawn visitors since Victorian times, when Queen Victoria's own 1861 visit gave Ladies View its name.

It's also genuinely the starting point for the Ring of Kerry, a signposted 179-kilometre loop road around the Iveragh Peninsula that strings together sea cliffs, sandy coves, mountain passes and tiny villages into one of Europe's classic scenic drives.

Most visitors genuinely spend two or three days here, splitting time between Killarney National Park's lakes and woodland and a full-day circuit of the Ring itself, best driven anticlockwise to avoid tour coach traffic.

Why visit

Why visit Killarney & the Ring of Kerry?

Killarney's case rests on genuinely combining an accessible national park with Ireland's most celebrated scenic drive.

  • The Ring of Kerry is genuinely Ireland's classic road trip

    A full day's loop past cliffs, beaches and mountain passes, different at every turn.

  • Killarney National Park is genuinely Ireland's oldest

    Lakes, ancient oak woodland and Ireland's only wild red deer herd, all within walking distance of town.

  • Muckross House offers genuinely real 19th-century grandeur

    A Victorian mansion and gardens that hosted Queen Victoria's own 1861 visit.

  • The Gap of Dunloe is genuinely dramatic on foot or by pony trap

    A glacial mountain pass between two of Ireland's highest peaks.

  • It works genuinely well as a base for the wider southwest

    Cork, Cobh and the Dingle Peninsula are all within a comfortable day's reach.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Killarney & the Ring of Kerry

Killarney's essential sights combine an accessible national park with the wider Ring of Kerry's dramatic coastal scenery.

  1. 01

    Killarney National Park

    Genuinely Ireland's oldest national park, three lakes surrounded by ancient oak woodland, mountains and Ireland's only wild native red deer herd.

    Tip The genuinely park is free to enter and easily reached on foot or by bike from Killarney town.

  2. 02

    Muckross House & Gardens

    A genuinely grand 19th-century mansion within the national park, its gardens and lakeside setting hosted Queen Victoria's 1861 visit.

    Tip Combine genuinely with the nearby Muckross Abbey ruins and traditional farms, an easy walking loop.

  3. 03

    The Ring of Kerry

    A genuinely spectacular 179-kilometre signposted loop around the Iveragh Peninsula, passing cliffs, coves and mountain passes.

    Tip Drive genuinely anticlockwise to avoid meeting large tour coaches head-on on narrow stretches.

  4. 04

    Gap of Dunloe

    A genuinely dramatic narrow mountain pass between Macgillycuddy's Reeks and Purple Mountain, walkable or reachable by traditional pony trap.

    Tip Genuinely start early — the narrow road gets busy with jaunting cars by midday in summer.

  5. 05

    Ladies View

    A genuinely sweeping panoramic viewpoint over the Lakes of Killarney, named for the delight of Queen Victoria's ladies-in-waiting in 1861.

    Tip The genuinely car park fills fast — an early or late visit avoids the tour bus crowds.

  6. 06

    Torc Waterfall

    A genuinely easy five-minute walk from the car park to a 20-metre waterfall tumbling through the national park's woodland.

    Tip Genuinely continue up the steep steps beside the falls for a rewarding wider valley view.

  7. 07

    Ross Castle

    A genuinely well-preserved 15th-century tower house on the shore of Lough Leane, and the departure point for lake boat tours.

    Tip Book genuinely a boat trip on Lough Leane from here for a different perspective on the park.

Things to do

Best things to do in Killarney & the Ring of Kerry

Killarney genuinely rewards slowing down, whether inside the national park or out on the wider Ring.

  1. Cycle or take a jaunting car through the park

    A genuinely classic way to cover the park's lakes and woodland, whether self-guided by bike or in a traditional horse-drawn jaunting car.

    Tip Genuinely jaunting car drivers double as local guides, pointing out details you'd otherwise miss.

  2. Drive the full Ring of Kerry loop

    A genuinely full-day undertaking, best started early to allow time at each of the many worthwhile stops along the way.

    Tip Genuinely fill up on fuel before setting out — stations are sparse on the peninsula's western stretch.

  3. Hike through the Gap of Dunloe

    A genuinely rewarding half-day walk through dramatic glacial scenery, often combined with a boat back across the lakes.

    Tip The genuinely classic route walks the Gap then boats back across the Lakes of Killarney to Ross Castle.

  4. Boat on the Lakes of Killarney

    A genuinely relaxed way to see the park from the water, departing regularly from Ross Castle.

    Tip Genuinely afternoon light on the lakes is often calmer and more photogenic than morning.

  5. Explore Muckross Abbey and gardens

    A genuinely atmospheric ruined 15th-century friary a short walk from Muckross House, framed by an ancient yew tree in its cloister.

    Tip Genuinely free to visit and rarely as busy as the main house.

  6. Stop at a Ring of Kerry viewpoint

    A genuinely essential part of the drive, with pull-offs at nearly every headland offering a different angle on the coastline.

    Tip Genuinely budget more time than you think — the stops add up quickly on the full loop.

Transport

Getting to and around Killarney & the Ring of Kerry

Killarney is genuinely well connected by road and rail from Ireland's main cities.

Getting there

  • Intercity train from Dublin or Cork

    Genuinely direct services run to Killarney's town-centre station.

    The Dublin route takes around three and a half hours.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely essential for actually driving the Ring of Kerry itself.

    Cork and Kerry airports both have car rental desks close to Killarney.

  • Flight to Kerry or Cork Airport

    Genuinely the fastest option from further afield, both within about an hour's drive.

    Kerry Airport has more limited routes than Cork, which has wider international connections.

Getting around

Killarney town is genuinely walkable, but the Ring of Kerry itself requires a car, bike or organised tour.

  • On foot or by bike

    Genuinely covers Killarney town and the national park easily.

    Bike rental shops in town offer an easy way to reach Muckross House and Ross Castle.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely the most flexible way to drive the full Ring of Kerry at your own pace.

    Drive anticlockwise to avoid oncoming tour coaches on the narrowest stretches.

  • Organised coach tour

    Genuinely a relaxed, no-driving option covering the Ring's highlights in a single day.

    Widely available from operators based in Killarney town.

When to go

Best time to visit Killarney & the Ring of Kerry

Killarney is genuinely a year-round destination, though the Ring of Kerry's narrow roads are far more pleasant outside peak summer.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely mild weather, long daylight and noticeably fewer tour coaches than midsummer.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    The warmest and busiest season, with the Ring of Kerry's narrow roads at their most crowded.

  • Early autumn (September)

    Good

    Genuinely a quieter, often mild window with the park's woodland beginning to turn.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Short days and frequent rain, though genuinely quiet, with the park especially peaceful.

Worth planning around: Driving the Ring of Kerry in the middle of a July or August day, when tour coach traffic genuinely peaks on the narrowest sections.

Travel tips

Killarney & the Ring of Kerry travel tips

Practical notes that make a Killarney and Ring of Kerry visit noticeably smoother.

  • Drive the Ring of Kerry genuinely anticlockwise to avoid meeting tour coaches head-on.
  • Start genuinely early if driving the full Ring in one day — the stops add up quickly.
  • Book jaunting car and boat tours genuinely a day ahead in peak summer.
  • Fill up on fuel genuinely before setting out — stations thin out on the peninsula's western stretch.
  • Pack a proper waterproof layer regardless of season — Kerry's weather genuinely shifts fast.
  • Consider splitting the Ring over two days, overnighting in Kenmare or Sneem, for a genuinely less rushed pace.

FAQ

Killarney & the Ring of Kerry: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need for Killarney and the Ring of Kerry?

Two to three days genuinely covers the national park and a full day driving the Ring of Kerry at a comfortable pace.

Can you drive the Ring of Kerry in one day?

Yes, genuinely, though starting early and limiting stops is essential — many visitors prefer splitting it over two days for a slower pace.

Which direction should you drive the Ring of Kerry?

Anticlockwise, genuinely — this avoids meeting large tour coaches head-on on the narrowest coastal stretches.

Is Killarney National Park free to visit?

Yes, genuinely free to enter, though Muckross House itself charges a separate admission fee.

What is Killarney known for?

Genuinely its national park's lakes and mountains, and as the starting point for the Ring of Kerry, Ireland's most celebrated scenic drive.

Is the Ring of Kerry worth it if you've done the Dingle Peninsula?

Yes, genuinely — the two are different in character, with the Ring's peninsula-wide loop offering more varied terrain than Dingle's shorter, more concentrated drive.
