Welcome to Galway

Galway is genuinely Ireland's most beloved small city, a compact tangle of medieval streets on Galway Bay with a reputation for live music and festival energy far bigger than its actual size.

It's also genuinely the natural base for the west coast, sitting within easy reach of the Cliffs of Moher, Connemara and the Aran Islands, while holding plenty of its own character in the Latin Quarter's colourful shopfronts and buskers.

Most visitors genuinely spend a couple of days here, splitting time between the city's compact centre and a day trip out toward the coast or the islands.

Why visit

Why visit Galway?

Galway's case rests on genuinely outsized character for its size and an unmatched base for the west coast.

  • The Latin Quarter is genuinely full of life

    Cobbled medieval streets packed with buskers, independent shops and pubs, day and night.

  • Live music here is genuinely everywhere, not occasional

    Trad sessions, buskers and festivals run through the calendar, not just on weekends.

  • It's genuinely the best base for the west coast

Main attractions

Top things to see in Galway

Galway's essential sights fit comfortably into a compact, walkable city centre.

  1. 01

    The Spanish Arch

    Genuinely Galway's best-known landmark, a 16th-century stone archway once part of the city's medieval walls, overlooking the River Corrib.

    Tip The genuinely riverside green beside the arch is a popular spot to sit on a fine evening.

  2. 02

    The Latin Quarter

    A genuinely lively tangle of cobbled medieval streets, packed with independent shops, buskers and pubs from morning until late.

    Tip Genuinely Quay Street and Shop Street are the liveliest, especially on a weekend evening.

  3. 03

    Galway Cathedral

    A genuinely striking mid-20th-century cathedral with a huge copper dome, unusual for combining Renaissance, Gothic and Irish architectural styles.

    Tip The genuinely riverside setting makes for one of Galway's best photo spots, especially at sunset.

  4. 04

    St Nicholas' Collegiate Church

    Genuinely Ireland's largest medieval parish church still in continuous use, founded in 1320, with a legend that Christopher Columbus prayed here.

    Tip The genuinely surrounding Saturday market is one of Galway's best, browsing food and crafts around the church.

  5. 05

    Eyre Square

    Genuinely the city's central public square, a green space bordered by shops and hotels, and the natural starting point for exploring on foot.

    Tip Genuinely a good landmark for getting oriented before heading into the Latin Quarter's tangle of streets.

  6. 06

    Salthill Promenade

    A genuinely classic seafront walk along Galway Bay, ending traditionally with visitors 'kicking the wall' at Blackrock diving tower.

    Tip Genuinely popular with locals for an evening walk or a bracing swim off the diving tower.

  7. 07

    The River Corrib

    Genuinely one of Ireland's fastest-flowing rivers, cutting right through the city centre on its short run from Lough Corrib to the bay.

    Tip Genuinely look for salmon leaping at the Salmon Weir Bridge in late spring.

Things to do

Best things to do in Galway

Galway genuinely rewards wandering without much of a plan, pub to pub and street to street.

  1. Wander the Latin Quarter's colourful shopfronts

    A genuinely easy, aimless pleasure, browsing independent shops and colourful storefronts through the medieval streets.

    Tip Genuinely many of the best shops are down small side lanes off the main streets.

  2. Walk Salthill Promenade

    A genuinely classic Galway walk along the bay, traditionally ending with a symbolic 'kick of the wall' at the diving tower.

    Tip Genuinely sunset here, looking back across the bay, is one of Galway's best free views.

  3. Cross the Spanish Arch to the Claddagh

    A genuinely short walk into the historic Claddagh fishing village, the origin of the famous Claddagh ring design.

    Tip Genuinely look for the traditional Claddagh ring in jewellery shop windows back in the city centre.

  4. Sample fresh oysters

    A genuinely essential Galway experience, native oysters harvested right from the bay, celebrated each September at a dedicated festival.

    Tip Genuinely book ahead if visiting during the Galway International Oyster Festival in September.

  5. Catch a trad session on Quay Street

    A genuinely nightly occurrence, with pubs along Quay Street and Shop Street hosting live sessions most evenings.

    Tip Genuinely arrive early for a seat near the musicians in the more popular spots.

  6. Take the ferry to the Aran Islands

    A genuinely easy day trip from Galway, with regular sailings out to Inis Mór and the other Aran Islands.

    Tip Genuinely book ferry tickets ahead in summer, and check the forecast — sailings depend on conditions.

Transport

Getting to and around Galway

Galway is genuinely well connected by road and rail, though its own airport has limited scheduled service.

Getting there

  • Intercity train from Dublin

    Genuinely direct and comfortable, taking around two and a half hours.

    Trains arrive at Galway Ceannt Station, in the city centre.

  • Flight to Shannon or Knock

    Genuinely the closest airports, both under an hour and a half's drive.

    Shannon has wider international connections than Ireland West Airport Knock.

  • Intercity bus

    Genuinely frequent and affordable from Dublin, Cork and other major towns.

    A practical, if slower, alternative to the train.

Getting around

Galway is genuinely compact enough to explore entirely on foot.

  • On foot

    Genuinely covers the entire city centre, from Eyre Square to the Spanish Arch, easily.

    Galway's small size means a car is unnecessary, and often impractical, within the city itself.

  • Local bus to Salthill

    Genuinely quick for reaching the promenade if not walking.

    Frequent services run from the city centre.

  • Rental car for day trips

    Genuinely useful for reaching Connemara or the Cliffs of Moher independently.

    Not needed within the city itself, only for excursions beyond it.

When to go

Best time to visit Galway

Galway is genuinely lively year-round, but its packed festival calendar makes summer the liveliest, and busiest, time to visit.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely mild weather and thinner crowds than the summer festival season.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Genuinely the liveliest season, with the Galway International Arts Festival and other events, also the busiest and priciest.

  • September

    Good

    Genuinely home to the famous Galway International Oyster Festival, with thinner crowds than midsummer.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Short days and frequent rain, though genuinely the pub and music scene runs year-round regardless.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Galway's genuinely compact, indoor-friendly character makes it a workable destination in any season.

Travel tips

Galway travel tips

Practical notes that make a Galway stay noticeably smoother.

  • Book accommodation genuinely well ahead during the July arts festival or September oyster festival.
  • Check the genuinely ferry schedule and weather before an Aran Islands day trip.
  • Wear genuinely comfortable shoes for the Latin Quarter's cobbled streets.
  • Pack a proper waterproof layer regardless of season — Galway's weather genuinely shifts fast off the bay.

FAQ

Galway: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Galway?

Two days genuinely covers the city itself comfortably, with more time useful for day trips to the Aran Islands or Connemara.

Is Galway a good base for the west coast?

Yes, genuinely one of the best — the Cliffs of Moher, Connemara and the Aran Islands are all a short trip away.

Is Galway walkable?

Yes, genuinely very — the entire city centre sits within a comfortable walk, and a car isn't needed within the city itself.

What is Galway famous for?

Genuinely its live music scene, native oysters, and the Latin Quarter's colourful medieval streets.

When is the Galway Oyster Festival?

Genuinely every September, one of Ireland's oldest and best-loved food festivals, celebrating the bay's native oysters.

Can you visit the Aran Islands from Galway in a day?

Yes, genuinely — regular ferries run from Galway and nearby Rossaveal, making it an easy, popular day trip.
