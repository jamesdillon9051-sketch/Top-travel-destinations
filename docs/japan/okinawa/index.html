Welcome to Okinawa

Okinawa was, until 1879, an independent kingdom — the Ryukyu Kingdom — with its own language, distinct culture and centuries of trade relationships across East and Southeast Asia, and that separate history still shapes the islands' identity within modern Japan. Annexed relatively late and devastated by one of the Pacific War's bloodiest ground battles, Okinawa's relationship with the rest of the country remains genuinely, visibly its own.

The islands' subtropical climate and coral reefs offer a genuinely different Japan from the mainland's temperate cities and mountains — turquoise water, white sand beaches and some of the country's best diving and snorkelling, alongside a slower, warmer pace of daily life often summed up in the local phrase 'Okinawa time'.

Food, language and even burial customs still carry distinct Ryukyuan influence, and Okinawa's reputation for longevity — among the world's most-studied populations for exceptional lifespan — is genuinely tied to this separate culture, its diet and its community structure rather than being simply a warmer version of mainland Japanese life.

Why visit

Why visit Okinawa?

Okinawa's case is that no other part of Japan carries this distinct a separate history, culture and coral-reef landscape.

  • The Ryukyu Kingdom history is genuinely distinct

    Centuries as an independent kingdom left a language, culture and architecture genuinely separate from mainland Japan.

  • The coral reefs are genuinely excellent

    Among Japan's best diving and snorkelling, with visibility and marine life that rival far more famous tropical destinations.

  • The food culture is genuinely its own

    Distinct dishes and ingredients set Okinawan cuisine clearly apart from anything found on the mainland.

  • The history is genuinely sobering and important

    The Battle of Okinawa's memorial sites offer essential context for understanding the islands' modern identity.

  • The pace is genuinely different

    A slower, warmer island rhythm offers real contrast to mainland Japan's more famously fast-paced cities.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Okinawa

Okinawa's essential sights, spanning its royal Ryukyu Kingdom history and its dramatic coastline.

  1. 01

    Shurijo Castle

    The former royal palace of the Ryukyu Kingdom, a UNESCO World Heritage site whose distinct red-lacquered architecture reflects centuries of Chinese and Japanese cultural exchange.

    Tip The castle's main hall was destroyed by fire in 2019 and remains under reconstruction — check current visitor access before planning around it.

  2. 02

    Churaumi Aquarium

    One of the world's largest aquariums, its enormous main tank home to whale sharks and manta rays, a genuinely spectacular centrepiece of the Okinawa Churaumi Marine Park.

    Tip Arrive near feeding times, posted at the entrance, for the most active viewing of the main tank's largest residents.

  3. 03

    Cape Manzamo

    A dramatic elephant-trunk-shaped cliff formation dropping into brilliant turquoise water, one of Okinawa's most photographed natural landmarks.

    Tip The short coastal walking path around the cape offers several genuinely striking angles beyond the main viewpoint.

  4. 04

    Kokusai-dori, Naha

    Naha's main shopping and dining street, genuinely lively with local crafts, Okinawan food stalls and a covered arcade market just off its length.

    Tip The Makishi Public Market, just off Kokusai-dori, offers a genuinely more local, less touristy food experience.

  5. 05

    Kabira Bay, Ishigaki

    A genuinely stunning bay of turquoise and deep-blue water dotted with small islands, among the most photographed views in the wider Yaeyama Islands.

    Tip Swimming is restricted in parts of the bay to protect its pearl farms — glass-bottom boat tours are the main way to see it up close.

  6. 06

    Peace Memorial Park, Itoman

    A genuinely moving memorial to the Battle of Okinawa, one of the Pacific War's bloodiest ground battles, listing the names of all who died regardless of nationality.

    Tip The adjacent Okinawa Prefectural Peace Memorial Museum provides essential context for the park's memorials.

Things to do

Best things to do in Okinawa

Beyond the historic sites, Okinawa rewards its coral reefs, its distinct culture and its genuinely different island pace.

  1. Snorkel or dive Okinawa's coral reefs

    Genuinely excellent visibility and reef health make Okinawa one of Japan's best diving and snorkelling destinations, rivalling far more famous tropical spots.

    Tip The Kerama Islands, a short boat trip from Naha, offer some of the clearest water and healthiest reefs in the region.

  2. Relax on a beach in Miyako or Ishigaki

    The outer islands hold genuinely some of Japan's best beaches, with white sand and consistently clear turquoise water.

    Tip Miyako's Yonaha Maehama Beach is regularly ranked among Japan's best, with a genuinely long, uncrowded stretch of sand.

  3. Visit a traditional Ryukyuan village

    Recreated historic villages offer genuine insight into pre-annexation Ryukyu Kingdom architecture, crafts and daily life.

    Tip Ryukyu Mura combines traditional buildings with craft demonstrations, a genuinely good introduction before visiting Shurijo Castle.

  4. Learn about Okinawan karate's roots

    Modern karate genuinely originated in Okinawa, developed from indigenous fighting traditions and Chinese martial arts exchange.

    Tip Several dojos across the islands offer visitor-friendly introductory sessions, a genuinely hands-on way to engage with the history.

  5. Eat Okinawa soba for lunch

    A genuinely different noodle from mainland soba, made with wheat rather than buckwheat, in a pork-based broth, an everyday island staple.

    Tip Look for small family-run soba shops away from Kokusai-dori for a genuinely more authentic, less tourist-priced bowl.

  6. Try awamori at a local bar

    Okinawa's own distilled spirit, made from long-grain rice using a technique distinct from mainland sake or shochu production, a genuine local specialty.

    Tip Aged awamori (kusu) is genuinely smoother and more complex than younger versions — worth the modest price difference.

Local food

What to eat in Okinawa

Okinawan food stands genuinely apart from the mainland, shaped by the islands' distinct history, climate and Ryukyu Kingdom trade connections.

  • Okinawa soba

    Wheat noodles in a pork-based broth, topped with stewed pork belly, genuinely distinct from mainland buckwheat soba despite the shared name.

    Where to try it Family-run soba shops throughout the islands, an everyday local staple rather than a special-occasion dish.

  • Goya champuru

    A stir-fry of bitter melon, tofu, egg and pork, a genuine Okinawan comfort food built around an ingredient the islands are particularly known for.

    Where to try it Traditional Okinawan restaurants (izakaya) throughout Naha and the outer islands.

  • Rafute

    Pork belly slow-braised in awamori, soy sauce and brown sugar until genuinely tender, a signature dish reflecting Okinawa's historically important pork culture.

    Where to try it Traditional Okinawan restaurants, often served as part of a larger set meal.

  • Awamori

    Okinawa's own distilled rice spirit, made using a distinct black koji fermentation method separate from mainland sake or shochu traditions.

    Where to try it Local bars and dedicated awamori distilleries offering tastings across the islands.

  • Taco rice

    Seasoned taco filling served over rice rather than a tortilla, a genuine Okinawan invention reflecting decades of American military presence on the islands.

    Where to try it Casual diners throughout Naha, a genuinely beloved local comfort food rather than a novelty.

  • Beni-imo (purple sweet potato) sweets

    Okinawa's distinctive purple sweet potato appears throughout the islands' desserts, tarts and even soft serve, a genuinely popular local specialty.

    Where to try it Bakeries and souvenir shops throughout Naha and Kokusai-dori, particularly for the tart known locally as beni-imo tart.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Okinawa

Okinawa's accommodation spreads across Naha's city convenience and the outer islands' beach resorts.

    The main island's capital, with Shurijo Castle, Kokusai-dori and the airport all within easy reach.

    Best for: History, food and transport connections

  • Onna, on the main island's west coast

    A genuinely popular resort strip putting Cape Manzamo and Churaumi Aquarium within reasonable driving distance.

    Best for: Resort beaches and the aquarium

    A short flight from Naha, these outer islands hold genuinely the region's best beaches and reef access.

Transport

Getting to and around Okinawa

Naha Airport is Okinawa's main gateway, with domestic flights the practical way to reach the outer islands.

Getting there

  • Naha Airport (OKA)

    Well connected to Tokyo, Osaka and other major Japanese cities, with a growing range of direct international routes.

    Flights from Tokyo take about 2h45, considerably faster than any rail or ferry alternative.

  • Domestic flights to the outer islands

    Short flights connect Naha to Ishigaki and Miyako in under an hour, the practical way to reach the region's best beaches.

    Book ahead in peak summer season, when outer island flights can sell out.

  • Ferries

    Longer, slower ferry connections link Naha to several of the outer islands for those with more time and a preference for sea travel.

    Ferries are genuinely more affordable than flights but take considerably longer, sometimes overnight.

Getting around

A rental car is genuinely the most practical way to explore Okinawa's main island beyond central Naha.

  • Rental car

    The most flexible way to reach Cape Manzamo, Churaumi Aquarium and the main island's beaches at your own pace.

    An International Driving Permit is required, and Okinawa drives on the left, as with the rest of Japan.

  • Yui Rail monorail, in Naha

    Naha's monorail covers the city centre and airport well, genuinely useful for a Naha-focused day or two.

    Beyond central Naha, the monorail's reach is limited — a car or bus becomes necessary.

  • Local buses

    Cover the main island reasonably, though with considerably less frequency and coverage than mainland Japan's urban transit.

    Routes and schedules can be genuinely confusing for first-time visitors — a car remains the more practical option for most itineraries.

When to go

Best time to visit Okinawa

Okinawa's subtropical climate means genuinely warm weather most of the year, with typhoon season and summer humidity the main factors to plan around.

  • Spring (April–June)

    Best time

    Warm, increasingly clear water and comfortable temperatures before the peak of summer humidity and crowds.

  • Summer (July–September)

    Shoulder

    Hot, humid and genuinely the peak of typhoon season — check forecasts closely if travelling during these months.

  • Autumn (October–November)

    Best time

    Still genuinely warm enough for swimming, with typhoon risk easing and crowds noticeably thinner than summer.

  • Winter (December–March)

    Good

    Mild by mainland standards but too cool for most swimming, a genuinely good season for sightseeing without crowds.

Worth planning around: Peak typhoon season (August–September) without flexible travel plans, when storms can genuinely disrupt flights and ferries with little notice.

Travel tips

Okinawa travel tips

Practical notes on weather, transport and booking that make a real difference to an Okinawa trip.

  • Check typhoon forecasts closely if travelling in August or September, and keep flight plans genuinely flexible during that window.
  • Rent a car for the main island — public transport beyond central Naha is genuinely limited.
  • Book outer island flights and accommodation ahead in peak summer season, when availability tightens.
  • Try Okinawa soba at a small family-run shop rather than a Kokusai-dori tourist restaurant for better value and flavour.
  • Check Shurijo Castle's current reconstruction status before visiting, following the 2019 fire.
  • Bring reef-safe sunscreen — Okinawa's coral reefs are genuinely sensitive to standard sunscreen chemicals.
  • Allow real travel time between the main island and the outer islands if attempting to combine both in one trip.

FAQ

Okinawa: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Okinawa?

Five to six for the main island alone; add two or three more if extending to Ishigaki or Miyako for the region's best beaches and diving.

Is Okinawa very different from mainland Japan?

Genuinely, yes — the islands' separate Ryukyu Kingdom history, distinct food culture, subtropical climate and slower pace set Okinawa clearly apart from the mainland experience.

Do I need to fly to the outer islands, or can I combine everything on the main island?

The main island alone offers genuinely worthwhile beaches, history and the aquarium, but the outer islands' best diving and beaches require a short domestic flight.

What is the best time of year to visit?

April to June and October to November offer warm weather with lower typhoon risk than peak summer, which runs genuinely humid with real storm potential.

Is Okinawa expensive compared to mainland Japan?

Broadly comparable, with domestic flights to the outer islands the main additional cost beyond a typical mainland Japan trip.
