Welcome to Szeged

Szeged is the largest city in southern Hungary, with about 160,000 people, and it sits on the Tisza river near the borders with Serbia and Romania. It receives more hours of sunshine than any other Hungarian city, and it is a lively university town, with students making up a big part of the population. It is known as the City of Sunshine.

In 1879, a devastating flood destroyed most of the city. Rebuilding created a rational plan of wide boulevards and ring roads, with grand Art Nouveau and eclectic buildings, such as the colourful Reök Palace. The centrepiece is the Votive Church, a twin-towered brick cathedral built after the flood, whose 91-metre spires dominate Dóm Square, where an open-air festival of theatre and music is held each summer.

Szeged is also a food city. It is the home of halászlé, the paprika fish soup made with river carp and catfish, of Pick salami, and of one of the country's main paprika-growing areas. It makes a good stop on the way to or from Serbia, and one or two days is enough.
