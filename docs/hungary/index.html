Why go to Hungary

Hungary lies in the Carpathian Basin in the heart of Europe, a landlocked country of about 9.5 million people, roughly the size of Portugal. Its language, Hungarian, is unrelated to its Slavic, Germanic and Romance neighbours, and its culture is distinctive, shaped by the Magyars who arrived in the 9th century, by Ottoman rule, by the Habsburg empire and by the Soviet era. It has been an EU member since 2004.

Most visitors come for Budapest, which is one of the great capitals of Europe. It has thermal baths fed by hot springs, a hilltop castle and grand boulevards, and a dining and nightlife scene that includes the famous ruin bars. But beyond the capital, Hungary has medieval towns, baroque wine regions, the biggest lake in Central Europe, folk traditions on the puszta plains and a thousand years of history.

It is also excellent value, with train and bus links that reach nearly everywhere, comfortable hotels, and food and wine that cost far less than in Austria next door. English is widely spoken by younger people in Budapest, and less so in the countryside. The best months are April to June and September to October.

Experiences

Best things to do in Hungary

The experiences that recur across the country and make a Hungarian trip feel like one.

  1. Soak in a thermal bath

    Hungary sits on hot springs, and its baths are part of daily life, from the palatial Széchenyi in Budapest to small spa towns. See Budapest.

    Tip Bring flip-flops, a towel and a swimsuit, or rent them.

  2. Cruise the Danube

    The river passes Parliament and Buda Castle, and reaches the Danube Bend. See Budapest and the Danube Bend.

    Tip Go at night for the lit-up views.

  3. Drink in a ruin bar

    Budapest's bars in derelict buildings are eclectic and lively. See Budapest.

    Tip Go on weekday evenings for elbow room.

  4. Taste Tokaji wine

    The sweet Tokaji Aszú has been called the wine of kings. See Tokaj.

    Tip Book a cellar tour.

  5. Ride on the puszta

    The great plains have horsemen, cattle and traditional farms. See Hortobágy.

    Tip Go in summer for the horse shows.

  6. Relax at Lake Balaton

    Central Europe's largest lake has beaches, vineyards and a lively summer season. See Lake Balaton.

    Tip Visit in June or September to avoid crowds.

  7. Wander a medieval castle town

    Eger, Sopron and Visegrád have castles and walls. See Eger and Sopron.

    Tip Combine with a wine tasting.

Food and drink

Hungary foods to try

Hungarian food is rich and paprika-red, with stews, dumplings, sausages and pastries, plus some of Europe's most underrated wines.

  • Goulash

    A paprika-spiced soup or stew of beef, potatoes and vegetables, traditionally cooked in a cauldron by herdsmen.

    Where to try it Restaurants across Budapest and the puszta.

  • Lángos

    Deep-fried flatbread topped with sour cream, garlic and cheese, a favourite street snack at markets and by Lake Balaton.

    Where to try it Markets, beaches and food stalls.

  • Kürtőskalács

    A sweet spiral cake baked over coals, rolled in sugar and cinnamon, and sold hot at fairs and in Budapest.

    Where to try it Christmas markets and street stalls.

  • Dobos torte

    A layered sponge cake with chocolate buttercream and a caramel top, created in Budapest in 1885.

    Where to try it Historic cafés such as Gerbeaud in Budapest.

  • Tokaji Aszú

    A sweet wine from grapes affected by noble rot, with honeyed and apricot flavours, produced in the Tokaj region.

    Where to try it Cellars in Tokaj.

  • Halászlé

    A hot, red fish soup with paprika, made with carp or catfish, a Danube and Tisza speciality.

    Where to try it Riverside restaurants in Szeged and along the Danube.

Before you go

Practical travel information for Hungary

The essentials, current for 2026. Hungary is easy and safe, though language, currency and a few local scams are worth knowing.

Capital
Budapest
Language
Hungarian (Magyar), unrelated to most European languages. English is widely spoken in Budapest and tourist areas, German is common in the west, and English is limited in rural areas.
Currency
Hungarian forint (HUF, Ft). Roughly 330–400 forints to the US dollar in recent years. Hungary is in the EU but not the eurozone, and some tourist places quote euro prices at poor rates.
Cards and cash
Cards are accepted almost everywhere in cities, and contactless payment is common. Use bank ATMs (OTP, K&H), decline dynamic currency conversion, and beware of currency exchange booths with poor rates. Carry some forints for markets and small towns.
Entry and visas
Hungary is in the EU and the Schengen Area. Many nationalities, including the United Kingdom, United States, Canada and Australia, can stay up to 90 days in any 180 without a visa. The EU's ETIAS authorisation has been planned, so check current rules.
Power sockets
Type C and F (two round pins), 230 V, 50 Hz. UK and US visitors need an adaptor.
Emergency number
112 for all emergencies.
Tipping
About 10–15% in restaurants if a service charge is not already included. Check the bill, since many places add it.
Safety
Hungary is generally safe. Watch for pickpockets in Budapest's tourist areas and on public transport, and be wary of overpricing scams in some nightlife venues and unlicensed taxis. Use licensed taxis or apps such as Bolt and Főtaxi.
Internet and SIM
Very good. EU roaming applies to EU SIMs, and prepaid SIMs and eSIMs are cheap. Free Wi-Fi is common in cafés.
Time zone
Central European Time, UTC+1, and UTC+2 in summer.

When to go

Best time to visit Hungary

Hungary has a continental climate, with hot summers, cold winters, and pleasant springs and autumns.

  • Spring (April–May)

    Best time

    Mild, green and less crowded, with blossom and outdoor terraces opening.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Good

    Hot, sometimes over 35°C. Lake Balaton is at its liveliest, and festivals fill Budapest.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Warm days, grape harvests and golden leaves, with lower prices.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Quiet

    Cold and grey, but Christmas markets and steaming outdoor baths are magical.

Worth planning around: July and August heatwaves in cities, and January and February for long sightseeing.

Trip length

How long to spend in Hungary

Hungary is small, and Budapest is the focus. A week covers the capital with a couple of day trips, and two weeks allows a loop through wine country and the south.

Getting around

Transportation in Hungary

Trains and buses are cheap and cover the country. Budapest is the hub, and most journeys radiate from it.

  • Trains (MÁV)

    InterCity trains link Budapest with Eger, Szeged, Pécs and Sopron in two to three hours. Book on the MÁV app.

    Cheap, though slower than in Western Europe.

  • Buses (Volánbusz)

    Serve smaller towns and Lake Balaton.

    Book online.

  • Budapest public transport

    Metro, trams and buses, with a 24- or 72-hour travel card.

    Validate your ticket before boarding.

  • Taxis and ride-hailing

    Use Bolt or Főtaxi, and avoid street taxis.

    Agree the fare.

  • Hire cars

    Useful for Tokaj, Hortobágy and Hollókő. Motorways need an e-vignette, and the alcohol limit is zero.

    Buy the vignette online.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Hungary

Hungarians are proud, direct and hospitable, with a strong sense of history and a dry sense of humour. Family meals and toasts matter, and formal manners are appreciated in first meetings.

  • Greet with a handshake

    Shake hands with everyone, including at the start and end of meetings.

  • Avoid clinking beer glasses

    By tradition, Hungarians do not clink beer glasses, a custom linked to the 1849 revolution, though wine and spirits are fine.

  • Use formal address

    Use titles and formal speech with strangers and elders.

  • Bring a gift

    Flowers or wine if invited home.

  • Remove your shoes

    In homes, guests are often offered slippers.

  • Respect baths etiquette

    Shower before entering pools, and wear swim caps where required.

Do

  • Learn a few words: köszönöm (thank you), szia (hi), egészségedre (cheers)
  • Take a swim cap or rent one at some baths
  • Try pálinka, the local fruit brandy
  • Validate transit tickets
  • Book popular baths and restaurants
  • Check the service charge on bills

Don't

  • Don't clink beer glasses
  • Don't use unlicensed taxis
  • Don't exchange money on the street
  • Don't drink and drive, since the limit is zero
  • Don't compare Hungary to its neighbours
  • Don't assume everyone speaks English

Travel tips

Hungary travel tips

The things that most often catch visitors out in Hungary, several of which are specific to how the country works.

  • Use Bolt or Főtaxi instead of hailing taxis.
  • Decline currency conversion on card machines, and pay in forints.
  • Validate metro and tram tickets before boarding.
  • Book Széchenyi Baths and the Parliament tour in advance.
  • Check the service charge before adding a tip.
  • Buy an e-vignette before driving on motorways.
  • Bring cash for small towns and markets.
  • Watch for pickpockets on trams and in Váci Street.

FAQ

Visiting Hungary: frequently asked questions

Is Hungary safe for tourists?

Yes, generally very safe. Watch for pickpockets and overpricing in tourist bars.

How many days do you need in Hungary?

Three to four days for Budapest, and ten to fourteen for a wider trip.

Is Hungary cheap?

Yes, compared with Western Europe, and Budapest is very good value.

What is the best time to visit Hungary?

April to June and September to October.

Do I need a visa for Hungary?

Many nationalities can visit visa-free for 90 days under Schengen rules. Check current rules, including ETIAS.

Can I use euros in Hungary?

Some places accept euros but at poor rates. Use forints.
