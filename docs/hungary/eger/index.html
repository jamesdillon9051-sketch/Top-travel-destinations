Welcome to Eger

Eger is a small city of about 55,000 people in the foothills of the Bükk mountains, known for baroque architecture, wine and a heroic story. In 1552, a garrison of about 2,000 defenders under István Dobó held Eger Castle against an Ottoman army of many times that size, a victory celebrated in Géza Gárdonyi's novel Eclipse of the Crescent Moon. Later the Ottomans took the town in 1596 and ruled it for about 90 years, leaving a minaret behind.

Today the town is a charming baroque centre of yellow and pink buildings, with a large cathedral, a pedestrian main street and the castle on a hill, where you can walk the walls and explore tunnels. The 40-metre minaret is the northernmost in Europe from the Ottoman period. Nearby, thermal baths at Eger and Egerszalók offer relaxation.

The wine is the other draw. Eger is the home of Egri Bikavér, or Bull's Blood, a red blend with a legendary name, and in the Valley of the Beautiful Women, a hollow on the edge of town, dozens of family cellars serve wine straight from the barrel. Two days is a good stay.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Eger

The town is compact, and the wine valley is a short walk.

  1. 01

    Eger Castle

    A fortress on a hill with walls, bastions, tunnels and museums that tell the story of the 1552 siege.

    Tip Allow two hours.

  2. 02

    The Minaret

    A 40-metre minaret from the Ottoman occupation, which you can climb for views over the town.

    Tip The stairs are narrow.

  3. 03

    Valley of the Beautiful Women

    A valley of small cellars dug into the hillside, where families pour their own wines by the glass.

    Tip Go in the evening.

  4. 04

    Eger Basilica

    A huge neoclassical cathedral, one of Hungary's largest.

    Tip Free.

  5. 05

    Dobó Square

    The baroque main square with a statue of the castle's hero.

    Tip Café stop.

  6. 06

    Bükk National Park

    Hills and caves nearby.

    Tip Hire a car.
