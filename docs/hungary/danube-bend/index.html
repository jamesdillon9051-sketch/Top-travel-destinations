Welcome to the Danube Bend

North of Budapest, the Danube turns sharply from its eastward course toward the south, forming an S-shaped bend between the Börzsöny and Pilis hills. The scenery is green and hilly, and the river valley has been settled for centuries. Three towns make up the classic day trip: Szentendre, Visegrád and Esztergom.

Szentendre is a small baroque town of cobbled lanes, colourful houses and art galleries, settled by Serbian refugees in the 17th century, whose Orthodox churches still stand. Visegrád was a royal seat in the 14th and 15th centuries, and its hilltop citadel gives the finest views of the bend. Esztergom, the birthplace of King Stephen and the seat of Hungary's Catholic church, has the country's largest church, a domed basilica above the river opposite Slovakia.

All three can be reached from Budapest by train, bus or boat, and together they make a rewarding day trip, though a long one. Many visitors pick two, for example Szentendre and Visegrád, or spend a night.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Danube Bend

The three towns are 20 to 50 km apart along the river.

  1. 01

    Szentendre

    A pretty town of cobbled lanes, galleries, museums and Serbian Orthodox churches, only 40 minutes from Budapest by suburban train.

    Tip Visit the Marzipan Museum.

  2. 02

    Visegrád Citadel

    A hilltop fortress with views of the Danube Bend, near the ruins of a royal palace of the 14th century.

    Tip Take a taxi or bus up.

  3. 03

    Esztergom Basilica

    Hungary's largest church, with a dome visible for miles, built in the 19th century on the site of the country's first cathedral.

    Tip Climb the dome.

  4. 04

    Royal Palace of Visegrád

    Ruins of a Renaissance palace, with a Hercules fountain.

    Tip Good for history fans.

  5. 05

    Esztergom Castle

    The birthplace of King Stephen, with a museum.

    Tip Combine with the basilica.

  6. 06

    Pilis hills

    Forests and hiking above the river.

    Tip Bring maps.
