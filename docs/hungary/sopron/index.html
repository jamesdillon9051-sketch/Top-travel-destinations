Welcome to Sopron

Sopron is a compact town in the far west of Hungary, pressed against the Austrian border, whose medieval core has survived largely intact. Its nickname, Civitas Fidelissima, the most loyal town, dates from a referendum in 1921 in which residents voted to remain in Hungary rather than join Austria. The old town, within a ring of former walls, holds more than 200 listed buildings.

The symbol of the town is the Fire Tower, a 61-metre tower built on Roman foundations, with a baroque top added in the 18th century, and a balcony that gives sweeping views over the roofs. Below it, Fő tér has Gothic and baroque buildings, including the Storno House, and the town's medieval streets are dotted with courtyards and cellars. Sopron also has a long wine tradition, especially for Kékfrankos, a spicy red grape.

Beyond the town is Lake Fertő, a shallow steppe lake shared with Austria, and a UNESCO cultural landscape, with reed beds, birds and cycling paths. Sopron is a natural stop between Vienna and Budapest, and one or two days is enough.
