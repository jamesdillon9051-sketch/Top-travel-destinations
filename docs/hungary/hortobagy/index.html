Welcome to Hortobágy

The puszta is the Great Hungarian Plain, an enormous grassland stretching across eastern Hungary. Hortobágy National Park, founded in 1973, protects 800 square kilometres of it, and is Hungary's largest protected area. The landscape is flat and vast, with grasses, wetlands, fish ponds and a vast sky, and it has been shaped by centuries of grazing by cattle, horses and sheep. UNESCO lists it as a cultural landscape.

The traditional life of the puszta is still on show. Herdsmen, called csikós when they tend horses, ride in a distinctive costume of blue shirts, wide trousers and hats, and perform horse shows in which riders control teams of five horses at a gallop. Curly-horned Racka sheep and long-horned Hungarian grey cattle graze, and the stone Nine-Hole Bridge, the longest in Hungary at 167 metres, dates from 1833.

For nature lovers, the park is a major bird area. In October, tens of thousands of common cranes stop here on their migration, and visitors also see spoonbills, great bustards and many others. The park is a day trip from Debrecen or Tokaj, or a stop on a road trip east of Budapest.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Hortobágy

The sights are in and around Hortobágy village.

  1. 01

    Nine-Hole Bridge

    A 167-metre stone bridge with nine arches, the longest in Hungary, built in 1833 on the old trade route.

    Tip Walk across.

  2. 02

    The puszta

    The open grassland with traditional well poles, grazing herds and mirages on hot days.

    Tip Take a horse-cart tour.

  3. 03

    Horse shows

    Demonstrations of csikós riding, including the five-in-hand.

    Tip Check show times.

  4. 04

    Herdsmen's Museum

    A museum on pastoral life.

    Tip Near the bridge.

  5. 05

    Hortobágy fish ponds

    Ponds for birdwatching.

    Tip Bring binoculars.

  6. 06

    Crane migration

    Thousands of cranes stop in October.

    Tip Go at dusk.
