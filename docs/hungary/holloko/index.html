Welcome to Hollókő

Hollókő is a small village in the Cserhát hills of northern Hungary, and one of the best-preserved examples of traditional rural life in Central Europe. It was listed by UNESCO in 1987, the first village in the world to be recognised as a living cultural landscape, and its old part, the Ófalu, consists of about 55 houses and a church, arranged along two streets, still occupied by residents.

The village belongs to the Palóc, an ethnic subgroup of Hungarians with distinctive dialect, costume and customs. The houses are of timber and whitewashed adobe, with tiled roofs, wooden porches and carved gables, and were rebuilt to the same pattern after a fire in 1909. Above the village, a 13th-century castle stands on a hill with views over the countryside.

The best time to visit is Easter, when villagers dress in colourful costumes and hold celebrations, and there is also a weaving, pottery and doll-making tradition. Hollókő is quiet, small and remote, best for a day trip from Budapest or an overnight stay in a guesthouse. It is not a place for nightlife.
