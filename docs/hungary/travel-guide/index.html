How the country divides, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to get around by train and bus, and what two weeks can realistically hold.

Hungary offers Budapest's baths and bridges, wine regions, a great lake, medieval towns and the plains of the puszta at very fair prices. This guide covers how the regions divide, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to travel, when to go, what it costs and three routes that work.

How Hungary divides

Hungary is about 500 km across, and it falls into five zones for a visitor.

Budapest and the Danube. Budapest is the hub, with the Danube Bend a short trip north.

The north. Eger and Tokaj are wine towns, and Hollókő is a preserved village.

The Great Plain. Hortobágy is the puszta, and Szeged is a lively southern city.

The south-west. Pécs is a sunny Mediterranean-feeling city, and Lake Balaton is the summer playground.

The west. Sopron is a medieval town at the Austrian border.

Worth knowing: The practical unit is a loop from Budapest, with one or two regions per trip. Distances are short, and trains are cheap.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If you have one week

    Budapest for four days, then the Danube Bend and Eger.

  • If you love wine

    Tokaj for sweet wines, Eger for Bull's Blood, and the vineyards of Balaton and Sopron.

  • If you love history

    Budapest, Pécs with its Roman and Ottoman layers, and Sopron&#39;s medieval core.

  • If you love nature

    Hortobágy&#39;s plains and Lake Balaton.

  • If you love villages and folk culture

    Hollókő for a UNESCO village and Hortobágy for horsemen.

  • If you want a lively city

    Szeged is a sunny university city, and Budapest is the capital of nightlife.

Getting around

Hungary's transport is centred on Budapest, and it is cheap but sometimes slow.

Trains. InterCity trains reach Eger, Szeged, Pécs and Sopron in two to three hours. Book on the MÁV app.

Buses. Volánbusz covers Balaton, Tokaj and smaller towns.

Budapest transit. Metro, trams and buses share a ticket. Validate before boarding.

Cars. Useful for Tokaj and Hortobágy. Buy an e-vignette for motorways.

Worth knowing: The legal alcohol limit for drivers is zero.

When to go

Hungary has four seasons, and the shoulder months are best.

April to June is spring and early summer, mild and green.

July and August are hot, with a lively Balaton season and Budapest's festivals.

September and October are autumn, with grape harvests, warm days and lower prices.

November to March is cold, with Christmas markets and steaming outdoor baths.

Worth knowing: Many Balaton facilities close from October to April.

Money, costs and safety

Hungary is good value. A restaurant meal costs US$8–20, a mid-range hotel US$70–130, and a long train ride US$10–25.

Cards are widely accepted. Pay in forints, and decline currency conversion.

Crime is low, but pickpockets and overcharging in tourist bars are real. Use licensed taxis or apps.

Check bills for service charges before tipping.

Three routes that work

  1. One week: Budapest and beyond

    Four nights in Budapest, a day in the Danube Bend, and two nights in Eger.

    Tip Buy a Budapest travel card.

  2. Ten days: adding the wine country

    The one-week route, plus Tokaj and Hortobágy.

    Tip Hire a car for a day.

  3. Two weeks: the classic

    The itinerary on the country page: Budapest, Eger, Tokaj, Hortobágy, Szeged, Pécs, Balaton and Sopron.

    Tip Skip Sopron if you prefer a slower pace.

A 77-km-long lake with the Tihany Peninsula, Badacsony's basalt hills and wine, Balatonfüred's promenade and the Festetics Palace at Keszthely, popular for swimming, sailing and wine.

A UNESCO-listed wine region and small town at the meeting of two rivers, with cellars, vineyard villages, sweet Aszú wine and dry Furmint.

Baranya, southern Hungary

A Mediterranean-feeling city with early Christian tombs, the Gazi Kasim Pasha Mosque, a four-towered cathedral, Zsolnay ceramics and nearby Villány red wines.

Don't miss The Mosque-Church on Széchenyi Square, a domed Ottoman mosque topped with a cross.

Hungary's oldest national park and a UNESCO cultural landscape, with the Nine-Hole Bridge, horse shows, traditional herding, birdwatching and crane migration.

A well-preserved medieval town on the Austrian border, with the Fire Tower, Roman and Gothic remains, Baroque streets, Kékfrankos wine and an easy trip to Lake Fertő.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Hungary

Is Budapest enough for a trip to Hungary?

It can be, with three or four days. But a couple of day trips to the Danube Bend or Eger add a lot for little effort.

Do I need a car?

Not for most trips. Trains and buses reach nearly all the places here. A car helps for Tokaj, Hortobágy and Hollókő.

Which thermal bath should I choose?

Széchenyi is the grandest and busiest, Gellért is elegant, and Rudas has an Ottoman dome. Book ahead for peak times.

Is Lake Balaton worth visiting outside summer?

Yes. Spring and September are quiet, and the vineyards, villages and views are lovely. Many facilities close from October to April.
