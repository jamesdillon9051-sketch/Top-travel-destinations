Welcome to Budapest

Budapest was created in 1873 from three towns, Buda, Óbuda and Pest, either side of the Danube, and it remains a city of two halves. Buda, on the west bank, is hilly and old, with the castle, the medieval Matthias Church and the Fisherman's Bastion on Castle Hill. Pest, on the east, is flat and busy, with wide boulevards, the neo-Gothic Parliament, cafés and the nightlife of the Jewish Quarter. Its riverbanks and Buda Castle Quarter are together a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Its golden age came in the late 19th century, when Budapest was the second capital of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, and much of its grandeur dates from then: the Opera House, St. Stephen's Basilica, the Chain Bridge and the Great Market Hall. Underneath, the city sits on more than 100 thermal springs, and bathing in hot pools, from the palatial Széchenyi to the Ottoman-era Rudas, is a central experience.

By night, Budapest turns lively. The ruin bars, set in abandoned buildings in the old Jewish Quarter, started the trend in the early 2000s, and Szimpla Kert is the best known. The food scene has grown too, with modern Hungarian restaurants alongside traditional goulash and chimney cake. Three or four days is ideal, and it is a good base for day trips.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Budapest

Budapest's sights are on both sides of the river, linked by bridges and trams.

  1. 01

    Parliament Building

    A neo-Gothic riverside palace opened in 1904, with 691 rooms, the Holy Crown of Hungary and a grand staircase, visited on guided tours.

    Tip Book tours in advance.

  2. 02

    Fisherman's Bastion and Matthias Church

    A fairy-tale terrace of white turrets with views across the Danube to Pest, beside the Gothic Matthias Church.

    Tip Go at sunrise for no queue.

  3. 03

    Széchenyi Thermal Bath

    A huge neo-baroque bath complex in City Park with outdoor pools, saunas and steam rooms, open year-round.

    Tip Book tickets online.

  4. 04

    Great Market Hall

    A grand 1897 market hall with stalls of paprika, salami and produce, and a food court upstairs serving lángos.

    Tip Go in the morning.

  5. 05

    Chain Bridge

    The first permanent bridge across the Danube in Budapest, opened in 1849, and lit at night.

    Tip Walk it at dusk.

  6. 06

    St. Stephen's Basilica

    The city's largest church, with a dome you can climb for 360-degree views.

    Tip Take the lift.

  7. 07

    Szimpla Kert

    The original ruin bar, in a former stove factory, with mismatched furniture and lively courtyards.

    Tip Visit at opening time.

Travel tips

Budapest travel tips

A few tips help you plan.

  • Book Parliament and Széchenyi in advance.
  • Use Bolt instead of street taxis.
  • Validate tickets.
  • Decline currency conversion.
  • Watch for pickpockets.
  • Check bills for service charges.
  • Go to Fisherman's Bastion early.

FAQ

Budapest: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Budapest?

Three or four.

Which bath is best?

Széchenyi is the most famous, and Gellért the most elegant.

Is Budapest safe?

Yes, with normal precautions.

Is it cheap?

Yes.

What are ruin bars?

Bars in derelict buildings with eclectic decor.

Can I use euros?

Some places accept them, but use forints.
