Welcome to Phuket

Phuket is Thailand's largest island and its best-known beach destination, a genuinely wide-ranging mix of high-rise resort strips, quieter coves and an atmospheric old town that together attract a considerably broader range of visitors than the party-island reputation alone suggests.

The island's beaches range hugely in character, from Patong's dense resorts, bars and nightlife to Kata and Karon's more relaxed family-friendly stretches and Kamala's quieter, upscale feel, giving Phuket a genuine range within a single, easily connected island.

Beyond the beaches, Phuket Old Town's pastel Sino-Portuguese shophouses, built by Chinese tin-mining merchants in the 19th century, offer a genuinely different side of the island, and Phuket's size and infrastructure make it Thailand's busiest launch point for day trips to Phang Nga Bay and the Phi Phi Islands.

Why visit

Why visit Phuket?

Phuket's case is that its size and infrastructure genuinely offer more range — beaches, culture and island access — than any other single spot on Thailand's Andaman coast.

  • The beaches genuinely span every mood

    From Patong's high-energy strip to Kata and Kamala's quieter, more relaxed stretches, all within a short drive of each other.

  • Phuket Old Town is genuinely distinct from the beach resorts

    Pastel Sino-Portuguese architecture and a serious food scene, a world apart from the coastal strips.

  • It's Thailand's easiest launch point for island-hopping

    Phang Nga Bay and the Phi Phi Islands are both straightforward day trips from Phuket's ports.

  • The infrastructure is genuinely the best on this coast

    A wide range of hotels, restaurants and an international airport make Phuket the most convenient Andaman base.

  • It works for a wide range of trip styles

    Genuinely suited to families, couples, nightlife-focused groups and quieter escapes, depending on which beach you choose.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Phuket

Phuket's essential sights, spanning its beaches, old town and surrounding waters.

  1. 01

    Phuket Old Town

    Pastel Sino-Portuguese shophouses built by 19th-century Chinese tin-mining merchants, genuinely one of Thailand's most distinctive architectural districts.

    Tip Visit on a Sunday evening for the Lard Yai Walking Street market, when the old town's streets close to traffic.

  2. 02

    Big Buddha Phuket

    A 45-metre white marble Buddha statue crowning a hill in the island's south, offering genuinely sweeping views across Phuket and the surrounding sea.

    Tip Visit in the late afternoon for the clearest views and cooler temperatures for the walk up to the statue's base.

  3. 03

    Wat Chalong

    Phuket's most important Buddhist temple, its ornate multi-tiered chedi holding relics of the Buddha, genuinely worth a stop for the island's most significant religious site.

    Tip Dress modestly, as with any Thai temple — shoulders and knees covered.

  4. 04

    Phang Nga Bay

    A genuinely dramatic seascape of limestone karst islands rising sheer from the water, including the famous James Bond Island, best explored by longtail boat or sea kayak.

    Tip A sea kayak tour into the bay's hidden lagoons (hongs) offers a genuinely more intimate experience than a large tour boat.

  5. 05

    The Phi Phi Islands

    A genuinely stunning day-trip destination of turquoise water and dramatic cliffs, including Maya Bay, made famous by film and now managed with visitor limits to protect the reef.

    Tip Book an early-departure boat tour to reach Maya Bay before the midday crowds, when visitor numbers are capped and controlled.

  6. 06

    Kata and Karon beaches

    Two of Phuket's most consistently recommended beaches, offering a genuinely good balance of scenery, facilities and a calmer atmosphere than Patong.

    Tip Kata's smaller cove and Karon's longer stretch both work well for swimming outside the rainy-season monsoon months.

Things to do

Best things to do in Phuket

Beyond the beaches, Phuket rewards its old town, food scene and easy access to the water.

  1. Take a longtail boat to a nearby island

    Coral Island and Racha Island both offer genuinely excellent snorkelling and clear water within a short boat ride of Phuket's beaches.

    Tip A half-day trip to Coral Island is a genuinely good option if you don't have time for a full Phi Phi Islands day.

  2. Explore Phuket Old Town's food scene

    A genuinely strong mix of Peranakan (Chinese-Malay) cuisine, street food and coffee shops set within the old town's historic shophouses.

    Tip Try Hokkien mee and moo hong, two genuinely distinctive Phuket-Peranakan dishes rarely found elsewhere in Thailand.

  3. Watch the sunset from Promthep Cape

    Phuket's southernmost point offers a genuinely spectacular sunset viewpoint over the Andaman Sea, one of the island's most popular evening spots.

    Tip Arrive at least 30 minutes ahead of sunset — the viewpoint's car park fills up quickly on clear evenings.

  4. Experience Patong's nightlife

    Bangla Road's bars, clubs and street performances offer a genuinely high-energy nightlife scene, Phuket's best-known after dark.

    Tip Patong's nightlife is genuinely distinct from the island's quieter beach towns — expect a considerably louder, livelier atmosphere.

  5. Visit an elephant sanctuary

    Several genuinely reputable ethical elephant sanctuaries near Phuket offer observation, feeding and bathing without riding or performances.

    Tip Research sanctuaries carefully, as with anywhere in Thailand — look for ones that explicitly prohibit riding.

  6. Go diving or snorkelling around the Similan Islands

    A multi-day boat trip further north to the Similan Islands offers genuinely some of Thailand's best diving, with visibility and marine life considerably beyond what's found close to Phuket itself.

    Tip The Similans are seasonal, typically open from mid-October to mid-May — book well ahead in peak season.

Local food

What to eat in Phuket

Phuket's food scene blends standard Thai cuisine with a genuinely distinct Peranakan (Chinese-Malay) heritage.

  • Hokkien mee (Phuket-style)

    A genuinely distinctive Phuket dish of stir-fried yellow noodles with pork, seafood and vegetables, a Peranakan specialty found almost nowhere else in Thailand.

    Where to try it Phuket Old Town's noodle shops, several with decades of history.

  • Moo hong

    Slow-braised pork belly in a sweet, dark soy sauce, a genuine Phuket-Peranakan comfort dish with real depth of flavour.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout Phuket Old Town.

  • Fresh seafood

    Grilled fish, prawns and shellfish, genuinely excellent around Phuket's coastal towns, typically priced by weight and grilled simply.

    Where to try it Seafood restaurants along Rawai and Chalong beaches, where the day's catch is often on direct display.

  • O aew

    A refreshing Phuket dessert of chilled grass jelly, coconut milk and syrup, a genuinely distinctive local specialty rarely found outside the island.

    Where to try it Street stalls throughout Phuket Old Town, especially around the Sunday Walking Street market.

  • Pad thai

    Available across the island as elsewhere in Thailand, genuinely good from Phuket's street stalls and markets.

    Where to try it Street stalls throughout the old town and near the main beaches.

Where to stay in Phuket

Phuket's beaches each offer a genuinely distinct atmosphere, from high-energy Patong to quieter southern coves.

  • Patong

    The island's busiest, liveliest beach town, with the widest range of accommodation and Bangla Road's nightlife strip.

    Best for: Nightlife and a wide range of hotels and restaurants

  • Kata and Karon

    Two of the island's most consistently recommended beaches, offering good facilities without Patong's intensity.

    Best for: A calmer beach atmosphere for families and couples

  • Phuket Old Town

    A genuinely different base, putting the Sino-Portuguese architecture and food scene within walking distance, a short drive from the beaches.

    Best for: History, food and a break from the beach resorts

Transport

Getting to and around Phuket

Phuket International Airport handles frequent domestic and international flights, making the island Thailand's most accessible beach destination.

Getting there

  • Phuket International Airport (HKT)

    Frequent direct flights from Bangkok and international routes from across Asia.

    A metered taxi, ride-hailing app or pre-booked transfer covers the roughly 45-minute journey to Patong.

  • Bus or minivan from Krabi

    Regular connections link Phuket to Krabi in about 3 hours by road.

    A private transfer avoids the multiple stops a shared minivan often makes along the way.

  • Ferry to Koh Samui or Koh Phi Phi

    Direct ferries connect Phuket to nearby islands, including a route toward Koh Samui via Krabi.

    High-season ferry schedules are more frequent than during the rainy season — check current timetables ahead.

Getting around

Phuket is genuinely large for an island, so most visitors rely on taxis, ride-hailing apps or a rented scooter to get between beaches.

  • Rented scooter

    A popular way to explore the island's beaches and viewpoints independently, though traffic and road conditions require genuine caution.

    A proper helmet and an International Driving Permit are genuinely important — insurance often excludes claims without one.

  • Taxis and ride-hailing apps

    Widely available, though metered taxis are less common than in Bangkok — negotiate or use an app for a fixed fare.

    Ride-hailing apps genuinely avoid the fare disputes that unmetered taxi trips can involve.

  • Songthaews between beaches

    Shared pickup trucks run fixed routes between Phuket Town and the main beaches, a genuinely cheap if less flexible option.

    Services typically stop in the early evening — plan accordingly for a late return.

When to go

Best time to visit Phuket

Phuket's dry season offers the most reliable weather for beaches and island-hopping, while the monsoon months bring rougher seas on this side of the country.

  • Cool, dry season (November–February)

    Best time

    The most comfortable and reliable weather for beaches, boat trips and island-hopping, alongside the year's biggest crowds.

  • Hot season (March–April)

    Good

    Still dry and swimmable, genuinely hot but with thinner crowds than the December–January peak.

  • Rainy season (May–October)

    Shoulder

    The Andaman coast's wetter, rougher-sea season — boat trips can be cancelled or curtailed, though rates drop noticeably.

Worth planning around: Late boat departures during the rainy season from May to October, when Andaman coast seas can turn genuinely rough with little warning.

Travel tips

Phuket travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Phuket trip.

  • Choose your beach carefully — Patong, Kata and Karon offer genuinely different atmospheres, and moving later is inconvenient.
  • Wear a proper helmet if renting a scooter, and check your travel insurance covers it before riding.
  • Book Phi Phi Islands and Phang Nga Bay tours through a reputable operator, and choose an early departure to beat the crowds.
  • Avoid late boat departures during the rainy season from May to October, when seas can turn genuinely rough.
  • Research elephant sanctuaries carefully — choose ones that explicitly prohibit riding.
  • Book accommodation well ahead for the December–January peak, when Phuket's prices and crowds both spike.

FAQ

Phuket: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Phuket?

Four to five. Three covers a beach stay with one island day trip at a rushed pace; five allows both Phang Nga Bay and the Phi Phi Islands without feeling hurried.

Which Phuket beach should I choose?

Patong for nightlife and the widest range of hotels; Kata or Karon for a calmer, more relaxed atmosphere suited to families and couples; Kamala for a quieter, more upscale feel.

Is Phuket or Krabi better for a first trip?

Phuket offers considerably more infrastructure, nightlife and flight options; Krabi and Railay offer more dramatic scenery and a quieter, climbing-focused atmosphere. Many first-time visitors combine both.

Is Phuket safe for tourists?

Yes, generally — the main practical risks are scooter accidents among inexperienced riders and rough seas during the rainy season, both manageable with proper precautions.

What is the best time of year to visit?

November to April offers the most reliable dry-season weather for beaches and island-hopping. May to October brings the Andaman coast's rainy season, with rougher seas and cancelled boat trips more common.
