Welcome to Khao Sok National Park

Khao Sok is one of the world's oldest rainforests, older than the Amazon, its dense jungle canopy sheltering wildlife including elephants, tigers, tapirs and hornbills across a genuinely vast, largely untouched national park in southern Thailand.

The park's most striking feature is Cheow Lan Lake, an artificial reservoir created by a dam in the 1980s that flooded a valley, leaving hundreds of sheer limestone karsts rising directly from the water — scenery that genuinely rivals Guilin or Halong Bay, reached by floating raft houses rather than hotels.

Despite sitting conveniently between the Andaman and Gulf coasts, Khao Sok remains genuinely overlooked by most Thailand itineraries, which is exactly what makes an overnight stay on the lake feel like a real discovery rather than another stop on a well-worn circuit.

Why visit

Why visit Khao Sok National Park?

Khao Sok's case is that it delivers a genuine wilderness experience most Thailand visitors never reach, despite sitting conveniently between two popular coasts.

  • The rainforest is genuinely ancient and biodiverse

    Older than the Amazon, home to elephants, tigers, tapirs, hornbills and gibbons across a vast protected area.

  • Cheow Lan Lake's scenery is genuinely spectacular

    Limestone karsts rising directly from still water rival Thailand's most photographed coastal scenery, in a setting few visitors reach.

  • Raft house stays are genuinely unlike anywhere else in Thailand

    Floating bamboo bungalows on the lake, with no road access, offer a singular overnight experience.

  • It's genuinely underrated relative to its beauty

    Most standard Thailand itineraries skip it entirely, keeping the park considerably quieter than its scenery deserves.

  • It sits conveniently between two popular coasts

    A genuinely easy addition between the Andaman coast around Krabi and Phuket and a Gulf coast island stay.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Khao Sok National Park

Khao Sok's essential sights, spanning the rainforest's trails and Cheow Lan Lake's karst-studded water.

  1. 01

    Cheow Lan Lake

    An artificial reservoir dotted with hundreds of sheer limestone karsts rising directly from the water, reached by a boat journey from the dam and explored by raft house or day tour.

    Tip An overnight raft house stay reveals the lake at dawn and dusk, genuinely the most atmospheric times, unlike a rushed day-trip visit.

  2. 02

    The rainforest canopy walkway and jungle trails

    A network of trails through genuinely ancient rainforest, some leading to waterfalls, others simply deep into the jungle's dense, towering canopy.

    Tip Hire a park-approved guide for longer treks — trails are genuinely easy to lose without local knowledge, and a guide considerably improves wildlife spotting odds.

  3. 03

    Nam Talu Cave

    A genuinely adventurous half-day trek involving wading and swimming through an underground river cave system, home to bats and cave-dwelling wildlife.

    Tip Wear clothes and shoes you don't mind getting thoroughly wet — this is a genuinely full-immersion cave trek, not a dry walking tour.

  4. 04

    Wildlife spotting on a night safari

    Khao Sok's dense rainforest shelters elephants, tigers, tapirs, gibbons and hornbills, with a night safari offering the best odds of spotting nocturnal wildlife.

    Tip Keep expectations realistic — genuine wild sightings of larger mammals are possible but far from guaranteed in dense rainforest.

  5. 05

    Ratchaprapha Dam

    The dam that created Cheow Lan Lake, and the departure point for boat trips onto the water, worth a look in its own right for the surrounding karst scenery visible even from the dam itself.

    Tip Arrive early for the boat transfer to your raft house — the journey across the lake is genuinely part of the experience.

  6. 06

    Bamboo rafting on the Sok River

    A genuinely relaxed way to see the park's jungle-lined river at a slower pace than a hiking trail, floating past limestone cliffs and dense forest.

    Tip Combine with a visit to a local elephant sanctuary along the same stretch of river for a fuller half-day.

Things to do

Best things to do in Khao Sok National Park

Beyond the lake and trails, Khao Sok rewards a genuinely slower, more immersive style of visit than most Thailand stops.

  1. Stay overnight in a raft house on the lake

    Floating bamboo bungalows on Cheow Lan Lake, reachable only by boat, offer a genuinely singular way to experience the park after the day-trip crowds leave.

    Tip Book at least a few days ahead in peak season — raft house capacity on the lake is genuinely limited.

  2. Take a guided jungle trek

    Khao Sok's park-approved guides lead treks ranging from a few hours to full-day routes through the rainforest's trail network.

    Tip A guide's local knowledge genuinely transforms the odds of spotting wildlife compared to walking the trails independently.

  3. Kayak on Cheow Lan Lake

    Paddling among the karsts at your own pace offers a genuinely quieter, more intimate way to experience the lake than a motorboat tour.

    Tip Early morning offers the calmest water and the best light for photographing the karsts' reflections.

  4. Visit an ethical elephant sanctuary near the park

    Several genuinely reputable sanctuaries near Khao Sok offer observation, feeding and bathing without riding or performances.

    Tip As anywhere in Thailand, choose a sanctuary that explicitly prohibits riding and prioritises animal welfare.

  5. Swim at a park waterfall

    Several waterfalls within the park's forest trails offer genuinely refreshing swimming spots after a hot, humid trek.

    Tip Trail conditions to the falls vary seasonally — ask at the visitor centre for the current route before setting out.

Local food

What to eat in Khao Sok National Park

Khao Sok's food centres on simple, fresh southern Thai fare served at raft houses and the park's small village.

  • Raft house set meals

    Most lake raft houses serve genuinely fresh, home-style Thai meals as part of an overnight stay, typically including rice, a curry and stir-fried vegetables.

    Where to try it Included with most raft house accommodation packages on Cheow Lan Lake.

  • Tom yum goong

    A hot and sour prawn soup, genuinely satisfying after a day of trekking or kayaking in the park's heat.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Khao Sok village, near the park's main entrance.

  • Fresh river fish

    Freshwater fish from the Sok River and Cheow Lan Lake, grilled simply, a genuinely reliable local specialty.

    Where to try it Restaurants and raft houses around the lake and river.

  • Grilled bamboo-cooked rice (khao lam)

    Sticky rice and coconut milk cooked inside a length of bamboo over a fire, a genuinely simple, satisfying local specialty.

    Where to try it Roadside stalls in Khao Sok village.

  • Fresh tropical fruit

    Rambutan, mangosteen and durian from local orchards, genuinely excellent when in season.

    Where to try it Roadside stalls and small markets around Khao Sok village.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Khao Sok National Park

Khao Sok offers a genuine choice between jungle-lodge stays near the park entrance and raft houses on Cheow Lan Lake itself.

  • Khao Sok village (near the park entrance)

    A small cluster of guesthouses and jungle lodges along the road into the park, convenient for rainforest trekking.

    Best for: Jungle lodges and easy access to trekking trails

  • Cheow Lan Lake raft houses

    Floating bamboo bungalows reachable only by boat, genuinely the highlight of a Khao Sok visit for most travellers.

    Best for: The park's signature overnight experience

Transport

Getting to and around Khao Sok National Park

Khao Sok sits inland between the Andaman and Gulf coasts, reachable by road from either side.

Getting there

  • From Krabi or Phuket

    A roughly 2–2.5 hour drive from Krabi or Phuket, often arranged as a minivan transfer.

    Several tour operators run direct transfers that combine the journey with a park entry package.

  • From Surat Thani

    The nearest airport with direct flights from Bangkok, about 1.5 hours from the park by road.

    A pre-booked transfer or taxi covers the journey from the airport.

  • From Koh Samui or Koh Phangan

    A combined ferry-and-road transfer from Koh Samui or Koh Phangan, typically arranged through a tour operator.

    Allow a full travel day given the ferry crossing and onward road journey.

Getting around

Khao Sok itself is reached by trekking trails and, for the lake, boat transfers arranged through the park or a tour operator.

  • Boat transfer to Cheow Lan Lake

    The only way to reach lake raft houses, departing from Ratchaprapha Dam and arranged as part of an overnight package.

    Book raft house accommodation ahead, since the transfer is typically included and scheduled around it.

  • Guided trekking

    The main way to explore the rainforest's trail network, with park-approved guides available near the entrance.

    Guides can be arranged directly at the visitor centre or through your accommodation.

  • Walking within Khao Sok village

    The small village near the park entrance is easily walkable, with guesthouses, restaurants and tour operators clustered together.

    Most jungle lodges are within a short walk of the park's visitor centre and trailheads.

When to go

Best time to visit Khao Sok National Park

Khao Sok's rainforest setting means rain is possible year-round, but the dry season offers the most reliable trekking and lake conditions.

  • Dry season (December–April)

    Best time

    The most reliable conditions for trekking and boat trips on Cheow Lan Lake, with genuinely clearer skies.

  • Rainy season (May–November)

    Good

    The rainforest at its lushest and most active for wildlife, though trails turn muddy and some raft house access can be affected by lake levels.

Worth planning around: Extended rainy-season treks without a guide — trails become genuinely more difficult to navigate and leeches are more common after rain.

Itinerary

Two to three days in Khao Sok

The rainforest's trails first, then an overnight raft house stay on Cheow Lan Lake.

  1. Day1

    Arrive and trek the rainforest

    • Morning Arrive at Khao Sok village and settle in.
    • Afternoon A guided jungle trek through the rainforest trails.
    • Evening Dinner in the village and an early night.
  2. Day2

    Cheow Lan Lake

    • Morning Boat transfer to the lake and check into a raft house.
    • Afternoon Kayaking among the limestone karsts.
    • Evening Sunset over the lake from the raft house.
  3. Day3

    The lake at dawn, then depart

    An early start for the lake's best light, then onward travel.

    • Morning Watch dawn break over the karsts before the boat transfer back.
    • Afternoon Depart for Krabi, Phuket or onward.
    • Evening Arrive at your next destination.

Travel tips

Khao Sok National Park travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Khao Sok trip.

  • Book raft house accommodation on Cheow Lan Lake several days ahead in peak season — capacity is genuinely limited.
  • Hire a park-approved guide for jungle treks — trails are genuinely easy to lose and a guide improves wildlife-spotting odds considerably.
  • Pack clothes and shoes you don't mind getting wet if attempting the Nam Talu cave trek.
  • Bring insect repellent and consider leech socks in the rainy season, when trails can genuinely have more of them.
  • Keep wildlife-sighting expectations realistic — dense rainforest makes guaranteed sightings of larger mammals genuinely unlikely.
  • Arrange transfers in advance if combining Khao Sok with the Andaman or Gulf coast — it sits inland between both.

FAQ

Khao Sok National Park: frequently asked questions

Is Khao Sok worth the detour from the beaches?

Yes, genuinely — it offers scenery and a rainforest ecosystem found nowhere else on a standard Thailand beach itinerary, and it sits conveniently between the Andaman and Gulf coasts rather than requiring a major detour.

How many days do you need in Khao Sok?

Two to three. Two covers a jungle trek and one night on Cheow Lan Lake; three allows a more relaxed pace with time for kayaking and wildlife spotting.

Is a raft house stay comfortable?

Reasonably, for most travellers — expect simple bamboo bungalows with basic amenities, genuinely more about the setting and access to the lake than luxury.

Will I actually see wild elephants or tigers?

Possibly, but not reliably — Khao Sok's dense rainforest shelters genuine wildlife, but sightings of larger mammals in the wild are inherently uncertain rather than guaranteed.

What is the best time of year to visit?

December to April offers the most reliable dry conditions for trekking and lake visibility. The rainy season from May to November brings a lusher, greener rainforest but muddier trails.
