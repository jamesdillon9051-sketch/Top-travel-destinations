Welcome to Koh Samui & Koh Phangan

Koh Samui and Koh Phangan sit on Thailand's Gulf coast, reachable directly from Bangkok without the transfer through Phuket or Krabi that the Andaman coast requires, and together offer a genuinely different island rhythm from the country's west side.

Koh Samui, the larger and more developed of the two, pairs palm-lined resort beaches like Chaweng and Lamai with an increasingly serious dining scene and family-friendly infrastructure, while Koh Phangan, a short ferry away, is best known internationally for its monthly Full Moon Party but holds a genuinely quieter side too, from yoga retreats to secluded northern coves.

The islands' position on the Gulf side also means their rainy season runs on different timing from the Andaman coast, keeping Koh Samui comparatively dry even when Phuket and Krabi are seeing their heaviest rains — a genuinely useful fact for planning around Thailand's seasons.

Why visit

Why visit Koh Samui & Koh Phangan?

Koh Samui and Koh Phangan's case is a genuinely different island rhythm from Phuket and Krabi, reachable directly from Bangkok without an Andaman coast transfer.

  • Koh Samui's beaches are genuinely excellent

    Chaweng and Lamai offer long, palm-lined sand with a well-developed range of hotels and restaurants behind them.

  • Koh Phangan delivers a genuinely singular nightlife experience

    The Full Moon Party is unlike anything else in Thailand, drawing visitors specifically for that one night each lunar month.

  • Both islands also have a genuinely quieter side

    Koh Phangan's north coast and Koh Samui's smaller bays offer real escape from the main resort strips.

  • The Gulf coast's rainy season runs differently

    Genuinely useful for planning a trip around the Andaman coast's wetter months from May to October.

  • Ang Thong Marine Park is a genuinely spectacular day trip

    A cluster of limestone islands and hidden lagoons reachable by boat from either island.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Koh Samui & Koh Phangan

The islands' essential sights, spanning Koh Samui's beaches and temples and Koh Phangan's coastline and party scene.

  1. 01

    Chaweng Beach

    Koh Samui's longest and most developed beach, its palm-lined sand backed by a genuinely wide range of resorts, restaurants and bars.

    Tip The northern end of Chaweng is noticeably quieter than the busy central strip, while staying within easy walking distance of it.

  2. 02

    Big Buddha (Wat Phra Yai)

    A 12-metre golden Buddha statue on a small island connected to Koh Samui by causeway, one of the island's most recognisable landmarks.

    Tip Visit for sunset, when the statue and surrounding temple are lit gold by the setting sun.

  3. 03

    Ang Thong National Marine Park

    A genuinely stunning archipelago of forty-two limestone islands, hidden lagoons and emerald water, reachable by day-trip boat from either Koh Samui or Koh Phangan.

    Tip The full-day tour genuinely rewards the extra time over a half-day version — there's considerably more to see across the park's islands.

  4. 04

    Haad Rin Beach, Koh Phangan

    The site of the monthly Full Moon Party, transforming from a quiet beach by day into one of Southeast Asia's most famous all-night parties.

    Tip Check the current Full Moon Party schedule ahead — dates shift slightly around the actual lunar full moon and local calendar adjustments.

  5. 05

    Bottle Beach (Haad Khuad), Koh Phangan

    A genuinely secluded northern-coast beach reachable only by boat or a challenging jungle trek, offering a considerably quieter alternative to Haad Rin.

    Tip Take the boat rather than the jungle trek unless you're an experienced, well-prepared hiker — the trail is genuinely difficult.

  6. 06

    The Hin Ta and Hin Yai rock formations

    Naturally formed rock shapes on Koh Samui's Lamai coast that have earned a cheeky local nickname for their resemblance to certain body parts, a genuinely popular quick photo stop.

    Tip A short, easy stop worth combining with a Lamai Beach visit rather than a dedicated trip on its own.

Things to do

Best things to do in Koh Samui & Koh Phangan

Beyond the beaches, the islands reward boat trips, wellness retreats and, on Koh Phangan, a genuinely famous night out.

  1. Attend the Full Moon Party

    Koh Phangan's monthly beach party at Haad Rin draws thousands for all-night dancing, fire shows and a genuinely unique atmosphere found nowhere else in Thailand.

    Tip Book accommodation well ahead if visiting specifically for the party — rooms on Koh Phangan fill up fast around the date.

  2. Take a boat trip to Ang Thong Marine Park

    A full-day boat tour through the marine park's limestone islands, including a climb to a viewpoint overlooking a hidden emerald lagoon.

    Tip The viewpoint climb is genuinely steep — comfortable shoes make a real difference on this trip.

  3. Try a yoga or wellness retreat on Koh Phangan

    Koh Phangan has developed a genuinely serious wellness and yoga retreat scene, particularly around the island's quieter west and north coasts.

    Tip Book well ahead for a dedicated retreat centre, several of which run structured multi-day or weeklong programmes.

  4. Explore Koh Samui's Fisherman's Village

    Bophut's old fishing village neighbourhood offers a genuinely more relaxed evening scene than Chaweng, with a weekly Friday night walking street market.

    Tip Time a visit for Friday evening, when the walking street market is at its liveliest.

  5. Snorkel or dive around Koh Tao

    A third nearby island, genuinely one of Thailand's best and cheapest places to learn to dive, reachable by ferry from either Koh Samui or Koh Phangan.

    Tip A day trip covers snorkelling well, but a multi-day stay is genuinely worth it if you want to get certified to dive.

  6. Visit a waterfall on Koh Samui

    Na Muang and Hin Lad waterfalls offer a genuinely refreshing inland break from the beaches, both an easy day trip from the main resort areas.

    Tip Visit after rain for the fullest flow, though the falls run year-round even in the dry season.

Local food

What to eat in Koh Samui & Koh Phangan

Both islands offer standard Thai cuisine alongside a genuinely strong seafood tradition and, on Koh Phangan, health-focused café culture.

  • Fresh grilled seafood

    Prawns, squid and whole fish grilled simply and priced by weight, genuinely excellent around both islands' beachfront restaurants.

    Where to try it Beachfront restaurants on Chaweng and Bophut, and around Haad Rin on Koh Phangan.

  • Pad thai

    Available across both islands as elsewhere in Thailand, genuinely good from street stalls and beachfront vendors.

    Where to try it Street stalls throughout Chaweng, Lamai and Haad Rin.

  • Som tam (green papaya salad)

    A genuinely fiery, refreshing salad widely available across both islands, a good counterpoint to a rich seafood meal.

    Where to try it Local Thai restaurants and street stalls on both islands.

  • Healthy café fare on Koh Phangan

    Koh Phangan's wellness and yoga scene has fuelled a genuinely strong café culture of smoothie bowls, fresh juices and vegetarian food.

    Where to try it Cafés around Srithanu and the island's west coast, the heart of its wellness scene.

  • Fresh tropical fruit

    Mango, dragon fruit, rambutan and mangosteen, genuinely at their best sold fresh from roadside stalls and markets on both islands.

    Where to try it Markets and roadside stalls throughout Koh Samui and Koh Phangan.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Koh Samui & Koh Phangan

Koh Samui and Koh Phangan each offer genuinely distinct areas depending on whether beaches, nightlife or wellness matter most.

  • Chaweng, Koh Samui

    Koh Samui's busiest beach strip, with genuinely good infrastructure for a first-time visit to the island.

    Best for: The widest range of hotels, restaurants and nightlife

  • Bophut (Fisherman's Village), Koh Samui

    A genuinely charming old fishing village neighbourhood with boutique hotels and a weekly walking street market.

    Best for: A quieter, more relaxed evening atmosphere

  • Haad Rin, Koh Phangan

    The island's main party hub, genuinely central for Full Moon Party access though considerably livelier than Koh Phangan's other coasts.

    Best for: The Full Moon Party and beach nightlife

  • Srithanu, Koh Phangan

    The island's quieter west-coast wellness hub, a genuinely different side of Koh Phangan from Haad Rin's party scene.

    Best for: Yoga retreats and a wellness-focused atmosphere

Transport

Getting to and around Koh Samui & Koh Phangan

Koh Samui has its own airport with direct flights from Bangkok, while Koh Phangan is reached onward by ferry.

Getting there

  • Koh Samui Airport (USM)

    Direct flights from Bangkok, landing at a genuinely distinctive open-air airport built and run by Bangkok Airways.

    A taxi or pre-booked transfer covers the journey to Chaweng or Bophut.

  • Ferry between Koh Samui and Koh Phangan

    Frequent daily ferries connect the two islands in about 30–45 minutes.

    Several ferry operators run the route — a through-ticket from the mainland often includes this leg.

  • Ferry from the mainland (Surat Thani)

    A more budget-friendly route via an overnight train or bus from Bangkok to Surat Thani, then a ferry onward.

    Combination train-and-ferry tickets are genuinely convenient and widely sold by travel agents in Bangkok.

Getting around

Both islands rely on songthaews, taxis and rented scooters, since neither has extensive public transport.

  • Songthaews (shared trucks)

    Circle Koh Samui's ring road and Koh Phangan's main routes, a genuinely cheap way to get between beaches.

    Confirm the fare before boarding, as prices can vary between drivers.

  • Rented scooter

    A popular way to explore both islands independently, particularly Koh Phangan's quieter, more spread-out coastline.

    An International Driving Permit and a proper helmet are genuinely important — insurance often excludes claims without one.

  • Taxi or ride-hailing app

    Widely available on Koh Samui, less consistently on Koh Phangan, where negotiation is more common.

    Agree the fare before setting off if no meter or app pricing is available.

When to go

Best time to visit Koh Samui & Koh Phangan

Koh Samui and Koh Phangan run on a different rainy-season timing from Thailand's Andaman coast, staying comparatively dry even when Phuket sees heavier rain.

  • Dry season (December–April)

    Best time

    The most reliable weather for beaches and boat trips, alongside the busiest crowds and highest prices.

  • Hot season overlap (March–May)

    Good

    Still largely dry, genuinely hot, with thinner crowds than the December–January peak.

  • Wetter season (October–December)

    Shoulder

    The Gulf coast's own rainy stretch, typically shorter and less severe than the Andaman coast's monsoon, but genuinely worth checking forecasts around.

Worth planning around: Late October to early December for the wettest stretch of the Gulf coast's own shorter monsoon period, which runs opposite to the Andaman coast's timing.

Travel tips

Koh Samui & Koh Phangan travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Koh Samui and Koh Phangan trip.

  • Book accommodation on Koh Phangan well ahead if timing your visit around the Full Moon Party — rooms fill up fast.
  • Check the current Full Moon Party date ahead, since it shifts slightly around the actual lunar cycle and local adjustments.
  • Wear a proper helmet if renting a scooter, and check your travel insurance covers it.
  • Book the full-day rather than half-day Ang Thong Marine Park tour if you have the time — it covers considerably more.
  • Consider the mainland train-and-ferry route via Surat Thani for a genuinely more budget-friendly journey than flying.
  • Pack for both beach days and a night out — the islands' dress codes are casual but the Full Moon Party gets genuinely messy.

FAQ

Koh Samui & Koh Phangan: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need for Koh Samui and Koh Phangan?

Four to five. Three covers Koh Samui's beaches with a day trip; five allows a proper ferry crossing to Koh Phangan and its quieter northern coast without feeling rushed.

Do I have to attend the Full Moon Party?

No — Koh Phangan has a genuinely quieter side too, from yoga retreats around Srithanu to secluded beaches like Bottle Beach, well suited to visitors who'd rather skip the party scene entirely.

Is Koh Samui or Phuket better for a first trip?

Koh Samui offers a quieter, more relaxed resort atmosphere and easier direct flights from Bangkok; Phuket offers more nightlife and a wider range of nearby islands to explore. Both work well as a first Thailand beach trip.

How do I get between Koh Samui and Koh Phangan?

By ferry — frequent daily crossings run between the two islands in about 30–45 minutes, and combination tickets from the mainland often include this leg automatically.

What is the best time of year to visit?

December to April offers the Gulf coast's driest, most reliable weather. Late October to early December brings the region's own shorter rainy stretch, worth checking forecasts around if your dates are flexible.
