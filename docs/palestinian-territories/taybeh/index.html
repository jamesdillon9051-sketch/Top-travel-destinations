Welcome to Taybeh

Taybeh is a village of about 1,500 people on a hilltop 13 km north-east of Ramallah, with a view that on a clear day stretches down to the Jordan Valley and the mountains of Jordan. Its name means 'delicious' or 'good' in Arabic, and it is said to have got its name from the reputation of its food. It is one of the last entirely Christian villages in the West Bank, with Greek Orthodox, Latin (Catholic) and Melkite communities, and it is often identified with the biblical town of Ephraim, where the Gospel of John says that Jesus withdrew before the Passover.

It is best known today for the Taybeh Brewery, founded in 1994 by Nadim Khoury, who returned from the United States with a dream of brewing beer in his home village. It is the first craft brewery in Palestine, and it has grown into a source of local pride. Its Oktoberfest, held each autumn, has drawn thousands of visitors, though it has been scaled down or cancelled in some recent years because of the security situation. There is also a small winery, and olive oil is produced in the village.

The village also has the ruins of a Byzantine church of St George, known as al-Khader, and several working churches, including the Latin church of the Good Shepherd and the Greek Orthodox church. Taybeh has been affected by land disputes and settler attacks in recent years, and its people are welcoming to visitors, who support the local economy.

Why visit

Why visit Taybeh?

Taybeh is small and easy, and it is one of the friendliest places in the West Bank.

  • Palestine's first craft brewery

    A tour and a tasting at the Taybeh Brewery gives you a taste of the village's story and its beer.

  • A Christian village

    Unusually for the West Bank, Taybeh is entirely Christian, and its churches and traditions give it a distinct character.

  • A Byzantine church

    The ruins of the church of St George, with its mosaic floors and carved stone, sit at the edge of the village.

  • Views and quiet

    The village is high and quiet, with olive groves, terraces and sweeping views.

  • An easy half-day from Ramallah

    Taybeh is only 20 minutes from Ramallah, so it fits easily into a day with the Palestinian Museum or the old town.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Taybeh

Taybeh is small, and a visit takes a few hours. Book a brewery tour in advance.

  1. 01

    The Taybeh Brewery

    A small brewery on the edge of the village, producing lager, golden, dark and amber beers, and a non-alcoholic malt drink. Tours cover the story and the process, and end with a tasting.

    Tip Book a tour in advance, and check the current opening hours.

  2. 02

    The Taybeh Winery

    A small family winery in the village, producing local wines from grapes grown in the hills. It offers tastings and sells bottles.

    Tip Ask about tours and tastings.

  3. 03

    The Church of St George (al-Khader)

    The ruins of a 5th-century Byzantine church at the southern edge of the village, with mosaic fragments and carved stone. A later Crusader church stands next to it.

    Tip Ask the villagers for the key or a guide.

  4. 04

    The Latin Church of the Good Shepherd

    A small church built in the 19th century, with a peaceful courtyard and a view over the valley.

    Tip Modest dress is required, and services are on Sundays.

  5. 05

    The Greek Orthodox Church of St George

    The active Greek Orthodox church, with icons and a carved wooden screen.

    Tip Check when it is open.

  6. 06

    The old village and viewpoint

    Narrow lanes with stone houses and terraces lead to a viewpoint that faces east across the Jordan Valley.

    Tip Go at sunset.

Things to do

Best things to do in Taybeh

A slow half-day in Taybeh is best spent talking to people and tasting the local products.

  1. Tour the brewery

    Visit the brewery, learn its story and taste the beers with a snack.

    Tip Book a tour and check hours.

  2. Have lunch in the village

    Small restaurants serve Palestinian dishes, often with local olive oil and beer.

    Tip Ask for a recommendation at the brewery.

  3. Visit the ruins of St George's church

    See the Byzantine mosaics and the view.

    Tip Ask a villager to show you.

  4. Attend the Oktoberfest, if it is held

    The annual festival brings music, food and beer to the village, though it is scaled down or cancelled in difficult years.

    Tip Check whether it is being held before you plan.

  5. Meet the villagers

    People are curious about visitors and often invite you to tea.

    Tip Accept, and bring a small gift.

  6. Combine with Ramallah and Birzeit

    All are close, and can be combined in a day.

    Tip Hire a driver.

Local food

What to eat in Taybeh

Taybeh's food is traditional Palestinian, and its most famous product is beer.

  • Taybeh beer

    Palestine's best-known beer, brewed locally, sold across the West Bank and exported to a few other countries.

    Where to try it The brewery and restaurants in Ramallah.

  • Local wine

    Wine from the village's winery, made in a small way from local grapes.

    Where to try it The winery.

  • Olive oil

    Made from village olives, with a fragrant and slightly bitter flavour.

    Where to try it Village shops.

  • Musakhan

    Roast chicken with sumac and onions on taboon bread.

    Where to try it Village restaurants and homes.

  • Village mezze

    Salads, dips and pickles.

    Where to try it Restaurants.

  • Tea and coffee

    Served as a welcome.

    Where to try it Homes and shops.

Transport

Getting to and around Taybeh

Taybeh is about 13 km north-east of Ramallah, on a road that climbs through the hills.

Getting there

  • Taxi from Ramallah

    About 20 minutes. Taxis can wait or come back for you.

    Agree the fare and return time.

  • Service taxi

    Shared vans run from Ramallah to nearby villages, but not always to Taybeh.

    Ask locals for the latest information.

  • Private driver or tour

    Many tours from Ramallah and Jerusalem include Taybeh with Birzeit or Jericho.

    Check the itinerary.

Getting around

The village is small, and everything is on foot.

  • Walking

    The brewery, the churches and the ruins are within a 15-minute walk of each other.

    Wear sturdy shoes.

  • Taxi

    For trips to Ramallah and other villages.

    Agree the fare.

  • Guided visit

    A local guide explains the village's history and gets you into places.

    Ask at the brewery.

When to go

Best time to visit Taybeh

Taybeh is in the hills at about 850 m. Spring and autumn are the best seasons, and the Oktoberfest, when held, is in October.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Green hills and warm days.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Shoulder

    Warm and dry. Evenings are pleasant.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm and clear, with the olive harvest and the Oktoberfest.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cool and wet. Christmas is celebrated in the village.

Worth planning around: Days of unrest or road closures, and Sundays for some shops.

Travel tips

Taybeh travel tips

Taybeh is small and friendly, and a few tips help.

  • Book a brewery tour in advance. It is a small business, and hours can be limited.
  • Check conditions before you go. The village has faced land disputes and attacks in recent years, and access can change.
  • Go with a driver. Public transport is limited.
  • Buy beer, wine and olive oil in the village. It supports the community directly.
  • Dress modestly for the churches.
  • Bring cash, as small shops do not always take cards.
  • Do not drink and drive. Ramallah is 20 minutes away.

FAQ

Taybeh: frequently asked questions

What is Taybeh known for?

The Taybeh Brewery, founded in 1994 and the first craft brewery in Palestine, and for being one of the last entirely Christian villages in the West Bank.

Can I visit the brewery?

Yes, by appointment. Tours cover the story of the brewery and end with a tasting. Book in advance and check the hours.

How long do you need in Taybeh?

Half a day is enough for the brewery, lunch and a walk to the church ruins.

Is Taybeh safe to visit?

It is a quiet and welcoming village, but it is in the West Bank, and conditions can change. The village has experienced land disputes and attacks in recent years. Check your government's advice and local information.

Is Taybeh the biblical Ephraim?

Many scholars and villagers identify it with Ephraim, the town where, according to the Gospel of John, Jesus withdrew with his disciples before Passover. The identification is not certain.

Can I visit Taybeh from Ramallah?

Yes. It is about 20 minutes by taxi or car, and is often combined with Birzeit or Jericho.
