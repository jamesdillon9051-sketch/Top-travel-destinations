Welcome to Ramallah

Ramallah is about 15 km north of Jerusalem, on a ridge at 850 metres above sea level, and it is the de facto administrative centre of the Palestinian Authority. It was a small Christian village until the 20th century, and grew rapidly after 1948 as refugees arrived and after the Oslo Accords as the PA's offices and foreign missions settled here. Together with its twin city of al-Bireh it now has about 70,000 people.

It is not an old or a beautiful city, but it is a lively one. The centre has traffic, cafés and shops, and around it are museums, art galleries, a busy music and theatre scene, universities, NGOs and a bar and restaurant scene that is unusual in the West Bank. It is the most cosmopolitan Palestinian city and the easiest to visit, with a reputation, sometimes called 'the Ramallah bubble', as more liberal and comfortable than other towns.

For visitors, Ramallah works as a base for the central and northern West Bank, and as a place to meet people and learn about the politics and culture in a relaxed setting. It has also been affected by the wider conflict, with periods of tension and Israeli raids, and its access to Jerusalem is governed by the Qalandiya checkpoint. Check the situation before you go.

Why visit

Why visit Ramallah?

Ramallah is a good place to see Palestinian life beyond the headlines.

  • Excellent museums

    The Palestinian Museum in Birzeit, the Mahmoud Darwish Museum and the Yasser Arafat Museum tell the story of Palestinian culture and politics.

  • A lively arts scene

    Galleries, theatres, film festivals and concert venues make Ramallah the centre of Palestinian contemporary culture.

  • Food and nightlife

    The city has some of the best restaurants, cafés and bars in the West Bank, from traditional Palestinian to international.

  • Easy to reach

    It is only 15 km from Jerusalem, with frequent buses and service taxis, and a good base for Nablus, Taybeh and Jericho.

  • Conversation

    Students, artists, journalists and activists are open to talking about their lives, and it is one of the best places to hear a range of Palestinian views.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Ramallah

Ramallah's sights are spread across the city, and a taxi is the easiest way to move between them.

  1. 01

    Yasser Arafat Museum and Mausoleum

    The museum tells the story of the Palestinian national movement through the life of its most famous leader, in a modern building next to his tomb. Guards stand watch at the mausoleum.

    Tip The museum is a good introduction to Palestinian history, told from a Palestinian perspective.

  2. 02

    The Mahmoud Darwish Museum

    A museum dedicated to the poet Mahmoud Darwish, who is widely regarded as the national poet of Palestine, with his manuscripts, personal belongings and a garden where he is buried.

    Tip Read a few of his poems before you go.

  3. 03

    Al-Manara Square

    The busy central roundabout, with its stone lions and a clock tower, is the heart of the city. Streets around it are lined with cafés, shops and food stalls.

    Tip Go in the evening, when it is at its liveliest.

  4. 04

    The Old Town and Rukab Street

    A small area of old stone houses and a market with pastry shops, including the famous Rukab ice cream, made with mastic and salep.

    Tip Try the ice cream and a coffee in the old town.

  5. 05

    Khalil Sakakini Cultural Centre

    A restored Ottoman-era house that hosts exhibitions, concerts and events, and a place to meet the city's artists and writers.

    Tip Check the programme before you go.

  6. 06

    The Palestinian Museum (Birzeit)

    A striking modern museum in the village of Birzeit, 10 km north of Ramallah, with exhibitions on Palestinian culture and history, and a hillside garden.

    Tip Check current exhibitions and opening hours.

  7. 07

    Birzeit and the Ottoman villages

    The university town of Birzeit and villages such as Jifna and Taybeh have old stone houses, restaurants and a country feel.

    Tip Go with a driver to combine several in a day.

Things to do

Best things to do in Ramallah

Ramallah is a place to spend time in, rather than to tick off, and its pleasures are cultural and social.

  1. Visit the Arafat and Darwish museums

    Two museums that together give you a view of the Palestinian story, political and poetic.

    Tip Allow half a day.

  2. Eat a proper Palestinian dinner

    Try modern Palestinian restaurants that revive old dishes, and traditional places that serve musakhan and maqluba.

    Tip Ask your host or hotel for a recommendation.

  3. Go out in the evening

    Ramallah has bars, cafés and live music venues, with a relaxed, cosmopolitan crowd of students, artists and professionals.

    Tip Ask locals about events and dress code.

  4. Visit galleries and cultural centres

    The Sakakini Centre, Qattan Foundation and Zawyeh Gallery show contemporary Palestinian art.

    Tip Check the programmes online.

  5. Take a walking tour

    Local guides run tours of the city, the refugee camps and the wall, discussing politics and history.

    Tip Book through your hotel.

  6. Use it as a base

    Nablus, Sebastia, Taybeh, Jericho and Birzeit are all within an hour or two.

    Tip Hire a driver, and check the conditions.

Local food

What to eat in Ramallah

Ramallah is the best place in the West Bank to eat, with a mix of traditional and modern Palestinian cooking, and a good café culture.

  • Musakhan and maqluba

    The classic Palestinian dishes, served at both traditional restaurants and modern ones.

    Where to try it Restaurants around Al-Manara and in the old town.

  • Rukab ice cream

    A stretchy, chewy ice cream made with mastic and salep, sold from a shop in the old town.

    Where to try it The old-town Rukab shop.

  • Falafel and hummus

    Hot falafel and creamy hummus, served everywhere.

    Where to try it Stalls and small restaurants.

  • Knafeh

    The hot cheese pastry, sold from trays in the centre.

    Where to try it Sweet shops near Al-Manara.

  • Taybeh beer

    A local craft beer from a village 12 km away, served in bars and restaurants.

    Where to try it Bars and restaurants in the centre.

  • Coffee and tea

    Ramallah's café culture ranges from traditional Arabic coffee to specialty roasters.

    Where to try it Cafés across the city.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Ramallah

Ramallah has the widest choice of hotels and guesthouses in the West Bank, from budget to upscale. Most are in the centre or the nearby neighbourhoods.

  • The centre near Al-Manara

    Hotels and apartments within walking distance of the main square and the cafés.

    Best for: Restaurants and nightlife

  • Al-Masyoun and the northern neighbourhoods

    Modern hotels and guesthouses in quieter areas, a short taxi ride from the centre.

    Best for: Quiet and residential streets

Transport

Getting to and around Ramallah

Ramallah is 15 km north of Jerusalem, and reached through the Qalandiya checkpoint.

Getting there

  • Bus 218 or service taxi from Jerusalem

    Palestinian buses and shared vans run from near Damascus Gate to the Qalandiya checkpoint, from which you take a bus or taxi to the centre. The trip takes 30–60 minutes, depending on the queues.

    Carry your passport, and expect delays at rush hour.

  • Private driver or taxi

    A Palestinian taxi can take you across the checkpoint and to your hotel.

    Agree the price in advance.

  • From other West Bank cities

    Service taxis link Ramallah with Nablus, Bethlehem, Jericho and Hebron.

    Ask locals where to catch them.

Getting around

Taxis are the easiest way to get around Ramallah. The city is hilly and spread out.

  • Taxi

    Cheap and plentiful, with fixed fares for common routes. Use a local app or ask your hotel to call.

    Agree the fare or check that the meter is on.

  • Walking

    The centre and the old town are walkable, though the hills and traffic can be tiring.

    Take care crossing the road.

  • Service taxi

    Shared vans go to other West Bank cities and Birzeit.

    Ask locals.

When to go

Best time to visit Ramallah

Ramallah's altitude gives it a milder climate than the coast or the Jordan Valley. It is pleasant for much of the year.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm, green and clear, with a lively cultural calendar.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Shoulder

    Warm and dry, with cool evenings. Festivals and outdoor events.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm and clear, with the olive harvest in the surrounding villages.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cool, wet and sometimes snowy. Museums and cafés are cosy.

Worth planning around: Days of unrest or clashes, when the checkpoint may close, and Ramadan afternoons if you want a restaurant lunch.

Itinerary

Two days in Ramallah

Museums and the old town on the first day, Birzeit and a long dinner on the second. Assumes a base in the centre.

  1. Day1

    Museums and the city

    History, poetry and the old town.

    • Morning The Arafat Museum and the Mahmoud Darwish Museum.
    • Afternoon Lunch, then the old town and Rukab ice cream.
    • Evening Al-Manara Square and dinner.
  2. Day2

    Birzeit and a long dinner

    A slower day outside the city.

    • Morning The Palestinian Museum and gardens in Birzeit.
    • Afternoon A village lunch, and a visit to a gallery.
    • Evening Live music or a bar.

Travel tips

Ramallah travel tips

Ramallah is easy and relaxed, and a few tips help.

  • Check conditions before you go. Ramallah is generally calm, but access through Qalandiya and the wider situation can change.
  • Take your passport. You will need it at the checkpoint and may be asked for it elsewhere.
  • Do not assume the whole West Bank is like Ramallah. It is more liberal and cosmopolitan than most Palestinian towns.
  • Dress smartly but modestly, particularly if you visit museums and offices.
  • Talk to people. It is one of the best places to hear Palestinian views and to ask questions.
  • Take cash in shekels for taxis and small purchases.
  • Plan for Fridays, when many shops open late and the city is quieter.

FAQ

Ramallah: frequently asked questions

Is Ramallah safe to visit?

Ramallah is generally regarded as one of the safer and easier places in the West Bank, and it is popular with foreign visitors and journalists. Conditions can change quickly, and there have been periods of tension. Check your government's advice.

Why is Ramallah important?

It is the administrative centre of the Palestinian Authority and the cultural and economic hub of the West Bank. It also holds the tomb of Yasser Arafat.

How do I get to Ramallah from Jerusalem?

By bus 218 or a service taxi from near Damascus Gate to the Qalandiya checkpoint, then a bus or taxi into the centre. It takes 30–60 minutes.

How long do you need in Ramallah?

One day covers the museums and old town, and two lets you add Birzeit and an evening out.

What is Ramallah like compared with other cities?

More cosmopolitan, more liberal and much more lively at night than most Palestinian cities, though it is smaller and more modern than Jerusalem. It is sometimes called a bubble.

Is it appropriate to drink alcohol in Ramallah?

Some restaurants and bars serve alcohol, particularly in Christian areas and Ramallah's centre. Elsewhere in the West Bank it is less common, so ask before you order.
