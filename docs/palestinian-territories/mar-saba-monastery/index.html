Welcome to Mar Saba Monastery

Mar Saba, called Deir Mar Saba in Arabic and the Great Lavra of Saint Sabbas in Greek, is a Greek Orthodox monastery about 12 km east of Bethlehem, built into the steep wall of the Kidron Valley in the Judean Desert. It was founded in 483 CE by Saint Sabbas, a monk from Cappadocia who lived in a cave here and gathered a community of hermits around him. It has been occupied ever since, making it one of the oldest continuously inhabited monasteries in the world.

The complex, a maze of chapels, terraces, cells and stairways carved into the cliff, was an important centre of Christian scholarship and hymn-writing. Saint John of Damascus, a theologian and poet of the 8th century, lived and is buried here, and monks such as Cosmas of Maiuma wrote hymns that are still sung in Orthodox churches. In 614, Persian invaders killed many of the monks, and their skulls are kept in a chapel.

The monastery has a rule that dates back to its founder: women are not allowed inside. The monastery remains a working community with a small number of monks. Men can visit at certain times, and women can see the monastery from the Women's Tower, a small building across the gorge. It lies in the West Bank in Area C, and access depends on the security situation and the monastery's own rules.

Why visit

Why visit Mar Saba Monastery?

Mar Saba is one of the most dramatic religious sites in the region, and it feels far away from modern life.

  • A monastery built into a cliff

    The blue and white domes, stone terraces and stairs cling to the side of a gorge, with the desert stretching away behind.

  • Living history

    Monks have lived here for more than 1,500 years, and the daily rhythm of prayer continues.

  • The tombs and relics

    The tomb of St Sabbas, the tomb of St John of Damascus and the chapel of skulls give a sense of the site's long history.

  • A desert landscape

    The Judean Desert here is bare and beautiful, with terraced hills, wadis and small Bedouin camps.

  • A short trip from Bethlehem

    It is only 30 minutes by car, and combines well with Herodium and the Church of the Nativity.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Mar Saba Monastery

Access is limited, and what you see depends on your gender and the monastery's opening hours. Check before you go.

  1. 01

    The monastery complex

    Chapels, cells, courtyards and stairways in golden stone, with a church decorated in icons and frescoes, and the tomb of St Sabbas. Male visitors can see the church and parts of the site, usually in a guided visit.

    Tip Dress modestly, and do not take photographs inside. Opening hours are limited and change.

  2. 02

    The Tomb of St John of Damascus

    A small chapel with the tomb of the 8th-century Syrian theologian and hymn writer, who is revered in the Orthodox and Catholic churches.

    Tip It is inside the monastery, so it is open to men only.

  3. 03

    The Chapel of Skulls

    A chapel that holds the skulls of monks killed by Persian invaders in 614 CE, kept as relics of the martyrs.

    Tip Visitors are usually taken to see it.

  4. 04

    The Women's Tower

    A small stone tower on the opposite side of the gorge, where women who could not enter the monastery historically waited and prayed. It offers the best view of the whole complex.

    Tip Go at sunset for the best photographs.

  5. 05

    The Kidron Valley viewpoint

    A viewing platform on the road above the monastery, with a panoramic view of the gorge, the cliffs and the domes.

    Tip It is free and open to all.

  6. 06

    The date palm of St Sabbas

    A tree in the monastery courtyard, said to have been planted by the saint himself.

    Tip Ask a monk about the story.

Things to do

Best things to do in Mar Saba Monastery

A visit to Mar Saba is short, and it is best enjoyed as part of a longer day.

  1. Visit the monastery (men only)

    Male visitors can join a short guided tour of the church, tomb and chapels. It is a quiet and moving visit.

    Tip Confirm the opening hours and any current restrictions before you go.

  2. Watch the monastery from the Women's Tower

    The view across the gorge is the best in the area, and it is open to everyone.

    Tip Bring a zoom lens or binoculars.

  3. Drive the Kidron Valley road

    The road descends through a bare, dramatic landscape, past Bedouin camps and eroded hills.

    Tip Go in the late afternoon.

  4. Combine with Herodium and Bethlehem

    The three fit into a single day with a driver, and give a picture of the area's history.

    Tip Start with the monastery, when it is open.

  5. Visit a Bedouin camp

    Several Bedouin families in the area offer tea, bread and a chance to meet.

    Tip Ask your driver to arrange it.

  6. Hike in the desert

    Trails lead through wadis and terraces around the monastery, with a guide.

    Tip Go in winter or spring, and take plenty of water.

Local food

What to eat in Mar Saba Monastery

There is no food at the monastery, and it is best to bring your own or eat in Bethlehem or with a Bedouin family.

  • Picnic supplies

    Bring bread, hummus, olives and fruit from Bethlehem, and eat at the viewpoint.

    Where to try it Shops in Bethlehem.

  • Bedouin tea and bread

    Hot, sweet tea and flat bread offered by Bedouin families in the desert.

    Where to try it Bedouin camps near the monastery.

  • Falafel and hummus

    The reliable staple in Bethlehem and Beit Sahour.

    Where to try it Stalls near Manger Square.

  • Fresh dates

    Sold by roadside stalls in season.

    Where to try it Roadside stands.

  • Local olives and olive oil

    Good for a picnic.

    Where to try it Shops in Bethlehem.

  • Arabic coffee

    Served at the monastery's gate and in the camps.

    Where to try it Ask your host.

Transport

Getting to and around Mar Saba Monastery

Mar Saba is about 12 km east of Bethlehem, on a road that runs through the desert. A taxi or driver is essential.

Getting there

  • Taxi from Bethlehem

    About 30 minutes each way. Drivers can wait for you and take you to the viewpoints.

    Agree the fare and waiting time.

  • Private driver or tour

    Many tours from Bethlehem and Jerusalem include Mar Saba, often with Herodium.

    Check the itinerary.

  • Hire car

    Possible, but check the terms of your rental agreement and current conditions.

    Israeli rental cars may not be insured for all roads.

Getting around

There is little to do on foot except at the monastery and the viewpoints.

  • Taxi or car

    The only practical way to move between the monastery, the viewpoint and Bethlehem.

    Ask the driver to stop at the viewpoints.

  • Walking

    A short walk from the car park to the gate and the Women's Tower.

    There are steep steps.

  • Guided hike

    Guides lead walks in the desert around the monastery.

    Book in advance.

When to go

Best time to visit Mar Saba Monastery

The Judean Desert is hot in summer and mild in winter. Spring and autumn are the best seasons for walking and photography.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm, clear and green after winter rain.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Think twice

    Very hot and dry. Visit at dawn or dusk.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm and clear, with good light.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Best time

    Mild and quiet, but rain can cause flash floods in the gorge.

Worth planning around: Midday in summer and rainy days, when flash floods can affect the road.

Travel tips

Mar Saba Monastery travel tips

Mar Saba has its own rules, and a few tips help.

  • Women cannot enter the monastery. They can see it from the Women's Tower and the viewpoint.
  • Dress modestly, with arms and legs covered, and do not photograph inside.
  • Check the opening hours before you go. They are limited, and the monastery may be closed on some days.
  • Take a driver. There is no public transport, and the roads are not always marked.
  • Take water and sun protection. The desert is hot and exposed.
  • Check the conditions. The area is in Area C, and access may be affected by closures or military zones.
  • Respect the monks. It is a place of prayer, and silence is expected.

FAQ

Mar Saba Monastery: frequently asked questions

Why can't women enter Mar Saba?

A rule dating from the monastery's founder, St Sabbas, in the 5th century, keeps the community of monks strictly separate from women. Women can see the monastery from the Women's Tower and the viewpoint.

Who lives at Mar Saba?

A small community of Greek Orthodox monks, who follow a strict monastic life. The monastery has been continuously inhabited since 483 CE.

How do I get to Mar Saba?

By taxi or private driver from Bethlehem, about 30 minutes each way. There is no public transport.

Can I take photographs?

Photography is not permitted inside the monastery, but you can photograph the complex from the Women's Tower and the viewpoints.

How long do you need at Mar Saba?

An hour for the monastery and viewpoints, or half a day with the drive and a stop at Herodium or a Bedouin camp.

Is Mar Saba safe to visit?

It is generally regarded as safe in normal times, but it is in the Judean Desert in Area C, and conditions can change. Check your government's advice and local information.
