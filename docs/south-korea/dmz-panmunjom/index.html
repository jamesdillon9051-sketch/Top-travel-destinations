Welcome to the DMZ

The Korean Demilitarized Zone is a strip of land about 250 km long and 4 km wide that cuts across the peninsula near the 38th parallel. It was set up by the armistice agreement of 1953, which halted the fighting of the Korean War without a peace treaty, and it remains a heavily guarded frontier, lined with fences, minefields and guard posts on both sides. Ironically, with few people allowed inside, it has also become a refuge for wildlife, including cranes and other rare birds.

For visitors, the DMZ is a strange, moving and sometimes tense place. Tours from Seoul usually visit Imjingak, a park with memorials and a bridge where prisoners were exchanged, one of the tunnels dug by North Korea under the border, and Dora Observatory, where binoculars look out over the North. Some tours reach the Joint Security Area at Panmunjom, where blue huts straddle the border and soldiers face each other, but access there is controlled and depends on the political situation, and is sometimes suspended.

You cannot visit the DMZ independently. Tours are run by the government-approved operators and the USO, they require a passport, and they have dress codes and rules about photos. Book ahead, follow instructions, and treat it with respect, since it is a place where a war remains unresolved and families are still separated.

Why visit

Why visit the DMZ?

The DMZ is a memorable, thought-provoking day that puts the Korean story in context.

  • A unique border

    One of the world's most militarised frontiers.

  • Cold War history

    Tunnels, memorials and observation posts.

  • Views of North Korea

    From Dora Observatory.

  • Wildlife

    A refuge for cranes and other species.

  • Close to Seoul

    About an hour north.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the DMZ

Tour routes vary, but most include some of these places.

  1. 01

    Imjingak

    A park near the border with memorials, a Peace Bell and the Freedom Bridge, where prisoners of war crossed in 1953. Ribbons and messages from separated families hang from the fences.

    Tip Free to visit without a tour.

  2. 02

    Dora Observatory

    An observation post where you can use binoculars to see the North's propaganda village and land beyond the border.

    Tip Photography is limited to a line.

  3. 03

    The Third Infiltration Tunnel

    A tunnel discovered in 1978, dug by North Korea under the border, which you can walk down. It is low and steep, and helmets are required.

    Tip Bring no large bags, and expect a stooping walk.

  4. 04

    Joint Security Area (Panmunjom)

    The blue conference huts on the border line, where soldiers of both sides stand face to face. Visits are strictly controlled and sometimes suspended.

    Tip Check availability when you book.

  5. 05

    Dorasan Station

    A train station built for a hoped-for rail link to the North, and never used for regular passenger service.

    Tip Often on tours.

  6. 06

    DMZ Theater and Exhibition Hall

    A museum about the DMZ's history and nature.

    Tip Short.

Things to do

Best things to do in the DMZ

The DMZ is a guided experience.

  1. Join a DMZ tour

    Half-day or full-day tours run from Seoul.

    Tip Book weeks ahead.

  2. Walk down a tunnel

    Go 73 metres below ground.

    Tip Not for claustrophobes.

  3. Look through binoculars

    See the North from the observatory.

    Tip Bring coins if required.

  4. Visit Imjingak

    Read the ribbons and memorials.

    Tip A quiet moment.

  5. Try the Peace Trail

    Some hiking routes reopen periodically.

    Tip Check the rules.

  6. Watch for cranes

    Migratory birds in winter.

    Tip Go in winter.

When to go

Best time to visit the DMZ

The DMZ is best visited in clear weather, since haze hides the North.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Clear and mild.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Think twice

    Hot, humid and hazy.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Clear skies.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Good

    Cold, with cranes.

Worth planning around: Rainy or foggy days, and any time when tours are suspended.

Travel tips

The DMZ travel tips

A few tips help you visit safely.

  • Book a tour weeks ahead.
  • Take your passport.
  • Follow the dress code.
  • Do not photograph restricted areas.
  • Check whether the JSA is open.
  • Be respectful.
  • Expect changes if tensions rise.

FAQ

The DMZ: frequently asked questions

Is the DMZ safe to visit?

Guided tours are run regularly and are generally safe. Check advice if tensions rise.

Can I go alone?

Not to most sites. Imjingak is open to independent visitors.

Do I need my passport?

Yes.

Can I see North Korea?

Yes, from observatories on a clear day.

Can I visit the JSA?

Sometimes. Access is restricted and can be suspended.

How long is a tour?

Half a day to a full day.
