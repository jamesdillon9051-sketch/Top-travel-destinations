Welcome to Seoraksan

Seoraksan is the crown of the Taebaek range in Gangwon Province, and South Korea's most beloved mountain park. Its highest peak, Daecheongbong, reaches 1,708 metres, the third highest in the country, and its landscape is one of sharp granite ridges, deep valleys, waterfalls and old pines. It is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, and it is the country's most reliable place to see autumn foliage, which usually peaks in the second half of October.

The park is easy to enjoy at any level. Short walks lead from the entrance to Sinheungsa Temple and a giant bronze Buddha, cable cars go up to the ruined fortress of Gwongeumseong for wide views, and longer trails climb to Ulsanbawi, a rock face reached by steep stairs, and Biryong Falls. Experienced hikers can cross to Daecheongbong on a long day, often with an overnight stay in a mountain shelter.

The gateway is Sokcho, a small port town on the coast, where fishing boats unload squid, and where you can eat sundae (blood sausage) and fresh raw fish at Sokcho Tourist Fish Market. It is about two and a half hours from Seoul by express bus, and the sea and mountains are so close that you can watch a sunrise on the beach and hike in the afternoon.

Why visit

Why visit Seoraksan?

Seoraksan gives Korea's grandest mountain scenery, with the sea next door.

  • Dramatic granite peaks

    Sharp ridges and rock spires.

  • Autumn foliage

    The most famous colour in the country.

  • Easy and hard trails

    From gentle walks to a full-day climb.

  • Temples and hermitages

    Sinheungsa and cliff-side sites.

  • Sokcho's seafood

    A lively fish market on the coast.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Seoraksan

Most visitors use Seoraksan's main entrance near Sokcho.

  1. 01

    Gwongeumseong and the cable car

    A cable car rises to a mountain fortress with sweeping views over the peaks, followed by a short climb.

    Tip Book early in October, since queues can be long.

  2. 02

    Ulsanbawi Rock

    A huge rock formation reached by a hike of about 3.8 km and 800 steps, with big views.

    Tip Start early, and expect a steep climb.

  3. 03

    Biryong Falls

    A waterfall reached by an easy trail through a forest.

    Tip A good short hike.

  4. 04

    Sinheungsa Temple

    A temple founded in the 7th century at the park's entrance, with a giant bronze Buddha.

    Tip Visit before hiking.

  5. 05

    Sokcho Tourist Fish Market

    A market of seafood stalls and restaurants, with fresh squid and crab.

    Tip Go in the evening.

  6. 06

    Abai Village

    A village of North Korean refugees on a small strip of land, reached by a hand-pulled ferry, known for its squid sundae.

    Tip Take the small ferry.

Things to do

Best things to do in Seoraksan

Seoraksan is for walking, and Sokcho is for eating.

  1. Hike Ulsanbawi

    Climb the stairs to a big view.

    Tip Go on a clear morning.

  2. Ride the cable car

    A short ride to the fortress.

    Tip Check the schedule.

  3. Climb Daecheongbong

    A full-day route to the summit.

    Tip Start before dawn.

  4. Soak in a hot spring

    Spa waters near the park.

    Tip Go after a hike.

  5. Watch a sunrise at Sokcho Beach

    See the sea change colour.

    Tip Check the time.

  6. Try a templestay

    Overnight at a local temple.

    Tip Book ahead.

Local food

What to eat in Seoraksan

The region is known for fresh seafood and simple mountain food.

  • Squid sundae (ojingeo sundae)

    Squid stuffed with rice and vegetables.

    Where to try it Abai Village.

  • Dakgangjeong

    Sweet, crispy chicken.

    Where to try it Sokcho market.

  • Raw fish and crab

    Snow crab and sashimi.

    Where to try it Sokcho fish market.

  • Sundubu (soft tofu) stew

    A local tofu dish.

    Where to try it Chodang village.

  • Mountain vegetable meals

    Herbs and roots on a table of small dishes.

    Where to try it Restaurants near the park.

Transport

Getting to and around Seoraksan

Sokcho is reached by bus from Seoul.

Getting there

  • Express bus from Seoul

    About two and a half hours from Dong Seoul Terminal.

    Book early in autumn.

  • Train and bus

    KTX to Gangneung, then a bus north.

    Longer.

  • Flight to Yangyang

    Limited flights.

    Check.

When to go

Best time to visit Seoraksan

Seoraksan is cold in winter and pleasant in spring and autumn.

  • Spring (April–May)

    Good

    Azaleas and fresh green.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Good

    Cool on the mountain, with rain.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Peak foliage in mid to late October.

  • Winter (December–March)

    Quiet

    Snow and ice, with limited trails.

Worth planning around: Weekends in peak foliage, and winter without equipment.

Itinerary

Two days at Seoraksan

A mountain day and a coast day.

  1. Day1

    The mountain

    • Afternoon Gwongeumseong cable car.
    • Evening Seafood in Sokcho.
  2. Day2

Travel tips

Seoraksan travel tips

A few tips help you hike Seoraksan.

  • Go on a weekday in October.
  • Start hiking early.
  • Wear hiking shoes and layers.
  • Check trail closures.
  • Book buses and rooms early in autumn.
  • Bring water and snacks.
  • Respect wildlife rules.

FAQ

Seoraksan: frequently asked questions

When is Seoraksan at its best?

The second half of October, for autumn colour.

How do I get there from Seoul?

An express bus to Sokcho, then a local bus to the park.

Do I need to be fit?

Not for the short walks. Ulsanbawi and Daecheongbong are strenuous.

Is it worth going in winter?

Yes for snow scenery, with care.

How long do I need?

Two days.

Can I combine it with Gangneung?

Yes, they are about an hour apart.
