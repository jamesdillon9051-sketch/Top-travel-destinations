Welcome to Jeonju

Jeonju is a city of about 650,000 in the fertile south-west, and it is widely regarded as the culinary heart of South Korea. UNESCO named it a Creative City of Gastronomy in 2012, and locals will tell you that a meal in Jeonju includes far more side dishes than anywhere else. It is the home of bibimbap, the rice bowl mixed with vegetables and chilli paste, and of a regional banquet tradition in which a table fills with small dishes as soon as you order drinks.

The old heart of the city is Jeonju Hanok Village, a district of about 700 traditional houses with curved tile roofs, alleys and courtyard cafés. It grew around the Gyeonggijeon Shrine, which holds a portrait of the founder of the Joseon dynasty, who was born in Jeonju's family line, and the area is the country's best place to try on hanbok and stroll through real streets rather than a museum set.

Jeonju is compact and easy: a day or two lets you eat, walk the village, browse the Nambu Market and taste the local makgeolli. It is about two hours from Seoul by KTX, and it fits between Seoul and the south-east, making it a natural stop on a longer trip.

Why visit

Why visit Jeonju?

Jeonju offers food and tradition in one compact, walkable place.

  • The home of bibimbap

    The most famous bowl in Korea.

  • A large hanok village

    About 700 traditional houses.

  • A UNESCO gastronomy city

    Banquets, market food and makgeolli.

  • Traditional crafts

    Paper, calligraphy and rice wine.

  • An easy stop

    Two hours from Seoul by KTX.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Jeonju

Everything worth seeing is within a short walk of the hanok village.

  1. 01

    Jeonju Hanok Village

    A district of about 700 hanok with cafés, workshops, guesthouses and hanbok rentals, and the main street for snacks.

    Tip Stay overnight, when the crowds go.

  2. 02

    Gyeonggijeon Shrine

    A shrine that houses a portrait of King Taejo, the founder of the Joseon dynasty, in a quiet compound.

    Tip Visit in the morning.

  3. 03

    Jeondong Cathedral

    A Romanesque-Byzantine church from the early 1900s, in red brick, beside the hanok village.

    Tip Visit before it closes.

  4. 04

    Nambu Market

    A traditional market with food stalls and a night market on weekends.

    Tip Go in the evening.

  5. 05

    Omokdae

    A pavilion on a hill with views across the hanok village's rooftops.

    Tip Go at sunset.

  6. 06

    Korean Traditional Wine Museum

    A museum where you can learn about and taste traditional wines.

    Tip Book a tasting.

Things to do

Best things to do in Jeonju

Jeonju is about eating and strolling.

  1. Eat Jeonju bibimbap

    Try the classic bowl with raw beef and bean sprouts.

    Tip Go at lunch.

  2. Wear a hanbok

    Rent traditional dress and stroll the village.

    Tip Rentals are cheap.

  3. Drink makgeolli

    Visit a makgeolli alley for a kettle of rice wine with a table of dishes.

    Tip Go in the evening.

  4. Try a hanok stay

    Sleep on a heated floor in a traditional house.

    Tip Book ahead.

  5. Make paper or craft

    Try hanji paper workshops.

    Tip Book a class.

  6. Snack along the street

    Choco pies, grilled skewers and egg bread.

    Tip Bring cash.

Local food

What to eat in Jeonju

Jeonju's food is a national point of pride, and a meal is generous.

  • Jeonju bibimbap

    Rice cooked in beef broth with about two dozen toppings, including bean sprouts and raw beef, mixed with chilli paste.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants in the village.

  • Kongnamul gukbap

    Bean sprout soup with rice, a cheap breakfast and hangover cure.

    Where to try it Local restaurants.

  • Makgeolli alley meals

    A kettle of rice wine that comes with a table of dishes.

    Where to try it Samcheon-dong Makgeolli Street.

  • Hanjeongsik

    A banquet table of many dishes.

    Where to try it Restaurants in the village.

  • Choco pie and street snacks

    Sweet and savoury treats.

    Where to try it Hanok village street.

Transport

Getting to and around Jeonju

Jeonju is reached by KTX or bus from Seoul.

Getting there

  • KTX from Seoul (Yongsan)

    About two hours, by KTX to Jeonju station.

    Book in advance.

  • Express bus

    About three hours from Seoul Express Bus Terminal.

    Frequent.

  • From Gyeongju or Busan

    Via a bus or a change of train.

    Plan a half day.

Getting around

The hanok village is walkable.

  • On foot

    The village and market are close together.

    Wear comfortable shoes.

  • Bicycle

    Rentals in the village.

    Good for the flat city.

  • Taxi and bus

    For the station.

    Cheap.

When to go

Best time to visit Jeonju

Jeonju has four seasons, with hot summers and cold winters.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Mild, with cherry blossom.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Think twice

    Hot and humid.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Clear, with gold leaves.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cold, but quiet.

Worth planning around: August heat.

Travel tips

Jeonju travel tips

A few tips help you make the most of Jeonju.

  • Come hungry.
  • Stay overnight in a hanok.
  • Carry cash for street stalls.
  • Go early to the village.
  • Try the makgeolli alley.
  • Book hanok stays early.
  • Wear comfortable shoes.
