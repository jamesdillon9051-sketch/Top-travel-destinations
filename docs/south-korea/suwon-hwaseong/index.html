Welcome to Suwon

Hwaseong Fortress was built in the 1790s by King Jeongjo of the Joseon dynasty, who wanted a new city to honour his father and to strengthen the royal power. It took about two years to build, and was designed by scholars who studied Chinese and Western military engineering, with stone and brick walls, four main gates, watchtowers, bastions and a pavilion for signalling with fire. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and much of it has been restored using detailed records kept at the time.

The wall runs for about 5.7 km around the old part of Suwon, and it can be walked in two or three hours. The route passes gates such as Paldalmun in the south and Janganmun in the north, and offers views across the modern city. Inside the wall, Haenggung is a temporary palace where the king stayed on his visits, and there are archery ranges and a martial-arts performance.

Suwon lies about 30 km south of Seoul, so it is one of the easiest day trips from the capital, by subway or train in under an hour. It is also famous for its fried chicken and a lively market around the walls, and a day is enough for the wall, the palace and lunch.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Suwon

The fortress is a loop, so choose your start.

  1. 01

    The fortress wall

    A 5.7 km wall of stone and brick with battlements, walkable in whole or in part, with views over the old and new city.

    Tip Walk clockwise from Paldalmun.

  2. 02

    Haenggung Palace

    A palace where King Jeongjo stayed while visiting his father's tomb, restored after the 1990s.

    Tip Watch the martial arts performance.

  3. 03

    Paldalmun Gate

    The grand south gate of the fortress.

    Tip A good starting point.

  4. 04

    Hwaseomun and Janganmun

    The west and north gates, with impressive walls.

    Tip Photograph at golden hour.

  5. 05

    Hwaseong Haenggung Archery

    Try traditional archery.

    Tip Short lesson.

  6. 06

    Suwon Hwaseong Trolley

    A small train that tours the walls.

    Tip Good for tired legs.
