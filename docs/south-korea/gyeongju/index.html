Welcome to Gyeongju

Gyeongju was the capital of the Silla kingdom, which unified most of the Korean peninsula in the 7th century and ruled for nearly a thousand years, until 935. At its height the city was one of the largest in the world, and it is now a small, low-rise city of about 250,000 people whose parks, hills and lanes still hold much of that heritage. It is often called a museum without walls.

The sights are outstanding. Bulguksa, a temple founded in the 8th century, and Seokguram Grotto, a granite cave holding a serene seated Buddha, are both UNESCO World Heritage sites. In the city centre, grassy burial mounds rise like green hills at Daereungwon, Cheomseongdae is a stone observatory from the 7th century, and Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond are lit up at night. Gyeongju's historic areas together are also on the World Heritage list.

Gyeongju is easy to see at a gentle pace. Distances are short, cycling between sights is a pleasure, and the Hwangnidan-gil district is full of cafés in restored hanok. The city is about two hours from Seoul by KTX and 30 minutes from Busan, and two days is a good visit, ideally with a night in town to see the lit-up ponds.

Why visit

Why visit Gyeongju?

Gyeongju is Korea's best window into its ancient past, and a relaxing place to be.

  • A great temple and a grotto

    Bulguksa and Seokguram are masterpieces of Buddhist art.

  • Royal tombs

    Grassy mounds in the middle of the city.

  • Silla heritage

    An observatory, palace ponds and museums.

  • Slow and walkable

    A low-rise city that suits cycling.

  • Beautiful at night

    Lit-up ponds and pavilions.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Gyeongju

Most sights are in or near the city centre, with Bulguksa and Seokguram on the hills to the east.

  1. 01

    Bulguksa Temple

    A temple founded in the 8th century, with stone bridges and stairways, wooden halls painted in bright colours, and two famed stone pagodas.

    Tip Go early or late to avoid tour groups.

  2. 02

    Seokguram Grotto

    A granite grotto on Mount Toham holding a large seated Buddha facing the sunrise over the sea. Photography inside is not allowed.

    Tip Take the bus or a taxi from Bulguksa.

  3. 03

    Cheomseongdae Observatory

    A stone tower from the 7th century, often described as the oldest surviving observatory in East Asia, in a park of flowers.

    Tip Visit in spring for rapeseed and cosmos blooms.

  4. 04

    Daereungwon Tomb Complex

    A park of large grassy burial mounds of Silla kings and nobles, including Cheonmachong, whose tomb can be entered and holds replicas of finds.

    Tip Walk it at sunset.

  5. 05

    Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond

    The site of a Silla royal pleasure garden, with reconstructed pavilions around a pond, beautiful when lit up after dark.

    Tip Come at dusk for the lights.

  6. 06

    Gyeongju National Museum

    A museum with Silla gold crowns, bells and treasures excavated from the tombs.

    Tip Allow two hours.

Things to do

Best things to do in Gyeongju

Gyeongju is a place to wander and cycle.

  1. Cycle among the tombs

    Rent a bicycle and ride between the parks and sights.

    Tip Electric bikes help on the hills.

  2. Stay in a hanok

    Sleep in a traditional house in the historic district.

    Tip Book in advance.

  3. Explore Hwangnidan-gil

    Cafés, shops and restaurants in old houses.

    Tip Go in the evening.

  4. Take a templestay

    Spend a night in a temple for tea and meditation.

    Tip Book through the Templestay site.

  5. Visit Yangdong Village

    A UNESCO-listed clan village of Joseon-era houses.

    Tip Combine with a bus tour.

  6. Watch the sunrise at Seokguram

    Arrive early for the light.

    Tip Check the bus times.

Local food

What to eat in Gyeongju

Gyeongju's food is simple, with local specialities like bread and rice dishes.

  • Gyeongju bread (Gyeongju-bbang)

    A small pastry filled with sweet red bean paste, the city's best-known snack.

    Where to try it Bakeries near Daereungwon.

  • Chalboribbang

    A soft barley bread with red bean filling.

    Where to try it Local bakeries.

  • Ssambap

    Rice with vegetables and dishes, wrapped in leaves.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants in Hwangnam.

  • Hanjeongsik

    A traditional multi-dish table meal.

    Where to try it Restaurants in the old town.

  • Gyo-dong tea and beopju

    Traditional tea and a local rice wine.

    Where to try it Gyochon Village.

Transport

Getting to and around Gyeongju

Gyeongju has a KTX station outside the city.

Getting there

  • KTX from Seoul

    About two hours to Singyeongju station, then a bus or taxi of about 20 minutes to the centre.

    Book in advance.

  • Bus from Busan

    About an hour by intercity bus, or about 30 minutes by KTX to Singyeongju.

    Frequent.

  • Intercity bus

    Buses from Seoul take around four hours.

    Cheaper than the KTX.

Getting around

The centre is flat, so bikes and buses work well.

  • Bicycle

    Rent one near the station or Hwangnidan-gil.

    Ride in the daytime.

  • Local buses

    Cover Bulguksa and the main sights.

    Use Naver Map.

  • Taxis

    Cheap for Seokguram.

    Agree the fare.

When to go

Best time to visit Gyeongju

Gyeongju has mild springs and autumns, hot summers and cold winters.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Cherry blossom around Bomun Lake in early April.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Think twice

    Hot and humid.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Clear weather and golden leaves.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cold and quiet.

Worth planning around: The peak of summer and long holiday weekends.

Travel tips

Gyeongju travel tips

A few tips help you see Gyeongju well.

  • Stay overnight to see the sights lit up.
  • Rent a bicycle for the central sights.
  • Go to Bulguksa early.
  • Check the bus times to Seokguram.
  • Combine a combination ticket for the central sights.
  • Wear comfortable shoes for the hills.
  • Try the local bread as a snack.

FAQ

Gyeongju: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Gyeongju?

Two days is ideal. A long day trip from Busan is possible.

Is Gyeongju worth visiting?

Yes. It is one of Korea's best historic places, with UNESCO sites.

How do I get there from Seoul?

KTX to Singyeongju in about two hours, then a bus or taxi.

Can I take photos in Seokguram?

Not inside the grotto, but you can outside.

Do I need a car?

No. Bikes, buses and taxis are enough.

When are the lights on?

Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond are lit each evening.
