Welcome to Busan

Busan is South Korea's second-largest city, home to about 3.3 million people, and the country's main port. It is squeezed between mountains and the sea on the south-east coast, and its streets climb steep hills and spill down to beaches, harbours and fish markets. It feels looser and saltier than Seoul, with a distinctive dialect and a reputation for friendliness.

The city is best known for its sights by the water. Gamcheon Culture Village, once a refugee settlement, is now a maze of painted houses, Haeundae is Korea's most famous beach, and Haedong Yonggungsa is a rare temple set on a rocky shore. Jagalchi Fish Market is the country's largest seafood market, where you choose your fish and eat it upstairs, and Busan holds a large film festival every autumn.

Busan is about two and a half hours from Seoul by KTX and is easy to combine with Gyeongju, an hour away. Two or three days are enough for the main sights, and the city is at its best in late spring and early autumn, when the sea is warm enough for the beach and the humidity of summer has eased.

Why visit

Why visit Busan?

Busan is Korea's seaside city, with the best seafood and a more relaxed pace than Seoul.

  • Colourful hillside villages

    Gamcheon's painted houses and alleys.

  • Beaches and coast

    Haeundae, Gwangalli and coastal walking paths.

  • Seafood

    The Jagalchi market and grilled shellfish.

  • A seaside temple

    Haedong Yonggungsa on the rocks.

  • Easy from Seoul

    About two and a half hours by KTX.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Busan

Busan's sights are spread out, so plan by area: the old port, the beaches and the coast.

  1. 01

    Gamcheon Culture Village

    A hillside neighbourhood of pastel houses, murals, art installations and cafés, built by refugees in the 1950s and revived as an arts district.

    Tip Go early, and expect steep lanes.

  2. 02

    Haeundae Beach

    Korea's most famous beach, a long sweep of sand backed by high-rise hotels, with a summer festival scene and a coastal walk.

    Tip Visit outside the July and August crowds.

  3. 03

    Haedong Yonggungsa

    A Buddhist temple founded in the 14th century and built along a rocky coast, with prayer halls, stone lanterns and sea views.

    Tip Go at sunrise or on a weekday.

  4. 04

    Jagalchi Fish Market

    Korea's largest seafood market, with stalls of live fish, squid, crabs and shellfish, and restaurants above where your choice is cooked.

    Tip Ask the price first.

  5. 05

    Gwangalli Beach and Gwangan Bridge

    A cafe-lined beach with views of a long suspension bridge that lights up at night.

    Tip Go at dusk.

  6. 06

    Taejongdae

    A park on a forested cape with cliffs, a lighthouse and walking paths above the sea.

    Tip Take the sightseeing train if you are tired.

Things to do

Best things to do in Busan

Busan is about the sea, the streets and the food.

  1. Eat at Jagalchi

    Choose your seafood and have it cooked.

    Tip Visit at lunch.

  2. Walk the coast

    The Igidae Coastal Walk and the Haeundae Dongbaek path offer sea views.

    Tip Bring water.

  3. Ride the Blue Line Park train

    A coastal train and sky capsule from Haeundae to Cheongsapo.

    Tip Book a seat in advance.

  4. Try street food

    Ssiat hotteok, eomuk fish cakes and tteokbokki at Bupyeong Kkangtong Market.

    Tip Go in the evening.

  5. Relax at a jjimjilbang

    A Korean spa such as Spa Land in Centum City.

    Tip Bring a towel, and check the rules.

  6. Watch a film festival

    The Busan International Film Festival in early October.

    Tip Book hotels far ahead.

Local food

What to eat in Busan

Busan food is seafood-heavy, spicy and hearty.

  • Dwaeji gukbap

    A rich pork and rice soup that is the city's signature dish.

    Where to try it Local restaurants near Busan Station.

  • Fresh raw fish (hoe)

    Sliced raw fish served with chilli paste and lettuce.

    Where to try it Jagalchi and Millak Raw Fish Town.

  • Milmyeon

    Wheat noodles in a cold or hot broth, invented in Busan.

    Where to try it Old noodle shops.

  • Ssiat hotteok

    A sweet pancake stuffed with seeds and nuts.

    Where to try it BIFF Square.

  • Eomuk (fish cake)

    Skewered fish cakes in broth.

    Where to try it Markets and stalls.

  • Grilled clams and shellfish

    A seaside barbecue tradition.

    Where to try it Restaurants at Gijang and Gwangalli.

Transport

Getting to and around Busan

Busan is easily reached by train, air or bus.

Getting there

  • KTX from Seoul

    About two and a half hours from Seoul Station to Busan Station.

    Book in advance.

  • Flight

    About an hour from Seoul Gimpo to Gimhae, and international flights from Japan and Asia.

    A light rail line links the airport to the metro.

  • Bus and train from Gyeongju

    About 30 minutes by KTX from Singyeongju to Busan.

    Frequent.

Getting around

The metro covers the centre and Haeundae, with buses and taxis for the rest.

  • Metro

    Four lines, and a light rail line.

    Use a T-money card.

  • Buses

    Cover Gamcheon and the coast.

    Use Naver Map.

  • Taxis

    Cheap for hills and late nights.

    Show a Korean address.

When to go

Best time to visit Busan

Busan is milder than Seoul in winter and warmer in the sea, but summer is humid and rainy.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Pleasant, with cherry blossom in late March.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Good

    Beach season, but hot, humid, rainy and crowded.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Clear and comfortable, with the film festival.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cool and dry, with fewer visitors.

Worth planning around: August typhoon days and beach weekends.

Itinerary

Two days in Busan

The hillside village and market on the first day, and the coast on the second.

  1. Day1

    The old port

    Colour and seafood.

    • Morning Gamcheon Culture Village.
    • Afternoon Jagalchi Fish Market and Nampo.
    • Evening BIFF Square street food.
  2. Day2

    The coast

    Temple and beach.

    • Morning Haedong Yonggungsa.
    • Afternoon Haeundae Beach and the Blue Line Park.
    • Evening Gwangalli Beach and the bridge lights.

Travel tips

Busan travel tips

A few tips help you enjoy Busan.

  • Wear good shoes for Gamcheon's steep lanes.
  • Go to Haedong Yonggungsa early to avoid crowds.
  • Eat at busy seafood spots and ask the price.
  • Use the metro for Haeundae, and buses for Gamcheon.
  • Book hotels early for the film festival and fireworks festival.
  • Bring a light jacket for the sea breeze.
  • Check typhoon forecasts in late summer.

FAQ

Busan: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Busan?

Two days for the highlights, three with a day trip to Gyeongju.

Is Busan better than Seoul?

They are different. Busan is more relaxed, with sea and seafood. Most visitors see both.

How far is Busan from Seoul?

About two and a half hours by KTX.

Can I swim at Haeundae?

Yes in the summer season, usually from July to August, when lifeguards are on duty.

Is raw fish safe to eat?

At busy, reputable markets and restaurants, yes.

Can I visit Busan from Gyeongju?

Yes, in about 30 minutes by KTX.
