South Korea packs palaces, mountain temples, beaches, a volcanic island and one of Asia's best food cultures into a country you can cross by train in a few hours. This guide covers how the regions divide, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to travel, when to go, what it costs and three routes that work.

How South Korea divides

South Korea is about 500 km from north to south, and for a visitor it falls into five zones.

Seoul and the north-west. Seoul is the hub, with Suwon&#39;s fortress an easy day trip and the DMZ just to the north.

The east coast. Seoraksan has the peaks and Gangneung has beaches, coffee and tea, both a train or bus ride from Seoul.

The south-east. Gyeongju, the ancient Silla capital, and Busan, the second city on the coast, are linked by short rail trips, with Andong and Hahoe Village inland.

The south-west. Jeonju is the centre for traditional houses and food.

Jeju. Jeju Island is a volcanic island an hour by air from Seoul and Busan.

Worth knowing: The practical unit is a line, Seoul to Busan by KTX with stops at Jeonju or Gyeongju, plus a flight to Jeju. The country is small enough to stitch these together in two weeks.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If you have one week

    Seoul with a day at Suwon or the DMZ, then Gyeongju and Busan by train.

  • If you love history and heritage

    Gyeongju for Silla tombs and temples, Hahoe Village for Joseon-era life and Suwon for its fortress.

  • If you love nature and hiking

    Seoraksan for granite peaks and Jeju for Hallasan, lava tubes and coastal walks.

  • If you love food

    Jeonju for bibimbap and banquet-style meals, Busan for seafood and street snacks, and Seoul for everything.

  • If you love beaches and coast

    Busan&#39;s Haeundae, Gangneung&#39;s pine-lined shores and Jeju&#39;s white-sand coves.

  • If you want something different

    The DMZ for one of the world's most unusual borders, or a templestay near Seoraksan.

Getting around

South Korea has one of the best transport systems in the world, cheap and punctual, and its apps do most of the work.

KTX and SRT trains. Seoul to Busan takes about two and a half hours. Trains also serve Gyeongju, Jeonju, Gangneung and other cities. Book seats on Korail and consider a KR Pass.

Express buses. Cover places without trains, including Andong and Sokcho, for a fraction of the price of flying.

Flights. Jeju is best reached by air from Seoul (Gimpo) or Busan, with many low-cost carriers.

City metros. A T-money card works on subways, buses and many taxis. Use Naver Map or Kakao Map rather than Google Maps for directions.

Worth knowing: Rental cars need an international driving permit, and are worth it only on Jeju and around the east coast.

When to go

South Korea has four clear seasons, and the shoulder seasons are the best.

March to May is spring, with cherry blossom in early April, mild days and green mountains. Air quality can dip on some days.

June to August is summer, hot and humid, with a rainy season from late June to July and possible typhoons. Beaches are busy, and hiking is best early in the day.

September to November is autumn, the finest season, with clear skies and colour, peaking in Seoraksan in mid to late October.

December to February is winter, cold and dry, with ski resorts near Gangneung and quieter, cheaper city sightseeing.

Worth knowing: Avoid the Lunar New Year and Chuseok holiday weeks if you can. Trains and flights are packed, and many shops close.

Money, costs and connectivity

South Korea is reasonable value, cheaper than Japan and most of Western Europe. A restaurant meal costs US$7–15, a comfortable hotel US$80–150, and a KTX ticket from Seoul to Busan around US$40–60.

Cards are accepted almost everywhere, though some foreign cards fail at ATMs. Tipping is not expected.

Mobile coverage is excellent. Buy an eSIM or rent a pocket Wi-Fi, and download Naver Map, Kakao Map and Papago before you land.

Check entry rules, including K-ETA registration, before you fly, since they have changed several times in recent years.

Three routes that work

  1. One week: Seoul, Gyeongju and Busan

    Three nights in Seoul with a day at Suwon, two in Gyeongju and two in Busan.

    Tip Take the KTX between each city.

  2. Ten days: adding Jeju

    The same route, then a flight from Busan to Jeju for three nights, and back to Seoul.

    Tip Rent a car on Jeju.

  3. Two weeks: the classic

    The itinerary on the country page: Seoul, the DMZ, Suwon, Jeonju, Gyeongju, Busan and Jeju.

    Tip Swap Jeonju for the east coast: Seoraksan and Gangneung if you prefer mountains and sea.

A UNESCO-listed clan village on the Nakdong River with thatched and tiled houses, Byeongsan Confucian Academy, the mask dance, Andong soju and jjimdak chicken.

FAQ

Planning a trip to South Korea

Can I see South Korea without speaking Korean?

Yes. Stations, major attractions and hotels have English signs, and translation apps handle menus. Few taxi drivers and small restaurants speak English, so carry your destination written in Korean.

Is it better to take the train or drive?

Take trains and buses. They are fast, cheap and reach nearly everywhere. A car is worth it only on Jeju and in the countryside around Andong and Gangneung.

How far ahead should I book?

Book KTX seats and DMZ tours a week or more ahead, and Jeju flights and hanok stays a month or more ahead for peak seasons. Cherry blossom weeks, October foliage and holidays such as Chuseok fill up early.

Is South Korea good for a first trip to Asia?

It is one of the easiest. It is safe, clean, well signposted and full of good food, and most sights are reachable by public transport.
