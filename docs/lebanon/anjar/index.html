Welcome to Anjar

Anjar sits on the plain of the Bekaa Valley in eastern Lebanon, about 58 km east of Beirut and a few kilometres from the Syrian border. In the early 8th century, the Umayyad caliph al-Walid I, or possibly his son, founded a city here, on the trade route between Damascus and the coast. It was laid out on a Roman-inspired grid, enclosed by walls with towers, and it contained palaces, a mosque, baths and arcaded shops.

It did not last long. The Umayyad dynasty fell in 750, and the Abbasids, who took power, abandoned the city, which was ruined by an earthquake and forgotten. Because it was abandoned so soon and never built over, the layout is unusually clear: two main streets crossing at a tetrapylon, lined with rows of shops, and palaces and a mosque behind. UNESCO listed the site in 1984 as an important example of early Islamic urban planning.

The modern village of Anjar was founded in 1939 by Armenian refugees from Musa Dagh, in what is now Turkey, and its inhabitants are still mostly Armenian. Nearby, the Bekaa is Lebanon's main wine region, with the wineries of Ksara and Kefraya. Anjar's position close to the Syrian border has meant that it has been affected by regional tension, and Israeli strikes hit the border area in 2024. Many governments advised against travel to the Bekaa at the time of writing, so check the current situation before you consider going.

Why visit

Why visit Anjar?

Anjar is one of the few surviving early Islamic cities, and it is easy to grasp in a couple of hours.

  • A planned Umayyad city

    A regular grid of streets, a walled enclosure and monumental gates show how the early Umayyads borrowed from Roman town planning.

  • Reused Roman stone

    Many of the columns and capitals were taken from earlier Roman buildings, and the builders alternated courses of stone and brick in the Byzantine manner.

  • An Armenian village

    The modern town has its own culture, with Armenian churches, food and shops, including restaurants known for their trout and mezze.

  • Wine country

    Ksara, Kefraya and other wineries lie nearby, with tastings and lunches.

  • Close to Baalbek

    Anjar and Baalbek are about an hour and a half apart, and are often visited together.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Anjar

The archaeological site takes an hour or two, and the wineries and caves can fill the rest of a day.

  1. 01

    The Umayyad city

    A walled rectangle of about 350 by 370 metres, with four gates and a grid of streets. The remains of two palaces, a mosque, baths and arcades survive, with several restored colonnades.

    Tip Go early and walk the main street from gate to gate.

  2. 02

    The tetrapylon and the cardo

    The crossroads at the heart of the city, marked by four groups of columns. The main street is flanked by arcades of small shops.

    Tip Look at the mix of stone and brick courses in the walls.

  3. 03

    The Grand Palace and the mosque

    The remains of the caliph's palace and of the mosque, with a court, a prayer hall and carved decoration.

    Tip Read the information boards to make sense of the ruins.

  4. 04

    The Ksara caves and winery

    About 20 km from Anjar, Ksara is the oldest and best-known winery in Lebanon, founded by Jesuit monks in 1857, with a network of Roman-era caves used to store wine.

    Tip Book a tour and tasting in advance.

  5. 05

    Kefraya and other wineries

    A number of wineries in the western Bekaa and near Zahle offer visits, tastings and meals.

    Tip Confirm that they are open.

  6. 06

    The Armenian village and Aanjar's church

    A small town with Armenian churches and shops, and the memorial to the Musa Dagh refugees.

    Tip Ask permission to enter churches.

  7. 07

    Zahle and the Berdawni river

    The largest city of the Bekaa, with riverside restaurants along the Berdawni, known for arak and mezze.

    Tip Have lunch at a riverside restaurant.

Things to do

Best things to do in Anjar

A day in the Bekaa combines ruins, food and wine.

  1. Walk the Umayyad city

    Wander the arcades and streets, and see how the city was laid out.

    Tip Go in the morning.

  2. Eat at an Armenian restaurant

    Try the local trout, kebabs, sujuk, mante and other Armenian dishes.

    Tip Book for lunch on weekends.

  3. Visit a winery

    Tasting at Ksara or Kefraya, with a tour of the vineyards and cellars.

    Tip Book in advance, and use a driver.

  4. Explore the Ksara caves

    A tour of the cellar tunnels, cut into the rock by the Romans.

    Tip Wear warm clothes.

  5. Lunch by the river at Zahle

    Riverside restaurants serve arak and mezze under the trees.

    Tip Go on a weekday to avoid crowds.

  6. Combine with Baalbek

    Baalbek is about 90 minutes away, and the two make a full day, if conditions allow.

    Tip Check the current situation.

Local food

What to eat in Anjar

The Bekaa is Lebanon's breadbasket, and its cooking includes both traditional Lebanese and Armenian dishes.

  • Bekaa wine

    Reds, whites and rosés from the high-altitude vineyards, including Cabernet Sauvignon, Cinsault and Obaideh.

    Where to try it Wineries and restaurants.

  • Fresh trout

    Trout from the rivers, grilled or fried, served with lemon.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Anjar.

  • Armenian sujuk and basturma

    Spiced sausage and cured beef, served as starters or in sandwiches.

    Where to try it Armenian shops and restaurants.

  • Mezze

    Lebanese starters, often with fresh herbs from the valley.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Anjar and Zahle.

  • Bekaa cheese and labneh

    Fresh dairy from the valley.

    Where to try it Shops and restaurants.

  • Arak

    The aniseed spirit distilled from Bekaa grapes, served with a meal.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Zahle.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Anjar

Accommodation in Anjar is limited, and most visitors visit on a day trip. Zahle has more choice.

  • Anjar

    A few guesthouses and small hotels near the site.

  • Zahle

    Hotels and guesthouses in a larger town, about 25 km from Anjar.

    Best for: Restaurants and wineries

    A wider choice, an hour and a half away.

Transport

Getting to and around Anjar

Anjar is about 58 km east of Beirut on the Beirut–Damascus highway. Check current conditions and advice.

Getting there

  • Private driver or car

    About 1 hour 30 minutes from Beirut, over the mountain pass. A driver can combine Anjar with wineries and Baalbek.

    Ask about road and border-area conditions.

  • Bus or service taxi

    Buses and shared taxis run to Chtaura and Anjar.

    Ask about current services.

  • Tour

    Some tours combine Anjar, Ksara and Baalbek.

    Check what is included.

Getting around

The site is on foot, and a car or taxi links the wineries and towns.

  • Walking

    The Umayyad city is flat and easy to walk.

    Bring a hat and water.

  • Taxi or driver

    The best way to reach Ksara, Kefraya and Zahle.

    Agree the fare.

  • Car

    Useful for the valley.

    Do not drive after wine tastings.

When to go

Best time to visit Anjar

The Bekaa is at about 900 m, with hot dry summers and cold winters. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable, and the grape harvest is in September and October.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Green fields, warm days and snow on the mountains.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Shoulder

    Hot and dry, but cooler than the coast. Visit in the morning.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    The grape harvest, golden light and warm days.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cold and often snowy. The pass over the mountains may close.

Worth planning around: Snowstorms and any period of tension near the border.

Travel tips

Anjar travel tips

The Bekaa needs more caution than most places.

  • Read your government's advice. Anjar is close to the Syrian border, and the area has been affected by conflict.
  • Check conditions on the day with your driver or hotel.
  • Go early. The site has little shade.
  • Book winery visits in advance and confirm they are open.
  • Do not drink and drive.
  • Carry your passport for checkpoints.
  • Do not photograph military sites.

FAQ

Anjar: frequently asked questions

Is Anjar safe to visit?

Anjar is a quiet village, but it lies close to the Syrian border, and the Bekaa was affected by Israeli strikes in 2024. Many governments advise against travel to the Bekaa. Check current advice.

Who built Anjar?

The Umayyad caliph al-Walid I, or possibly his son al-Walid II, in the early 8th century. It was abandoned after the fall of the dynasty in 750.

Why are there Armenians in Anjar?

The modern village was founded in 1939 by refugees from Musa Dagh, in Turkey, who fled the Armenian genocide of 1915 and then the transfer of the region to Turkey.

How long do you need?

Half a day for the ruins and lunch, and a full day with a winery and Zahle.

Where is Lebanon's wine region?

The Bekaa Valley, at about 900 to 1,100 metres, where Ksara, Kefraya, Musar and other wineries are located.

Can I combine Anjar with Baalbek?

Yes, in normal conditions. They are about 90 minutes apart, and make a full day out of Beirut. Check the security situation first.
