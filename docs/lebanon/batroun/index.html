Welcome to Batroun

Batroun is a small town on a rocky headland about 55 km north of Beirut. It was a Phoenician port, known as Botrys, and it later became a Roman and Crusader town, and its old quarter is a tight cluster of stone houses, churches, mosques and shops around a small harbour. It is less historic than Byblos and less of a tourist site, and it has become in recent years one of the most fashionable places on the coast.

The best-known relic is the Phoenician sea wall, a line of rock about 225 metres long that stretches into the sea, shaped and reinforced by Phoenician builders to make a natural breakwater for the harbour. The town also has a 19th-century Greek Orthodox cathedral, a Maronite church, and a market where fishermen sell their catch. In the summer, its beaches, beach clubs, cafés and restaurants make it a favourite weekend spot for Beirutis.

Behind the coast, the hills are dotted with vineyards, and Batroun has become the centre of a small wine region. The town also has a reputation for its lemonade, made from local lemons and served in the old town. It is in the north, a part of the country that has generally been calm compared with the south and the Bekaa, but check your government's advice.

Why visit

Why visit Batroun?

Batroun combines sea, food and wine in a small and attractive setting, and is a good place to rest between sightseeing days.

  • The Phoenician wall

    A natural rock breakwater reinforced by the Phoenicians, believed to be about 2,500 years old, which still shelters the harbour.

  • A walkable old town

    Narrow lanes of stone houses, churches and workshops lead to the harbour and the sea.

  • Beaches and beach clubs

    Small sandy beaches and rocky bays with clubs offer swimming and sunset drinks from May to October.

  • Food and lemonade

    Seafood restaurants, bakeries and the famous Batroun lemonade make it a good place to eat.

  • Wine and hills

    Wineries in the hills behind the town welcome visitors for tastings and lunches.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Batroun

The old town, the wall and the harbour can be seen in a couple of hours, and the beaches and wineries fill the rest of a day.

  1. 01

    The Phoenician Wall

    A rocky sea wall of about 225 metres, part natural and part shaped by Phoenician builders, which formed a harbour and a defence. It is best seen from the shore near the old port.

    Tip Walk along the rocks at sunset.

  2. 02

    The old town and the souk

    A network of narrow lanes, restored stone houses, small shops and cafés around the harbour. It is pleasant to wander in the morning or evening.

    Tip Try the lemonade at a café.

  3. 03

    The Cathedral of St Stephen

    A 19th-century Maronite cathedral in the old town, with a dome and a bell tower, and a Greek Orthodox church nearby.

    Tip Dress modestly.

  4. 04

    The Roman theatre and rock-cut remains

    The remains of a small Roman theatre carved out of the rock, and ancient rock-cut tombs on the outskirts.

    Tip Ask locally for directions.

  5. 05

    The harbour and fish market

    A small port with fishing boats, a fish market and restaurants with tables at the water's edge.

    Tip Go in the late afternoon.

  6. 06

    Beaches and beach clubs

    Small sandy beaches and clubs along the coast, some public and some private.

    Tip Check entry fees and dress codes.

Things to do

Best things to do in Batroun

Batroun is a place for slow days, sunsets and long lunches.

  1. Watch the sunset at the wall

    The sun sets over the sea behind the Phoenician wall and the harbour.

    Tip Bring a drink and a jacket.

  2. Drink lemonade in the old town

    Batroun's lemonade, sometimes mixed with orange blossom water, is a local speciality.

    Tip Try it at several cafés.

  3. Visit a winery

    Vineyards in the hills produce good wines, and several offer tastings and meals.

    Tip Book ahead and hire a driver.

  4. Spend a day at a beach

    Swim, sunbathe and have lunch at a beach club.

    Tip Go on a weekday to avoid crowds.

  5. Eat fresh seafood

    Restaurants serve fish, calamari and prawns.

    Tip Choose your fish at the counter.

  6. Go out in the evening

    Bars and restaurants stay busy until late in summer.

    Tip Take a taxi or driver.

Local food

What to eat in Batroun

Batroun is a good place to eat well, with seafood, mezze and local produce.

  • Batroun lemonade

    Fresh lemon juice with sugar, mint or orange blossom water, served over ice.

    Where to try it Cafés in the old town.

  • Grilled fish

    Sea bream, sea bass and red mullet, grilled and served whole.

    Where to try it Harbour restaurants.

  • Mezze

    Hummus, tabbouleh and other starters, served with local wine or arak.

    Where to try it Restaurants in the old town.

  • Local wine

    Wines from vineyards in the hills, including whites and rosés.

    Where to try it Wineries and restaurants.

  • Bread and manakish

    Freshly baked flatbread with za'atar or cheese.

    Where to try it Bakeries.

  • Sweets and ice cream

    Knefe, baklava and ice cream.

    Where to try it Shops in the old town.

Transport

Getting to and around Batroun

Batroun is about 55 km north of Beirut on the coastal highway, about 20 minutes beyond Byblos.

Getting there

  • Private driver or car

    About an hour from Beirut without traffic, and longer at the weekend.

    Avoid leaving Beirut on Friday evening or returning on Sunday evening.

  • Bus or service taxi

    Buses and shared taxis run from Beirut to Batroun.

    Ask for Batroun.

  • Tour

    Some tours combine Batroun with Byblos and Jeita.

    Check the itinerary.

Getting around

The old town is walkable, and a car or taxi helps to reach the beaches and wineries.

  • Walking

    The old town, the harbour and the wall are within 10 minutes' walk.

    Wear comfortable shoes.

  • Taxi

    Taxis take you to the beaches and wineries.

    Agree the fare.

  • Car

    Useful for the wine region.

    Do not drink and drive.

When to go

Best time to visit Batroun

Batroun has a Mediterranean climate, and the beach season runs from May to October. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm and green, with the sea still cool.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Shoulder

    Hot and humid, and the busiest and most expensive season.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm sea, fewer people, and the grape harvest.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Mild and wet, with many places closed.

Worth planning around: August weekends, when the town is packed.

Travel tips

Batroun travel tips

Batroun is relaxed, and a few tips help.

  • Go on a weekday in summer. The weekend crowd from Beirut can be heavy.
  • Book restaurants and beach clubs in advance in July and August.
  • Bring cash in US dollars, and check card fees.
  • Do not drink and drive. Use a driver for the wineries.
  • Check the current situation. Batroun has generally been calm, but conditions in Lebanon can change.
  • Dress modestly in the old town and churches.
  • Bring a jacket for the evening.

FAQ

Batroun: frequently asked questions

What is Batroun known for?

Its Phoenician sea wall, its lemonade, its old town, its beaches and its growing wine scene.

How long do you need in Batroun?

One day is enough for the town, a swim and a winery. A night lets you enjoy the evening.

Is Batroun safe to visit?

It is in the north, which has generally been calm compared with the south and the Bekaa, but conditions in Lebanon can change. Check your government's advice.

How far is Batroun from Beirut?

About 55 km, or an hour without traffic.

When is the beach season?

From May to October, with the water at its warmest in August and September.

Can I combine Batroun with Byblos?

Yes. They are about 20 minutes apart, and are often visited together.
