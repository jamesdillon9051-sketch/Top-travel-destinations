Welcome to the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars

In the mountains of northern Lebanon, the Qadisha River has cut a deep gorge that the Maronite Christians called the Holy Valley, in Aramaic and Arabic Wadi Qadisha. From the early centuries of Christianity, monks and hermits settled in the caves and cliffs, and from the 7th century onwards the valley became a refuge for the Maronite community from persecution. Monasteries, chapels and hermitages were built along its walls, many of them still in use.

At the top of the valley, about 2,000 metres up, is a grove of cedars called the Cedars of God (Arz al-Rab), which contains about 375 trees. Some are estimated to be more than a thousand years old, and a few are said to be older. Cedar wood from Lebanon was traded by the Phoenicians and used by Egyptians and Mesopotamians, and the Bible mentions the trees dozens of times. The tree appears on the Lebanese flag. UNESCO listed the valley and the grove together in 1998.

The nearby town of Bsharri is the birthplace of Kahlil Gibran, the author of The Prophet, and holds a museum that houses his paintings and manuscripts and, in the rock beneath, his tomb. In winter, the Cedars become Lebanon's oldest ski resort. The region is in the north of the country, and has generally been calm compared with the south and the Bekaa, but check your government's advice before you go.

Why visit

Why visit the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars?

This is the most beautiful and least commercial part of mountain Lebanon, and it has a unique combination of nature and religious history.

  • A gorge of monasteries

    Chapels, caves and monasteries cling to the valley wall, some occupied since the 5th century, and can be reached by trails and roads.

  • The oldest cedars in Lebanon

    The grove is small but atmospheric, with gnarled trunks that have stood for centuries, in a landscape of high mountain peaks.

  • Kahlil Gibran

    The museum in Bsharri shows the work of one of the best-selling poets in history, in the setting of a former hermitage.

  • Walking and skiing

    Trails follow the valley and form part of the Lebanon Mountain Trail, and the small ski resort at the Cedars is popular from December to March.

  • Mountain villages and food

    Villages such as Bsharri, Hadchit and Ehden have guesthouses, restaurants and local produce, with a cool climate in summer.

Main attractions

Top things to see in the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars

The valley and the grove are about 30 minutes apart by road. A day covers the main sights, and a second allows a walk.

  1. 01

    The Cedars of God (Horsh Arz el-Rab)

    A protected grove of about 375 cedars at an altitude of about 1,900 metres, with a small chapel in the middle and a visitor centre. The oldest trees are thought to be well over a thousand years old.

    Tip Go in the morning, when the light through the trees is best.

  2. 02

    The Qadisha Valley

    A steep-sided gorge, about 35 km long, cut by the Qadisha River, with terraced fields and villages on its rim. A road runs along the edge, with viewpoints.

    Tip Walk down into the valley for a different perspective.

  3. 03

    Qannoubin Monastery

    A Maronite monastery built into the valley wall, which was the seat of the patriarchs from the 14th to the 19th century. It is reached by a footpath or a steep road and is still in use.

    Tip Modest dress is required. Check the opening hours.

  4. 04

    Deir Mar Elisha and Deir Qozhaya

    Two more monasteries, one with a chapel with hermit cells cut in the rock, and the other with a museum and a printing press, the first in the Middle East. Qozhaya is one of the most dramatic sites in the valley.

    Tip Combine them on a day's walk or drive.

  5. 05

    Bsharri and the Gibran Museum

    A small town at 1,400 metres on the rim of the valley, and the birthplace of Kahlil Gibran (1883–1931). The museum, in the former monastery of Mar Sarkis, houses his paintings, manuscripts and tomb.

    Tip The museum is small, and can be seen in an hour.

  6. 06

    The Lebanon Mountain Trail

    A long-distance walking route through the mountains, with a well-marked stage through the valley and the cedars.

    Tip Hire a local guide for longer sections.

  7. 07

    The Cedars ski area

    A small ski resort above the cedar grove, at 2,000–2,800 metres, with a few lifts and simple hotels. It is popular in winter, and has spectacular views.

    Tip Check snow conditions in advance.

Things to do

Best things to do in the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars

This is a landscape for walking, and for slowing down.

  1. Walk in the Qadisha Valley

    Trails lead from the villages to the monasteries, with views over the valley walls and terraces. Short and long routes are available.

    Tip Wear good shoes and take water.

  2. Visit the cedar grove early

    See the trees in the morning light and read about their history at the visitor centre.

    Tip The grove can be cold and snowy from November to April.

  3. Visit the Gibran Museum

    See the paintings and manuscripts of Lebanon's most famous writer, and the tomb.

    Tip Read a few pages of The Prophet first.

  4. Eat in a mountain village

    Restaurants in Bsharri, Ehden and other villages serve mountain cooking, with grills, mezze and local sweets.

    Tip Try the local kibbeh nayyeh.

  5. Ski at the Cedars

    The resort's slopes are small but scenic, and lifts run from December to March.

    Tip Book equipment and accommodation ahead.

  6. Stay in a village guesthouse

    Small guesthouses in Bsharri, Hasroun and Ehden offer home cooking and mountain views.

    Tip Book in advance in summer and winter.

Local food

What to eat in the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars

Northern mountain cooking is hearty and built on local produce: meat, dairy, grains and preserved fruit.

  • Kibbeh nayyeh

    Raw minced lamb pounded with bulgur and spices, eaten with mint, onion and olive oil. A specialty of the north.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Bsharri and Ehden.

  • Mezze

    A spread of salads, dips and pastries made with local ingredients.

    Where to try it Mountain restaurants.

  • Grilled meats

    Lamb, chicken and kafta grilled over charcoal, served with bread and pickles.

    Where to try it Grill houses in the villages.

  • Mountain cheese and labneh

    Cheese and strained yoghurt made from goat's and sheep's milk, sold in village shops.

    Where to try it Village shops and restaurants.

  • Apples and mountain fruit

    The mountain villages grow apples, cherries and other fruit, sold fresh and as jam.

    Where to try it Roadside stalls in summer and autumn.

  • Sweets and arak

    Local sweets, and arak, distilled in the mountains, served with meals.

    Where to try it Restaurants and bakeries.

Accommodation

Where to stay in the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars

Accommodation is simple and mostly in Bsharri, Hasroun, Ehden and at the Cedars. Booking ahead is wise for summer and the ski season.

  • Bsharri

    Small hotels and guesthouses on the rim of the valley, with restaurants and shops.

    Best for: Views and the Gibran Museum

  • The Cedars

    A handful of hotels and chalets, most open in the ski season and summer.

    Best for: Skiing and the grove

  • Ehden and Hasroun

    Mountain villages with guesthouses, restaurants and the Horsh Ehden nature reserve nearby.

    Best for: Summer and a nature reserve

Transport

Getting to and around the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars

The valley and the cedars are about 120 km north of Beirut, up a winding mountain road from the coast near Tripoli.

Getting there

  • Private driver or hire car

    About 2 hours 30 minutes from Beirut and about an hour from Tripoli. The road from the coast climbs steeply, with many bends.

    Avoid driving in the dark or in snow.

  • Bus or service taxi

    Shared taxis and minibuses run from Tripoli to Bsharri, but not to the Cedars in winter.

    Ask locally.

  • Tour

    Some tours from Beirut include the valley and the cedars.

    Check what is included.

Getting around

A car or a driver is the practical way to reach the different points. Walking is the way to explore the valley.

  • Car or driver

    Roads link Bsharri, the Cedars and the valley viewpoints.

    The roads are narrow and steep in places.

  • Walking

    Paths lead down into the valley and to the monasteries.

    Wear sturdy shoes.

  • Taxi

    Local taxis take you between villages.

    Agree the fare.

When to go

Best time to visit the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars

The valley is at about 1,400 m and the cedars at about 1,900 m, so summers are cool and winters cold and snowy. Late spring and autumn are the best seasons for walking, and winter for skiing.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Snowmelt and waterfalls, with fresh green in the valley. The cedars may still be snowy in March.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Cool and clear, with a lot of visitors from the coast. Good for walking.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm days, cool nights and autumn colours.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Cold and snowy. The Cedars ski resort operates, and roads may close after heavy snow.

Worth planning around: Snowstorms and days of heavy rain when the mountain roads can be dangerous.

Itinerary

Two days in the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars

The cedar grove and the Gibran Museum on the first day, and the valley's monasteries on the second. Assumes a base in Bsharri.

  1. Day1

    Cedars and Gibran

    The trees and the poet.

    • Morning The Cedars of God and the visitor centre.
    • Afternoon The Gibran Museum and a walk around Bsharri.
    • Evening Dinner and a night in the village.
  2. Day2

    The Holy Valley

    A day of walking and monasteries.

    • Morning Walk down the valley to Qannoubin and the monasteries.
    • Afternoon Lunch in a village and a viewpoint on the way out.
    • Evening Drive back to the coast.

Travel tips

The Qadisha Valley and the Cedars travel tips

The mountains are cool and rewarding, and a few tips help.

  • Check your government's advice, and the weather. The north has been calmer than the south, but conditions can change.
  • Bring warm layers. It is cool at altitude even in summer, and cold in winter.
  • Do not drive in the dark or in snow. Mountain roads are steep and narrow.
  • Wear proper walking shoes for the valley, and take water and snacks.
  • Dress modestly at the monasteries, and keep your voice down.
  • Book accommodation ahead for July, August and the ski season.
  • Bring cash, as shops and small guesthouses may not accept cards.
  • Do not damage or remove anything from the cedar grove.

FAQ

The Qadisha Valley and the Cedars: frequently asked questions

How old are the Cedars of God?

The oldest trees are estimated to be more than a thousand years old, and some claim up to three thousand, but exact ages are hard to establish. The grove has about 375 trees.

Why is the valley called the Holy Valley?

Because Christian hermits and monks lived in its caves and cliffs from the early centuries, and because it was a refuge for the Maronite Church from the 7th century. The Aramaic name Qadisha means holy.

When is the best time to visit?

May to June and September to October for walking, and December to March for skiing. Summer is popular with Lebanese visitors, and winter can close the roads.

How long do you need?

One long day covers the cedars, Bsharri and a viewpoint, and two days allows you to walk in the valley and visit the monasteries.

Is the Qadisha Valley safe?

The north has generally been calm compared with the south and the Bekaa, but conditions in Lebanon can change quickly. Check your government's advice.

Who was Kahlil Gibran?

A Lebanese-American poet, painter and writer, born in Bsharri in 1883, best known for The Prophet (1923). He died in New York in 1931 and is buried in Bsharri.
