Welcome to Tyre

Tyre, called Sour in Arabic, was one of the greatest cities of the ancient Mediterranean. It was built on an island just off the coast, which made it almost impregnable, and it grew rich as a trading power and a colonising one: according to tradition, Tyrian settlers founded Carthage in the 9th century BCE. It was famous for Tyrian purple, a dye extracted from murex sea-snails that was worth more than gold and was reserved for royalty.

In 332 BCE, after a seven-month siege, Alexander the Great built a causeway from the mainland to capture the city, and the sand that gathered against it eventually turned the island into a peninsula, which is why Tyre is now joined to the land. Under the Romans it became a major city, and its ruins, the Al Mina site near the old harbour and the Al Bass site to the north, include a colonnaded street, baths, a triumphal arch, a vast necropolis and a hippodrome, the largest in Lebanon. UNESCO inscribed the site in 1984.

Tyre is about 80 km south of Beirut and 20 km from the Israeli border, and it has been strongly affected by conflict since 2023. It was the subject of Israeli evacuation orders and strikes in 2024, including near the archaeological sites, and UNESCO gave provisional enhanced protection to the site. Many governments advised against travel to south Lebanon at the time of writing. Check current advice and conditions before you consider going.

Why visit

Why visit Tyre?

In normal times, Tyre is one of the most rewarding day trips from Beirut, and one of the few places where Phoenician and Roman history can be seen side by side.

  • The Roman hippodrome

    About 480 metres long, with seating for around 20,000 spectators, it is one of the largest and best-preserved Roman circuses anywhere.

  • A Phoenician and Roman harbour city

    The Al Mina site has streets, baths, a colonnade and a Byzantine church, and stands near the old port, which is still in use.

  • The Al Bass necropolis and arch

    A huge cemetery with hundreds of sarcophagi and a Roman road that leads to a monumental triumphal arch.

  • A long sandy beach

    The Tyre Coast Nature Reserve has one of the longest public beaches in Lebanon, and sea turtles nest there in summer.

  • A working fishing town

    The old harbour has fishing boats, seafood restaurants and a small souk in narrow lanes.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Tyre

The two archaeological sites, Al Mina and Al Bass, are about 2 km apart. This describes the sites as they were before 2023, subject to current access.

  1. 01

    Al Mina archaeological site

    The main city excavation, on the peninsula near the port, with a Roman colonnaded street, a Byzantine road, public baths, an arena and remains of shops and houses.

    Tip Go in the morning, when it is cool.

  2. 02

    The Roman hippodrome

    A circus about 480 metres long, dating from the 2nd century CE, with the remains of seating, a central spine and starting gates. It is one of the best-preserved in the Roman world.

    Tip Walk the length of the track to appreciate the size.

  3. 03

    The Al Bass necropolis and Roman road

    A vast burial ground with hundreds of sarcophagi, stone tombs and mausolea, and a paved Roman road that leads to a triumphal arch and an ancient aqueduct.

    Tip Combine it with the hippodrome, which is next to it.

  4. 04

    The Tyre Coast Nature Reserve

    A protected stretch of sand dunes and beach south of the town, where green and loggerhead turtles nest from May to August. It is one of the last unspoiled stretches of Lebanese coast.

    Tip Visit the beach in the morning and observe the turtle-protection rules.

  5. 05

    The old port and fishing harbour

    A lively harbour with a working fish market, a few restaurants and cafés, and views of the old town.

    Tip Go in the late afternoon.

  6. 06

    The Christian quarter and the Cathedral ruins

    Narrow lanes in the old town, with churches and the ruins of a Crusader cathedral, a reminder of Tyre's role as one of the last Crusader strongholds.

    Tip Ask permission to enter churches.

Things to do

Best things to do in Tyre

In normal times, Tyre is a day trip built around the ruins and the sea.

  1. Walk through the Roman ruins

    See the streets, the baths and the hippodrome, with a guide or an audio guide if available.

    Tip Go early, as there is little shade.

  2. Have fish at the harbour

    Local restaurants serve the day's catch with mezze and arak.

    Tip Ask the price before you order.

  3. Swim at the public beach

    The nature reserve's long sandy beach is one of the best in Lebanon, with clean water.

    Tip Check current conditions and the rules on protected areas.

  4. Wander the old town

    See the fish market, small shops and the harbour.

    Tip Dress modestly, as the town is conservative.

  5. Visit in the sea turtle nesting season

    From May to August, turtles come ashore to nest at the reserve. Conservation groups run guided watches.

    Tip Follow the instructions and use no lights.

  6. Combine with Sidon

    Sidon is 40 km north, with a sea castle and old souks, and the two make a good day.

    Tip Start in Tyre, and head north in the afternoon.

Local food

What to eat in Tyre

Tyre is a seafood town, and its restaurants serve the day's catch. The nearby south also has good fruit, particularly citrus.

  • Grilled fish

    Sea bream, sea bass and red mullet, grilled with olive oil and lemon.

    Where to try it Restaurants at the harbour.

  • Sayyadieh

    Fish cooked with rice, onions and spices, a coastal dish across the Levant.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants in the old town.

  • Mezze

    Hummus, tabbouleh and other starters, served with arak.

    Where to try it Restaurants in the old town.

  • Fresh citrus and bananas

    Oranges, lemons and bananas grown around Tyre and sold by the roadside.

    Where to try it Roadside stalls and markets.

  • Sweets

    Knefe, baklava and other Arab sweets, sold in shops in the old town.

    Where to try it Sweet shops.

  • Ice cream

    Rose, orange blossom and pistachio flavours.

    Where to try it Shops in the centre.

Transport

Getting to and around Tyre

Tyre is about 80 km south of Beirut along the coastal highway. Check current advice and conditions before you travel.

Getting there

  • Private driver

    About 1 hour 30 minutes from Beirut, often more in traffic. Drivers know the latest conditions and checkpoints.

    Ask about road and checkpoint conditions.

  • Bus or service taxi

    Buses and shared taxis run from Beirut's Cola station to Sidon and Tyre.

    Check current services.

  • Tour

    Some tours combine Tyre with Sidon and Beiteddine.

    Check the itinerary and whether it operates.

Getting around

The two sites are 2 km apart, with the town in between.

  • Taxi

    Cheap and convenient between the sites and the harbour.

    Agree the fare.

  • Walking

    The old town and Al Mina are walkable.

    Wear a hat.

  • Car

    Easiest for the two archaeological sites and the beach.

    Parking is available at both sites.

When to go

Best time to visit Tyre

Tyre has a hot Mediterranean climate. The beach season is May to October, and spring and autumn are best for the ruins.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Warm, clear days and green fields.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Shoulder

    Hot and humid. Good for the beach, and the turtles nest.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Warm and calm, with a warm sea.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Quiet

    Mild and rainy. Few visitors.

Worth planning around: Any period of conflict or tension in the south, and midday in summer.

Travel tips

Tyre travel tips

Tyre needs more caution than most places.

  • Read your government's advice before you plan a visit. The south has been the focus of conflict since 2023, and advice is strict.
  • Check conditions on the day with your driver or hotel. Access can change at short notice.
  • Follow any instructions from the army or UN forces, and do not enter closed areas.
  • Go in the morning, and take water and sun protection.
  • Avoid photographing military positions, checkpoints and political posters.
  • Dress modestly. The town is conservative.
  • Respect the turtle nesting areas on the beach.

FAQ

Tyre: frequently asked questions

Is Tyre safe to visit?

Tyre and the south have been the focus of conflict since 2023, and Israeli evacuation orders and strikes affected the city in 2024. Many governments advise against travel to south Lebanon. Check current advice and do not visit if advised against it.

What is Tyre known for?

For being a great Phoenician city, the source of Tyrian purple dye, the legendary founder of Carthage, and a major Roman city with a huge hippodrome.

How long do you need in Tyre?

Half a day for the ruins, and a full day with lunch and the beach.

Is Tyre a UNESCO World Heritage Site?

Yes, since 1984. UNESCO gave it provisional enhanced protection during the 2024 conflict.

How do I get to Tyre?

By car or driver from Beirut, about 90 minutes, or by bus or shared taxi. Check conditions first.

What is Tyrian purple?

A dye made from the mucus of murex sea-snails, extremely expensive in antiquity and associated with royalty. Phoenician Tyre made its fortune on it.
