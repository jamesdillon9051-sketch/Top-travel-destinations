Welcome to Malbork

Malbork Castle, called Marienburg by its builders, is the largest castle in the world by land area and the biggest brick Gothic castle in Europe. The Teutonic Order, a military religious order of crusading knights, began building it in the late 13th century as a convent, and it became the capital of the Order's state in 1309. Over generations it grew into a vast complex of three castles, the High Castle, the Middle Castle and the Lower Castle, ringed by walls, moats and towers.

Malbork was the seat of the Grand Master, and its interiors include a Gothic chapel, a refectory with a delicate palm-vaulted ceiling, and the Grand Master's Palace. The castle passed to Poland in 1466, later to Prussia, and was heavily damaged in the Second World War, then painstakingly restored. It was listed by UNESCO in 1997.

A visit involves a lot of walking, and an audio guide is included with tickets. It takes about three to four hours, and the castle is best combined with Gdańsk, 60 km away, by train in under an hour. Summer evenings sometimes bring a light and sound show.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Malbork

The castle is the attraction, with a few extra museums.

  1. 01

    The High Castle

    The original convent, with a courtyard, chapter house, Gothic chapel and refectory.

    Tip Allow two hours.

  2. 02

    Panoramic view from across the Nogat

    The best view of the whole castle, from the opposite riverbank.

    Tip Go at sunset.

  3. 03

    The Grand Master's Palace

    The lavish residence of the Order's leader, in the Middle Castle, with a famous refectory.

    Tip Look up at the vaults.

  4. 04

    Amber Museum

    A collection of Baltic amber items.

    Tip Included in some tickets.

  5. 05

    Armoury and weapons

    Displays of medieval arms.

    Tip Good for families.

  6. 06

    The Lower Castle

    Workshops, stables and outbuildings.

    Tip Often quiet.
