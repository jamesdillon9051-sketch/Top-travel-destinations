Welcome to Zakopane

The Tatras are the highest mountain range in the Carpathians and the only truly alpine range in Poland, forming a natural border with Slovakia. Jagged granite peaks rise to 2,499 metres at Rysy, above deep valleys, glacial lakes and pine forests, and most of the Polish side is a national park. Zakopane, at the foot, is the region's unofficial capital.

The town has its own architecture and culture. In the late 19th century, artists developed the Zakopane Style, a fanciful wooden architecture with carved gables that is visible in villas and churches such as the small chapel at Jaszczurówka. The highland (górale) people have their own music, costumes and food, above all oscypek, a smoked sheep's cheese grilled and served with cranberry jam. Krupówki, the main pedestrian street, is a busy line of shops and restaurants.

In summer, people come to hike: the trail to Morskie Oko, Poland's most famous lake, is a wide road that is easy for families, and harder routes climb to Giewont, with its cross, or to the ridge. A cable car goes up to Kasprowy Wierch for high views, and in winter Zakopane is Poland's ski capital. Two or three days is a good stay.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Zakopane

The town is the base, and the mountains are a short bus ride away.

  1. 01

    Morskie Oko

    A glacial lake at 1,395 metres, ringed by peaks, reached by a 9-km road walk from Palenica Białczańska.

    Tip Go early, since the road gets crowded.

  2. 02

    Giewont

    A mountain topped with a steel cross whose profile looks like a sleeping knight, reached by a demanding hike.

    Tip Avoid storms; the summit is exposed.

  3. 03

    Krupówki Street

    The main pedestrian street, lined with shops, restaurants and stalls selling oscypek and highland goods.

    Tip Best in the evening.

  4. 04

    Jaszczurówka Chapel

    A small wooden chapel of 1907 that is a masterpiece of the Zakopane Style.

    Tip Walk from the town.

  5. 05

    Kasprowy Wierch

    A cable car to a 1,987-metre summit on the Slovak border, with big views.

    Tip Book in summer.

  6. 06

    Rysy

    Poland's highest peak at 2,499 metres, a serious hike for experienced walkers.

    Tip Only in summer, with good weather.
