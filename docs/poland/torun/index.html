Welcome to Toruń

Toruń is a medieval town on the Vistula river in northern Poland, founded by the Teutonic Knights in the 13th century, and a member of the Hanseatic League. It survived the Second World War almost untouched, and its Gothic old town, with more than 1,000 historic buildings, was listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997. It is one of the best-preserved medieval towns in Central Europe.

The centre of the old town is the Rynek, a square dominated by the huge Gothic Old Town Hall, built of red brick, with a tower that you can climb. Around it are Gothic churches, burgher houses with decorated gables, and remnants of the town walls, including a leaning tower. Toruń is also the birthplace of Nicolaus Copernicus, the astronomer, born here in 1473, and his family home is now a museum.

The other famous thing is gingerbread. Toruń's pierniki have been made since the 14th century, and shops sell them in shapes and flavours, and the Living Museum of Gingerbread lets visitors bake their own. Toruń is a good day trip from Gdańsk, Poznań or Warsaw, or an overnight stop.
