Welcome to Warsaw

Warsaw is Poland's capital and largest city, with about 1.8 million people. It has been the capital since 1596, and it suffered terribly in the Second World War: after the 1944 Warsaw Uprising, the German army systematically destroyed most of the city, and about 85 percent of the historic centre was lost. After the war, Poles rebuilt the Old Town using paintings by the 18th-century artist Canaletto and surviving drawings, and it is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The city today is a mix of eras. The pastel houses of the Old Town and the Royal Castle stand near the massive Palace of Culture and Science, a Stalin-era tower that dominates the skyline. Łazienki Park has a palace on a lake and free Chopin recitals in summer. Museums tell the story of the city's Jewish community at the POLIN Museum, of the 1944 uprising and of communism, and there are plenty of parks, riverside cafés and a booming food and bar scene.

Warsaw is not as picturesque as Kraków, but it is more real, and for many visitors it is the more moving city. Two or three days is a good stay, and it is a hub for trains to Kraków, Gdańsk and beyond.

Why visit

Why visit Warsaw?

Warsaw tells the story of modern Poland, and it is a lively, affordable capital.

  • A reconstructed Old Town

    A UNESCO site rebuilt after the war.

  • Powerful museums

    POLIN, the Uprising Museum and more.

  • Green parks

    Łazienki and Wilanów.

  • Food and nightlife

    Modern Polish cooking and riverside bars.

  • A rail hub

    Fast trains to all major cities.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Warsaw

Sights are spread along the Royal Route, with museums a short tram ride away.

  1. 01

    Old Town Market Square

    A square of coloured townhouses reconstructed after 1945, with a statue of the Warsaw mermaid, the city's symbol, at its centre.

    Tip Go early, before the crowds.

  2. 02

    Royal Castle

    A rebuilt royal residence with lavish state rooms and paintings, including works by Canaletto.

    Tip Free on some days.

  3. 03

    Łazienki Park

    A large royal park with the Palace on the Isle, peacocks and free Chopin concerts on summer Sundays.

    Tip Bring a picnic.

  4. 04

    Palace of Culture and Science

    A 237-metre tower given by the Soviet Union in the 1950s, with a viewing terrace at the top.

    Tip Go up for city views.

  5. 05

    POLIN Museum

    A museum on 1,000 years of Jewish life in Poland, on the site of the Warsaw Ghetto.

    Tip Allow three hours.

  6. 06

    Warsaw Uprising Museum

    An immersive museum on the 1944 uprising against German occupation.

    Tip Book in advance.

Things to do

Best things to do in Warsaw

Warsaw rewards museums, parks and evenings.

  1. Walk the Royal Route

    From the castle to Łazienki.

    Tip Start at Castle Square.

  2. Attend a Chopin concert

    Free recitals in Łazienki on summer Sundays.

    Tip Arrive early.

  3. Visit the Vistula beaches

    Riverside bars and sand in summer.

    Tip Go on the Praga side.

  4. Explore Praga

    A hip, artsy district across the river.

    Tip Go in the evening.

  5. See the Copernicus Science Centre

    A hands-on science museum.

    Tip Good for families.

  6. Try a vodka tasting

    Learn about Polish spirits.

    Tip Book ahead.

Travel tips

Warsaw travel tips

A few tips help you plan.

  • Book the Uprising Museum in advance.
  • Use the metro and trams.
  • Visit Łazienki on a Sunday.
  • Try milk bars.

  • Explore Praga.
