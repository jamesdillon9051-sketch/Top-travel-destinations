Welcome to Auschwitz-Birkenau

Auschwitz-Birkenau was the largest of the Nazi German concentration and extermination camps. Established in 1940 by Germany in occupied Poland, near the town of Oświęcim, it became the main site of the Holocaust: about 1.1 million people were murdered here, roughly one million of them Jews from across Europe, along with Polish prisoners, Roma and Sinti, Soviet prisoners of war and others. The camp was liberated by the Red Army on 27 January 1945.

Today the site is the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum, a memorial and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It has two main parts: Auschwitz I, the original camp of brick barracks, with exhibitions in preserved blocks and the gate with the words 'Arbeit macht frei', and Birkenau, 3 km away, a vast site with railway tracks, ruined gas chambers and rows of surviving barracks. Visitors come to bear witness, and to learn.

A visit is emotionally demanding, and it deserves preparation. Entry is by timed ticket and usually with a guided tour, which lasts about 3.5 hours, and it is not suitable for children under 14. It is best treated as a memorial rather than an attraction, with quiet, respect and time afterwards to reflect.

Why visit

Why visit Auschwitz-Birkenau?

A visit is not enjoyable, but many people find it one of the most important things they do in Europe.

  • Bearing witness

    To the scale of the Holocaust.

  • Understanding history

    Through the exhibitions and guides.

  • A UNESCO memorial

    Preserved as a warning.

  • Educational guides

    Trained museum educators.

  • Close to Kraków

    About 1.5 hours away.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Auschwitz-Birkenau

The memorial has two sites, linked by a shuttle bus.

  1. 01

    Auschwitz I

    The original camp, with brick barracks that now house exhibitions on the history of the camp, the victims and the perpetrators, including personal belongings.

    Tip Take the guided tour.

  2. 02

    Auschwitz II-Birkenau

    The vast extermination camp, with the railway ramp, ruins of the gas chambers, the memorial monument and surviving barracks.

    Tip Wear comfortable shoes and bring water.

  3. 03

    The exhibitions

    Permanent exhibitions in blocks, including national exhibitions and the display of victims' belongings.

    Tip Photography is limited in some blocks.

  4. 04

    The International Monument

    A memorial at Birkenau with plaques in the languages of the victims.

    Tip Take a moment of silence.

  5. 05

    The Auschwitz Jewish Center

    A museum and synagogue in the town of Oświęcim.

    Tip Optional addition.

  6. 06

    Oświęcim town

    The town outside the camp, with a Jewish history before the war.

    Tip Stay respectful.

Things to do

Best things to do in Auschwitz-Birkenau

A visit is a serious experience.

  1. Book a guided tour

    Educators lead groups through both sites.

    Tip Book weeks in advance.

  2. Take the shuttle

    Between Auschwitz I and Birkenau.

    Tip Included in tours.

  3. Visit early or late

    Crowds are smaller.

    Tip Book the first slot.

  4. Read beforehand

    Survivor testimonies such as Primo Levi.

    Tip Prepare emotionally.

  5. Allow time afterwards

    Do not schedule anything intense.

    Tip Take a quiet evening.

  6. Combine with Kraków's Jewish quarter

    To see the wider story.

    Tip Visit Kazimierz.

Transport

Getting to and around Auschwitz-Birkenau

Auschwitz is about 65 km west of Kraków.

Getting there

  • Organised tour

    Coach transfers and guides from Kraków, about 6 to 8 hours in total.

    Simple and popular.

  • Bus

    Regular buses from Kraków take about 1.5 hours.

    Book an entry slot.

  • Train

    From Kraków to Oświęcim, then a local bus.

    Slower.

Getting around

A shuttle links the two sites.

  • Museum shuttle

    Between Auschwitz I and Birkenau.

    Included.

  • On foot

    Within each site.

    Wear comfortable shoes.

  • Taxi

    From Oświęcim.

    Agree a fare.

When to go

Best time to visit Auschwitz-Birkenau

The site is open all year, and the atmosphere is heavy whatever the weather.

  • Spring (April–May)

    Best time

    Mild and smaller crowds.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Good

    Busy and hot.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Calm.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Quiet

    Cold and quiet, and the atmosphere is stark.

Worth planning around: Peak July and August, and 27 January, the anniversary of liberation, when there are ceremonies.

Travel tips

Auschwitz-Birkenau travel tips

A few tips help you prepare.

  • Book tickets weeks ahead, directly from the museum.
  • Take the guided tour.
  • Dress respectfully and quietly.
  • Bring ID, and expect security checks.
  • Do not take large bags.
  • Not suitable for children under 14.
  • Do not pose for selfies or treat it as a photo opportunity.

FAQ

Auschwitz-Birkenau: frequently asked questions

How long does a visit take?

About 3.5 hours for a guided tour.

Can I visit without a guide?

Yes at certain times, but book a slot online.

Is it free?

Entry without a guide is free at certain hours, though a ticket is still needed. Guided tours cost money.

Are children allowed?

It is not recommended for those under 14.

Can I take photos?

Yes in most places, but not in some rooms. Be respectful.

How far is it from Kraków?

About 65 km, or 1.5 hours.
