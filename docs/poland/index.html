Why go to Poland

Poland sits in the middle of Europe, between Germany and Ukraine, with a Baltic coastline to the north and the Tatra mountains to the south. It is a country of about 37 million people, and its history has been turbulent: partitioned out of existence in the 18th century, reborn in 1918, devastated by the Second World War, and then under communist rule until 1989. Since then it has become one of Europe's most dynamic economies.

For visitors, that history is the theme. Kraków's old town survived the war intact, while Warsaw, Gdańsk and Wrocław were largely destroyed and painstakingly rebuilt from paintings and records. Sites such as Auschwitz-Birkenau are sobering places of memory. But Poland is far more than its wartime story: it also offers Gothic brick castles, mountain hiking in the Tatras, Baltic beaches, Europe's last primeval forest, lively student cities and a growing food scene.

It is also easy and inexpensive. Trains connect the main cities, hotels and restaurants cost far less than in Western Europe, and English is widely spoken by younger people. Poland's weather is continental, with warm summers and cold winters, and its cities are at their best from May to September.

Experiences

Best things to do in Poland

The experiences that recur across the country and make a Polish trip feel like one.

  1. Stand in a medieval square

    Poland's market squares, the largest in Europe at Kraków, are surrounded by colourful townhouses and cafés. See Kraków, Wrocław and Poznań.

    Tip Visit early or late, when the tour groups are gone.

  2. Reflect at Auschwitz-Birkenau

    The former Nazi concentration camp is a sobering memorial and museum. See Auschwitz-Birkenau.

    Tip Book tickets weeks ahead, and allow half a day.

  3. Go underground at a salt mine

    The Wieliczka Salt Mine near Kraków has chapels and sculptures carved from salt. See Kraków.

    Tip Book a timed slot.

  4. Hike in the Tatras

    Poland's high mountains have lakes, ridges and mountain huts. See Zakopane and the Tatras.

    Tip Go between July and September for open trails.

  5. See European bison

    Białowieża Forest is one of the last primeval lowland forests in Europe, and home to bison. See Białowieża.

    Tip Book a guide, since the strict reserve requires one.

  6. Visit a castle

    Malbork, built by the Teutonic Knights, is one of the largest brick castles in the world. See Malbork.

    Tip Allow at least four hours.

  7. Drink in a milk bar

    Bar mleczny, communist-era canteens, serve cheap traditional food at unbeatable prices.

    Tip Bring cash and order at the counter.

Food and drink

Poland foods to try

Polish food is hearty and warming, with dumplings, soups, sausages and pickles, plus excellent cakes and vodka.

  • Pierogi

    Boiled dumplings filled with potato and cheese, meat, cabbage and mushroom, or fruit, served with fried onion or sour cream.

    Where to try it Milk bars and restaurants across the country.

  • Żurek

    A sour rye soup with sausage and egg, often served in a hollowed bread loaf.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants in Warsaw and Kraków.

  • Bigos

    A hunter's stew of sauerkraut, fresh cabbage and several meats, cooked slowly and often better on the second day.

    Where to try it Old-town restaurants.

  • Obwarzanek

    A ring-shaped bread, boiled and baked, sold from blue carts in Kraków and topped with poppy or sesame seeds.

    Where to try it Street stalls in Kraków.

  • Kiełbasa and oscypek

    Smoked sausage, and smoked sheep's cheese from the Tatras, grilled and served with cranberry sauce.

    Where to try it Markets and Zakopane.

  • Vodka and mead

    Polish vodka is served chilled with food, and honey mead (miód pitny) is a traditional favourite.

    Where to try it Bars and restaurants.

Before you go

Practical travel information for Poland

The essentials, current for 2026. Poland is safe, cheap and easy, though card acceptance and language can vary outside cities.

Capital
Warsaw
Language
Polish. English is widely spoken by younger people and in tourism, less so among older people and in small towns.
Currency
Polish złoty (PLN, zł). Roughly 3.6–4.3 złoty to the US dollar in recent years. Poland is in the EU but not the eurozone.
Cards and cash
Cards and contactless payments are accepted almost everywhere, including small shops. Use bank ATMs and decline dynamic currency conversion. Carry some cash for markets and small towns.
Entry and visas
Poland is in the EU and the Schengen Area. Many nationalities, including the United Kingdom, United States, Canada and Australia, can stay up to 90 days in any 180 without a visa. The EU's ETIAS authorisation has been planned, so check current rules.
Power sockets
Type C and E (two round pins), 230 V, 50 Hz. UK and US visitors need an adaptor.
Emergency number
112 for all emergencies, 997 for police, 998 for fire and 999 for ambulance.
Tipping
About 10% in restaurants if service is not included, and rounding up for taxis.
Safety
Poland is generally very safe. Watch for pickpockets in crowded tourist areas and on trains, use licensed taxis or ride-hailing apps, and be careful with alcohol-fuelled nightlife. Check travel advice for areas near the Ukraine and Belarus borders.
Internet and SIM
Very good. EU roaming applies to EU SIMs, and prepaid SIMs are cheap. Free Wi-Fi is common in cafés.
Time zone
Central European Time, UTC+1, and UTC+2 in summer.

When to go

Best time to visit Poland

Poland has a continental climate, with warm summers, cold winters and pleasant springs and autumns.

  • Spring (April–May)

    Best time

    Mild and green, with fewer crowds and lower prices.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Best time

    Warm, with long days, Baltic beaches and mountain hiking. Busiest and most expensive.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Golden leaves and pleasant temperatures, with rain increasing.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Quiet

    Cold and grey, but Christmas markets are magical and the Tatras have skiing.

Worth planning around: November and February, which can be cold, wet and grey.

Trip length

How long to spend in Poland

Poland is large, but the main cities are well connected by fast trains. A week covers Kraków and Warsaw, and two weeks allows Gdańsk and the mountains.

  • 4–5 days

    Kraków with Auschwitz and Wieliczka.

    A first taste.

  • 8–10 days

    Kraków, Warsaw and Gdańsk.

    The classic short trip.

  • 12–14 days

    Our recommendation.

  • 3 weeks or more

    Adds Białowieża, Malbork and more of the countryside.

    For nature and slow travel.

Getting around

Transportation in Poland

Trains and buses are cheap and extensive. Fast trains link the big cities, and a car helps only in the countryside.

  • Express trains (PKP Intercity)

    Link Warsaw with Kraków in about 2.5 hours and Gdańsk in about 3 hours.

    Book on the PKP Intercity site or app.

  • Regional trains and buses

    Serve smaller towns and the Tatras. FlixBus and PolskiBus-style services are cheap.

    Check the timetables.

  • Domestic flights

    Rarely needed, but useful for the Baltic coast.

    Compare to the train.

  • Ride-hailing

    Uber and Bolt are cheap and safe in cities.

    Use the app.

  • Hire cars

    Useful for Białowieża and the countryside. Roads are good, but drivers can be aggressive.

    Avoid night driving in rural areas.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Poland

Poles are warm and hospitable once you get to know them, and formal in first meetings. Family, faith and history matter, and the wartime past is present, so it is best to approach it with respect.

  • Greet formally

    Use a handshake, and address people by title until invited to use first names.

  • Respect history

    Be sensitive about the war and the Holocaust, and avoid jokes at memorial sites.

  • Take off your shoes

    In a home, you may be offered slippers.

  • Bring a gift

    Flowers, an odd number, or chocolate if invited to a home.

  • Dress modestly in churches

    Many Poles are Catholic, and churches are active places of worship.

  • Toast properly

    Say 'Na zdrowie' (to your health) and make eye contact.

Do

  • Learn a few words: dzień dobry (good day), dziękuję (thank you), proszę (please)
  • Try milk bars and local food
  • Book Auschwitz and Wieliczka far ahead
  • Carry a little cash
  • Validate tickets on trams and buses
  • Respect quiet at memorials

Don't

  • Don't call it a 'Polish concentration camp'
  • Don't joke about the war
  • Don't jaywalk, since it can be fined
  • Don't drink in public parks
  • Don't assume everyone speaks English
  • Don't skip tram ticket validation

Travel tips

Poland travel tips

The things that most often catch visitors out in Poland, several of which are specific to how the country works.

  • Book Auschwitz-Birkenau tickets weeks ahead, especially in summer.
  • Use trains for the big cities, and buy tickets online in advance.
  • Eat in milk bars for cheap, traditional meals.
  • Validate tram and bus tickets as soon as you board.
  • Carry small notes for public toilets and markets.
  • Check shop opening hours, since many close on Sundays.
  • Bring layers, since weather can change quickly, especially in the Tatras.
  • Use Uber or Bolt rather than street taxis.

FAQ

Visiting Poland: frequently asked questions

Is Poland safe for tourists?

Yes, generally very safe. Watch for pickpockets in tourist areas.

How many days do you need in Poland?

Eight to fourteen days for a first trip, covering Kraków, Warsaw and Gdańsk.

Is Poland cheap?

Yes. It is one of the cheapest EU countries for travellers.

What is the best time to visit Poland?

May to September, with spring and early autumn offering fewer crowds.

Do I need a visa for Poland?

Many nationalities can visit visa-free for 90 days under Schengen rules. Check current rules, including ETIAS.

Can I use euros in Poland?

Not generally. Poland uses the złoty, so pay in local currency.
