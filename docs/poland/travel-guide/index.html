Poland combines rebuilt medieval cities, mountains, a Baltic coast and profound history at low prices. This guide covers how the regions divide, how to choose between the ten destinations, how to get around, when to go, what it costs and three routes that work.

How Poland divides

Poland is about 700 km across, and it falls into five zones for a visitor.

The south. Kraków is the heart of tourism, with Auschwitz-Birkenau nearby and the Tatras to the south.

The south-west. Wrocław is a lively riverside city of bridges and gnomes.

The centre and west. Warsaw, the capital, and Poznań have rebuilt old towns and a modern energy.

The north. Gdańsk on the Baltic, with Toruń and Malbork Castle nearby.

The east. Białowieża preserves an ancient forest and its bison.

Worth knowing: The practical route is a loop, Kraków to Wrocław to Warsaw to Gdańsk, using fast trains.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If you have one week

    Kraków with Auschwitz, then Warsaw by train.

  • If you love history

    Kraków, Warsaw, Gdańsk and Malbork cover the medieval to the modern.

  • If you love the outdoors

    The Tatras for mountains and Białowieża for forest and bison.

  • If you love cities and nightlife

    Wrocław, Poznań and Gdańsk all have student energy and good bars.

  • If you like fairy-tale towns

    Toruń has a gingerbread tradition and a medieval core, and Malbork has the huge brick castle.

  • If you want a reflective visit

    Auschwitz-Birkenau and Warsaw&#39;s Jewish and uprising museums tell the story of the war.

Getting around

Poland's rail network is good on main lines and cheap.

Express trains. Intercity and EIP trains link Kraków, Warsaw, Gdańsk, Wrocław and Poznań. Reserve in advance.

Regional trains and buses. Serve smaller towns. Buses are best for Zakopane.

Ride-hailing. Uber and Bolt are cheap.

Cars. Useful for Białowieża and the countryside.

Worth knowing: Validate tickets when boarding trams and buses, or risk a fine.

When to go

Poland has four seasons, and the shoulder months are the best value.

April and May are spring, mild and green.

June to August is summer, warm, with festivals, beaches and mountain hikes. Prices peak.

September and October are autumn, golden and calm.

November to March is winter, cold and grey, with Christmas markets and skiing near Zakopane.

Worth knowing: Snow can stay in the Tatras until June.

Money, costs and safety

Poland is very good value. A restaurant meal costs US$8–20, a mid-range hotel US$70–130, and a long-distance train ticket US$10–30.

Cards are widely accepted. Tipping about 10% is normal in restaurants.

Crime is low. Keep an eye out for pickpockets in crowded places.

Shops often close on Sundays, so plan your shopping.

Three routes that work

  1. One week: Kraków and Warsaw

    Four nights in Kraków with a day at Auschwitz, then three in Warsaw.

    Tip Take the express train between them.

  2. Ten days: adding the coast

    The one-week route, then Gdańsk for three nights with a trip to Malbork.

    Tip Book trains early.

  3. Two weeks: the classic

    The itinerary on the country page: Kraków, the Tatras, Wrocław, Poznań, Warsaw, Toruń and Gdańsk.

    Tip Swap Poznań for Białowieża if you love nature.

Gdańsk

Pomerania, Baltic coast

A Baltic port with a reconstructed Main Town, the Neptune Fountain, St. Mary's Church, the Crane, the European Solidarity Centre, the Museum of the Second World War and beaches at Sopot.

Don't miss The Long Market at dusk, with its ornate gables, Neptune's Fountain and the Motława waterfront glowing beyond.

Lower Silesia, south-western Poland

A lively riverside city with a pastel Market Square, Ostrów Tumski cathedral island, the UNESCO-listed Centennial Hall, the Racławice Panorama and a trail of gnome statues.

Don't miss Hunting for the city's little bronze gnomes across the colourful Market Square.

Lesser Poland, Tatra Mountains

The gateway to the Polish Tatras, with the carved-wood Zakopane style, Morskie Oko lake, the Kasprowy Wierch cable car, Giewont, hiking, skiing and smoked oscypek cheese.

Don't miss Morskie Oko, a glacial lake ringed by granite peaks, reached by an easy walk from the road.

Lesser Poland, Oświęcim

The Auschwitz-Birkenau Memorial and Museum, a UNESCO World Heritage Site near Kraków, where over a million people, most of them Jews, were murdered between 1940 and 1945.

Don't miss Standing at the gate of Birkenau, a place that asks visitors to remember and reflect.

Poznań

Greater Poland, western Poland

A relaxed western city with a colourful Old Market Square, a Renaissance Town Hall and its famous goats, Ostrów Tumski cathedral, the Croissant Museum and a base for the first Polish capital at Gniezno.

Toruń

Kuyavia-Pomerania, northern Poland

A UNESCO-listed medieval town on the Vistula with a Gothic Old Town Hall, Copernicus's birthplace, brick churches and a gingerbread tradition dating back to the Middle Ages.

A UNESCO-listed Teutonic castle of red brick, the largest castle in the world by land area, with three sections, a Grand Master's palace, museums and an easy day trip from Gdańsk.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Poland

Which Polish cities should a first-timer see?

Kraków and Warsaw are the essentials, and Gdańsk adds the Baltic coast. Wrocław and Poznań are excellent if you have more time.

Is it easy to travel by train?

Yes. Express trains link the main cities in two to four hours and are cheap. Book online in advance for the best fares.

How far ahead should I book Auschwitz?

Several weeks in summer. Entry is by timed ticket, and slots sell out.

Is Poland good in winter?

It is cold, but Christmas markets and skiing in the Tatras are big draws, and prices are lower.
