Welcome to Telč

Telč is one of the Czech Republic's most complete Renaissance towns, a small settlement on the Bohemia-Moravia border built almost entirely around one extraordinary square.

That square's gabled, arcaded houses date largely from a 16th-century rebuilding after a fire, giving the whole town centre a rare architectural unity.

The old town sits on a narrow strip of land between two artificial ponds, a defensive design choice that today makes for some of the country's most photographed reflections.

Why visit

Why visit Telč?

Telč's case rests on one of the most architecturally unified squares anywhere in Central Europe.

  • The main square is astonishingly complete

    Renaissance and baroque gabled houses line the entire square in near-unbroken harmony.

  • The setting is striking

    The old town sits on a narrow strip between two ponds, doubling as reflections in still water.

  • Telč Castle is an easy, worthwhile addition

    A Renaissance chateau attached directly to the square itself.

  • It's far less crowded than Český Krumlov

    A similarly beautiful UNESCO town with a fraction of the visitors.

  • It works well as a Moravia gateway

    A natural stop on the way between Prague and Brno or South Moravia.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Telč

Telč's essential sights are concentrated on and around its single extraordinary square.

  1. 01

    Náměstí Zachariáše z Hradce (the main square)

    One of the most architecturally unified squares in Central Europe, lined edge to edge with gabled arcaded houses.

    Tip Visit early morning or evening, when the square is at its quietest and best for photos.

  2. 02

    Telč Castle

    A Renaissance chateau built directly onto the square, its interiors among the finest of their kind in the country.

    Tip Book the guided tour, the only way to see the castle's period-furnished rooms.

  3. 03

    The square's Renaissance gables

    Worth a slow look, each gable subtly different from its neighbours after the town's 16th-century rebuilding.

    Tip Walk the arcades on both sides of the square to see the full variety.

  4. 04

    The view from St James's Church tower

    The best view in town, looking straight down the square and out over the surrounding ponds.

    Tip Worth the climb for photographers especially — the layout below makes real sense from above.

  5. 05

    The ponds surrounding the old town

    The town's original defensive feature, artificial ponds that now double as the source of its best reflections.

    Tip Walk the path around at least one pond for the classic reflected view.

  6. 06

    The castle's arcaded courtyard

    A beautiful Renaissance courtyard, arcaded on multiple levels around a central space.

    Tip Free to enter even without the interior tour ticket.

  7. 07

    The old town reflected in the water

    The town's signature photograph, gabled rooftops mirrored perfectly on a still day.

    Tip Calmest water and best reflections come early morning, before any wind picks up.

Things to do

Best things to do in Telč

Telč rewards an unhurried few hours, best spent on and around its remarkable square.

  1. Wander the square's arcades

    An essential first stop, walking beneath the covered arcades on both sides of the square.

    Tip Look for small cafés and shops tucked beneath the arcades themselves.

  2. Tour Telč Castle's interior

    A worthwhile hour, taking in the chateau's Renaissance halls and period furnishings.

    Tip A couple of different tour routes are offered — check which rooms each one covers.

  3. Climb St James's Church tower

    A rewarding climb, with the best overview of the square and surrounding ponds.

    Tip Check opening hours ahead — the tower doesn't always keep the same schedule as the square below.

  4. Walk the path around the ponds

    A peaceful way to spend half an hour, circling the water for the classic reflected view.

    Tip A nice way to see the town from outside its own walls.

  5. Photograph the gabled facades at golden hour

    A rewarding time to visit, warm evening light picking out every colour on the square's facades.

    Tip Stay for sunset if your schedule allows — the light here is special.

  6. Relax at a café beneath the arcades

    A pleasant way to slow down, watching the square from a shaded arcade table.

    Tip A nice break between the castle tour and the church tower climb.

Local food

What to eat in Telč

Telč's food means simple, reliable Czech classics, best enjoyed at a café table on the square itself.

  • Svíčková (marinated beef in cream sauce)

    A reliable classic, found at restaurants right on the main square.

    Where to try it Restaurants around Náměstí Zachariáše z Hradce.

  • Guláš with dumplings

    A warming, satisfying lunch between sightseeing stops.

    Where to try it Restaurants and pubs around the square.

  • Smažený sýr (fried cheese)

    A satisfying, inexpensive option for a quick lunch.

    Where to try it Local restaurants around the square.

  • Czech beer (pivo)

    A pleasant way to relax at an arcade café table after a morning of sightseeing.

    Where to try it Cafés and restaurants around the square.

  • Koláče (fruit-filled pastries)

    A nice sweet stop, found at bakeries just off the main square.

    Where to try it Bakeries near Náměstí Zachariáše z Hradce.

  • Trdelník (chimney cake)

    Available from a stall or two in season, though less common here than in Prague.

    Where to try it Occasional seasonal stalls near the square.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Telč

Most visitors see Telč as a half-day or full-day stop, though a night on the square itself is memorable.

  • On the main square

    A handful of small guesthouses occupy historic buildings right on the square.

    Best for: Being inside the postcard view itself

  • Just outside the old town

    Simple guesthouses within easy walking distance of the square.

    Best for: A better-value stay a short walk from the centre

Transport

Getting to and around Telč

Telč is a manageable stop between Prague and South Moravia, though it takes some planning by public transport.

Getting there

  • Train or bus from Prague

    Doable in around 2.5 to 3 hours, often with one change.

    Direct buses run on some schedules; check connections ahead.

  • Train or bus from Brno

    A shorter, easier option if arriving from Moravia.

    Around 1.5 to 2 hours, depending on the connection.

  • Car or private driver

    The most flexible option, especially combined with other Bohemia-Moravia stops.

    About two hours from either Prague or Brno.

Getting around

Telč's tiny historic centre is entirely walkable, with nothing more than a car needed to arrive.

  • On foot

    The only way to see the town — it's small enough to cover in under an hour.

    The square, castle and ponds all sit within a few minutes' walk of each other.

  • Walking the pond paths

    Easy and well maintained.

    A short loop around either pond takes 20 to 30 minutes.

When to go

Best time to visit Telč

Telč is a year-round destination, though photographers should aim for calm mornings for the best pond reflections.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Mild, comfortable weather with good light for photography.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    The busiest season, though the square rarely feels as crowded as Prague or Český Krumlov.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Golden light and calm mornings, ideal for photographing the ponds.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Quiet and atmospheric, though some interiors reduce their hours.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Telč stays pleasant and uncrowded across most of the year.

Itinerary

One day in Telč

A single relaxed day covering the square, castle and ponds.

  1. Day1

    The square and castle

    A full but unhurried day in Telč.

    • Morning Wander the main square and its arcades.
    • Afternoon Tour Telč Castle, then climb St James's Church tower.
    • Evening Walk the pond path at golden hour before dinner on the square.

Travel tips

Telč travel tips

Practical notes that make a Telč visit noticeably smoother.

  • Visit early morning or evening for the square at its quietest.
  • Check bus and train connections ahead — Telč sits a little off the main rail lines.
  • Book the castle interior tour a little ahead in peak summer season.
  • Walk at least one full loop around a pond for the classic reflected view.
  • Combine Telč with a Prague-to-Brno or South Moravia route for an easy stop.
  • Bring a camera — this is one of the country's most photogenic towns.

FAQ

Telč: frequently asked questions

Is Telč worth visiting?

Yes — its main square is one of the most architecturally complete Renaissance squares in Central Europe.

How do you get to Telč?

By train or bus from Prague or Brno, both taking a few hours, or more flexibly by car.

How many days do you need in Telč?

One day covers the town comfortably; it works well as a stop between Prague and Brno rather than a standalone base.

Is Telč less crowded than Český Krumlov?

Yes, noticeably — it sees a fraction of Český Krumlov's visitor numbers despite similarly remarkable architecture.

Is Telč Castle worth touring inside?

Yes — its Renaissance interiors are among the finest of their kind in the Czech Republic.

Can you combine Telč with other Moravia stops?

Yes, easily — it sits conveniently between Prague, Brno and the wider Vysočina and Moravia region.
