Why go to Czech Republic

The Czech Republic is genuinely one of Central Europe's most rewarding destinations, its capital Prague among the continent's most beautiful and best-preserved historic cities, its hundred spires largely untouched by the wars that levelled so much of the region.

Beyond Prague, the country genuinely rewards slower exploration — fairy-tale towns like Český Krumlov, spa resorts like Karlovy Vary, and a Moravian region in the east with its own wine culture, dialect and noticeably slower pace.

It's also genuinely a country built for beer, home to the world's first pale lager and a pub culture that runs through daily life as a genuine social institution rather than sitting apart from it.

Experiences

Best things to do in Czech Republic

The experiences that recur across the country and make a Czech Republic trip feel like more than a checklist of castles.

  1. Drink a proper Czech pilsner

    A genuinely essential Czech experience, in the country that invented pale lager and still drinks more beer per capita than anywhere else on Earth.

    Tip Genuinely order by the round in a proper pub — a server will keep bringing beers until you place a coaster over your glass.

  2. Watch the Prague Astronomical Clock strike the hour

    A genuinely medieval spectacle, the clock's procession of apostle figures still drawing a crowd every hour, just as it has since 1410.

    Tip Genuinely the view is better than the show itself — watch from a café terrace rather than fighting the crowd below.

  3. Soak up a thermal spa town's colonnades

    A genuinely elegant tradition, sipping mineral spring water from a spa cup while strolling ornate 19th-century colonnades.

    Tip Genuinely buy a lázeňský oplatek (spa wafer) from a colonnade kiosk to go with the mineral water.

  4. Wander a fairy-tale old town away from Prague's crowds

    A genuinely different pace from the capital, Bohemia's smaller UNESCO towns offering the same architecture with a fraction of the visitors.

    Tip Genuinely stay overnight if you can — day-trippers clear out by early evening, leaving these towns wonderfully quiet.

  5. Taste Moravian wine straight from a village cellar

    A genuinely different side of Czech drinking culture, South Moravia's rolling vineyards producing wine most visitors never expect to find here.

    Tip Genuinely rent a bicycle to tour several village cellars along the region's marked wine trails.

  6. Explore a country castle or chateau garden

    A genuinely rewarding day out, the Czech countryside scattered with more castles and chateaux per square kilometre than almost anywhere in Europe.

    Tip Genuinely book interior tours ahead in summer — the most famous chateaux sell out their guided time slots.

Food and drink

Czech Republic foods to try

Czech food genuinely means hearty, warming classics built around meat, dumplings and, of course, beer.

  • Svíčková (marinated beef in cream sauce)

    Genuinely the national dish for many Czechs, tender beef in a vegetable cream sauce, served with bread dumplings and a dollop of cranberry sauce.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout the country.

  • Guláš with dumplings

    Genuinely a Czech take on goulash, a rich, paprika-spiced beef stew mopped up with sliced bread dumplings.

    Where to try it Pubs and traditional restaurants nationwide.

  • Trdelník (chimney cake)

    Genuinely more a Prague tourist-street tradition than an old Czech recipe, a sugar-and-cinnamon pastry cooked on a rotating spit.

    Where to try it Street stalls throughout central Prague's tourist areas.

  • Czech beer (pivo)

    Genuinely the country's defining drink, from world-famous Pilsner Urquell to countless excellent small regional breweries.

    Where to try it Pubs and breweries in every town, large and small.

  • Smažený sýr (fried cheese)

    Genuinely a beloved pub classic, breaded and deep-fried cheese served with tartar sauce and fries or potatoes.

    Where to try it Pubs throughout the country.

  • Koláče (fruit-filled pastries)

    Genuinely a homely regional favourite, soft yeasted pastry rounds topped with fruit, poppyseed or sweet cheese.

    Where to try it Bakeries and cafés throughout Bohemia and Moravia.

When to go

Best time to visit Czech Republic

The Czech Republic genuinely has warm, long days in summer and a magical Christmas market season, with the shoulder months offering the best overall value.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely mild temperatures, blooming parks and manageable crowds before peak summer arrives.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Genuinely the warmest, busiest season, with long evenings perfect for riverside terraces, though central Prague gets genuinely crowded.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Genuinely golden light over the castles and vineyards, with noticeably thinner crowds than summer.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Genuinely magical around Christmas markets and snow-dusted spires, though short days and cold call for proper layers.

Worth planning around: Genuinely nothing strongly, though August's heat and crowds in central Prague are worth planning around.

Getting around

Transportation in Czech Republic

Genuinely one of Europe's best rail networks for a country this size, with buses filling in the gaps.

  • Trains (České dráhy and private operators)

    Genuinely the backbone of Czech travel between Prague, Brno, Olomouc and Plzeň.

    Frequent, comfortable and inexpensive compared to Western Europe.

  • RegioJet and FlixBus coaches

    Genuinely a comfortable, budget-friendly alternative on popular routes.

    Often cheaper than the train, with onboard snacks and entertainment on RegioJet.

  • Organised day trips from Prague

    Genuinely the easiest way to reach Český Krumlov or Kutná Hora without a car.

    Widely available and reasonably priced, though a direct train or bus is often just as easy.

  • Renting a car

    Genuinely useful for Bohemian Switzerland and Lednice-Valtice's wine country.

    Public transport reaches both, but a car gives far more flexibility.

  • Prague's public transport

    Genuinely excellent — trams, metro and buses all run on one unified ticket.

    The easiest way to get around the capital itself.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Czech Republic

Czech culture genuinely blends a deep literary and musical tradition with a famously dry, understated sense of humour and one of the world's great beer cultures.

  • Greetings are genuinely polite but reserved

    A firm handshake and direct eye contact are standard; save hugs for people you know well.

  • Beer culture runs genuinely deep

    Ordering by the round and saying 'na zdraví' (cheers) before drinking is expected pub etiquette.

  • Punctuality is genuinely valued

    Arrive on time for both social plans and reserved restaurant tables.

  • Dress is genuinely practical and modest

    Smart-casual works almost everywhere; only the fanciest restaurants expect more.

  • Small talk is genuinely kept brief

    Czechs tend to be direct and reserved with strangers, warming up considerably once you know them.

  • Tipping is genuinely modest but expected

    Round up or add about 10% in restaurants, telling the server the total rather than leaving cash on the table.

Do

  • Say 'dobrý den' (good day) when entering small shops, cafés and pubs.
  • Validate public transport tickets before boarding — inspectors do check, and fines are steep.
  • Try a proper neighbourhood pub over a touristy one near major squares for better beer and prices.
  • Book Prague Castle's interiors and popular restaurants ahead in peak summer months.

Don't

  • Don't expect tap water automatically at restaurants — ask, or you may be served, and charged for, bottled.
  • Don't jaywalk carelessly — Czech drivers expect pedestrians to use marked crossings.
  • Don't block a tram or metro doorway — step fully inside to let others board.
  • Don't rush Prague's Old Town at the expense of Bohemia and Moravia beyond it.

Travel tips

Czech Republic travel tips

Practical notes that make travel through the Czech Republic noticeably smoother.

  • Buy genuinely a Prague public transport ticket or pass rather than risk an unvalidated-ticket fine.
  • Carry genuinely some cash for small pubs, markets and rural areas, even though cards are widely accepted.
  • Book genuinely Prague Castle's interiors and popular restaurants ahead in summer.
  • Learn genuinely a few basic Czech phrases — English is widely spoken in Prague but less so in smaller towns.
  • Pack genuinely comfortable shoes — most historic centres are best explored on foot over cobblestones.
  • Avoid genuinely currency exchange booths advertising 'no commission' — compare rates before handing over cash.
  • Try genuinely a lunch menu (denní menu) for the best-value midday meals at sit-down restaurants.
  • Consider genuinely basing yourself in Brno for a few days to explore Moravia at an easier pace.

FAQ

Visiting Czech Republic: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in the Czech Republic?

Seven to ten days genuinely covers Prague plus day trips and a stretch of Moravia; three to four days suits a Prague-only visit.

Is the Czech Republic on the euro?

No, genuinely not — it uses the Czech koruna (Kč), even though it's an EU member.

Is Prague expensive?

Genuinely more affordable than Western Europe overall, though central Prague's restaurants and attractions have crept close to Western prices in recent years.

Do you need a car to see the Czech Republic?

No, genuinely not — trains and buses cover the country well, though a car helps for Bohemian Switzerland and the Moravian wine country.

What's the difference between Bohemia and Moravia?

Genuinely the country's two historic regions — Bohemia in the west around Prague, Moravia in the east around Brno, with its own dialect, wine and traditions.

Is the Czech Republic safe?

Yes, genuinely — one of Europe's safer countries, with petty theft in busy Prague tourist spots the main thing to watch for.
