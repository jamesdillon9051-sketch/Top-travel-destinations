Welcome to Brno

Brno is genuinely the Czech Republic's second city and Moravia's capital, a lively university town that most Prague-focused visitors skip entirely, to their own loss.

Its sights genuinely range from a hilltop fortress and a mummified monastic crypt to one of the most influential private houses of the 20th century, the UNESCO-listed Villa Tugendhat.

It's also genuinely South Moravia's natural gateway, well placed for day trips into the region's wine country and smaller historic towns.

Why visit

Why visit Brno?

Brno's case rests genuinely on an unusually varied set of sights for a city that receives a fraction of Prague's visitors.

  • Villa Tugendhat is genuinely a landmark of modern architecture

    A UNESCO-listed 1930 house that helped define modernism worldwide.

  • The Capuchin Crypt is genuinely a singular, startling sight

    Naturally mummified monks and nobles, preserved by the crypt's own ventilation system.

  • Špilberk Castle is genuinely a real hilltop fortress

    Centuries as both a stronghold and a notorious political prison.

  • It's genuinely a lively, youthful city

    A large student population keeps Brno's bars, cafés and nightlife genuinely buzzing.

  • It works genuinely well as a Moravia base

    Close to Telč, Lednice-Valtice and the wider South Moravian wine country.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Brno

Brno's essential sights are genuinely more varied than its low profile among visitors would suggest.

  1. 01

    Špilberk Castle

    Genuinely a hilltop fortress with a dark history as one of the Habsburg Empire's most feared political prisons.

    Tip Genuinely walk the castle walls for one of the best panoramic views over the city.

  2. 02

    Cathedral of St Peter and Paul (Petrov)

    Genuinely Brno's defining skyline landmark, its twin neo-Gothic spires visible across the city.

    Tip Genuinely listen for the noon bells, which famously ring an hour 'early' to commemorate a local legend.

  3. 03

    Villa Tugendhat

    Genuinely one of the most influential private houses of the 20th century, designed by Ludwig Mies van der Rohe in 1930.

    Tip Genuinely book the tour weeks ahead — timed entry slots are limited and sell out.

  4. 04

    Freedom Square (náměstí Svobody)

    Genuinely the city's social heart, a triangular square ringed by Renaissance and Art Nouveau facades.

    Tip Genuinely check for one of the square's regular markets or events during your visit.

  5. 05

    The Capuchin Crypt

    Genuinely a startling sight, monks and local nobles naturally mummified by the crypt's own ventilation system.

    Tip Genuinely a small, quick visit — allow 20 to 30 minutes.

  6. 06

    Brno's Astronomical Clock

    Genuinely a striking modern black granite obelisk on Freedom Square, releasing a glass marble each day at noon.

    Tip Genuinely arrive at noon and watch the crowd scramble to catch the falling marble.

  7. 07

    The view from Špilberk's walls

    Genuinely one of the city's best viewpoints, taking in Petrov's spires and the rooftops below.

    Tip Genuinely a lovely spot for sunset, with the cathedral lit up as the light fades.

Things to do

Best things to do in Brno

Brno genuinely rewards a full day or two, mixing fortress history with modernist architecture and a lively café scene.

  1. Tour Špilberk Castle's casemates

    A genuinely fascinating, slightly chilling tour through the fortress's former prison cells.

    Tip Genuinely combine with the castle museum for the fuller history of the site.

  2. Climb Petrov Cathedral's towers

    A genuinely rewarding climb, one of the best views over Brno's rooftops and surrounding hills.

    Tip Genuinely check the tower's separate opening hours before making the trip up Petrov Hill.

  3. Visit the Capuchin Crypt

    A genuinely memorable, if slightly eerie, half-hour taking in the crypt's naturally preserved remains.

    Tip Genuinely treat it with the same quiet respect as the Sedlec Ossuary — this is a real burial site.

  4. Tour Villa Tugendhat

    A genuinely essential visit for anyone interested in architecture, walking through a house that shaped modernism worldwide.

    Tip Genuinely book the 'basic' tour first if slots for the 'extended' version aren't available — both cover the key rooms.

  5. Watch the Astronomical Clock's noon marble

    A genuinely fun, low-key local tradition, a small crowd gathering to catch the clock's daily glass marble.

    Tip Genuinely arrive a few minutes before noon to get a good spot.

  6. Wander Freedom Square and its cafés

    A genuinely relaxed way to spend an afternoon, taking in the square's mix of historic and modern architecture.

    Tip Genuinely Brno's café culture rivals Prague's, with noticeably fewer tourists competing for a table.

Local food

What to eat in Brno

Brno's food genuinely reflects its Moravian setting, with local wine standing in for Bohemia's beer-first culture.

  • Svíčková (marinated beef in cream sauce)

    Genuinely a reliable classic, found at traditional restaurants throughout the city.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants around Freedom Square and the old town.

  • Guláš with dumplings

    Genuinely a warming, filling classic, common on menus throughout the city.

    Where to try it Traditional pubs throughout central Brno.

  • Moravian wine

    Genuinely a defining part of Brno's identity as Moravia's capital, with excellent regional wine on nearly every wine bar's list.

    Where to try it Wine bars throughout the city centre.

  • Starobrno beer

    Genuinely the city's own brewery, a solid Moravian alternative to Bohemia's more famous pilsners.

    Where to try it Pubs and beer halls throughout central Brno.

  • Smažený sýr (fried cheese)

    Genuinely a satisfying, inexpensive pub classic, just as popular here as in Bohemia.

    Where to try it Traditional pubs throughout the city.

  • Koláče (fruit-filled pastries)

    Genuinely a homely sweet treat, found at bakeries and cafés across the city.

    Where to try it Bakeries and cafés throughout central Brno.

Transport

Getting to and around Brno

Brno is genuinely well connected to both Prague and Vienna, making it an easy addition to a wider Central Europe trip.

Getting there

  • Train from Prague

    Genuinely fast and frequent, taking about 2.5 hours.

    Direct high-speed services run throughout the day.

  • Bus from Prague

    Genuinely a comfortable, budget-friendly alternative.

    RegioJet and FlixBus both run frequent services, similar in journey time to the train.

  • Train or bus from Vienna

    Genuinely an easy cross-border option.

    Takes under two hours, making Brno an easy Central Europe add-on.

Getting around

Brno's genuinely compact centre is easily walkable, with trams covering the rest of the city well.

  • On foot

    Genuinely the best way to see the old town's main sights.

    Petrov, Freedom Square and the crypt all sit within a comfortable walk of each other.

  • Trams

    Genuinely useful for reaching Špilberk Castle or Villa Tugendhat.

    A frequent, easy-to-use network covering the wider city.

  • Taxi or rideshare app

    Genuinely useful late at night or for luggage.

    Widely available and inexpensive compared to Prague.
