Welcome to Lednice-Valtice

Lednice-Valtice is one of Europe's largest deliberately landscaped areas, a UNESCO-listed cultural landscape connecting two aristocratic chateaux across 200 square kilometres of parkland and vineyards.

Its fairy-tale centrepiece is Lednice Chateau, a neo-Gothic palace surrounded by an English-style park scattered with follies, including a striking Ottoman-style minaret.

The wider region is also the heart of Moravian wine country, a landscape of rolling vineyards and small villages built around family-run wine cellars.

Why visit

Why visit Lednice-Valtice?

Lednice-Valtice's case rests on an extraordinary landscaped estate paired with the best of Moravian wine country.

  • Lednice Chateau is a fairy-tale palace

    A neo-Gothic showpiece surrounded by one of Europe's largest English-style landscaped parks.

  • The Minaret is a delightful surprise

    A 60-metre Ottoman-style tower rising incongruously from the Moravian countryside.

  • It's the heart of Moravian wine country

    Rolling vineyards and village cellars producing wine most visitors never expect to find in the Czech Republic.

  • The scale is built for slow exploration

    Cycling paths connect the chateaux, follies and surrounding wine villages.

  • It works well as a Brno day trip or overnight stay

    Close enough for a day, rewarding enough to justify staying longer.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Lednice-Valtice

The region's essential sights are spread across two chateaux, their shared parkland and the surrounding vineyards.

  1. 01

    Lednice Chateau

    The region's fairy-tale centrepiece, a neo-Gothic palace remodelled in the 19th century for the powerful Liechtenstein family.

    Tip Book the interior tour ahead in summer — time slots do sell out.

  2. 02

    The Lednice Minaret

    One of the park's most surprising follies, a 60-metre Ottoman-style tower with a viewing platform at the top.

    Tip Climb it for one of the best views over the entire landscaped park.

  3. 03

    The Lednice Castle Gardens

    One of the largest English-style landscaped parks in Europe, dotted with follies, temples and a glasshouse.

    Tip Rent a bicycle to properly cover the park's scale.

  4. 04

    Valtice Chateau

    The Liechtenstein family's former main residence, a grand baroque chateau at the estate's southern end.

    Tip Pair a visit with the Salon of Wines in the chateau's cellars, showcasing the country's best vintages.

  5. 05

    The Reistna Colonnade

    A grand neoclassical colonnade on a hilltop near Valtice, built purely as a scenic folly with sweeping views.

    Tip A rewarding short walk or cycle from Valtice itself.

  6. 06

    The surrounding vineyards

    The backbone of the local economy, rolling vineyard hills scattered with small wine-producing villages.

    Tip Several villages have observation towers offering a wide view over the vineyard landscape.

  7. 07

    The pond in Lednice's park

    A peaceful spot within the wider park, reflecting the surrounding follies and mature trees.

    Tip A lovely stop partway around a longer park walk or cycle.

Things to do

Best things to do in Lednice-Valtice

The region rewards slow travel, best covered by bicycle between its chateaux and wine villages.

  1. Tour Lednice Chateau's interior

    An essential visit, taking in the chateau's ornately carved neo-Gothic interiors.

    Tip Several different tour routes cover different parts of the chateau — pick based on your interest and time.

  2. Climb the Minaret for the view

    A rewarding short climb, with sweeping views over the park and surrounding countryside.

    Tip Combine with a chateau visit — both sit within the same park.

  3. Walk or cycle the Castle Gardens

    A relaxed way to spend a few hours, exploring the park's follies, glasshouse and quiet paths.

    Tip A bicycle covers the park's scale far more comfortably than walking alone.

  4. Tour Valtice Chateau

    A worthwhile stop, the Liechtenstein family's grander baroque residence at the estate's other end.

    Tip Combine with a wine tasting at the Salon of Wines in the chateau's cellars.

  5. Cycle the wine trails between villages

    A lovely way to spend an afternoon, pedalling quiet lanes between vineyard villages.

    Tip Well-marked wine trails connect several villages worth stopping in along the way.

  6. Taste wine at a village cellar

    An essential regional experience, tasting local wine directly from the family that produced it.

    Tip Many cellars welcome walk-ins, though calling ahead for a proper tasting is worth the effort.

Local food

What to eat in Lednice-Valtice

Food here centres on Moravian wine country cooking, best enjoyed alongside a glass of the local vintage.

  • Moravian wine

    The whole point of a visit here, this region producing the great majority of Czech wine.

    Where to try it Village wine cellars throughout Lednice, Valtice and the surrounding area.

  • Svíčková (marinated beef in cream sauce)

    A reliable classic, found at restaurants in both Lednice and Valtice.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Lednice and Valtice town centres.

  • Guláš with dumplings

    A warming option, best after a day of cycling between the region's villages.

    Where to try it Local restaurants throughout the region.

  • Grilled meats at a wine cellar

    A common pairing, simple grilled sausage or meat served alongside a wine tasting.

    Where to try it Village wine cellars, especially those with outdoor seating.

  • Smažený sýr (fried cheese)

    A satisfying, inexpensive option away from the more wine-focused menus.

    Where to try it Local restaurants in Lednice and Valtice.

  • Koláče (fruit-filled pastries)

    A nice sweet stop between vineyard visits, found at local bakeries.

    Where to try it Bakeries in Lednice and Valtice.

Where to stay in Lednice-Valtice

The region offers everything from simple guesthouses to wine-focused boutique stays among the vineyards.

Transport

Getting to and around Lednice-Valtice

Lednice-Valtice is an easy day trip or overnight stop from Brno, less straightforward directly from Prague.

Getting there

  • Bus or car from Brno

    The easiest route, taking about 45 minutes to an hour.

    A natural add-on to a Brno-based South Moravia trip.

  • Train to Břeclav, then local bus

    Workable from Prague, with a change partway.

    Takes around three hours total from Prague.

  • Car or private driver

    The most practical option for covering the wider wine region.

    About 45 minutes from Brno, or three hours from Prague.

Getting around

The region covers a wide area, making a bicycle the classic and most practical way to explore it.

  • Bicycle

    The best way to see the region, with well-marked cycling paths throughout.

    Rental widely available in both Lednice and Valtice.

  • On foot within Lednice's park

    Fine for the chateau and immediate park area.

    The wider estate is too large to cover comfortably on foot alone.

  • Car

    Useful for reaching more distant wine villages.

    Roads throughout the region are well maintained and quiet.

When to go

Best time to visit Lednice-Valtice

The region is at its best in the warmer months, when the parks are in bloom and wine cellars are in full swing.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Mild, comfortable weather for cycling between the chateaux and villages.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Warm and lively, with vineyards at their most lush.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Harvest season, with fresh wine (burčák) available and vineyards turning gold.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Quiet, with some chateau interiors closed for the season.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly, though winter closures at the chateaux are worth checking ahead.

Itinerary

Two days in Lednice-Valtice

A relaxed pace across the region's two chateaux and surrounding wine villages.

  1. Day1

    Lednice Chateau and park

    The chateau, Minaret and landscaped gardens.

    • Morning Tour Lednice Chateau's interior.
    • Afternoon Cycle the Castle Gardens and climb the Minaret.
    • Evening Dinner in Lednice village.
  2. Day2

    Valtice and the wine villages

    A day of chateau architecture and wine tasting.

    • Morning Tour Valtice Chateau and the Salon of Wines.
    • Afternoon Cycle between wine villages for tastings.
    • Evening A final dinner paired with local Moravian wine.

Travel tips

Lednice-Valtice travel tips

Practical notes that make a Lednice-Valtice visit noticeably smoother.

  • Rent a bicycle on arrival — the estate's scale rewards it.
  • Book chateau interior tours ahead in peak summer season.
  • Call ahead for a proper wine cellar tasting rather than just walking in.
  • Pack a picnic for the park — options inside are limited compared to the villages.
  • Visit in September or October for the region's wine harvest season.
  • Base yourself in Brno if you'd rather day-trip than stay locally.

FAQ

Lednice-Valtice: frequently asked questions

Is Lednice-Valtice worth visiting?

Yes — it's one of Europe's largest landscaped cultural sites, and the heart of the country's wine-producing region.

How do you get to Lednice-Valtice?

Easiest from Brno by bus or car, taking under an hour; from Prague it's a longer trip via Břeclav.

How many days do you need?

One day covers Lednice Chateau and its park; two days allows Valtice and proper wine tasting time too.

Is this a good destination for non-drinkers?

Yes — the chateaux, park follies and cycling routes are all worthwhile without any wine tasting at all.

Do you need a car to visit?

No, not — a bicycle covers the core area well, though a car helps for more distant wine villages.

Can you combine this with Brno?

Yes, easily — it's a natural day trip or overnight add-on to a Brno-based Moravia itinerary.
