Welcome to Kutná Hora

Kutná Hora is one of Bohemia's most rewarding day trips from Prague, a once-fabulously wealthy medieval town built on one of Europe's richest silver deposits.

That wealth built an extraordinary Gothic cathedral rivalling Prague's own, alongside a royal mint that once produced coinage used across Central Europe.

Its most famous sight is far stranger — the Sedlec Ossuary, a small chapel decorated entirely with the bones of tens of thousands of people, arranged into chandeliers, garlands and a coat of arms.

Why visit

Why visit Kutná Hora?

Kutná Hora's case rests on extraordinary Gothic architecture paired with one of Europe's strangest, most memorable sights.

  • The Sedlec Ossuary is unlike anywhere else

    A small chapel decorated entirely in human bone, arranged with genuine artistry rather than morbid excess.

  • St Barbara's Cathedral rivals Prague's own Gothic churches

    Flying buttresses and soaring vaults built on the wealth of the town's silver mines.

  • It's an easy, well-connected day trip

    A direct train from Prague takes under an hour.

  • The history here is fascinating

    A medieval boomtown whose silver once funded much of the Bohemian kingdom.

  • It's far quieter than Prague itself

    A real, lived-in Czech town rather than a purely tourist-facing centre.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Kutná Hora

Kutná Hora's essential sights are spread between Sedlec's ossuary and the historic centre's Gothic core.

  1. 01

    Sedlec Ossuary

    The town's most famous and startling sight, a small chapel decorated with the bones of an estimated 40,000 to 70,000 people.

    Tip Book a timed-entry ticket online ahead — this small chapel gets busy with day-trippers.

  2. 02

    St Barbara's Cathedral

    One of Bohemia's most spectacular Gothic cathedrals, built by the town's silver miners' guild and dedicated to their patron saint.

    Tip Look up at the ribbed vaulting — it's considered one of the finest examples of late Gothic architecture anywhere.

  3. 03

    The Italian Court (Vlašský dvůr)

    The former royal mint and palace, where medieval Bohemia's silver groschen coins were struck.

    Tip Tour the interior to see the original minting workshop and royal chapel.

  4. 04

    Church of St James

    The town's tallest landmark, its single Gothic tower visible from across the historic centre.

    Tip Pair a visit with the neighbouring Italian Court, just steps away.

  5. 05

    The Stone Fountain

    A beautifully carved late-Gothic water reservoir, once part of the town's medieval water supply.

    Tip Easy to combine with a wander through the surrounding old town streets.

  6. 06

    The Hrádek Silver Mine Museum

    The place to understand the wealth behind the town, with tours descending into a genuine medieval mine shaft.

    Tip Wear warm layers — the underground tour is noticeably cooler than the surface.

  7. 07

    Kutná Hora's historic streets

    Worth simply wandering, a compact UNESCO-listed old town of Gothic and baroque facades with far fewer crowds than Prague.

    Tip Allow time to wander without a fixed plan between the main sights.

Things to do

Best things to do in Kutná Hora

Kutná Hora rewards a full day, split between Sedlec's ossuary and the historic centre proper.

  1. Visit the Sedlec Ossuary

    An essential, slightly eerie highlight, the small chapel's bone chandelier the most photographed detail.

    Tip Visit first thing, as it's a short bus or taxi ride from the main town centre.

  2. Tour St Barbara's Cathedral

    An essential stop, taking in the cathedral's soaring nave and medieval mining-themed frescoes.

    Tip Walk the terrace outside for a view back over the Vrchlice valley.

  3. Walk through the Italian Court

    A worthwhile stop, touring the former royal mint where Bohemia's medieval silver coinage was struck.

    Tip Combine with the Church of St James next door in the same visit.

  4. Descend into a medieval silver mine

    A fascinating underground tour, descending into the shaft that built the town's medieval fortune.

    Tip Book ahead in peak season — tour group sizes are limited underground.

  5. Photograph the Stone Fountain

    A quick, worthwhile stop, one of the finest surviving pieces of Gothic civic architecture in the country.

    Tip Easy to fit in while walking between the main square and St James's church.

  6. Wander the UNESCO-listed old town

    A relaxed way to spend an hour or two, with far fewer crowds than Prague's equivalent streets.

    Tip Stop for lunch at a local restaurant well away from the ossuary's tour-bus car park.

Local food

What to eat in Kutná Hora

Kutná Hora's food means the same hearty Czech classics found across Bohemia, without Prague's tourist markups.

  • Svíčková (marinated beef in cream sauce)

    A reliable local classic, often better value here than in central Prague.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout the old town.

  • Guláš with dumplings

    A warming, filling lunch after a morning at the ossuary or cathedral.

    Where to try it Local pubs throughout Kutná Hora.

  • Czech beer (pivo)

    As good and inexpensive here as anywhere else in Bohemia.

    Where to try it Local pubs throughout the old town.

  • Smažený sýr (fried cheese)

    A satisfying, inexpensive pub lunch between sightseeing stops.

    Where to try it Local pubs throughout Kutná Hora.

  • Koláče (fruit-filled pastries)

    A nice sweet stop, found at local bakeries around the main square.

    Where to try it Bakeries near the historic centre.

  • Trdelník (chimney cake)

    Available from a stall or two near the main sights, though less ubiquitous than in Prague.

    Where to try it Occasional stalls near St Barbara's Cathedral.

Transport

Getting to and around Kutná Hora

Kutná Hora is one of the easiest day trips from Prague, well served by direct train.

Getting there

  • Direct train from Prague

    The easiest option, taking under an hour from Prague's main station.

    Trains run frequently throughout the day; the station is a short bus ride from the historic centre.

  • Organised day trip

    Convenient if you'd rather not navigate independently.

    Widely available from Prague, often combined with transport directly to the ossuary.

  • Car or private driver

    The fastest, most flexible option.

    About an hour from Prague on well-maintained roads.

Getting around

Kutná Hora's compact centre is easy on foot, though Sedlec sits a little further out.

  • On foot in the historic centre

    The best way to see St Barbara's, the Italian Court and the old town.

    Everything in the centre sits within a comfortable walk.

  • Local bus to Sedlec

    Useful for reaching the ossuary, about 2km from the centre.

    A short, inexpensive local bus ride, or a manageable walk.

  • Taxi

    An easy option for combining Sedlec and the centre in one trip.

    Widely available, particularly near the train station.

When to go

Best time to visit Kutná Hora

Kutná Hora is a year-round day trip, though its outdoor sights are most pleasant outside peak summer heat.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Mild, comfortable weather for a full day of walking between sights.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    The busiest season for the ossuary, worth an early start to beat the crowds.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Comfortable temperatures with noticeably fewer day-trippers than summer.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Quiet and atmospheric, though some sights keep reduced hours.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly, though a summer weekend brings the heaviest tour-bus crowds to the ossuary.

Itinerary

One day in Kutná Hora

A single full day covering the ossuary and the historic centre's Gothic core.

  1. Day1

    Sedlec and the historic centre

    The ossuary in the morning, the old town in the afternoon.

    • Morning The Sedlec Ossuary, ideally right at opening.
    • Afternoon St Barbara's Cathedral, the Italian Court and the Church of St James.
    • Evening A relaxed dinner before returning to Prague.

Travel tips

Kutná Hora travel tips

Practical notes that make a Kutná Hora day trip noticeably smoother.

  • Book a timed-entry Sedlec Ossuary ticket online ahead in peak season.
  • Take the train over an organised tour if you'd like more flexibility on timing.
  • Wear comfortable shoes — the historic centre is built on a hill with plenty of cobblestones.
  • Pack a light layer for the underground silver mine tour, which runs noticeably cooler.
  • Eat lunch away from the streets nearest the ossuary for better value.
  • Combine Sedlec and the historic centre in one visit — they're an easy bus ride apart.

FAQ

Kutná Hora: frequently asked questions

Is Kutná Hora worth a day trip from Prague?

Yes — the Sedlec Ossuary and St Barbara's Cathedral are both extraordinary, and the direct train makes it an easy half-day or full-day trip.

How do you get from Prague to Kutná Hora?

Easiest by direct train, taking under an hour from Prague's main station.

Is the Sedlec Ossuary respectful to visit?

Yes, treated as a place of quiet reflection — visitors are asked to stay respectful and photography rules are enforced.

How much time do you need in Kutná Hora?

A full day covers both the ossuary and the historic centre comfortably; a rushed half-day covers the essentials.

Can you combine Kutná Hora with another day trip?

It's possible but rushed — most visitors find one destination per day trip from Prague works better.

Is Kutná Hora crowded?

The Sedlec Ossuary gets busy with tour groups by mid-morning; the historic centre itself stays noticeably quieter.
