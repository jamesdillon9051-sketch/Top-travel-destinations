Welcome to Český Krumlov

Český Krumlov is one of Central Europe's most complete fairy-tale towns, its pastel old town wrapped almost entirely by a dramatic horseshoe bend in the Vltava River.

Above it all sits the country's second-largest castle complex after Prague's own, a sprawling hilltop fortress with its own bear-filled moat and a colourfully painted tower.

It's small enough to see on a day trip from Prague, though an overnight stay lets the town's cobbled lanes empty out into something magical once the tour buses leave.

Why visit

Why visit Český Krumlov?

Český Krumlov's case rests on being one of the best-preserved small medieval towns anywhere in Europe.

  • The setting is unmatched

    A near-complete horseshoe bend of the Vltava wraps the old town on three sides.

  • The castle is the country's second-largest

    A sprawling complex with its own bear moat, painted tower and elevated Cloak Bridge.

  • The old town is astonishingly intact

    A UNESCO World Heritage Site with barely a modern building in sight.

  • It's far quieter overnight

    Day-trippers clear out by early evening, leaving the streets wonderfully calm.

  • It works well paired with Prague

    An easy add-on to a Bohemia-focused itinerary, whether as a day trip or overnight stay.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Český Krumlov

Český Krumlov's essential sights are centred on its castle complex and the old town beneath it.

  1. 01

    Český Krumlov Castle

    The country's second-largest castle complex, its colourfully painted tower visible from almost anywhere in town.

    Tip Book the interior tour ahead in summer — time slots do sell out.

  2. 02

    The Cloak Bridge (Plášťový most)

    A dramatic multi-tiered bridge connecting two parts of the castle high above the old town.

    Tip Stop halfway across for one of the town's best photo viewpoints.

  3. 03

    The castle's bear moat

    A centuries-old tradition, live bears kept in the castle's moat since the 1700s.

    Tip Visit late morning, when the bears tend to be most active.

  4. 04

    The Castle Gardens

    A peaceful baroque garden above the town, complete with a revolving summer theatre.

    Tip Free to enter and a lovely, quieter escape from the busier old town streets.

  5. 05

    St Vitus Church

    The old town's Gothic parish church, its tower offering one of the best views over the rooftops.

    Tip Climb the tower if it's open — the view over the river bend is worth it.

  6. 06

    The Vltava's horseshoe bend

    The geographic feature that defines the whole town, the river wrapping the old town on three sides.

    Tip The castle tower and gardens both offer excellent views of the bend.

  7. 07

    The old town's historic lanes

    Worth simply wandering, a near-complete medieval townscape of narrow lanes and pastel facades.

    Tip Explore after the day-trip buses leave for the quietest, most atmospheric version of the town.

Things to do

Best things to do in Český Krumlov

Český Krumlov rewards slow wandering, split between the castle complex and the old town beneath it.

  1. Tour Český Krumlov Castle's interiors

    An essential visit, taking in the castle's period-furnished rooms and elaborate masquerade hall.

    Tip Several separate tour routes cover different parts of the castle — pick one or two rather than all.

  2. Cross the Cloak Bridge

    A quick but essential stop, the bridge's elevated walkway giving one of the town's best views.

    Tip Visit close to sunset for the best light over the old town below.

  3. Spot the castle's resident bears

    A fun stop for all ages, watching the moat's bears from the bridge above.

    Tip A quick, easy stop on the way into the main castle courtyard.

  4. Canoe or raft the Vltava

    A popular warm-weather activity, paddling beneath the castle and through the old town's bend.

    Tip Rent gear from one of several outfitters right along the riverbank.

  5. Wander the Castle Gardens

    A peaceful way to spend an hour, strolling formal baroque gardens above the town.

    Tip Check for a summer performance at the garden's revolving auditorium theatre.

  6. Photograph the old town from a lookout

    An essential evening ritual, watching the light turn golden over the river bend and rooftops.

    Tip The castle tower and the Cloak Bridge both make excellent sunset viewpoints.

Local food

What to eat in Český Krumlov

Český Krumlov's food means South Bohemian classics, including a regional specialty in freshwater carp.

  • Svíčková (marinated beef in cream sauce)

    A reliable classic, found on nearly every traditional restaurant menu in town.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout the old town.

  • South Bohemian carp

    A true regional specialty — South Bohemia's centuries-old fish ponds make carp a local staple, especially in winter.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants specialising in regional South Bohemian cuisine.

  • Guláš with dumplings

    A warming, filling option after a day of hilly castle sightseeing.

    Where to try it Traditional pubs throughout the old town.

  • Budweiser Budvar

    Worth seeking out here — the original Budvar brewery sits in nearby České Budějovice, the region's capital.

    Where to try it Pubs and restaurants throughout Český Krumlov.

  • Smažený sýr (fried cheese)

    A satisfying pub classic, breaded fried cheese with tartar sauce and potatoes.

    Where to try it Traditional pubs throughout the old town.

  • Trdelník (chimney cake)

    A popular sweet treat sold from stalls throughout the old town's tourist streets.

    Where to try it Street stalls throughout the old town.

Transport

Getting to and around Český Krumlov

Český Krumlov is a popular, well-connected day trip or stopover from Prague.

Getting there

  • Direct bus from Prague

    The easiest option, taking about 2.5 to 3 hours.

    RegioJet and FlixBus both run frequent, comfortable direct services.

  • Train via České Budějovice

    A scenic if slightly slower option, with one change.

    Takes roughly 3.5 hours including the connection.

  • Organised day trip or private transfer

    Convenient, especially if combined with other South Bohemian stops.

    Widely available from Prague hotels and tour operators.

Getting around

Český Krumlov's compact old town is entirely walkable, with no need for a car once you've arrived.

  • On foot

    The only way to properly explore the old town's narrow lanes.

    Everything sits within a comfortable walk, though the castle hill involves some climbing.

  • Local bus from the train station

    Useful for the uphill walk from the station to the old town.

    A short, inexpensive ride connects the two.

  • Bicycle

    A nice way to explore the wider area beyond the old town itself.

    Rental available from several shops near the river.

When to go

Best time to visit Český Krumlov

Český Krumlov is at its best outside the peak summer day-tripper season.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Mild, comfortable weather with manageable crowds.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Warm and lively, though the old town gets busy with day-trippers by midday.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Golden light over the river and rooftops, with noticeably fewer visitors than summer.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Quiet and atmospheric under snow, though some attractions reduce their hours.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly, though midday in July and August brings the heaviest day-tripper crowds.

Itinerary

Two days in Český Krumlov

An overnight stay, splitting the castle complex and old town across two unhurried days.

  1. Day1

    The castle complex

    Arrival and the castle's main sights.

    • Morning Arrive and settle into the old town.
    • Afternoon Český Krumlov Castle, the Cloak Bridge and the bear moat.
    • Evening Dinner beside the river, once the day-trippers have left.
  2. Day2

    The old town and gardens

    A slower day at ground level.

    • Morning The Castle Gardens and St Vitus Church.
    • Afternoon Wander the old town's lanes, or canoe the Vltava.
    • Evening A final sunset view from the castle tower.

Travel tips

Český Krumlov travel tips

Practical notes that make a Český Krumlov visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book the castle interior tour online ahead in summer.
  • Stay overnight if you can — the town transforms once day-trippers leave.
  • Wear comfortable shoes — the old town and castle hill involve plenty of cobblestones and stairs.
  • Visit the bear moat late morning, when the bears tend to be more active.
  • Book a canoe or raft trip ahead in peak summer season.
  • Arrive early or stay late to see the old town without the day-trip crowds.

FAQ

Český Krumlov: frequently asked questions

Is Český Krumlov worth visiting from Prague?

Yes — it's one of Central Europe's best-preserved medieval towns, easily reached by direct bus or train.

Is Český Krumlov a day trip or an overnight stay?

Either works, though an overnight stay lets you see the old town once the day-trip crowds have left, which is worth the extra time.

How do you get from Prague to Český Krumlov?

Easiest by direct bus, taking about 2.5 to 3 hours; a train via České Budějovice is a scenic alternative.

Is the castle worth touring inside?

Yes — its period-furnished rooms and masquerade hall are among South Bohemia's finest interiors.

Are there really bears in the castle moat?

Yes — live bears have been kept in the moat since the 18th century, a well-established local tradition.

How many days do you need in Český Krumlov?

One full day covers the essentials; two days allows a far more relaxed pace and an evening without the crowds.
