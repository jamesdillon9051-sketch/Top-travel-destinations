Welcome to Prague

Prague is genuinely one of Europe's most beautiful and best-preserved capitals, its medieval, gothic and baroque architecture largely untouched by the wars that devastated so many other cities on the continent.

Its layout genuinely rewards wandering on foot — Prague Castle looms above the Vltava, Charles Bridge connects the old town to the castle district, and the whole historic core fits within an easily walkable area.

It's also genuinely a city built for beer, with pubs on nearly every corner pouring some of the best, cheapest pilsner anywhere in the world.

Why visit

Why visit Prague?

Prague's case rests genuinely on an intact historic core paired with a living, everyday beer and pub culture.

  • Prague's historic centre is genuinely astonishingly intact

    Spared the wartime bombing that levelled so many other European capitals.

  • Prague Castle is genuinely the largest ancient castle complex in the world

    A thousand-year-old seat of power still central to Czech political life today.

  • The beer culture here is genuinely unmatched

    World-class, inexpensive pilsner served in pubs on almost every corner.

  • It's genuinely one of Europe's most walkable capitals

    Old Town, Malá Strana and the castle district all connect easily on foot.

  • It works genuinely well as a springboard for Bohemia

    Day trips to Kutná Hora and Český Krumlov are both easy from here.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Prague

Prague's essential sights are genuinely concentrated within an easily walkable historic core.

  1. 01

    Prague Castle

    Genuinely the largest ancient castle complex in the world, a thousand-year-old seat of Czech power above the Vltava.

    Tip Genuinely arrive right at opening to see the changing of the guard without the midday crowds.

  2. 02

    Charles Bridge

    Genuinely a 14th-century stone bridge lined with baroque statues, the city's most iconic crossing.

    Tip Genuinely cross at sunrise for photos without the crowds and street vendors that fill it by mid-morning.

  3. 03

    Old Town Square and the Astronomical Clock

    Genuinely the city's medieval heart, a working 15th-century astronomical clock still drawing crowds every hour.

    Tip Genuinely climb the Old Town Hall tower for a view over the square itself, rather than just the clock.

  4. 04

    St Vitus Cathedral

    Genuinely a soaring Gothic cathedral within the castle complex, its stained glass and vaulted ceilings centuries in the making.

    Tip Genuinely look for the Art Nouveau stained glass window by Alfons Mucha in the third chapel.

  5. 05

    Malá Strana

    Genuinely Prague's best-preserved baroque district, a maze of cobbled lanes, palace gardens and quiet squares below the castle.

    Tip Genuinely get lost on purpose here — the quieter side streets are the district's real reward.

  6. 06

    The Jewish Quarter (Josefov)

    Genuinely one of Europe's best-preserved former Jewish quarters, its synagogues and old cemetery a moving, essential visit.

    Tip Genuinely buy a combined ticket covering several synagogues and the Old Jewish Cemetery together.

  7. 07

    Petřín Hill and Lookout Tower

    Genuinely a green escape from the crowds, a hilltop park topped by a miniature Eiffel-style lookout tower.

    Tip Genuinely ride the funicular up and walk back down through the orchards.

Things to do

Best things to do in Prague

Prague genuinely rewards unhurried wandering, though a few essential experiences anchor most visits.

  1. Cross Charles Bridge at sunrise

    A genuinely essential Prague ritual, having the bridge's statues almost to yourself before the crowds arrive.

    Tip Genuinely bring a coffee to go and continue on toward the castle while the light is still soft.

  2. Tour Prague Castle's grounds

    A genuinely essential half-day, taking in St Vitus Cathedral, the Old Royal Palace and Golden Lane.

    Tip Genuinely buy your ticket online in advance to skip the entrance queue.

  3. Watch the Astronomical Clock strike the hour

    A genuinely medieval spectacle, apostle figures processing past two small windows as the clock strikes.

    Tip Genuinely temper expectations — the show itself is brief, the clock's mechanism is the real marvel.

  4. Wander Malá Strana's palace gardens

    A genuinely peaceful escape, a string of baroque terraced gardens climbing the hill below the castle.

    Tip Genuinely several gardens connect by internal stairways — a single ticket often covers more than one.

  5. Climb Petřín Hill for the view

    A genuinely rewarding walk or funicular ride, with one of the city's best panoramic views from the top.

    Tip Genuinely visit at golden hour, when the light over the castle and river is at its best.

  6. Explore the Jewish Quarter's synagogues

    A genuinely moving, essential few hours, tracing centuries of Prague's Jewish history through its synagogues and cemetery.

    Tip Genuinely allow at least two to three hours to do the quarter justice.

Local food

What to eat in Prague

Prague's food genuinely means hearty Czech classics best paired with a proper pilsner.

  • Svíčková (marinated beef in cream sauce)

    Genuinely the dish most Czechs would call their national favourite, tender beef with cream sauce and dumplings.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout the city.

  • Guláš with dumplings

    Genuinely a pub staple, rich paprika-spiced beef stew mopped up with sliced bread dumplings.

    Where to try it Traditional pubs throughout Prague.

  • Vepřové koleno (roast pork knee)

    Genuinely a hearty, shareable pub classic, slow-roasted pork knee with crackling skin and mustard.

    Where to try it Traditional beer halls throughout the city.

  • Trdelník (chimney cake)

    Genuinely more a Prague street tradition than an old family recipe, a sugar-and-cinnamon pastry cooked on a spit.

    Where to try it Street stalls throughout the Old Town and near Charles Bridge.

  • Czech beer (pivo)

    Genuinely essential, some of the best and cheapest beer anywhere, poured properly in pubs across the city.

    Where to try it Pubs on nearly every street in the city centre.

  • Smažený sýr (fried cheese)

    Genuinely a beloved pub classic, breaded and deep-fried cheese served with tartar sauce and fries.

    Where to try it Traditional pubs throughout Prague.

Transport

Getting to and around Prague

Prague is genuinely well connected by air, rail and road to the rest of Europe.

Getting there

  • Flight into Václav Havel Airport Prague

    Genuinely the standard way in for most international visitors.

    A direct bus or taxi reaches the city centre in about 30–45 minutes.

  • Train from Vienna, Berlin or Budapest

    Genuinely a comfortable, scenic overland option from several neighbouring capitals.

    Journey times range from about four to seven hours depending on the route.

  • Long-distance bus

    Genuinely a budget-friendly option from across Central Europe.

    RegioJet and FlixBus both run frequent, comfortable international routes.

Getting around

Prague's genuinely excellent public transport makes a car entirely unnecessary.

  • Metro and trams

    Genuinely fast and frequent, covering the whole city on one unified ticket.

    Trams are often the more scenic way to cross the city centre.

  • On foot

    Genuinely the best way to see the historic core itself.

    Old Town, Malá Strana and the castle district all connect easily on foot.

  • Taxis and rideshare apps

    Genuinely useful late at night or for luggage.

    Use a rideshare app or a registered taxi rank rather than hailing on the street.

When to go

Best time to visit Prague

Prague is genuinely a year-round destination, though shoulder-season months offer the best overall balance.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely mild temperatures and blooming parks, before peak summer crowds arrive.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Genuinely warm and lively, though the Old Town and castle get genuinely crowded.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Genuinely golden light over the rooftops, with noticeably thinner crowds than summer.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Genuinely atmospheric around the December Christmas markets, though short days and cold call for warm layers.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Prague genuinely works year-round, though August's crowds are worth planning around.

Travel tips

Prague travel tips

Practical notes that make a Prague visit noticeably smoother.

  • Buy genuinely a multi-day public transport pass if you're staying more than a couple of days.
  • Book genuinely Prague Castle's interiors online ahead to skip the entrance queue.
  • Carry genuinely some cash for small pubs and markets, even though cards are widely accepted.
  • Avoid genuinely restaurants with staff actively calling people in near major squares — check prices first.
  • Validate genuinely any paper transport ticket before boarding — inspectors do check.
  • Cross genuinely Charles Bridge at sunrise or late evening to avoid the worst of the crowds.

FAQ

Prague: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Prague?

Three to four days genuinely covers the essential sights at a comfortable pace, with time for a day trip.

Is Prague expensive?

Genuinely more affordable than Western European capitals overall, though prices in the most touristy central areas have risen close to Western levels.

Is Prague Castle worth the visit?

Yes, genuinely — it's the largest ancient castle complex in the world, and St Vitus Cathedral alone justifies the trip up the hill.

Do you need to book Charles Bridge or Old Town Square ahead?

No, genuinely not — they're open public spaces, though visiting early morning avoids the worst crowds.

Is Prague walkable?

Yes, genuinely one of Europe's most walkable capitals — Old Town, Malá Strana and the castle district all connect easily on foot.

Can you day-trip from Prague?

Yes, genuinely easily — Kutná Hora and Český Krumlov are both popular, straightforward day trips by train or organised tour.
