How to plan a Czech Republic trip: whether to focus on Prague, Bohemia or Moravia, when to go for the best weather and crowds, what it costs, and a practical nine-day itinerary.

How the Czech Republic divides

The Czech Republic genuinely divides into three distinct parts — Prague, the rest of Bohemia, and Moravia — and a first trip realistically covers the first two well.

Prague. The capital and the country's single biggest draw, genuinely easily worth three or four days alone.

Bohemia beyond Prague. Český Krumlov, Kutná Hora, Karlovy Vary, Plzeň and Bohemian Switzerland — fairy-tale towns, a grand spa resort, the home of pilsner and dramatic sandstone scenery, all within easy reach of the capital.

Moravia. Brno, Telč, Lednice-Valtice and Olomouc — the quieter, wine-producing east, with its own dialect and a noticeably slower pace.

Worth knowing: Most first-time visitors genuinely spend the bulk of their trip in Prague and Bohemia. Moravia is where a longer or second trip belongs, and it rewards the extra travel time.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If this is your first trip

    Prague and a couple of easy day trips to Kutná Hora or Český Krumlov, genuinely the classic starting point.

  • If you want a fairy-tale old town

    Český Krumlov or Telč, genuinely two of Central Europe's best-preserved historic towns.

  • If you want to relax at a spa

    Karlovy Vary, genuinely the grandest of Bohemia's 19th-century spa towns.

  • If you're a beer enthusiast

    Plzeň, genuinely the birthplace of pilsner, home to the original Pilsner Urquell brewery.

  • If you want dramatic nature

    Bohemian Switzerland, genuinely a landscape of sandstone towers and deep gorges near the German border.

  • If you want wine country and a slower pace

    Brno, Lednice-Valtice and Olomouc in Moravia, genuinely Czech travel with far fewer international visitors.

Getting around: trains, buses, and one common mistake

The Czech Republic's compact size and genuinely excellent rail network mean trains and buses cover almost the entire country comfortably.

Take the train between major cities. Prague to Brno or Olomouc genuinely runs frequently, comfortably and cheaply by Czech rail or RegioJet.

Don't try to see Bohemia and Moravia in a rushed loop. Genuinely the most common planning mistake — Moravia deserves at least two or three unhurried days, not a single rushed day trip from Prague.

Rent a car for Bohemian Switzerland and the wine country. Bohemian Switzerland and Lednice-Valtice's villages are genuinely far easier to explore with your own wheels.

Use organised day trips sparingly. Kutná Hora and Český Krumlov are genuinely both reachable by direct train or bus, often cheaper and just as easy as a group tour.

Worth knowing: Validate every train and bus ticket before boarding, and keep it until you leave the station — Czech ticket inspectors genuinely do check, and fines for an unvalidated ticket are steep.

When to go

The Czech Republic genuinely has a distinct case for each season, though May, June and September offer the best overall balance.

Late spring and early autumn bring genuinely mild temperatures and thinner crowds, the sweet spot for most itineraries.

Summer is genuinely warm and lively, with long evenings on beer garden terraces, though central Prague gets genuinely crowded and accommodation prices peak.

Winter, especially December, is genuinely magical around Christmas markets and snow-dusted spires, though days are short and some rural attractions reduce their hours.

Worth knowing: Book Prague accommodation well ahead for the December Christmas market season and for any summer weekend — both genuinely sell out the best-located options early.

Four routes that work

  1. Four days: Prague alone

    Prague at an unhurried pace, genuinely enough time for the castle, Old Town and a single day trip.

    Tip Buy a multi-day public transport pass — you'll use it more than you expect.

  2. Nine days: the classic Bohemia and Moravia loop

    The itinerary on the Czech Republic country page: Prague, Kutná Hora, Český Krumlov, Karlovy Vary, Brno and Lednice-Valtice. A genuinely complete first look at both regions.

    Tip Book Český Krumlov Castle's interior tour ahead in summer — time slots genuinely do sell out.

  3. A week focused on Bohemia

    Prague, Kutná Hora, Český Krumlov, Karlovy Vary and Plzeň, skipping Moravia entirely.

    Tip This route genuinely keeps travel times short, ideal for a first trip without a car.

  4. A slower Moravia add-on for a second trip

    Brno, Telč, Lednice-Valtice and Olomouc, genuinely the region most international visitors skip entirely.

    Tip Rent a bicycle around Lednice-Valtice — the wine trails are genuinely built for cycling between villages.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Czech Republic

How many days do you need in the Czech Republic?

Seven to ten days genuinely covers Prague plus Bohemia and a taste of Moravia, which is the itinerary in this guide. Four days is enough for Prague alone.

Is Prague worth visiting if it's crowded?

Yes, genuinely — its Old Town and castle district earn the crowds, and visiting the castle early morning or staying an extra day to explore beyond the main sights both help.

Do you need to speak Czech to travel there?

No, genuinely not in Prague and other tourist areas, though a few basic phrases go a long way in smaller Bohemian and Moravian towns.

Is Moravia worth the extra travel time?

Yes, genuinely, for a second trip especially — its wine country, historic towns and noticeably fewer crowds make it feel like a different, quieter Czech Republic.
