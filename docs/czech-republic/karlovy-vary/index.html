Welcome to Karlovy Vary

Karlovy Vary is genuinely Bohemia's grandest spa town, built up around a dozen mineral hot springs that have drawn visitors since the 14th century.

Its 19th-century golden age genuinely left behind an extraordinary collection of ornate colonnades and grand hotels, all set along the narrow Teplá River valley.

The town is genuinely still a working spa destination today, where sipping mineral water from a distinctive spa cup remains as central to a visit as any sightseeing.

Why visit

Why visit Karlovy Vary?

Karlovy Vary's case rests genuinely on extraordinary spa-town architecture paired with a still-living thermal spa tradition.

  • The colonnades are genuinely architectural showpieces

    Ornate 19th-century ironwork and stone arcades built specifically to frame the mineral springs.

  • The springs are genuinely still in daily use

    A centuries-old tradition of sipping warm mineral water from a spa cup continues today.

  • The setting is genuinely beautiful

    A narrow river valley wrapped in forested hills, with a funicular up to a hilltop lookout.

  • It's genuinely home to Becherovka

    The town's own herbal liqueur, distilled here since 1807.

  • It works genuinely well as a change of pace from Prague

    A relaxed, unhurried spa-town atmosphere quite different from the capital.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Karlovy Vary

Karlovy Vary's essential sights are genuinely its colonnades, grand hotels and the springs themselves.

  1. 01

    The Mill Colonnade

    Genuinely the town's largest and grandest colonnade, a neo-Renaissance arcade covering five separate mineral springs.

    Tip Genuinely bring or buy a spa cup here to sample the water as you walk.

  2. 02

    The Hot Spring Colonnade

    Genuinely home to the town's hottest and most powerful spring, shooting water up to 12 metres into the air.

    Tip Genuinely the modern glass building looks stark from outside, but the spring itself is genuinely impressive.

  3. 03

    Grandhotel Pupp

    Genuinely the town's grandest hotel since 1701, and a regular filming location for international movies.

    Tip Genuinely stop for a coffee in the lobby even if you're not staying — it's open to non-guests.

  4. 04

    The Diana Lookout Tower

    Genuinely the best view over the town, an observation tower reached by a short funicular ride.

    Tip Genuinely walk back down through the forest instead of taking the funicular both ways.

  5. 05

    Church of St Mary Magdalene

    Genuinely the town's baroque parish church, designed by the same architect behind Prague's St Nicholas Church.

    Tip Genuinely visible from most of the colonnade district, an easy landmark to navigate by.

  6. 06

    The Market Colonnade

    Genuinely a delicate white wooden colonnade, among the most photogenic in town.

    Tip Genuinely a lovely spot in the late afternoon light, just up the valley from the Mill Colonnade.

  7. 07

    The Jan Becher Museum

    Genuinely the home of Becherovka, the town's herbal liqueur distilled here since 1807.

    Tip Genuinely book the tour, which ends with a tasting of several Becherovka varieties.

Things to do

Best things to do in Karlovy Vary

Karlovy Vary genuinely rewards an unhurried pace, built around its springs, colonnades and forested hills.

  1. Sip mineral water from a spa cup

    A genuinely essential Karlovy Vary ritual, strolling between colonnades sampling different mineral springs.

    Tip Genuinely buy a spa cup with a built-in straw from any souvenir shop — the water is often too hot to sip normally.

  2. Ride the funicular to the Diana Lookout Tower

    A genuinely rewarding short trip, climbing above the colonnades for a sweeping view over the town.

    Tip Genuinely go late afternoon for the best light over the valley.

  3. Tour the Jan Becher Museum and taste Becherovka

    A genuinely worthwhile hour, learning the story behind the town's own herbal liqueur before a tasting.

    Tip Genuinely pick up a bottle at the museum shop — it's a classic, packable Karlovy Vary souvenir.

  4. Buy a spa wafer (lázeňské oplatky)

    A genuinely traditional pairing, thin sweet wafers sold specifically to eat alongside the mineral water.

    Tip Genuinely try a few different flavours from one of the colonnade kiosks.

  5. Have a drink at the Grandhotel Pupp

    A genuinely elegant way to spend an hour, taking in the hotel's grand historic interiors over a coffee or drink.

    Tip Genuinely the terrace is a lovely spot to sit in warmer months.

  6. Photograph the Market Colonnade

    A genuinely photogenic stop, the delicate white woodwork among the prettiest in town.

    Tip Genuinely visit early morning for photos without other visitors in frame.

Local food

What to eat in Karlovy Vary

Karlovy Vary's food genuinely centres on its spa traditions alongside standard Czech classics.

  • Lázeňské oplatky (spa wafers)

    Genuinely the town's signature treat, thin sweet wafers traditionally eaten alongside the mineral water.

    Where to try it Colonnade kiosks and bakeries throughout the spa district.

  • Becherovka

    Genuinely the town's own herbal liqueur, distilled here since 1807 and often served as a digestif.

    Where to try it Bars and restaurants throughout Karlovy Vary, or the Jan Becher Museum itself.

  • Svíčková (marinated beef in cream sauce)

    Genuinely a reliable classic, found at traditional restaurants throughout town.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants around the colonnade district.

  • Guláš with dumplings

    Genuinely a warming option away from the colonnades' more tourist-focused cafés.

    Where to try it Local pubs slightly outside the main spa district.

  • Czech beer (pivo)

    Genuinely a good counterpoint to a day of mineral water, though this is first and foremost a spa town.

    Where to try it Pubs a short walk from the main colonnade district.

  • Smažený sýr (fried cheese)

    Genuinely a satisfying, inexpensive option away from the spa district's pricier cafés.

    Where to try it Local restaurants slightly outside the colonnade area.

Transport

Getting to and around Karlovy Vary

Karlovy Vary is genuinely an easy trip from Prague, whether as a day trip or overnight stay.

Getting there

  • Direct bus from Prague

    Genuinely the easiest and fastest option, taking about two hours.

    Frequent daily services run from Prague's main bus terminals.

  • Train from Prague

    Genuinely a scenic alternative, taking slightly longer than the bus.

    Around three hours, with pleasant countryside views along the way.

  • Organised day trip

    Genuinely convenient for a rushed visit without your own transport.

    Widely available from Prague hotels and tour operators.

Getting around

Karlovy Vary's genuinely compact spa core is entirely walkable, with a funicular for the surrounding hills.

  • On foot

    Genuinely the only way to properly experience the colonnades.

    The spa district's main sights all sit within an easy walk of each other.

  • Funicular to Diana Lookout Tower

    Genuinely the easiest way up to the best viewpoint over town.

    A short, inexpensive ride from near the Grandhotel Pupp.

  • Local bus from the train station

    Genuinely useful, as the station sits a little outside the spa core.

    A short ride connects the station to the colonnade district.

When to go

Best time to visit Karlovy Vary

Karlovy Vary is genuinely a year-round spa destination, with the indoor springs open regardless of season.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely mild, comfortable weather for strolling between colonnades.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Genuinely warm and busy, especially during the town's film festival in early July.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Genuinely golden light over the forested valley, with thinner crowds than summer.

  • Winter (November–February)

    Good

    Genuinely atmospheric with the warm colonnades a cosy contrast to the cold outside.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly, though early July's film festival fills hotels well ahead.

Travel tips

Karlovy Vary travel tips

Practical notes that make a Karlovy Vary visit noticeably smoother.

  • Buy genuinely a spa cup from any souvenir shop before trying the mineral springs.
  • Book genuinely the Jan Becher Museum tour ahead in peak season.
  • Pack genuinely comfortable shoes — the funicular walk back down covers some distance.
  • Try genuinely more than one spring — each one genuinely tastes noticeably different.
  • Visit genuinely as a day trip or add one night — a single day covers the essentials well.
  • Book genuinely well ahead for early July, when the town's film festival fills every hotel.

FAQ

Karlovy Vary: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Karlovy Vary?

One full day genuinely covers the essential colonnades and a lookout tower; an overnight stay allows a more relaxed pace.

Can you actually drink the spa water?

Yes, genuinely — it's intended for drinking, traditionally sipped from a spa cup while walking between colonnades.

Is Karlovy Vary worth visiting from Prague?

Yes, genuinely — its 19th-century spa architecture and thermal springs are unlike anything in the capital.

How do you get from Prague to Karlovy Vary?

Genuinely easiest by direct bus, taking about two hours; the train is a scenic alternative taking slightly longer.

What is Becherovka?

Genuinely Karlovy Vary's own herbal liqueur, distilled here since 1807 and still produced in the town today.

Is Karlovy Vary a day trip or worth staying overnight?

Both genuinely work — a day trip covers the highlights, while staying overnight allows a properly unhurried spa-town pace.
