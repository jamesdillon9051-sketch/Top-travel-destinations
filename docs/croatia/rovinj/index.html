Welcome to Rovinj

Rovinj is genuinely Istria's most photographed town, its pastel old houses rising in tiers straight from the Adriatic on a former island now joined to the mainland.

The whole townscape is genuinely watched over by St Euphemia's Church, its Venetian-style bell tower visible from boats and hilltops across much of the peninsula.

It's also genuinely a natural base for exploring Istria's celebrated food and wine country, from white truffles to the region's own Malvazija wine, all within easy reach inland.

Why visit

Why visit Rovinj?

Rovinj's case rests genuinely on a setting that ranks among the most beautiful on the entire Croatian coast.

  • The setting is genuinely postcard-perfect

    Pastel houses stacked in tiers straight above the Adriatic, unlike anywhere else in Croatia.

  • St Euphemia's Church is genuinely a defining landmark

    Its Venetian bell tower visible from across the surrounding coastline.

  • It's genuinely a quieter alternative to the Dalmatian coast

    A slower, more art-focused pace than Dubrovnik or Split.

  • It's genuinely a gateway to real Istrian food and wine

    White truffles, olive oil and Malvazija wine all sit just inland from the coast.

  • It works genuinely well paired with Pula

    Croatia's Roman amphitheatre town sits under an hour down the coast.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Rovinj

Rovinj's essential sights are genuinely centred on its hilltop church and the tiered old town beneath it.

  1. 01

    St Euphemia's Church

    Genuinely the town's defining landmark, a baroque church with a Venetian-style bell tower crowning the old town's hill.

    Tip Genuinely inside, look for the tomb of St Euphemia herself, the town's patron saint.

  2. 02

    The view from St Euphemia's bell tower

    Genuinely the best view in town, a sweeping panorama over the old town's rooftops and the Adriatic beyond.

    Tip Genuinely worth the climb up narrow stairs for a view found nowhere else in Rovinj.

  3. 03

    St Euphemia's interior

    Genuinely worth a proper look inside, a richly decorated baroque interior beneath the landmark tower.

    Tip Genuinely check for opening hours — the interior doesn't keep the same schedule as the tower climb.

  4. 04

    The old town seen from the water

    Genuinely the classic Rovinj view, tiered pastel houses rising straight from the Adriatic.

    Tip Genuinely a short boat trip or even a walk along the harbour both offer this iconic angle.

  5. 05

    Rovinj's harbour

    Genuinely the town's working waterfront, fishing boats and yachts sharing space beneath the old town walls.

    Tip Genuinely a lovely spot for an evening walk once the day's heat has eased.

  6. 06

    Rovinj at sunrise

    Genuinely a magical time to see the town, soft morning light catching the tiered rooftops before the day's crowds arrive.

    Tip Genuinely worth the early start for photographers especially.

  7. 07

    The old town's streets

    Genuinely worth simply wandering, narrow lanes climbing steeply between pastel houses toward the church above.

    Tip Genuinely head uphill without a fixed route — every lane eventually leads toward St Euphemia's.

Things to do

Best things to do in Rovinj

Rovinj genuinely rewards a slow pace, split between the old town's lanes and Istria's food and wine country inland.

  1. Climb St Euphemia's bell tower

    A genuinely essential Rovinj experience, climbing for the town's best panoramic view.

    Tip Genuinely check the tower's opening hours ahead — they can vary by season.

  2. Wander Grisia Street's artist stalls

    A genuinely charming climb, a steep cobbled street lined with small galleries and artists' studios leading up to the church.

    Tip Genuinely visit in August for the street's long-running annual open-air art exhibition.

  3. Walk or cycle Zlatni Rt forest park

    A genuinely peaceful escape from the old town, pine-shaded paths along the coast just south of the centre.

    Tip Genuinely rent a bicycle for a longer loop, or simply walk the closer sections.

  4. Photograph the old town from the water

    A genuinely rewarding pursuit, the town's tiered silhouette photographing beautifully from nearly any angle on the water.

    Tip Genuinely a short kayak or paddleboard rental gets you the classic view without an organised tour.

  5. Watch sunrise over the harbour

    A genuinely lovely, quiet ritual, watching the light change over the water before the town wakes up.

    Tip Genuinely bring a coffee and find a bench along the harbour for the full effect.

  6. Swim at a rocky cove near town

    A genuinely easy way to cool off, small swimming spots dotted along the coast just outside the old town.

    Tip Genuinely pack water shoes — most of Rovinj's swimming spots are rocky rather than sandy.

Local food

What to eat in Rovinj

Rovinj's food genuinely reflects Istria's distinct culinary identity, built around truffles, olive oil and its own wine.

  • Istrian white truffle

    Genuinely one of Istria's most celebrated products, shaved fresh over pasta or eggs at restaurants across the region.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout Rovinj and the wider Istrian interior.

  • Istrian pršut (prosciutto)

    Genuinely distinct from its Dalmatian cousin, cured with a different process and noticeably milder flavour.

    Where to try it Restaurants and delis throughout Rovinj.

  • Fuži with game or truffles

    Genuinely a beloved Istrian pasta shape, traditionally paired with rich game sauces or shaved truffle.

    Where to try it Traditional konobas throughout Rovinj's old town.

  • Malvazija (Istrian white wine)

    Genuinely the region's signature grape, a crisp, aromatic white produced throughout the Istrian countryside.

    Where to try it Wine bars throughout Rovinj, or wineries a short drive inland.

  • Fresh grilled seafood

    Genuinely a highlight of dining here, fish and shellfish grilled simply at harbourfront restaurants.

    Where to try it Konobas throughout Rovinj's harbour and old town.

  • Fritule (small fried doughnuts)

    Genuinely a nice sweet treat, found at bakeries and cafés throughout the old town.

    Where to try it Bakeries throughout Rovinj.

Transport

Getting to and around Rovinj

Rovinj is genuinely reached via Pula's airport or by road from the rest of Istria and beyond.

Getting there

  • Flight into Pula, then transfer

    Genuinely the fastest route from further afield.

    Pula Airport sits about 40 minutes by road from Rovinj.

  • Bus from Zagreb or the Italian border

    Genuinely a straightforward overland option.

    Takes around three to four hours from Zagreb; closer from Trieste, Italy.

  • Car or private driver

    Genuinely the most flexible option for exploring Istria's interior.

    Well-maintained roads connect Rovinj to Pula, Poreč and inland hill towns.

Getting around

Rovinj's genuinely car-free old town is entirely walkable, with the rest of Istria best explored by car.

  • On foot

    Genuinely the only way to explore the old town's steep, narrow lanes.

    Cars are largely restricted from the historic centre.

  • Bicycle

    Genuinely a pleasant way to reach Zlatni Rt forest park and nearby coves.

    Widely available for rent throughout town.

  • Car for Istria's interior

    Genuinely essential for reaching truffle country and hill towns beyond Rovinj.

    A car opens up the wider region far more than staying coastal alone.

When to go

Best time to visit Rovinj

Rovinj is genuinely busiest in July and August, with shoulder months offering better value and a calmer pace.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely warm enough to swim, with noticeably thinner crowds than peak summer.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Genuinely warm and lively, though the old town gets genuinely busy.

  • Early autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Genuinely still pleasant for the sea, and the start of white truffle season inland.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Genuinely quiet, with many businesses reducing hours, though the setting stays beautiful.

Worth planning around: Genuinely nothing strongly, though peak July and August bring the heaviest old town crowds.

Travel tips

Rovinj travel tips

Practical notes that make a Rovinj visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book genuinely accommodation well ahead for a July or August visit.
  • Wear genuinely comfortable, grippy shoes — the old town's lanes are steep and cobbled.
  • Visit genuinely Grisia Street's galleries during the annual August art exhibition if your dates align.
  • Pack genuinely water shoes for the area's mostly rocky swimming spots.
  • Rent genuinely a car for a day to explore Istria's inland truffle and wine country.
  • Climb genuinely St Euphemia's bell tower early in the day for the clearest views.

FAQ

Rovinj: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Rovinj?

Two to three days genuinely covers the old town, and allows time to explore Istria's food and wine country inland.

How do you get to Rovinj?

Genuinely easiest by flying into Pula and transferring by road, about 40 minutes away.

Is Rovinj worth visiting compared to Dalmatia?

Yes, genuinely — it offers a quieter, more art-focused version of the classic Croatian coastal town, with genuinely excellent food and wine nearby.

Do you need a car in Rovinj?

Not genuinely for the old town itself, though a car helps significantly for exploring Istria's interior.

When is truffle season in Istria?

White truffles genuinely peak from September to December, though black truffles are found for much of the rest of the year.

Can you combine Rovinj with Pula?

Yes, genuinely easily — Pula sits under an hour down the coast, an easy day trip or onward stop.
