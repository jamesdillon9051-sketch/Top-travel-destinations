Welcome to Plitvice Lakes

Plitvice Lakes is genuinely Croatia's most famous natural attraction, sixteen terraced lakes cascading into each other through travertine barriers built up naturally over thousands of years.

Wooden boardwalks genuinely wind directly over and beside the water, putting visitors within touching distance of pools so clear the fish seem to hover in mid-air.

It's genuinely a full-day destination at minimum, the park's scale meaning even a focused visit to the Lower and Upper Lakes takes several hours of walking.

Why visit Plitvice Lakes?

Plitvice Lakes' case rests genuinely on natural scenery that looks retouched even in an unedited photograph.

  • The water's colour is genuinely not exaggerated in photos

  • The boardwalks put you genuinely right at the water's edge

  • It's genuinely one of Europe's oldest national parks

Main attractions

Top things to see in Plitvice Lakes

The park's essential sights are genuinely its cascading lakes, waterfalls and the boardwalks connecting them.

  1. 01

    Veliki Slap (the Great Waterfall)

    Genuinely the park's tallest and most famous waterfall, dropping 78 metres at the end of the Lower Lakes.

    Tip Genuinely the viewing platform gets crowded — arrive early or late in the day for a clearer view.

  2. 02

    The Lower Lakes' cascades

    Genuinely a dramatic run of smaller waterfalls and travertine terraces leading down toward Veliki Slap.

    Tip Genuinely the most photogenic stretch of boardwalk in the entire park.

  3. 03

    The Upper Lakes' turquoise terraces

    Genuinely quieter and wilder-feeling than the Lower Lakes, forested terraces stepping gently down the valley.

    Tip Genuinely allow a full morning or afternoon if you want to properly cover this section.

  4. 04

    Lake Kozjak

    Genuinely the park's largest lake, crossed by an electric tourist boat connecting the Lower and Upper sections.

    Tip Genuinely factor the boat crossing into your route planning — it's included in the park entry ticket.

  5. 05

    The wooden boardwalks

    Genuinely the park's defining feature, paths winding directly over cascades and along shorelines throughout.

    Tip Genuinely wear non-slip shoes — the boardwalks can be damp and slippery, especially near waterfalls.

  6. 06

    Šupljara Cave

    Genuinely a short, easy detour, a shallow cave the boardwalk trail passes directly through.

    Tip Genuinely a nice change of pace and shade partway through a longer walking route.

  7. 07

    The electric boat across Kozjak

    Genuinely a restful, scenic interlude between long stretches of walking, included in the park entry fee.

    Tip Genuinely check the timetable at the boat dock — services run on a schedule rather than continuously.

Things to do

Best things to do in Plitvice Lakes

Plitvice genuinely rewards a full day of walking, best planned around one of the park's marked routes.

  1. Walk beneath Veliki Slap

    A genuinely essential stop, the boardwalk bringing you close enough to feel the waterfall's spray.

    Tip Genuinely one of the busiest single spots in the park — patience pays off for an uncrowded photo.

  2. Cross the boardwalks over turquoise water

    A genuinely magical, sustained experience, following wooden paths directly over and beside the cascading lakes.

    Tip Genuinely pace yourself — the full boardwalk network covers real distance across a full day.

  3. Take the electric boat across Lake Kozjak

    A genuinely relaxing way to cover ground, gliding across the park's largest lake between the Lower and Upper sections.

    Tip Genuinely a nice rest for tired legs partway through a longer route.

  4. Explore Šupljara Cave

    A genuinely quick, fun detour, the boardwalk trail leading straight through this shallow cave.

    Tip Genuinely easy to combine with the main Lower Lakes walking route.

  5. Hike the Upper Lakes trail

    A genuinely rewarding, quieter alternative to the busier Lower Lakes, with a more forested, wild feel.

    Tip Genuinely start here first if you'd like to see the park before the day's crowds build.

  6. Photograph the cascading pools

    A genuinely rewarding pursuit throughout the park, with new photogenic terraces around nearly every bend.

    Tip Genuinely visit after rain for the waterfalls at their fullest, most dramatic flow.

Food near the park genuinely means hearty Lika region cooking, built around lamb, trout and local honey.

  • Lička janjetina (Lika lamb)

    Where to try it Restaurants near the park's entrances.

  • Ćevapi

    Genuinely a reliable, filling classic found throughout continental Croatia, grilled minced meat served with flatbread.

    Where to try it Restaurants near the park's entrances and along the main road.

  • Fresh river trout

    Genuinely a natural fit for the region's clean, cold waterways, simply grilled or pan-fried.

    Where to try it Restaurants near the park, especially those along the Korana River.

  • Local honey

    Genuinely a real Lika product, sold at small stalls and shops near the park entrances.

    Where to try it Roadside stalls and shops around the park.

  • Croatian wine

    Where to try it Restaurants near the park's entrances.

  • Fritule (small fried doughnuts)

    Genuinely a nice sweet treat after a long day of walking, found at cafés near the park.

    Where to try it Cafés near the park entrances.

Transport

Getting to and around Plitvice Lakes

Plitvice Lakes is genuinely reached by road, sitting roughly halfway between Zagreb and the Dalmatian coast.

Getting there

  • Bus from Zagreb

    Genuinely the easiest option without a car, taking about two to 2.5 hours.

    Frequent daily services run from Zagreb's main bus station.

  • Bus or car from Split or Zadar

    Genuinely straightforward from the coast, taking around 2.5 to 3.5 hours.

    A popular stop for those heading between the coast and Zagreb.

  • Organised day trip

    Genuinely convenient from either Zagreb or Zadar.

    Widely available, though an overnight stay allows a much less rushed visit.

Getting around

Getting around the park genuinely means walking the boardwalks, with a shuttle bus and boat covering longer stretches.

  • On foot along the boardwalks

    Genuinely the only way to see the lakes themselves.

    Marked routes range from about two to eight hours depending on how much you want to cover.

  • Park shuttle bus

    Genuinely useful for covering longer distances between entrances and trailheads.

  • Electric boat across Lake Kozjak

    Genuinely the way to connect the Lower and Upper Lakes sections.

    Also included in the park entry ticket.

Plitvice genuinely looks different across the seasons, with each bringing its own case for a visit.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

Travel tips

Plitvice Lakes travel tips

Practical notes that make a Plitvice Lakes visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book genuinely park entry tickets online ahead — daily visitor numbers are capped in peak season.
  • Wear genuinely non-slip shoes — boardwalks get damp and slippery, especially near waterfalls.
  • Start genuinely early to see the most popular sections before the day's crowds build.
  • Bring genuinely water and snacks — options inside the park itself are limited.
  • Pack genuinely a light rain layer, even in summer — weather can shift quickly in the hills.
  • Never genuinely swim in the lakes — it's prohibited throughout the park to protect the ecosystem.

FAQ

Plitvice Lakes: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need at Plitvice Lakes?

One full day genuinely covers both the Lower and Upper Lakes comfortably; an overnight stay allows a more relaxed pace.

Can you swim in Plitvice Lakes?

No, genuinely not — swimming is prohibited throughout the national park to protect the travertine formations and ecosystem.

Is Plitvice Lakes worth visiting from Zagreb or the coast?

Yes, genuinely — it's Croatia's most celebrated natural attraction, and its position makes it an easy stop between the two.

How much walking is involved?

Genuinely a fair amount — even a focused route covers several kilometres of boardwalks and trails.

Do you need to book tickets ahead?

Yes, genuinely recommended in peak season — daily visitor numbers are capped and popular dates can sell out.

Is Plitvice Lakes worth visiting in winter?

Yes, genuinely — the frozen waterfalls and snow-covered boardwalks offer a completely different, quieter experience.
