Welcome to Hvar

Hvar is Croatia's most glamorous island, a sun-drenched strip of the Adriatic known for lavender fields, Venetian architecture and a harbour town that fills up with yachts every summer.

Its centrepiece is Hvar Town, a car-free medieval port overlooked by the hilltop Fortica fortress and framed by the pine-covered Pakleni Islands just offshore.

Beyond the harbour's nightlife, the island is also one of Croatia's sunniest and most fertile, its interior still scented with lavender and planted with some of the country's best vineyards.

Why visit

Why visit Hvar?

Hvar's case rests on combining postcard Dalmatian scenery with a lively, sociable atmosphere.

  • Hvar Town is one of Croatia's most beautiful harbours

    A car-free medieval port town backed by a hilltop fortress.

  • The Pakleni Islands are a short boat ride away

    Pine-covered islets with clear water and quiet coves, right offshore from the main harbour.

  • It's one of Europe's sunniest islands

    Among the highest annual sunshine counts anywhere on the continent.

  • The lavender and wine are real, not just marketing

    A fertile interior producing both alongside the coast's beach-club scene.

  • It works well for every kind of traveller

    Lively harbourfront nightlife alongside quiet coves and hiking trails, depending on what you're after.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Hvar

Hvar's essential sights are spread between its harbour town, hilltop fortress and the islands offshore.

  1. 01

    Hvar Town's harbour

    The island's social heart, a car-free medieval port lined with cafés, yachts and Venetian-era architecture.

    Tip Arrive by late afternoon for the harbour at its liveliest, golden-hour best.

  2. 02

    Fortica Španjola

    The best view on the island, a 16th-century fortress on the hill directly above Hvar Town.

    Tip Time the climb for sunset — the view over the town and Pakleni Islands is spectacular.

  3. 03

    St Stephen's Square and Cathedral

    One of the largest and oldest squares in Dalmatia, ringed by the town's baroque cathedral and arsenal.

    Tip A lovely spot for an evening coffee once the day's heat has faded.

  4. 04

    The Pakleni Islands

    A scatter of pine-covered islets just offshore, with quiet coves and clear water a world away from the harbour.

    Tip A water taxi from the harbour reaches them in minutes — no need for an organised tour.

  5. 05

    Hvar's lavender fields

    A real island industry, not just a photo prop, with purple fields scenting the island's interior each summer.

    Tip Visit in late June or early July, when the lavender is in full, fragrant bloom.

  6. 06

    Stari Grad

    One of the oldest towns in Europe, its surrounding UNESCO-listed agricultural plain still farmed as it was in ancient Greek times.

    Tip A quieter, more low-key alternative base to Hvar Town itself.

  7. 07

    The view from Fortica

    One of Dalmatia's best panoramas, the harbour town and islands laid out together below.

    Tip Bring a camera with a wide lens — the view sweeps across the whole coastline.

Things to do

Best things to do in Hvar

Hvar rewards splitting time between the harbour's energy and the quieter islands and countryside beyond it.

  1. Climb up to Fortica Španjola

    An essential walk, climbing through the old town's back lanes to the fortress and its panoramic view.

    Tip Go earlier in the day if you'd rather avoid the steepest part of the climb in full heat.

  2. Boat to the Pakleni Islands

    An essential half or full day, swimming and relaxing on the quieter islets just offshore.

    Tip Pack your own snacks and water if visiting a quieter cove without beach bars.

  3. Wander St Stephen's Square

    A relaxed way to spend an hour, taking in the square's cathedral, arsenal and café terraces.

    Tip A good spot to get your bearings before exploring the town's smaller side streets.

  4. Visit the lavender fields in bloom

    A fragrant, photogenic detour into the island's interior, best timed for peak bloom.

    Tip Rent a scooter or join a small tour to reach the fields easily from town.

  5. Day-trip to Stari Grad

    A worthwhile change of pace, exploring one of Europe's oldest continuously inhabited towns.

    Tip An easy bus ride across the island from Hvar Town.

  6. Watch sunset from the harbour

    An essential evening ritual, the harbour glowing as yachts and day-trip boats return for the night.

    Tip Grab a waterfront table well before sunset in peak season, when the best spots fill early.

Local food

What to eat in Hvar

Hvar's food spans classic Dalmatian seafood alongside the island's own respected wine.

  • Fresh grilled seafood

    A highlight of dining here, fish and shellfish grilled simply at harbourfront konobas.

    Where to try it Konobas throughout Hvar Town's harbour and back streets.

  • Hvar wine (Plavac Mali)

    A real point of local pride, bold red wines grown on the island's sun-soaked southern slopes.

    Where to try it Wine bars and konobas throughout Hvar Town.

  • Gregada (Hvar fish stew)

    A distinctive island specialty, a simple stew of fish, potatoes and white wine cooked slowly together.

    Where to try it Traditional konobas away from the busiest harbourfront strip.

  • Dalmatinski pršut (dry-cured ham)

    A reliable starter, often served with local cheese and olives.

    Where to try it Restaurants and konobas throughout Hvar Town.

  • Crni rižot (black risotto)

    A Dalmatian classic, worth trying at least once during a Hvar visit.

    Where to try it Seafood restaurants throughout Hvar Town.

  • Fritule (small fried doughnuts)

    A nice sweet treat, found at bakeries and market stalls around town.

    Where to try it Bakeries throughout Hvar Town.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Hvar

Hvar runs from budget guesthouses to some of Croatia's most exclusive hotels, with prices climbing steeply in peak season.

  • Hvar Town

    The most convenient, liveliest base, though also the priciest and busiest.

    Best for: Being in the middle of the island's nightlife and restaurant scene

  • Stari Grad

    A short bus ride from Hvar Town, with noticeably better value accommodation.

    Best for: A quieter, more low-key stay

Transport

Getting to and around Hvar

Hvar is reached by sea, with no airport of its own on the island.

Getting there

  • Catamaran or ferry from Split

    The most common route, with frequent daily sailings in summer.

    The fast catamaran takes about an hour; the car ferry to Stari Grad takes around two.

  • Ferry from Dubrovnik

    A longer but scenic option, sometimes with a change.

    Seasonal routes run more frequently in summer than winter.

  • Private boat transfer

    A flexible, if pricier, option from Split.

    Widely available through hotels and private operators.

Getting around

Hvar Town's car-free centre is entirely walkable, with boats and buses covering the rest of the island.

  • On foot

    The only way to explore Hvar Town's old streets.

    The harbour, square and fortress all sit within a comfortable walk of each other.

  • Water taxi

    The standard way to reach the Pakleni Islands.

    Frequent, inexpensive departures from the harbour throughout the day.

  • Scooter or bus to Stari Grad and beyond

    Useful for reaching the lavender fields and other towns.

    A scooter offers the most flexibility for exploring the island's interior.

When to go

Best time to visit Hvar

Hvar is at its busiest and most expensive in July and August, with late June and September offering a better balance.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Warm enough to swim, with lavender fields at their fragrant best in late June.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Hot and lively, though Hvar Town gets crowded and prices peak.

  • Early autumn (September)

    Best time

    Still warm enough for the sea, with crowds and prices both easing off noticeably.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Very quiet, with much of the island's hospitality scene closed for the season.

Worth planning around: Deep winter, when most restaurants and hotels close until spring.

Itinerary

Two days in Hvar

Built around Hvar Town's essential sights and a full day on the Pakleni Islands.

  1. Day1

    Hvar Town and Fortica

    The harbour town and its hilltop fortress.

    • Morning St Stephen's Square and the harbour.
    • Afternoon Climb up to Fortica Španjola.
    • Evening Sunset views and dinner in the harbour.
  2. Day2

    The Pakleni Islands

    A full day on the water.

    • Morning Water taxi out to the Pakleni Islands.
    • Afternoon Swim and relax on a quiet cove.
    • Evening A final dinner back in Hvar Town.

Travel tips

Hvar travel tips

Practical notes that make a Hvar visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book ferry and catamaran tickets ahead for peak summer sailings.
  • Book accommodation well ahead — Hvar Town sells out early in July and August.
  • Pack water shoes — many of the island's best coves are pebbly or rocky rather than sandy.
  • Visit in late June for the lavender fields at their fragrant, photogenic best.
  • Escape to the Pakleni Islands when Hvar Town's harbour feels too crowded.
  • Consider Stari Grad for better-value accommodation a short bus ride from the action.

FAQ

Hvar: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Hvar?

Two to three days covers Hvar Town, Fortica and a day on the Pakleni Islands comfortably.

How do you get to Hvar?

By ferry or catamaran from Split, taking one to two hours depending on the service.

Is Hvar expensive?

One of Croatia's pricier islands, especially in July and August, when it draws a notably glamorous crowd.

When do the lavender fields bloom?

Late June into early July is the classic window, when the island's fields are at their most fragrant.

Do you need a car on Hvar?

No, not for Hvar Town itself, though a scooter or bus helps for reaching Stari Grad and the lavender fields.

How does Hvar compare to Korčula?

Livelier and more upscale — Korčula offers a quieter, more low-key version of a similar Dalmatian island experience.
