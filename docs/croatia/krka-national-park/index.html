Welcome to Krka National Park

Krka National Park is genuinely Croatia's other great waterfall park, a quieter, more coastal alternative to the better-known Plitvice Lakes further inland.

Its centrepiece is genuinely Skradinski Buk, a wide, stepped travertine waterfall reached by boardwalks through the Dalmatian hinterland's karst landscape.

A boat trip upriver genuinely reaches Visovac Island, a tiny monastery island sitting in the middle of the Krka River, one of the park's most photographed sights.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Krka National Park

The park's essential sights are genuinely its cascading waterfalls, boardwalks and a monastery island upriver.

  1. 01

    Skradinski Buk

    Genuinely the park's centrepiece, a wide, stepped travertine waterfall and the most visited spot in the entire park.

    Tip Genuinely arrive early or late in the day to see it with fewer visitors on the boardwalks.

  2. 02

    The boardwalks around Skradinski Buk

    Genuinely a network of wooden paths winding directly beside and over the cascading falls.

    Tip Genuinely allow at least an hour to properly cover the full boardwalk loop.

  3. 03

    Visovac Island Monastery

    Genuinely a working Franciscan monastery on a tiny island, reachable only by the park's own boat service.

    Tip Genuinely book the boat trip through the park's official ticket system, included with certain entry tickets.

  4. 04

    Roški Slap

    Genuinely a broader, quieter waterfall area further upstream, with far fewer visitors than Skradinski Buk.

    Tip Genuinely worth the extra effort to reach if you'd like a calmer alternative to the main falls.

  5. 05

    The park's historic watermills

    Genuinely a real piece of local history, restored mills that once ground grain using the river's power.

    Tip Genuinely look for the small ethnographic displays inside some of the restored mill buildings.

  6. 06

    Visovac's monastery church

    Genuinely a peaceful, still-active place of worship, its bell tower visible from the boat long before arrival.

    Tip Genuinely dress modestly, as this remains an active religious site.

  7. 07

    Skradinski Buk's cascading pools

    Genuinely a striking natural formation, dozens of small pools stepping down through travertine barriers.

    Tip Genuinely photograph from multiple boardwalk viewpoints for the most dramatic angles.

Things to do

Best things to do in Krka National Park

Krka genuinely rewards a full day, built around its boardwalks and a boat trip to the monastery island.

  1. Walk the boardwalks around Skradinski Buk

    A genuinely essential first stop, following wooden paths directly beside the park's widest, most dramatic falls.

    Tip Genuinely wear non-slip shoes — the boardwalks can be damp near the falls.

  2. Take a boat to Visovac Island

    A genuinely lovely, calm interlude, gliding upriver to the tiny monastery island.

    Tip Genuinely check whether the boat trip is included in your ticket or needs booking separately.

  3. Visit Roški Slap, a quieter waterfall

    A genuinely worthwhile detour for a calmer experience, a broader falls area with far fewer visitors than Skradinski Buk.

    Tip Genuinely check the park shuttle schedule if you're not driving between the two areas yourself.

  4. Explore the park's historic watermills

    A genuinely interesting cultural stop, restored mills showing how the river once powered daily life.

    Tip Genuinely a nice change of pace between longer stretches of walking.

  5. Photograph Skradinski Buk's cascading pools

    A genuinely rewarding pursuit, the layered travertine pools photographing beautifully from multiple boardwalk angles.

    Tip Genuinely visit after rainfall for the falls at their fullest, most dramatic flow.

  6. Learn about the park's ethnographic heritage

    A genuinely worthwhile stop for context, small exhibits showing traditional Dalmatian rural life along the river.

    Tip Genuinely look for information panels near the restored mill buildings.

Transport

Getting to and around Krka National Park

Krka National Park is genuinely one of the easiest national parks to reach from the Dalmatian coast.

Getting there

  • Car or organised day trip from Split

    Genuinely the most common route, taking about an hour each way.

    Widely available as a guided day trip if you'd rather not drive.

  • Car or day trip from Zadar

    Genuinely similarly convenient, around an hour by road.

    A popular add-on for those based in or passing through Zadar.

  • Boat trip from Skradin

    Genuinely a scenic way to enter the park directly from the river.

    Boats run from Skradin town to the Skradinski Buk entrance.

Getting around

Getting around the park genuinely means walking the boardwalks, with a boat covering the river sections.

  • On foot along the boardwalks

    Genuinely the way to see Skradinski Buk itself.

    A marked loop covers the main falls area in about an hour or two.

  • Park boat to Visovac and Roški Slap

    Genuinely the only way to reach the monastery island and the upper falls.

    Included with certain ticket types, or bookable separately at the dock.

  • Shuttle bus between park areas

    Genuinely useful for covering the distance between Skradinski Buk and Roški Slap.

    Runs on a schedule during the main visitor season.

When to go

Best time to visit Krka National Park

Krka is genuinely at its best outside the peak midsummer crowds, when the falls are fuller and the boardwalks less busy.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely lush, with waterfalls at their fullest after spring rain.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Genuinely the busiest season, with the boardwalks crowded at peak midday hours.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Genuinely comfortable temperatures with noticeably thinner crowds than summer.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Genuinely quiet, with some park facilities and boat services reduced for the season.

Worth planning around: Genuinely peak midday hours in July and August, when the Skradinski Buk boardwalks get most crowded.

Travel tips

Krka National Park travel tips

Practical notes that make a Krka National Park visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book genuinely park entry tickets online ahead in peak season.
  • Note genuinely that swimming at Skradinski Buk has been prohibited since 2021 to protect the ecosystem, despite older photos and guides suggesting otherwise.
  • Wear genuinely non-slip shoes — boardwalks get damp and slippery near the falls.
  • Start genuinely early to see Skradinski Buk before the day's crowds build.
  • Check genuinely whether the Visovac Island boat trip is included in your ticket type.
  • Bring genuinely water and snacks — options inside the park itself are limited.

FAQ

Krka National Park: frequently asked questions

Can you swim at Krka National Park?

No, genuinely not anymore — swimming at Skradinski Buk has been prohibited since 2021 to protect the travertine formations, despite this once being one of the park's most famous features.

How does Krka compare to Plitvice Lakes?

Genuinely similar in style, with waterfalls and boardwalks, though Krka is noticeably closer to the coast and sees fewer visitors.

How many days do you need at Krka?

One full day genuinely covers Skradinski Buk and a boat trip to Visovac Island comfortably.

Is Krka worth visiting from Split or Zadar?

Yes, genuinely — it's about an hour from either city, an easy and rewarding day trip.

Do you need to book the boat to Visovac Island ahead?

It's genuinely worth checking — some ticket types include it, while others require a separate booking at the dock.

Is Krka busy in summer?

Skradinski Buk genuinely gets crowded at midday in July and August; arriving early or visiting Roški Slap instead both help avoid the worst of it.
