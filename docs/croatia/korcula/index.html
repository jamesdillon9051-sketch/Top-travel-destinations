Welcome to Korčula

Korčula is genuinely a quieter, equally beautiful alternative to Hvar, a walled medieval town on its own small peninsula off Croatia's Pelješac Peninsula.

Its old town is genuinely famous for a clever herringbone street plan, angled to funnel cooling sea breezes through the main streets while blocking the harsher winter winds.

The island genuinely also claims to be the birthplace of Marco Polo, and produces some of Croatia's most respected white wine from vineyards right outside the old town walls.

Things to do

Best things to do in Korčula

Korčula genuinely rewards a slow pace, best spent wandering the old town and tasting the island's own wine.

  1. Walk the old town's herringbone streets

    A genuinely fascinating piece of town planning, streets deliberately angled to channel sea breezes and block winter winds.

    Tip Genuinely compare the straight main street to the curved side streets to see the clever design in action.

  2. Tour Marco Polo's alleged birthplace

    A genuinely fun, low-key stop, climbing the modest house's tower for a view over the old town's rooftops.

    Tip Genuinely don't expect much in the way of historical artefacts — this is more atmosphere than museum.

  3. Explore St Mark's Cathedral

    A genuinely worthwhile visit, taking in the cathedral's carved stonework and Venetian-era artwork.

    Tip Genuinely combine with the Bishop's Treasury next door for a fuller sense of the island's history.

  4. Watch a Moreška sword dance performance

    A genuinely unique local tradition, a centuries-old choreographed sword dance performed in the old town in summer.

    Tip Genuinely check performance schedules ahead — shows typically run on a set evening each week in season.

  5. Swim at a beach outside the walls

    A genuinely easy way to cool off, small pebble beaches dotted around the peninsula just outside the old town.

    Tip Genuinely rent a kayak or paddleboard for a closer look at the walls from the water.

  6. Photograph the old town from the water

    A genuinely rewarding pursuit, the walled peninsula town photographing beautifully from nearly any angle offshore.

    Tip Genuinely golden hour light works especially well on the old town's pale stone walls.

Local food

What to eat in Korčula

Korčula's food genuinely centres on its own respected white wine alongside classic Dalmatian cooking.

  • Pošip wine

    Genuinely one of Croatia's most respected white wines, a grape grown almost exclusively on this island.

    Where to try it Wine bars throughout the old town, or wineries a short drive inland.

  • Žrnovski makaruni

    Genuinely a distinctive local pasta, hand-rolled in the nearby village of Žrnovo and traditionally served with a rich meat sauce.

    Where to try it Traditional konobas, especially those specialising in Korčula home cooking.

  • Fresh grilled seafood

    Genuinely a highlight of dining here, fish and shellfish grilled simply at old town konobas.

    Where to try it Konobas throughout Korčula's old town.

  • Dalmatinski pršut (dry-cured ham)

    Genuinely a reliable starter, often served with local cheese and olives.

    Where to try it Restaurants and konobas throughout the old town.

  • Crni rižot (black risotto)

    Genuinely a Dalmatian classic, worth trying at least once during a Korčula visit.

    Where to try it Seafood restaurants throughout the old town.

  • Fritule (small fried doughnuts)

    Genuinely a nice sweet treat, found at bakeries around the old town.

    Where to try it Bakeries throughout Korčula's old town.

Transport

Getting to and around Korčula

Korčula is genuinely reached by ferry, most commonly from Dubrovnik or Split.

Getting there

  • Catamaran from Dubrovnik

    Genuinely the most common route, with seasonal daily sailings.

    Takes around two to three hours depending on the service.

  • Ferry from Split

    Genuinely a longer but scenic option through the Dalmatian islands.

    Journey times vary depending on stops, generally three to five hours.

  • Car ferry via Pelješac Peninsula

    Genuinely the quickest option if driving.

    A short car ferry crossing connects Korčula to the mainland peninsula.

Getting around

Korčula's genuinely compact old town is entirely walkable, with a car or scooter useful for the wider island.

  • On foot

    Genuinely the only way to explore the old town's herringbone streets.

    Everything within the walls sits within a few minutes' walk.

  • Bus or scooter to nearby villages

    Genuinely useful for reaching Lumbarda's beaches and vineyards.

    A short ride connects the old town to the island's other villages.

  • Bicycle

    Genuinely a pleasant way to explore the island's quieter roads.

    Widely available for rent near the old town.
