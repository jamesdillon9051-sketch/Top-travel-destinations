Welcome to Pula

Pula is genuinely home to one of the best-preserved Roman amphitheatres anywhere on Earth, a full oval arena still standing nearly two thousand years after it was built.

Beyond the Arena, the city's genuinely Roman core continues with a well-preserved temple, a triumphal arch and a cathedral, all within an easy walk of the harbour.

It's also genuinely Istria's largest city and its main gateway, well positioned for day trips to Cape Kamenjak's wild coastline and the Brijuni Islands National Park just offshore.

Local food

What to eat in Pula

Pula's food genuinely shares Istria's distinct culinary identity, built around truffles, wine and a rare local cattle breed.

  • Istrian white truffle

    Genuinely one of Istria's most celebrated products, shaved fresh over pasta or eggs at restaurants across the region.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout Pula's old town.

  • Istrian pršut (prosciutto)

    Genuinely distinct from Dalmatian pršut, cured with a different process and noticeably milder flavour.

    Where to try it Restaurants and delis throughout Pula.

  • Boškarin (Istrian ox)

    Genuinely a rare, protected local cattle breed, its rich meat a real point of regional culinary pride.

    Where to try it Traditional Istrian restaurants, especially those specialising in regional cuisine.

  • Malvazija (Istrian white wine)

    Genuinely the region's signature grape, a crisp, aromatic white produced throughout Istria.

    Where to try it Wine bars throughout Pula's old town.

  • Fresh grilled seafood

    Genuinely a highlight of dining here, fish and shellfish grilled simply at restaurants near the harbour.

    Where to try it Restaurants along Pula's waterfront.

  • Fritule (small fried doughnuts)

    Genuinely a nice sweet treat, found at bakeries and cafés throughout the old town.

    Where to try it Bakeries throughout Pula.

Pula is genuinely Istria's main transport hub, with its own international airport.

Pula's genuinely compact old town is entirely walkable, with a car or bus needed for Cape Kamenjak and the Brijuni ferry dock.

FAQ

Pula: frequently asked questions

Is Pula Arena worth visiting?

Yes, genuinely — it's one of only six Roman amphitheatres left this complete anywhere in the world.

How many days do you need in Pula?

One to two days genuinely covers the Roman old town, with a second day for Cape Kamenjak or the Brijuni Islands.

How do you get to the Brijuni Islands?

Genuinely by an official boat and guided tour from Fažana, a short drive from Pula — independent access isn't permitted.

Is Pula worth visiting compared to Rovinj?

Yes, genuinely — the two offer quite different experiences, with Pula's Roman history complementing Rovinj's postcard coastal setting well.

Is Cape Kamenjak good for swimming?

Yes, genuinely — it's known for some of Istria's clearest water, though most coves require your own transport to reach.

Can you see concerts at Pula Arena?

Yes, genuinely — the Arena regularly hosts concerts and film festivals through the summer months.
