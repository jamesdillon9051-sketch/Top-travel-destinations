Why go to Croatia

Croatia is genuinely one of Europe's most photogenic countries, a slim crescent of coastline where medieval walled cities meet a thousand islands scattered across the Adriatic.

Its case rests genuinely on more than just the coast — Zagreb's café culture and Plitvice Lakes' cascading turquoise pools both hold their own against the Dalmatian islands that usually steal the spotlight.

Getting around genuinely means a mix of ferries, coastal driving and island-hopping, the whole country built around water in a way few destinations in Europe can match.

Experiences

Best things to do in Croatia

The experiences that recur across the country and make a Croatia trip feel like more than a checklist of walled towns.

  1. Walk Dubrovnik's city walls

    A genuinely essential Croatia experience, circling the entire Old Town on a walkway that's stood since the Middle Ages.

    Tip Genuinely start at opening time in summer — both the heat and the crowds build fast along the walls.

  2. Wander Split's living Roman palace

    A genuinely unique experience, an entire neighbourhood of homes, shops and cafés built inside a 1,700-year-old Roman emperor's retirement palace.

    Tip Genuinely visit the cathedral's bell tower for a view straight down into the palace's ancient layout.

  3. Island-hop the Dalmatian coast by ferry

    A genuinely essential part of Croatian travel, hopping between islands by an extensive, reliable ferry network.

    Tip Genuinely book car ferries ahead in peak summer; foot-passenger tickets are usually fine to buy on the day.

  4. Eat fresh seafood at a konoba

    A genuinely essential ritual, simply grilled fish and seafood at a traditional family-run konoba.

    Tip Genuinely ask what came in that day — the best konobas cook around the morning's catch, not a fixed menu.

  5. Follow Plitvice's boardwalks over turquoise water

    A genuinely magical walk, wooden boardwalks winding directly over and beside cascading turquoise pools.

    Tip Genuinely swimming isn't permitted in the lakes — the boardwalk views are the main event here.

  6. Drink Croatian wine

    A genuinely underrated part of a Croatia trip, with Istria and the Pelješac Peninsula both producing excellent, increasingly respected wine.

    Tip Genuinely seek out a small family winery rather than a resort wine list for the best, most distinctive bottles.

Food and drink

Croatia foods to try

Croatian food genuinely splits along its coastline and interior, from Adriatic seafood to heartier Central European cooking inland.

  • Peka (meat or seafood slow-cooked under a bell)

    Genuinely a Dalmatian classic, meat, seafood and vegetables slow-cooked together under a metal dome buried in embers.

    Where to try it Traditional konobas throughout Dalmatia — usually order ahead, as it takes hours to cook.

  • Crni rižot (black risotto)

    Genuinely a striking Dalmatian classic, rice cooked in cuttlefish or squid ink for a deep, briny flavour.

    Where to try it Seafood restaurants throughout the Dalmatian coast.

  • Dalmatinski pršut (dry-cured ham)

    Genuinely a source of real regional pride, wind-cured ham often served simply with local cheese and olives.

    Where to try it Restaurants and konobas throughout Dalmatia.

  • Fresh grilled seafood

    Genuinely a highlight of coastal dining, fish and shellfish grilled simply to let the freshness speak for itself.

    Where to try it Konobas and seafood restaurants along the entire coast.

  • Croatian wine

    Genuinely worth exploring beyond the obvious, with Istria's whites and Pelješac's bold reds both increasingly recognised internationally.

    Where to try it Wine bars and family wineries throughout Istria and Dalmatia.

  • Fritule (small fried doughnuts)

    Genuinely a beloved sweet treat, bite-sized fried dough often flavoured with rum, raisins or citrus zest.

    Where to try it Bakeries and markets throughout the country, especially around winter holidays.

When to go

Best time to visit Croatia

Croatia's genuinely long, hot summer draws the biggest crowds and highest prices, with shoulder months offering the best overall balance.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely warm enough to swim, with noticeably thinner crowds than July and August.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    Genuinely hot and lively, though Dubrovnik and Hvar get genuinely packed and prices peak.

  • Early autumn (September)

    Best time

    Genuinely still warm enough for the sea, with the summer crowds noticeably thinned out.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Genuinely quiet along the coast, with many island businesses closed for the season; Zagreb stays lively year-round.

Worth planning around: Genuinely nothing strongly, though peak July and August bring the coast's highest prices and heaviest crowds.

Croatia's genuinely elongated coastline means a rushed itinerary spends more time travelling than exploring.

Getting around

Transportation in Croatia

A mix of ferries, buses and driving is genuinely the standard way to see Croatia — the coastline and islands make this less a single road trip than a series of connected hops.

  • Ferries and catamarans

    Genuinely essential for reaching Hvar, Korčula and other islands from Split or Dubrovnik.

    Jadrolinija and several private operators run frequent routes in summer, fewer in winter.

  • Long-distance buses

    Genuinely comfortable and reliable for coastal routes between Zagreb, Zadar, Split and Dubrovnik.

    The coastal road offers genuinely spectacular views for most of the journey.

  • Renting a car

    Genuinely useful for reaching Plitvice Lakes, Krka National Park and Rovinj in Istria at your own pace.

    Not needed on the smaller car-free islands, where walking covers everything.

  • Domestic flights

    Genuinely useful for connecting Zagreb to Dubrovnik quickly.

    A short hop that saves the better part of a day compared to the coastal drive.

  • Walking within old towns

    Genuinely the only way to explore Dubrovnik, Split and Rovinj's historic centres.

    All three are compact, car-free and genuinely made for wandering on foot.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Croatia

Croatian culture genuinely blends Central European and Mediterranean influences, with real regional pride running deep between Dalmatia, Istria and the inland capital.

  • Greetings are genuinely warm but not overly familiar

    A handshake is standard on first meeting; closer acquaintances may greet with a cheek kiss.

  • Coffee culture runs genuinely deep

    Sitting for a slow coffee is a genuine daily ritual, not a quick grab-and-go habit.

  • Regional pride is genuinely real

    Dalmatian, Istrian and Zagreb identities are each distinct — avoid generalising too broadly about 'Croatia' to locals.

  • Dress modestly for churches

    Covered shoulders and knees are genuinely expected when visiting religious sites.

  • Tipping is genuinely modest but appreciated

    Around 10% at restaurants, rounding up for cafés and casual service.

  • Swimming spots are genuinely taken seriously

    Respect posted rules around protected areas like Plitvice Lakes, where swimming is banned to protect the ecosystem.

Do

  • Book ferries and accommodation well ahead for July and August travel.
  • Try the regional specialities — Dalmatian and Istrian cuisine genuinely differ from each other.
  • Wear comfortable shoes — old town streets are almost entirely stone and can be slippery when wet.
  • Carry a little cash for smaller island businesses, even though cards are widely accepted.

Don't

  • Don't swim or wade in Plitvice Lakes National Park — it's prohibited to protect the travertine formations.
  • Don't expect a rushed pace on the islands — service and ferries alike run on a relaxed island schedule.
  • Don't underestimate summer heat — pace outdoor sightseeing for the cooler morning and evening hours.
  • Don't skip travel insurance for ferry-heavy itineraries — schedules can shift with weather.

Travel tips

Croatia travel tips

Practical notes that make travel through Croatia noticeably smoother.

  • Book genuinely ferries and popular accommodation well ahead for a July or August trip.
  • Pack genuinely comfortable, grippy shoes — old town streets are polished stone and can be slippery.
  • Carry genuinely a little cash for smaller island businesses and konobas.
  • Check genuinely ferry schedules the day before travel — routes can shift with weather or season.
  • Visit genuinely Dubrovnik's walls first thing in the morning to beat both heat and crowds.
  • Pace genuinely outdoor sightseeing around the cooler morning and evening hours in peak summer.
  • Book genuinely Plitvice Lakes tickets online ahead in peak season — daily visitor numbers are capped.
  • Consider genuinely basing a trip around fewer islands rather than rushing between many.

FAQ

Visiting Croatia: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Croatia?

Ten to fourteen days genuinely covers Zagreb, Plitvice Lakes and the main Dalmatian coast at a reasonable pace, which is the itinerary in this guide. A week is enough for a focused Dubrovnik-and-Split trip.

Is Croatia expensive?

Genuinely mid-range by European standards outside peak summer, though Dubrovnik and Hvar in July and August run noticeably higher.

Do you need a car in Croatia?

No, genuinely not for the main Dalmatian coast — buses and ferries cover it well, though a car helps for Istria and the national parks.

Is Croatia on the euro?

Yes, genuinely — Croatia adopted the euro in January 2023, alongside joining the Schengen Area.

Can you swim in Plitvice Lakes?

No, genuinely not — swimming is prohibited throughout the national park to protect its travertine formations.

Is Croatia safe?

Yes, genuinely — one of Europe's safer countries, with petty theft in busy tourist areas the main thing to watch for.
