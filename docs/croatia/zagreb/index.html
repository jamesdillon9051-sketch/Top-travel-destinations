Welcome to Zagreb

Zagreb is genuinely Croatia's most underrated city, a Central European capital most visitors skip entirely on their way to the coast, to their own real loss.

Its Upper Town genuinely preserves a compact medieval core, crowned by St Mark's Church and its instantly recognisable tiled roof, while the Lower Town's grid of Austro-Hungarian boulevards holds museums, parks and squares.

What Zagreb genuinely does best is a slower pace than the coast — long coffees on Tkalčićeva Street, a proper daily market at Dolac, and a lively, youthful energy that runs year-round rather than just in summer.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Zagreb

Zagreb's essential sights are genuinely split between the historic Upper Town and the grid-planned Lower Town below it.

  1. 01

    St Mark's Church

    Genuinely the city's most photographed landmark, its tiled roof displaying the coats of arms of Zagreb and the Triune Kingdom.

    Tip Genuinely the small square out front gets crowded — visit early for photos without a crowd in frame.

  2. 02

    Zagreb Cathedral

    Genuinely the country's tallest building, its twin neo-Gothic spires visible across much of the city.

    Tip Genuinely a short walk from Dolac Market — an easy pairing for a morning wander.

  3. 03

    Ban Jelačić Square

    Genuinely the city's central meeting point, a grand square named for the 19th-century ban whose statue dominates it.

    Tip Genuinely a natural starting point for exploring both the Upper and Lower Towns.

  4. 04

    Dolac Market

    Genuinely a real, working farmers' market, its red umbrellas covering stalls of fresh produce, cheese and flowers.

    Tip Genuinely go in the morning, when the market is at its freshest and liveliest.

  5. 05

    Lotrščak Tower

    Genuinely a medieval defensive tower, famous today for a cannon still fired from its top every day at noon.

    Tip Genuinely climb up for one of the best views over the Lower Town's rooftops.

  6. 06

    The Stone Gate

    Genuinely the only surviving gate from the medieval city walls, now home to a revered shrine to the Virgin Mary.

    Tip Genuinely a quiet, atmospheric stop, often with candles lit by visitors passing through.

  7. 07

    The Museum of Broken Relationships

    Genuinely one of the city's most memorable, unusual museums, objects and stories donated from real failed relationships worldwide.

    Tip Genuinely allow more time than you'd expect — the stories are genuinely absorbing.

Things to do

Best things to do in Zagreb

Zagreb genuinely rewards a slower pace, built around markets, museums and a proper café culture.

  1. Watch the Lotrščak Tower's noon cannon

    A genuinely fun daily ritual, a cannon fired from the tower every day at exactly noon, a tradition dating back generations.

    Tip Genuinely time your Upper Town visit to be nearby at midday for the moment itself.

  2. Wander Tkalčićeva Street

    A genuinely essential Zagreb experience, a pedestrian street lined with cafés built for exactly this kind of slow wandering.

    Tip Genuinely at its liveliest in the early evening, when locals fill every outdoor table.

  3. Browse Dolac Market

    A genuinely authentic slice of daily Zagreb life, browsing stalls of fresh produce, cheese and flowers.

    Tip Genuinely a great spot to pick up picnic supplies or local cheese to take home.

  4. Visit the Museum of Broken Relationships

    A genuinely unusual, moving hour, reading real stories behind donated objects from relationships around the world.

    Tip Genuinely worth going in without reading too much beforehand — let the exhibits surprise you.

  5. Touch the Stone Gate's shrine

    A genuinely quiet, reflective stop, joining locals who still stop to pray as they pass through.

    Tip Genuinely keep your voice down — this remains an active, respected place of devotion.

  6. Sit for coffee on Ban Jelačić Square

    A genuinely essential Zagreb pastime, watching the city's trams and crowds go by from a square-side café.

    Tip Genuinely a good spot to get your bearings before heading up to the Upper Town.

Local food

What to eat in Zagreb

Zagreb's food genuinely leans more Central European than the coast, with its own distinct specialities.

  • Zagrebački odrezak (Zagreb-style schnitzel)

    Genuinely the city's namesake dish, a breaded veal cutlet stuffed with ham and cheese.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout the Lower Town.

  • Štrukli

    Genuinely a beloved regional specialty, thin dough layered with cottage cheese, baked or boiled.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout the city, especially in the Upper Town.

  • Ćevapi

    Genuinely a reliable, inexpensive classic, grilled minced meat served with flatbread and onion.

    Where to try it Casual restaurants throughout the city centre.

  • Kremšnita (custard cream cake)

    Genuinely a nearby regional favourite from Samobor, layers of custard and cream between crisp pastry.

    Where to try it Cafés and patisseries throughout Zagreb.

  • Croatian wine

    Genuinely worth exploring in Zagreb's wine bars, with continental Croatia's whites alongside coastal reds.

    Where to try it Wine bars throughout the Lower Town.

  • Fritule (small fried doughnuts)

    Genuinely a nice sweet treat, found at bakeries and cafés throughout the city.

    Where to try it Bakeries and cafés throughout Zagreb.

Transport

Getting to and around Zagreb

Zagreb is genuinely well connected by air and rail to the rest of Europe, and by road to the Dalmatian coast.

Getting there

  • Direct international flights

    Genuinely available from many European cities year-round.

    Zagreb Airport sits about 30 minutes from the city centre.

  • Train from nearby capitals

    Genuinely a comfortable option from Vienna, Budapest or Ljubljana.

    Journey times range from about two to six hours depending on the route.

  • Bus or car to the Dalmatian coast

    Genuinely straightforward via a modern motorway.

    About five to six hours to Split, or a shorter hop to Plitvice Lakes.

Getting around

Zagreb's genuinely compact centre is easily walkable, with trams and a funicular covering the rest.

  • On foot

    Genuinely the best way to see the Upper and Lower Towns together.

    Most major sights sit within a comfortable 20-minute walk of Ban Jelačić Square.

  • Trams

    Genuinely useful for reaching neighbourhoods further from the centre.

    A frequent, easy-to-use network across the wider city.

  • The Zagreb funicular

    Genuinely one of the shortest funiculars in the world, connecting the Lower and Upper Towns.

    A fun, quick alternative to the short uphill walk.

FAQ

Zagreb: frequently asked questions

Is Zagreb worth visiting?

Yes, genuinely — it's an underrated Central European capital with real café culture, quirky museums and a lively year-round atmosphere.

How many days do you need in Zagreb?

Two to three days genuinely covers the Upper and Lower Towns comfortably, with time for a museum or two.

Is Zagreb cheaper than the Croatian coast?

Yes, genuinely — accommodation, food and drink all tend to run noticeably cheaper than Dubrovnik or Hvar.

Can you day-trip to Plitvice Lakes from Zagreb?

Yes, genuinely — it's about two hours by road, a popular day trip or first stop heading toward the coast.

Is Zagreb walkable?

Yes, genuinely — the Upper and Lower Towns both sit within easy walking distance of the central square.

When is Zagreb's Christmas market?

Genuinely throughout December, widely regarded as one of Europe's best, spread across the Upper and Lower Towns.
