Why go to Chile

Chile is a ribbon of land about 4,300 kilometres long and rarely more than 180 wide, squeezed between the Andes and the Pacific. That shape gives it extreme variety: the driest desert on Earth in the north, Mediterranean vineyards in the middle, lakes and volcanoes in the south and glaciers and granite peaks in Patagonia.

Santiago is the capital and the usual starting point, a modern city beneath the Andes. From there, most visitors head north to the Atacama for stargazing and salt flats, or south to the Lake District and Patagonia for volcanoes, forests and the Torres del Paine trekking circuit. Easter Island, a Polynesian outpost 3,700 km offshore, is a destination of its own.

Chile is safe, well organised and easy to travel in, with excellent food and wine. The long distances mean flights are often the best option, so choose a few regions rather than trying to see everything. Seasons are reversed from the northern hemisphere, so plan for December to February as summer.

Experiences

Best things to do in Chile

The experiences that recur across the country and are worth planning around.

  1. Hike in Patagonia

    Torres del Paine and the Carretera Austral offer some of the best trekking in the world.

    Tip Book refugios and campsites months ahead.

  2. Stargaze in the Atacama

    Some of the clearest skies on Earth, with observatory tours.

    Tip Book tours in advance.

  3. Taste Chilean wine

    Visit the Maipo, Colchagua or Casablanca valleys.

    Tip Try Carmenere.

  5. See the moai

    Easter Island's giant statues.

    Tip Buy the park ticket on arrival.

  6. Wander Valparaiso

    Funiculars, street art and views.

    Tip Wear sturdy shoes.

  7. Eat seafood

    Fresh fish and shellfish along the coast.

    Tip Try curanto on Chiloe.

Food and drink

Chile foods to try

Chilean food is built on seafood, beef, corn and good wine, with regional specialities from the desert to Chiloe.

  • Empanada de pino

    A baked pastry filled with beef, onion, olive and egg.

    Where to try it Bakeries and markets nationwide.

  • Pastel de choclo

    A corn pie baked over beef and chicken.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants.

  • Completo

    A loaded hot dog with avocado, tomato and mayonnaise.

    Where to try it Fast-food stands in every city.

  • Pisco sour

    Pisco with lime and sugar, the national cocktail.

    Where to try it Bars and restaurants.

  • Curanto

    A feast of seafood, meat and potatoes cooked in a pit.

    Where to try it Chiloe restaurants.

  • Chilean wine

    Carmenere, Cabernet and Sauvignon Blanc.

    Where to try it Wine bars and valley estates.

Before you go

Practical travel information for Chile

The essentials, current for 2026. Chile is straightforward for most visitors, but check official sources before you travel.

Capital
Santiago
Language
Spanish is the official language. English is spoken in tourism, but less so elsewhere.
Currency
Chilean peso (CLP). Roughly 900-1,000 pesos to the US dollar in recent years.
Cards and cash
Cards are widely accepted. Carry cash for rural areas, markets and small shops. ATMs are common.
Entry and visas
Many nationalities, including the US, UK, EU and Australia, can enter visa-free for tourism for up to 90 days, but rules change, so check before you go.
Power sockets
Type C and L, 220 V, 50 Hz. Bring an adaptor.
Emergency number
133 for police, 131 for ambulance, 132 for fire. 911 is not used.
Tipping
Around 10% is customary in restaurants and is often added to the bill as a suggested tip.
Safety
Chile is one of the safest countries in South America. Take normal precautions against pickpockets in cities, follow advice on volcanoes and trails, and be prepared for earthquakes.
Internet and SIM
Good in cities and towns, patchy in Patagonia and the Atacama. Local SIMs from Entel, Movistar or Claro are inexpensive.
Time zone
Chile Standard Time (UTC-4), with daylight saving in the southern summer. Easter Island is two hours behind the mainland.

When to go

Best time to visit Chile

Southern summer, from November to March, is best for Patagonia, the Lake District and Chiloe.

  • November-March

    Best time

    Long days and open trails in the south.

  • March-May

    Best time

    Autumn colours, wine harvest and fewer crowds.

  • September-November

    Good

    Spring, with the Atacama and Santiago at their best.

  • June-August

    Good

    Ski season in the Andes, but cold and wet in the south.

Worth planning around: Winter in Patagonia, when trails, refugios and ferries close.

Trip length

How long to spend in Chile

Chile is long, so be selective. Two regions plus Santiago is enough for most.

  • 5-7 days

    Santiago, Valparaiso and the Atacama, or Santiago and the Lake District.

    A taste of the country.

  • 10 days

    Santiago, the Atacama and Patagonia.

    The classic combination.

  • 14 days

    Add the Lake District and Chiloe.

    A fuller picture.

  • 3 weeks

    Add the Carretera Austral and Easter Island.

    For slow travellers.

Getting around

Transportation in Chile

Flights are the practical way to cover the long distances. Buses are comfortable for shorter routes.

  • Domestic flights

    Santiago to Calama, Punta Arenas, Puerto Montt and Easter Island.

    LATAM and Sky Airline are the main carriers.

  • Long-distance buses

    Comfortable semi-cama and cama seats.

    Book ahead for holidays.

  • Rental car

    Best for the Lake District, Elqui and the Carretera Austral.

    Check insurance and gravel roads.

  • Ferries

    Across fjords and to Chiloe.

    Book ahead on the Carretera Austral.

  • Metro and taxis

    In Santiago and Valparaiso.

    Use apps at night.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Chile

Chileans are polite, warm and a little formal, and family and conversation matter. Greeting and farewell customs are important, and punctuality is relaxed socially.

  • Greet properly

    A handshake in business, a single cheek kiss in social settings.

  • Be polite with titles

    Use senor and senora until invited.

  • Expect late dinners

    Dinner is often after 8:30 pm.

  • Join the sobremesa

    Lingering after meals is normal.

  • Be patient with time

    Social events start late.

  • Respect nature

    Follow Leave No Trace in parks.

Do

  • Learn basic Spanish phrases
  • Say buenos dias to shopkeepers
  • Tip about 10% at restaurants
  • Carry ID or a copy
  • Respect trail rules in parks
  • Be on time for tours

Don't

  • Do not leave valuables visible
  • Do not photograph military sites
  • Do not drink and drive
  • Do not litter in parks
  • Do not touch moai
  • Do not ignore volcano or weather alerts

Travel tips

Chile travel tips

The things that most often catch visitors out in Chile.

  • Book Torres del Paine refugios and Easter Island flights months ahead.
  • Remember the seasons are reversed.
  • Flights are cheaper if booked early.
  • Carry layers, since weather changes quickly.
  • Bring cash for remote areas.
  • Check for ferry and road closures in Patagonia.
  • Watch for pickpockets in city centres.
  • Know earthquake procedures at your hotel.

FAQ

Visiting Chile: frequently asked questions

Is Chile safe for tourists?

Yes, it is one of the safest countries in South America.

How many days do I need?

Ten to fourteen days for a good trip.

Do I need a visa?

Many nationalities do not for stays up to 90 days, but check.

What is the best time to visit?

November to March for Patagonia, and March to May or September to November for the north and centre.

Is Chile expensive?

It is moderately pricey, especially Patagonia and Easter Island.

Can I see everything in one trip?

No, choose two or three regions.
