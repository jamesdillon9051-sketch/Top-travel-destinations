Welcome to Valparaiso

Valparaiso rises from the Pacific in a jumble of steep hills, brightly painted houses and winding staircases. Once the most important port on the Pacific coast of South America, it declined after the opening of the Panama Canal, and its faded grandeur and bohemian spirit are now part of the charm. The historic quarter is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The best way to see it is to wander the hills, especially Cerro Alegre and Cerro Concepcion, with their murals, cafes, galleries and views. Old funiculars, called ascensores, connect the hills with the flat port area below. Pablo Neruda's house, La Sebastiana, is a short ride away and is full of curious objects.

Nearby Vina del Mar is a more formal seaside resort with beaches and gardens, and the area also has seafood restaurants and coastal towns. Expect to climb a lot, and watch your valuables in the port area.

Travel tips

Valparaiso travel tips

Tips for visiting.

  • Wear comfortable shoes for steep streets.
  • Watch valuables near the port.
  • Visit on weekdays for fewer crowds.
  • Carry small change for ascensores.
  • Take taxis at night.
  • Book hills hotels ahead.
  • Check ascensor opening times.
