Welcome to Chiloe

Chiloe is a large island off the coast of southern Chile, shaped by rain, sea and a distinct culture. The Chilotes developed their own architecture, cuisine, myths and traditions, and the island is full of wooden churches, stilt houses and misty shores. Sixteen of its churches are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Castro, the capital, is known for its palafitos, wooden houses on stilts above the tide, and the colourful San Francisco church. Elsewhere, Achao and Nercon have beautiful churches, and the nearby islands of Quinchao and Aucar offer a slower pace. The island also has national parks, tidal shores, and colonies of Magellanic and Humboldt penguins at Puñihuil.

Chilote food is a highlight, especially curanto, a stew of seafood, meat and potatoes cooked in a pit. Weather is wet and changeable, but the sun often appears, and the island is a gentle, atmospheric place to slow down.

When to go

Best time to visit Chiloe

Summer is the driest and warmest, and penguins nest from October to March.

    Best time

  • October-November

    Good

    Quiet

Chiloe travel tips

Tips for Chiloe.

  • Pack rain gear.
  • Book accommodation early in summer.
