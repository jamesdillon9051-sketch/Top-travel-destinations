Welcome to Easter Island (Rapa Nui)

Rapa Nui, as locals call it, lies some 3,700 km west of Chile and is one of the most isolated inhabited places in the world. Polynesian settlers arrived here around a thousand years ago and carved nearly 900 monumental statues, called moai, from volcanic rock. Most of the island is a UNESCO World Heritage Site within Rapa Nui National Park.

The big sights are the quarry at Rano Raraku, where hundreds of moai lie in various stages of completion, the fifteen restored moai of Ahu Tongariki, the white sand of Anakena beach, and Orongo, a ceremonial village on the rim of a crater, linked to the birdman cult. Hanga Roa, the only town, has restaurants, craft markets and a tiny harbour.

The island is small, about 24 km across, and easy to explore by car, scooter or bike. Most visitors fly in from Santiago, and the island is culturally Polynesian, with the Rapa Nui people and their language, music and tattoo art alive today. Flights and accommodation are expensive, so book early.

Travel tips

Easter Island (Rapa Nui) travel tips

Plan carefully for a remote island.

  • Book flights and lodging early.
  • Buy the national park ticket on arrival.
  • Respect moai and do not climb.
  • Bring cash since cards are not accepted everywhere.
  • Carry sun protection.
  • Hire a guide for context.
  • Pack layers.
