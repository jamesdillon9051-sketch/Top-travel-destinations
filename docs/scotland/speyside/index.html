Welcome to Speyside

Speyside is genuinely Scotland's whisky heartland, home to the single densest concentration of distilleries found anywhere in the country, strung along the beautiful River Spey valley.

The region produces genuinely more than half of all Scotch whisky, its distinctly smooth, often fruity style shaped by the same river water that also makes the Spey one of the world's most celebrated salmon fishing rivers.

Most visitors genuinely base themselves in or near Dufftown, following the signposted Malt Whisky Trail between distilleries, historic bridges and small towns built almost entirely around whisky's long history here.

Why visit

Why visit Speyside?

Speyside's case rests on genuinely being the single best place in the world to understand and taste Scotch whisky at its source.

  • It's genuinely home to the highest distillery density in Scotland

    More working distilleries here than anywhere else in the country, many open for tours.

  • The Malt Whisky Trail is genuinely a real, signposted route

    A practical way to visit several distilleries and historic sites without getting lost.

  • Glenfiddich is genuinely one of the world's best-known distilleries

    Still family-owned, with a real working history stretching back to 1886.

  • The River Spey is genuinely beautiful beyond its whisky connection

    A celebrated salmon river with scenic walking paths along much of its length.

  • It works genuinely well alongside the Cairngorms and Loch Ness

    A natural stop on a wider Highland loop, both within easy reach.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Speyside

Speyside's essential sights combine working distilleries with the scenic river valley that shapes them.

  1. 01

    Glenfiddich Distillery

    Genuinely one of the world's best-known single malt distilleries, still family-owned and operating since 1886.

    Tip Genuinely book a tour ahead — it remains one of the most popular distillery visits in Speyside.

  2. 02

    Dufftown Clock Tower

    Genuinely the centrepiece of Dufftown's town square, a Victorian landmark once housing the town's jail.

    Tip Genuinely a good orientation point before setting out to explore the surrounding distilleries.

  3. 03

    Craigellachie Bridge

    Genuinely a striking 19th-century iron bridge designed by Thomas Telford, spanning the River Spey in a single dramatic arch.

    Tip Genuinely especially photogenic in autumn, when the surrounding trees turn colour.

  4. 04

    The River Spey at Aberlour

    Genuinely one of Scotland's most celebrated salmon rivers, its clean water also central to the region's whisky production.

    Tip The genuinely riverside path through Aberlour makes for an easy, scenic walk.

  5. 05

    Allt-a-Bhainne Distillery

    Genuinely a quieter, less-visited distillery, a good contrast to Speyside's larger, more famous names.

    Tip Genuinely check ahead, as smaller distilleries have more limited public tour schedules.

  6. 06

    The Malt Whisky Trail

    Genuinely an official signposted route connecting several of Speyside's distilleries and historic sites.

    Tip Genuinely pick up a printed trail map locally to plan a realistic day or two.

  7. 07

    Glenfiddich's pagoda roofs

    Genuinely one of Speyside's most recognisable architectural details, distinctive kiln roofs found across the region's distilleries.

    Tip Genuinely a great photo opportunity, especially with the surrounding hills as a backdrop.

Things to do

Best things to do in Speyside

Speyside genuinely rewards pacing distillery visits with time along the river itself.

  1. Tour Glenfiddich Distillery

    A genuinely thorough introduction to whisky-making, from mash tun to warehouse, ending with a tasting.

    Tip Genuinely several tour tiers exist, from a basic introduction to more in-depth tasting experiences.

  2. Walk the Speyside Way along the river

    A genuinely scenic long-distance path following the river, walkable in short, easy sections between towns.

    Tip Genuinely the Aberlour to Craigellachie stretch is an easy, well-marked introduction to the trail.

  3. Cross Craigellachie Bridge

    A genuinely quick, worthwhile stop, walking Telford's dramatic iron span over the river below.

    Tip Genuinely combine with a stop at the nearby distillery of the same name.

  4. Explore Dufftown's old jail

    A genuinely quick historical stop in the town centre, learning about the tower's varied past uses.

    Tip Genuinely combine with a wander through Dufftown's small, walkable town centre.

  5. Follow the Malt Whisky Trail

    A genuinely practical way to structure a Speyside visit, linking several distilleries by signposted road.

    Tip Genuinely designate a driver, or use a local tour, given the trail's multiple tasting stops.

  6. Cross the Victoria Footbridge at Aberlour

    A genuinely pleasant, easy river crossing on foot, with good views up and down the Spey.

    Tip Genuinely a nice spot to pause and watch for salmon fishers working the river.

FAQ

Speyside: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Speyside?

Two days genuinely covers Glenfiddich, Dufftown and a stretch of the Speyside Way comfortably.

How many distilleries are in Speyside?

Genuinely dozens — more working distilleries than anywhere else in Scotland, making it the country's whisky heartland.

Do you need to book distillery tours ahead?

Yes, genuinely recommended, especially for well-known names like Glenfiddich, which can sell out in peak season.

What is Speyside known for?

Genuinely its dense concentration of whisky distilleries and the distinctly smooth, often fruity style of Scotch they produce.

Can you visit Speyside without drinking whisky?

Yes, genuinely — the Speyside Way, Craigellachie Bridge and the region's scenery are worthwhile on their own.

How do you get between distilleries safely?

Genuinely arrange a designated driver, taxi or organised tour — driving after tastings is never advisable.
