Welcome to Cairngorms National Park

Cairngorms National Park is genuinely the UK's largest national park, a vast stretch of Highland wilderness considerably bigger than many small countries, built around a range of ancient, ice-carved mountains.

Its real character is genuinely in its scale and wildness — ancient Caledonian pine forest, some of Britain's last remaining fragments, shelters red deer, red squirrels and golden eagles across glens that feel largely untouched.

Most visitors genuinely base themselves in Aviemore, a small outdoor-focused town at the park's heart, using it as a launchpad for hiking, wildlife watching and, in winter, some of Scotland's only skiing.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Cairngorms National Park

The park's essential sights combine ancient forest, genuine wildlife and some of Scotland's highest mountain terrain.

  1. 01

    Loch Morlich

    Genuinely a striking freshwater loch backed directly by the Cairngorm mountains, with a sandy beach unusual for inland Scotland.

    Tip Genuinely popular for watersports in summer, with rental kayaks and paddleboards available lakeside.

  2. 02

    Aviemore

    Genuinely the park's main town, a small, outdoor-focused hub of gear shops, cafés and easy access to the surrounding wilderness.

    Tip Genuinely well stocked for last-minute hiking or ski gear before heading into the park.

  3. 03

    The Cairngorm Mountain Railway

    Genuinely the UK's highest railway, climbing to near the summit of Cairn Gorm without requiring a serious mountain hike.

    Tip The genuinely summit can be significantly colder and windier than the base — dress accordingly.

  4. 04

    Rothiemurchus Forest

    Genuinely one of Scotland's finest surviving fragments of ancient Caledonian pine forest, home to red squirrels and capercaillie.

    Tip Genuinely well-marked trails suit every fitness level, from gentle loch walks to longer forest treks.

  5. 05

    The park's red deer

    Genuinely a real, common sight across the park's glens, Britain's largest native land mammal in its natural Highland habitat.

    Tip Genuinely dawn and dusk offer the best chances of a close, unhurried sighting.

  6. 06

    The park's red squirrels

    Genuinely one of the park's most reliable wildlife sightings, native red squirrels thriving in the ancient pine forest.

    Tip Genuinely feeding stations near Aviemore and Rothiemurchus offer near-guaranteed close sightings.

  7. 07

    The Cairngorms' heather moorland

    Genuinely spectacular in late summer, when the park's open moorland turns a deep purple with flowering heather.

    Tip Genuinely late August into September is peak bloom for the heather.

Getting to and around Cairngorms National Park

The Cairngorms are genuinely well connected by rail, sitting directly on Scotland's main Highland line.

  • Intercity train to Aviemore

    Genuinely direct and scenic from both Glasgow and Inverness.

    One of Scotland's most useful rail links for reaching the Highlands without a car.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely useful for reaching the park's more remote glens and trailheads.

  • Flight to Inverness Airport

Getting around

A car is genuinely the most flexible way to explore the park's spread-out trailheads and glens.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely essential for reaching the park's more remote hiking trailheads.

    Roads within the park are generally well maintained, if occasionally single-track.

  • On foot or by bike in Aviemore

When to go

Best time to visit Cairngorms National Park

The Cairngorms are genuinely a year-round destination, offering hiking and wildlife in summer and Scotland's best skiing in winter.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely good hiking weather with fewer midges than midsummer.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

    The warmest season, with heather beginning to bloom by late summer, also genuinely peak midge season.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good

    Genuinely excellent for wildlife, including the dramatic red deer rutting season.

  • Winter (December–March)

    Good

    Genuinely home to some of Scotland's only skiing, at the Cairngorm Mountain resort.

Worth planning around: Attempting higher mountain trails without proper gear in winter — genuinely serious alpine conditions above the tree line.

How many days do you need in the Cairngorms?

Two days genuinely covers Loch Morlich, the mountain railway and Rothiemurchus Forest comfortably.

Yes, genuinely — it's an easy way to reach real mountain altitude and views without a serious hike.

Can you ski in the Cairngorms?

Yes, genuinely — the Cairngorm Mountain resort is one of Scotland's main ski areas, typically open December to March conditions permitting.

What is Cairngorms National Park known for?

Genuinely its vast scale, ancient Caledonian pine forest and real Highland wildlife, including red deer and red squirrels.

Is the Cairngorms good for wildlife spotting?

Yes, genuinely one of the best places in Britain, with real, reliable chances of red deer, red squirrels and birds of prey.
