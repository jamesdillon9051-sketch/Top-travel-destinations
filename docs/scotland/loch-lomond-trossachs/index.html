Welcome to Loch Lomond & the Trossachs

Loch Lomond and the Trossachs is genuinely Scotland's first national park, and for good reason a favourite easy escape from Glasgow, offering a real first taste of Highland scenery within an hour's drive of the city.

Loch Lomond itself is genuinely Britain's largest lake by surface area, its southern shores gentle and accessible, its northern reaches narrowing into properly dramatic Highland terrain around Ben Lomond.

Most visitors genuinely combine a loch cruise or lakeside walk with time in the wooded Trossachs further east, where Loch Katrine's historic steamship and the area's genuinely inspired Sir Walter Scott's romantic writing about the Highlands.

  • It's genuinely Scotland's first national park

  • Loch Lomond is genuinely Britain's largest lake by area

Main attractions

Top things to see in Loch Lomond & the Trossachs

The park's essential sights combine Loch Lomond's accessible scale with the Trossachs' more intimate, wooded scenery.

  1. 01

    Loch Lomond

    Genuinely Britain's largest lake by surface area, its southern shores gentle and its northern reaches narrowing into dramatic Highland terrain.

    Tip Genuinely the loch's character changes dramatically from south to north — see both if time allows.

  2. 02

    Ben Lomond

    Genuinely one of Scotland's most accessible Munros, a mountain over 3,000 feet, with sweeping views over the loch from the summit.

    Tip The genuinely standard route takes four to six hours round trip and needs no technical climbing.

  3. 03

    Luss village

    Genuinely one of Scotland's prettiest lochside villages, its stone cottages and pier a popular, easy stop on the loch's western shore.

    Tip Genuinely arrive early or late to avoid the busiest midday tour bus crowds.

  4. 04

    Loch Katrine

    Genuinely the scenic heart of the Trossachs, a loch that helped inspire Sir Walter Scott's romantic writing about the Highlands.

    Tip The genuinely surrounding forest offers excellent, easy cycling and walking trails.

  5. 05

    The SS Sir Walter Scott

    Genuinely a historic Victorian steamship, still carrying passengers across Loch Katrine over a century after entering service.

    Tip Genuinely book ahead in summer — it's one of the Trossachs' most popular attractions.

  6. 06

    The Trossachs

    Genuinely a more intimate, wooded landscape than Loch Lomond itself, often called 'the Highlands in miniature.'

    Tip Genuinely a rewarding scenic drive, best taken slowly with several stops.

  7. 07

    Loch Lomond near Tarbet

    Genuinely where the loch begins to narrow and turn properly Highland in character, a striking contrast to its wider southern reaches.

    Tip Genuinely a good stop for photos looking north toward the narrower, more dramatic upper loch.

Things to do

Best things to do in Loch Lomond & the Trossachs

The park genuinely rewards combining time on the water with a proper walk or hike.

  1. Cruise Loch Lomond

    A genuinely relaxed way to appreciate the loch's real scale, with regular sailings departing from Balloch and Luss.

    Tip Genuinely a shorter cruise from Luss covers some of the loch's most scenic stretches.

  2. Hike Ben Lomond

    A genuinely achievable first Munro for many visitors, rewarded with sweeping summit views over the loch.

    Tip Genuinely start early and check the weather — conditions change quickly at altitude even here.

  3. Wander Luss village

    A genuinely easy, pretty stop, strolling stone cottages and a short pier out onto the loch itself.

    Tip Genuinely combine with a coffee stop at one of the village's small cafés.

  4. Sail Loch Katrine on the SS Sir Walter Scott

    A genuinely memorable way to see the Trossachs, a slow cruise aboard a working Victorian steamship.

    Tip Genuinely book ahead in peak summer, when sailings can sell out.

  5. Drive through the Trossachs

    A genuinely rewarding, unhurried drive through wooded hills often described as 'the Highlands in miniature.'

    Tip Genuinely stop often — the scenery here rewards a slow pace more than covering distance.

  6. Photograph Ellen's Isle on Loch Katrine

    A genuinely scenic small island, named for the heroine of Sir Walter Scott's poem set on the loch.

    Tip Genuinely best viewed from the SS Sir Walter Scott's regular cruise route.

When to go

Best time to visit Loch Lomond & the Trossachs

Loch Lomond and the Trossachs are genuinely visitable in any season, though hiking and cruising are easiest in the warmer months.

  • Late spring (May–June)

    Best time

    Genuinely mild weather and thinner crowds than midsummer.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Good

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Good
