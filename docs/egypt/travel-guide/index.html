How to plan an Egypt trip: whether to focus on Cairo, a Nile cruise or the Red Sea coast, when to go to avoid the worst heat, what it costs, and a practical ten-day itinerary.

How Egypt divides

Egypt genuinely divides into distinct zones along and beyond the Nile, and a first trip realistically covers the river corridor plus one coastal or desert extension.

Cairo and the pyramids. Cairo and Giza — the capital's museums and the Pyramids of Giza, the near-universal starting point for any Egypt trip.

The Nile corridor. Luxor, Aswan and a Nile cruise between them — ancient Egypt's temple-lined heartland.

The Red Sea coast. Sharm El Sheikh, Dahab and Hurghada — extraordinary diving and a relaxed beach counterpoint to the ruins.

The north and the desert. Alexandria on the Mediterranean, and the White Desert and Siwa Oasis further into the Western Desert, both genuinely rewarding but off the classic first-timer route.

Worth knowing: Most first-time visitors genuinely combine Cairo with a Nile cruise between Luxor and Aswan. The Western Desert oases are where a longer or second trip belongs.

Choosing between the ten destinations

  • If this is your first trip

    Cairo and Giza, genuinely the essential starting point for any Egypt itinerary.

  • If you want the classic ancient Egypt route

    A Nile cruise between Luxor and Aswan, genuinely the most efficient way to see the temple sites.

  • If you want world-class diving

    Dahab or Sharm El Sheikh, both genuinely among the best diving destinations on Earth.

  • If you want a relaxed beach extension

    Hurghada, genuinely the most resort-focused option on the Red Sea coast.

  • If you want somewhere genuinely different from the ruins

    The White Desert, with its otherworldly chalk formations and desert camping.

  • If you want real remoteness

    Siwa Oasis, genuinely one of Egypt's most isolated and culturally distinct destinations.

Getting around: flights, trains, and one common mistake

Egypt's genuinely long north-south distances mean no single mode of transport covers the whole country — a mix of flights, trains and cruise boats is standard.

Fly for long distances, don't drive. Cairo to Aswan genuinely takes most of a day by road versus about an hour and a half by air.

Book a Nile cruise for Luxor to Aswan. It's genuinely the classic, most comfortable way to see the temples along the river without repeated hotel changes.

Don't underestimate the heat. Sightseeing in Luxor or Aswan outside winter genuinely requires pacing around the midday sun.

Use overnight trains for a budget-friendly link. Sleeper cabins between Cairo and Luxor or Aswan genuinely save both time and a hotel night.

Book a private driver for the desert oases. The White Desert and Siwa genuinely require experienced desert driving best left to local operators.

Worth knowing: Book your Nile cruise and major site tickets well ahead for peak winter travel — Egypt's most comfortable season genuinely sells out its best cruise cabins early.

When to go

Egypt's desert climate genuinely means 'the best time' is mostly about avoiding extreme heat rather than chasing good weather, which is reliable and dry nearly year-round.

Winter, December to February, brings genuinely the mildest, most comfortable temperatures for sightseeing, also the busiest and priciest season.

Shoulder months, October-November and March-April, offer genuinely warm but manageable heat with thinner crowds than peak winter.

Summer, May to September, brings genuinely intense heat, especially in Luxor and Aswan, though the Red Sea coast stays comparatively more bearable.

The Red Sea coast is genuinely workable nearly year-round, making it a sensible add-on regardless of when you visit the Nile sites.

Worth knowing: Book Nile cruise cabins and major site tickets many months ahead for a December-February trip — this is genuinely the busiest and most tightly booked window of the year.

Four routes that work

  1. Four days: Cairo and Giza

    Cairo and Giza alone, genuinely the most popular short Egypt itinerary for a reason.

    Tip Add the Grand Egyptian Museum to your Giza day for a genuinely complete pyramid experience.

  2. Ten days: the classic Nile route

    The itinerary on the Egypt country page: Cairo, a Nile cruise through Luxor and Aswan, and a Red Sea extension. A genuinely complete first look at Egypt's classic highlights.

    Tip Book a four-night cruise rather than a shorter option — it allows a genuinely unrushed pace at each temple stop.

  3. A week focused on the Red Sea

    Hurghada, Dahab or Sharm El Sheikh as a dedicated diving and relaxation trip, skipping the Nile entirely.

    Tip Dahab genuinely offers a more laid-back, budget-friendly diving scene than Sharm El Sheikh's larger resorts.

  4. A Western Desert add-on for a second trip

    The White Desert and Siwa Oasis, genuinely two of Egypt's most remote and rewarding corners.

    Tip Book a genuinely reputable desert safari operator — independent desert driving here is not recommended.

FAQ

Planning a trip to Egypt

How many days do you need in Egypt?

Ten to twelve days covers Cairo, a Nile cruise and a Red Sea extension at a reasonable pace, which is the itinerary in this guide. Four to five days is enough for Cairo and Giza alone, and genuinely still worthwhile on its own.

Is a Nile cruise worth the cost?

Yes, genuinely — it's the classic, most comfortable way to see Luxor, Edfu, Kom Ombo and Aswan's temples without repeated hotel changes, and the river scenery itself is part of the experience.

Do you need a guide at the pyramids and temples?

Not required, but genuinely recommended — a licensed guide adds real historical context that's easy to miss exploring independently, and helps navigate persistent touting.

When should I visit to avoid the worst heat?

October to April offers genuinely the most comfortable temperatures, especially in Luxor and Aswan, where summer heat can exceed 40°C and become genuinely dangerous for sightseeing.
