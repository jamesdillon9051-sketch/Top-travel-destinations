Welcome to A Nile Cruise

A Nile cruise is genuinely the classic way to see Upper Egypt, a multi-night journey between Luxor and Aswan that covers ancient temple sites at Edfu and Kom Ombo without the hassle of repeated hotel changes.

The river itself is genuinely part of the experience, its banks unchanged in essence for millennia — palm groves, mudbrick villages and farmland worked much as it always has been, right alongside the boat's route.

Most visitors genuinely book a three- or four-night cruise, sailing between Luxor and Aswan with guided stops at each temple along the way, all meals and cabin accommodation included in a single package.

Why visit A Nile Cruise?

A Nile cruise's case rests on genuinely combining efficient sightseeing with a uniquely relaxed, scenic way to travel.

  • It's genuinely the most efficient way to see multiple temples

    Edfu and Kom Ombo are both reached without a single extra hotel change.

  • The river scenery is genuinely part of the appeal

    Palm groves and riverside village life passing by, largely unchanged for centuries.

  • Edfu's temple is genuinely one of Egypt's best preserved

    Its roof and walls remarkably intact, giving a real sense of how these temples once looked complete.

  • Kom Ombo's dual design is genuinely unique

  • It's genuinely a comfortable, all-inclusive way to travel

    Meals, cabin and guided excursions all bundled into a single, relaxed itinerary.

Main attractions

Top things to see in A Nile Cruise

A Nile cruise's essential sights combine genuinely life aboard the boat with major temple stops along the route.

  1. 01

    The Nile cruise ship

    Genuinely a floating hotel for the journey, most boats offering cabins, a sun deck, dining rooms and often a small pool.

    Tip Genuinely book a cabin with a river-facing balcony if your budget allows — the views are worth it.

  2. 02

    The Temple of Horus at Edfu

    Genuinely one of Egypt's best-preserved ancient temples, its roof and walls remarkably intact after being buried in sand for centuries.

    Tip Genuinely reached by horse-drawn carriage from the dock, part of the classic Edfu stop experience.

  3. 03

    The Temple of Kom Ombo

    Genuinely unique among Egyptian temples, built as mirrored twin sanctuaries dedicated to two different gods, Sobek and Horus.

    Tip Genuinely visit at sunset if your cruise schedule allows — the temple is especially striking in golden light.

  4. 04

    Kom Ombo's twin sanctuaries

    Genuinely a rare architectural symmetry, with matching entrances, halls and sanctuaries mirrored down the temple's central axis.

    Tip Genuinely spot the ancient crocodile mummies displayed in the temple's small on-site museum.

  5. 05

    Edfu's hypostyle hall

    Genuinely one of the most complete surviving examples of ancient Egyptian temple architecture, its full roof structure still intact.

    Tip Genuinely a real, rare chance to imagine these temples as they originally looked, roof and all.

  6. 06

    Nile riverbank scenery

    Genuinely part of the cruise's real appeal, palm groves, farmland and village life passing slowly by the boat's rails.

    Tip Genuinely the sun deck offers the best vantage point for watching the riverbank scenery unfold.

  7. 07

    A felucca crossing the cruise route

    Genuinely a common sight along the way, traditional wooden sailboats still working the same stretch of river.

    Tip Genuinely many cruises include a short felucca excursion of their own, separate from the main boat.

Things to do

Best things to do in A Nile Cruise

A Nile cruise genuinely rewards embracing its slower pace, alternating temple visits with real time on the water.

  1. Sail between temples on the cruise boat

    A genuinely relaxed core of the experience, watching the riverbank pass by between each guided temple stop.

    Tip Genuinely much of the sailing happens overnight, so daytime hours are largely free for sightseeing or relaxing.

  2. Explore the Temple of Horus at Edfu

    A genuinely essential stop, walking through one of Egypt's most completely preserved ancient temples.

    Tip Genuinely the horse-drawn carriage ride from the dock is a memorable part of the Edfu stop.

  3. Visit the twin temple of Kom Ombo

    Tip Genuinely don't miss the small crocodile mummy museum on the temple grounds.

  4. Watch the riverbank scenery pass by

    Tip Genuinely bring a good book or simply enjoy the unhurried pace between temple stops.

  5. Enjoy a traditional show onboard

  6. Relax on the sun deck

    A genuinely essential part of the cruise experience, whether by the small pool or simply taking in the view.

    Tip Genuinely the sun deck offers the best photo opportunities as the boat passes each new stretch of riverbank.

Transport

Getting to and around A Nile Cruise

A Nile cruise genuinely begins or ends in either Luxor or Aswan, both reached by flight or train from Cairo.

Getting there

  • Flight to Luxor or Aswan

    Genuinely the standard way to reach your cruise's embarkation point from Cairo.

    Frequent daily flights connect Cairo to both cities.

  • Overnight train to Luxor or Aswan

    Genuinely a budget-friendly, if slower, alternative to flying.

    Comfortable sleeper cabins save a hotel night.

  • Booking through a tour operator

    Genuinely the standard way to arrange a cruise, often bundled with flights and airport transfers.

    Most cruise packages handle logistics between your flight and the boat directly.

Getting around

Once aboard, all movement is genuinely handled by the cruise itself, with guided excursions at each stop.

  • The cruise boat

    Genuinely covers the full journey between Luxor and Aswan, sailing mostly overnight between stops.

    No independent transport needed once your cruise begins.

  • Guided shore excursions

    Genuinely included at each temple stop, led by an onboard Egyptologist guide.

    The standard, and genuinely recommended, way to see Edfu and Kom Ombo.

When to go

Best time to visit A Nile Cruise

A Nile cruise genuinely runs year-round, though winter and shoulder season offer far more comfortable conditions for the temple stops.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Best time

    Genuinely the mildest temperatures for both onboard time and temple excursions, also the busiest season.

  • Shoulder months (October–November, March–April)

    Best time

    Genuinely warm but manageable heat with thinner crowds than peak winter.

  • Summer (May–September)

    Good

    Genuinely intense heat ashore, though the boat's air-conditioned cabins and pool offer real relief.

Worth planning around: Booking a cruise without checking your cabin's air conditioning — genuinely essential given Upper Egypt's heat most of the year.

FAQ

A Nile Cruise: frequently asked questions

How many nights should a Nile cruise be?

Three to four nights genuinely covers the classic Luxor to Aswan route comfortably, with stops at Edfu and Kom Ombo.

What's included in a Nile cruise price?

Genuinely most packages include your cabin, all meals, and guided shore excursions at each temple stop — check specifics before booking.

Do you need to book a Nile cruise in advance?

Yes, genuinely recommended, especially for the best cabins in peak winter season, when demand is highest.

What is a Nile cruise known for?

Genuinely being the classic, efficient way to see Upper Egypt's temples at Edfu and Kom Ombo, combined with the Nile's own timeless riverside scenery.

Can you do a Nile cruise in the other direction, from Aswan to Luxor?

Yes, genuinely — cruises run both directions, so the route works equally well starting from either city.

Is a Nile cruise suitable for families?

Generally yes, genuinely — most boats offer a relaxed pace and a pool, though check specific excursion intensity for younger children.
