Welcome to Alexandria

Alexandria is genuinely Egypt's Mediterranean second city, founded by Alexander the Great in 331 BCE and once home to the ancient world's greatest library and one of its Seven Wonders, the Lighthouse of Alexandria.

That ancient legacy genuinely lives on in a striking modern form, the Bibliotheca Alexandrina rising directly on the shore as a deliberate tribute to its lost predecessor, alongside genuine Roman-era catacombs still being explored.

Most visitors genuinely treat it as a refreshing change of pace from the Nile Valley's desert heat, a breezy seafront city with its own long corniche, seafood restaurants and a noticeably more relaxed, cosmopolitan character.

FAQ

Alexandria: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Alexandria?

Two days genuinely covers the Bibliotheca Alexandrina, Qaitbay Citadel and the main ancient sites comfortably.

Can you visit Alexandria as a day trip from Cairo?

Yes, genuinely — the train takes under three hours each way, making it a workable, if full, day trip.

Is the original Library of Alexandria still standing?

No, genuinely lost to history — the modern Bibliotheca Alexandrina was built nearby as a deliberate tribute to it.

What is Alexandria known for?

Genuinely its Mediterranean seafront setting, ancient history founded by Alexander the Great, and a striking modern library built in tribute to its lost predecessor.

Is Alexandria warmer or cooler than Cairo?

Genuinely cooler, thanks to its Mediterranean coastal position and sea breeze, a welcome contrast to inland Egypt's desert heat.

Is Qaitbay Citadel really built on the site of an Ancient Wonder?

Yes, genuinely — it stands on the site of, and partly built from, the fallen stones of the ancient Lighthouse of Alexandria.
