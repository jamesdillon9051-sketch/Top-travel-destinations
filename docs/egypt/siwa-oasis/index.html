Welcome to Siwa Oasis

Siwa Oasis is genuinely Egypt's most remote and culturally distinct destination, a Berber-speaking desert town closer to the Libyan border than to the Nile Valley.

Its history runs genuinely deep — Alexander the Great made a famous, gruelling journey here to consult the Oracle of Amun, whose temple ruins still stand above the palm groves.

Modern Siwa genuinely revolves around mudbrick architecture, natural springs and salt lakes, and the vast dunes of the Great Sand Sea rolling away toward Libya.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Siwa Oasis

Siwa's essential sights are genuinely spread across its ancient ruins, natural springs and surrounding desert.

  1. 01

    Shali Fortress

    Genuinely Siwa's dramatic old town, a mudbrick fortress-village largely abandoned after rare heavy rains damaged it in 1926.

    Tip Genuinely climb up at sunset, when the eroded walls glow gold against the sky.

  2. 02

    Temple of the Oracle of Amun

    Genuinely the site that drew Alexander the Great across the desert, an oracle so respected he had himself declared a son of the god here.

    Tip Genuinely pair the visit with the nearby Temple of Amun's ruins and the view over the palm groves.

  3. 03

    Cleopatra's Spring (Spring of Juba)

    Genuinely a natural spring-fed pool said to have been visited by Cleopatra herself, still a popular swimming spot today.

    Tip Genuinely modest swimwear is appropriate — this remains a conservative, traditional community.

  4. 04

    Siwa's salt lakes

    Genuinely a striking landscape of turquoise pools and white salt crusts scattered around the oasis.

    Tip Genuinely float in one of the lakes — the salt concentration makes sinking almost impossible.

  5. 05

    The Great Sand Sea

    Genuinely one of the world's largest sand seas, its dunes rolling away toward the Libyan border just beyond Siwa.

    Tip Genuinely a 4x4 dune safari is the classic way to experience its scale.

  6. 06

    Gebel al-Mawta (Mountain of the Dead)

    Genuinely a small hill honeycombed with painted rock tombs from Siwa's Greco-Roman period.

    Tip Genuinely ask the site guard to unlock the best-preserved tombs, which aren't always open by default.

  7. 07

    Siwa's olive and date groves

    Genuinely the oasis's economic backbone, dense palm and olive groves fed by hundreds of natural springs.

    Tip Genuinely rent a bicycle to explore the groves' quiet dirt lanes.

Things to do

Best things to do in Siwa Oasis

Siwa genuinely rewards a slow, unhurried pace, built around its ruins, springs and surrounding desert.

  1. Climb Shali Fortress at sunset

    A genuinely essential Siwa ritual, watching the light turn the old town's mudbrick walls gold.

    Tip Genuinely wear sturdy shoes — the old town's paths are uneven and partly ruined.

  2. Swim in Cleopatra's Spring

    A genuinely refreshing, easy stop, a natural pool shaded by palms right in the oasis.

    Tip Genuinely quieter early morning or late afternoon, outside the midday tour-group rush.

  3. Float in a salt lake

    A genuinely novel experience, the water's salinity keeping you effortlessly buoyant.

    Tip Genuinely avoid getting salt water in your eyes, and rinse off with fresh water afterward.

  4. Take a 4x4 safari into the Great Sand Sea

    A genuinely thrilling half or full day, dune bashing across some of the Sahara's largest sand formations.

    Tip Genuinely book through an experienced local operator familiar with the dunes' terrain.

  5. Explore the rock tombs at Gebel al-Mawta

    A genuinely worthwhile stop, painted tombs cut into the hillside from Siwa's Greco-Roman period.

    Tip Genuinely a quick visit, easily combined with Shali Fortress in the same afternoon.

  6. Cycle through the palm and olive groves

    A genuinely peaceful way to spend an afternoon, pedalling quiet dirt lanes between springs and groves.

    Tip Genuinely rent a bicycle from almost any guesthouse in town.

  • Siwan olives and olive oil

    Genuinely one of Siwa's real exports, olive groves feeding both the oasis's own tables and oil production.

    Where to try it Local shops and family-run groves throughout Siwa.

  • Siwan dates

    Genuinely a Siwa staple, sweet dates grown across the oasis's palm groves.

    Where to try it Local shops and market stalls in Siwa town.

  • Siwan bread and tagen

    Genuinely traditional Siwan cooking, bread and stews slow-cooked in clay pots.

    Where to try it Local guesthouses and family-run restaurants in Siwa town.

  • Koshari

  • Ful medames

    Where to try it Guesthouses and cafés in Siwa town.

    Where to try it Cafés and guesthouses throughout Siwa.

Transport

Getting to and around Siwa Oasis

Siwa is genuinely remote, reached by a long but straightforward road journey.

Getting there

  • Bus from Cairo

    Genuinely the standard route, a long overnight or full-day journey.

    Takes around nine to eleven hours; overnight buses save a travel day.

  • Via Marsa Matrouh

    Genuinely a shorter alternative for those already on the north coast.

    About four hours by road from Marsa Matrouh to Siwa.

  • Private car or driver

    Genuinely a comfortable, flexible option for a small group.

    Roughly eight hours from Cairo on a well-paved desert road.

Getting around

Siwa town is genuinely small and easy to explore without a car of your own.

  • Bicycle

    Genuinely the classic, most popular way to get around Siwa.

    Widely available for rent from guesthouses throughout town.

  • Donkey cart (karetta)

    Genuinely a traditional, still-common way to get around locally.

    Easy to arrange for short trips around town or to nearby sites.

  • 4x4 with driver

    Genuinely essential for reaching the Great Sand Sea and more remote sites.

    Arrange through your guesthouse or a local safari operator.

Travel tips

Siwa Oasis travel tips

Practical notes that make a Siwa visit noticeably smoother.

  • Dress genuinely modestly — Siwa is a conservative, traditional community, more so than Cairo or the Red Sea resorts.
  • Carry genuinely cash — Siwa has very limited card acceptance and few ATMs.
  • Book genuinely a Great Sand Sea safari through a reputable local guide.
  • Pack genuinely for cold desert nights, even if the day has been hot.
  • Bring genuinely a good book or two — Siwa's real pleasure is its slow pace.
  • Respect genuinely local customs around photographing Siwan women without permission.

FAQ

Siwa Oasis: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Siwa?

Two to three days genuinely covers the main sights, though the journey itself makes a longer stay worthwhile.

How do you get to Siwa?

Genuinely by long-distance bus from Cairo, or a shorter road trip via Marsa Matrouh on the north coast.

Is Siwa worth the long journey?

Yes, genuinely — its culture, ruins and landscape are unlike anywhere else in Egypt.

What language is spoken in Siwa?

Siwan, genuinely a Berber (Amazigh) language distinct from Egyptian Arabic, though Arabic is widely understood.

Can you combine Siwa with the White Desert?

It's genuinely possible but demanding — the two sit on opposite sides of the Western Desert, so most visitors choose one or budget significant extra travel time for both.

Is Siwa safe to visit?

Yes, genuinely — Siwa is a well-established, welcoming destination, though it sits in a border region, so it's worth checking current travel advice before an independent trip.
