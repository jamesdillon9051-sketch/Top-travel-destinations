Welcome to Dahab

Dahab is genuinely Egypt's laid-back answer to Sharm El Sheikh, a former Bedouin fishing village strung along the Gulf of Aqaba about an hour and a half up the Sinai coast.

Its reputation rests genuinely on house-reef diving — walking straight off the shore into some of the Red Sea's best diving and freediving, including the legendary, genuinely notorious Blue Hole.

Life here genuinely moves at a different pace than Sharm's resort strip, built around cushion-strewn seafront cafés, budget guesthouses and a close-knit diving and freediving community.

Why visit

Why visit Dahab?

Dahab's case rests genuinely on world-class shore diving paired with an unusually relaxed, unpretentious atmosphere.

  • The diving here is genuinely shore-based and exceptional

    Walk straight off the beach into reef walls that would require a boat almost anywhere else.

  • It's genuinely one of the world's great freediving hubs

    Multiple international freediving schools and a calm, deep bay draw the discipline's top athletes.

  • The pace here is genuinely far more relaxed than Sharm

    A backpacker, Bedouin-influenced town with no big resort strip to speak of.

  • The lagoon is genuinely excellent for windsurfing and kitesurfing

    Flat, shallow water and reliable wind make it a genuine watersports draw.

  • It works genuinely well as a quieter Red Sea base

    Easily combined with Sharm El Sheikh or a wider Sinai itinerary.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Dahab

Dahab's essential sights are genuinely centred on its famous dive sites and its laid-back seafront.

  1. 01

    The Blue Hole

    Genuinely one of the world's most famous — and most notorious — dive sites, a sinkhole dropping more than 100 metres just off the shore.

    Tip Genuinely only dive here with a qualified local guide and well within your certification level; the site has a serious accident history.

  2. 02

    The Canyon

    Genuinely a dramatic underwater crevice a short way up the coast, its narrow walls opening onto a sandy floor around 30 metres down.

    Tip Genuinely a rewarding, slightly easier alternative to the Blue Hole for certified divers.

  3. 03

    Dahab's lagoon

    Genuinely a shallow, sheltered bay just south of town, its flat water and steady wind making it one of the Red Sea's best windsurfing and kitesurfing spots.

    Tip Genuinely beginner lessons are easy to arrange right at the lagoon's watersports centres.

  4. 04

    Masbat and Mashraba promenade

    Genuinely Dahab's social heart, a seafront strip of cushion-scattered restaurants and cafés facing the Gulf of Aqaba.

    Tip Genuinely arrive for sunrise — the town faces east across the water toward Saudi Arabia's mountains.

  5. 05

    Eel Garden

    Genuinely a shallow, easy shore dive and snorkel site named for its colony of garden eels swaying from the sandy bottom.

    Tip Genuinely a good site for beginners and underwater photographers alike.

  6. 06

    Ras Abu Galum Protected Area

    Genuinely a remote Bedouin village and marine reserve reachable only by camel or boat along the coast north of Dahab.

    Tip Genuinely go on an organised camel trek that includes snorkelling stops along the way.

  7. 07

    The Coloured Canyon

    Genuinely a striking slot canyon inland toward Nuweiba, its walls streaked red, purple and gold by mineral deposits.

    Tip Genuinely a popular full-day desert excursion, often paired with a Bedouin lunch.

Things to do

Best things to do in Dahab

Dahab genuinely rewards a slower pace, built around diving, watersports and simply relaxing by the water.

  1. Dive the Blue Hole with a qualified guide

    A genuinely bucket-list dive for the qualified, best done early morning with a reputable local dive centre.

    Tip Genuinely stick to depths well within your certification — the site's notoriety is well earned.

  2. Try freediving

    A genuinely major draw for Dahab, home to some of the world's top freediving schools and a calm, deep bay perfect for training.

    Tip Genuinely beginner courses are widely available and need no prior experience.

  3. Windsurf or kitesurf the lagoon

    A genuinely reliable watersports session, the lagoon's flat water and steady wind suiting beginners and experts alike.

    Tip Genuinely lessons and equipment rental are easy to arrange right at the lagoon.

  4. Snorkel the Eel Garden

    A genuinely easy, rewarding shore snorkel, drifting over a colony of garden eels and healthy coral.

    Tip Genuinely rent gear from any of the dive shops lining the promenade.

  5. Take a camel or jeep safari into the desert

    A genuinely classic Dahab excursion, riding out toward the mountains or the Coloured Canyon with a Bedouin guide.

    Tip Genuinely sunset or sunrise trips are the most atmospheric time to go.

  6. Watch sunrise over the Gulf of Aqaba

    A genuinely lovely, easy ritual, the town's east-facing coast turning the water pink before the day's heat sets in.

    Tip Genuinely grab a coffee from a promenade café and watch from a cushioned seafront seat.

Local food

What to eat in Dahab

Dahab's food scene genuinely reflects its backpacker, Bedouin-influenced character, relaxed and inexpensive.

  • Fresh grilled Red Sea seafood

    Genuinely a Dahab staple, fish grilled simply and served right along the promenade.

    Where to try it Seafront restaurants throughout Masbat and Mashraba.

  • Bedouin tea

    Genuinely a Dahab ritual, sweet tea brewed with fresh herbs and served on cushions at a seafront camp.

    Where to try it Any of the promenade's Bedouin-style seafront cafés.

  • Koshari

    Genuinely available at Egyptian-run restaurants throughout town, a comforting, filling staple.

    Where to try it Local restaurants away from the busier promenade strip.

  • Ful medames

    Genuinely a traditional, hearty breakfast, found at cafés throughout Dahab.

    Where to try it Local cafés, especially around the older Masbat quarter.

  • Taameya (Egyptian falafel)

    Genuinely Egypt's own fava-bean falafel, a reliable, inexpensive staple.

    Where to try it Street stalls and restaurants throughout town.

  • Fresh fruit juice

    Genuinely a refreshing staple after a day of diving, blended fresh at juice bars along the promenade.

    Where to try it Juice bars and cafés throughout Masbat.

Transport

Getting to and around Dahab

Dahab is genuinely reached overland from Sharm El Sheikh or Cairo, with no airport of its own.

Getting there

  • Transfer or taxi from Sharm El Sheikh

    Genuinely the most common route, about 1.5 hours by road.

    Shared or private transfers are easy to arrange through hotels or dive centres.

  • Bus from Cairo

    Genuinely a long but budget-friendly overland option.

    Takes around eight to nine hours via the Sinai Peninsula.

  • Fly into Sharm El Sheikh

    Genuinely the standard gateway, with frequent domestic and international flights.

    Continue on to Dahab by road transfer.

Getting around

Dahab is genuinely small and walkable, with most of daily life happening along the promenade.

  • On foot along the promenade

    Genuinely the way most visitors get around central Dahab.

    Masbat, Mashraba and the lagoon all sit within easy walking distance of each other.

  • Bicycles

    Genuinely a popular, easy way to cover slightly longer distances.

    Widely available for rent from guesthouses and shops.

  • Taxis

    Genuinely useful for reaching the lagoon or outlying dive sites.

    Agree the fare before starting your journey.

When to go

Best time to visit Dahab

Dahab is genuinely a year-round destination, with warm water even in the cooler months.

  • Spring (March–May)

    Best time

    Genuinely comfortable temperatures for diving, freediving and desert excursions alike.

  • Autumn (September–November)

    Best time

    Genuinely similarly excellent conditions, with warm water and fewer crowds than summer.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Good

    Genuinely hot on land, though the water stays pleasant and windsurfing conditions are strong.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Good

    Genuinely mild and popular with visitors escaping colder climates, though desert nights can turn cold.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Dahab's genuinely warm Red Sea waters make it workable year-round.

Travel tips

Dahab travel tips

Practical notes that make a Dahab visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book genuinely with a reputable, qualified dive centre, especially for the Blue Hole.
  • Bring genuinely your own certification card if you're a qualified diver.
  • Pack genuinely reef-safe sunscreen and modest clothing for walking through town.
  • Never dive genuinely beyond your certification level, whatever a dive guide suggests.
  • Carry genuinely cash — many smaller promenade restaurants and shops don't take cards.
  • Bargain genuinely and politely for camel treks and jeep safaris arranged independently.

FAQ

Dahab: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Dahab?

Three to five days genuinely allows time to dive multiple sites and simply enjoy the relaxed pace.

Is the Blue Hole as dangerous as its reputation suggests?

Its accidents are genuinely almost always tied to divers going beyond their training; with a qualified guide and sensible limits, it's a well-managed dive.

Do you need to be a certified diver to visit?

No, genuinely not — snorkelling, beginner courses and freediving taster sessions are all widely available.

How does Dahab compare to Sharm El Sheikh?

Genuinely much more laid-back and budget-friendly, with shore diving replacing Sharm's bigger resort strip and boat-based diving.

Can you combine Dahab with Sharm El Sheikh?

Yes, genuinely easily — they're about 1.5 hours apart by road, often combined in one Red Sea trip.

Is Dahab good for non-divers?

Yes, genuinely — windsurfing, kitesurfing, desert excursions and simply relaxing on the promenade all make for a worthwhile stay.
