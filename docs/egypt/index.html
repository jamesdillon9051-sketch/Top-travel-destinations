Why go to Egypt

Egypt is genuinely home to some of the world's oldest and most recognisable monuments, the Pyramids of Giza still standing after more than 4,500 years, built before Stonehenge and outlasting entire civilisations that came after.

That ancient history genuinely runs the length of the Nile, from Cairo's museums and Giza's pyramids down to Luxor's temple complexes and Aswan's Nubian culture, a corridor of green farmland carved through the desert by a single river.

It's also genuinely a country of real contrasts beyond the ancient sites — Red Sea coral reefs among the best diving anywhere on Earth, and a Western Desert of white chalk formations and remote oases that feel like an entirely different country.

Experiences

Best things to do in Egypt

The experiences that recur across the country and that make an Egypt trip feel like more than a checklist of ruins.

  1. Stand before the Great Pyramid

    Genuinely the essential Egypt experience, standing at the base of the last surviving Ancient Wonder of the World.

    Tip Genuinely arrive at opening time to beat both the heat and the tour bus crowds.

  2. Sail the Nile on a felucca

    A genuinely timeless way to see the river, a traditional wooden sailboat drifting past palm groves and riverbank villages.

    Tip Genuinely sunset sails are the most popular, and for good reason — the light on the water is extraordinary.

  3. Dive the Red Sea's reefs

    Genuinely among the best diving on Earth, extraordinary visibility and coral reef biodiversity along Egypt's eastern coast.

    Tip Genuinely Dahab and Sharm El Sheikh both offer excellent, accessible diving for every certification level.

  4. Explore the Valley of the Kings

    Genuinely one of archaeology's great sites, the burial ground of pharaohs including Tutankhamun, hidden in a desert valley.

    Tip Genuinely a limited number of tombs are open on rotation — check which are accessible before visiting.

  5. Camp under the stars in the White Desert

    A genuinely surreal overnight experience, sleeping among wind-carved chalk formations that look like nowhere else on Earth.

    Tip Genuinely pack warm layers — desert nights get cold even after scorching daytime heat.

  6. Wander a Cairo bazaar

    Genuinely a sensory overload in the best way, centuries-old Khan el-Khalili packed with spices, lanterns and workshops.

    Tip Genuinely polite bargaining is expected and part of the experience here.

Food and drink

Egypt foods to try

Egyptian food's understated reputation abroad rarely does justice to genuinely hearty, flavourful staples eaten daily across the country.

  • Koshari

    Genuinely Egypt's national dish, a hearty mix of rice, lentils, pasta and chickpeas topped with spiced tomato sauce and crispy onions.

    Where to try it Koshari shops nationwide, especially throughout Cairo.

  • Ful medames

    A genuinely essential daily breakfast, slow-cooked fava beans mashed with olive oil, lemon and spices.

    Where to try it Breakfast stalls and cafés nationwide.

  • Taameya (Egyptian falafel)

    Genuinely Egypt's own version of falafel, made with fava beans rather than chickpeas, giving it a distinct green colour and flavour.

    Where to try it Street stalls and cafés nationwide.

  • Molokhia

    Genuinely a beloved home-style dish, a thick green soup made from jute leaves, typically served with rice or bread.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants nationwide.

  • Karkade (hibiscus tea)

    Genuinely a refreshing national drink, a tart, deep-red tea made from dried hibiscus, served hot or cold.

    Where to try it Cafés and restaurants nationwide.

  • Fresh Red Sea seafood

    Genuinely excellent along the coast, grilled fish and seafood fresh from the Red Sea's waters.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout Hurghada, Dahab and Sharm El Sheikh.

When to go

Best time to visit Egypt

Egypt is genuinely a year-round destination, but its desert climate makes shoulder season far more comfortable for sightseeing.

  • Winter (December–February)

    Best time

    Genuinely the mildest, most comfortable temperatures for sightseeing, also the busiest and priciest season.

  • Shoulder months (October–November, March–April)

    Best time

    Genuinely warm but manageable heat with thinner crowds than peak winter.

  • Summer (May–September)

    Good

    Genuinely intense heat, especially in Luxor and Aswan, though the Red Sea coast stays more bearable.

Worth planning around: Extensive sightseeing in Luxor or Aswan during peak summer — genuinely dangerous heat, often exceeding 40°C.

Trip length

How long to spend in Egypt

Egypt's genuinely spread-out sites along the Nile and at the Red Sea make a rushed itinerary the most common planning mistake.

  • 4–5 days

    Cairo and Giza alone, or paired with a short Red Sea extension.

    Enough for the essential pyramid sites at a reasonable pace.

  • 6–8 days

    Cairo, Luxor and Aswan, connected by a short flight or overnight train.

    A genuinely solid introduction to Egypt's ancient sites along the Nile.

  • 10–12 days

    The itinerary on this page: Cairo, a Nile cruise between Luxor and Aswan, and a Red Sea extension.

    A genuinely complete first look at Egypt's classic highlights.

  • 2 weeks or more

    Add Alexandria and the Western Desert oases in full, both genuinely worth dedicated time.

    Siwa and the White Desert are where a second Egypt trip belongs.

Getting around

Transportation in Egypt

A mix of domestic flights, trains and Nile cruise boats is genuinely the standard way to see Egypt's spread-out sites.

  • Domestic flights

    Genuinely the fastest way to connect Cairo to Luxor, Aswan and the Red Sea coast.

    Frequent daily flights make Egypt's long distances manageable on a limited schedule.

  • Nile cruise boats

    Genuinely the classic way to travel between Luxor and Aswan, covering ancient sites along the way.

    A multi-night Nile cruise combines transport with guided sightseeing at each stop.

  • Overnight trains

    Genuinely a practical, budget-friendly link between Cairo and Luxor or Aswan.

    Sleeper cabins save a hotel night while covering real distance.

  • Private drivers and organised tours

    Genuinely the standard way to reach the White Desert and Siwa Oasis.

    Both require genuine desert driving experience best left to local operators.

  • Taxis and rideshare apps

    Genuinely the easiest way around within Cairo and other major cities.

    Widely available and inexpensive, though traffic can be heavy.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Egypt

Egyptian culture is shaped by a genuine warmth toward guests, deep religious and family values, and a real, living pride in a history stretching back over 5,000 years.

  • Hospitality is genuinely taken seriously

    Being offered tea or food is common and a real gesture of welcome, not just politeness.

  • Modest dress is genuinely appreciated, especially outside resorts

    Covering shoulders and knees is respectful when visiting mosques, markets and non-touristic areas.

  • Bargaining is genuinely expected at markets

    Politely negotiating prices at bazaars is normal and part of the experience.

  • Tipping (baksheesh) is genuinely a real part of daily life

    Small tips for services large and small are expected almost everywhere, not optional extras.

  • Friday is genuinely the main day of rest

    Some businesses and sites keep different hours around Friday prayers.

  • Photographing people genuinely requires asking first

    Especially in more traditional or rural settings — a small tip is often appropriate.

Do

  • Dress modestly when visiting mosques, markets and areas outside resorts.
  • Carry small notes for tips, which are genuinely expected throughout your trip.
  • Politely negotiate prices at markets and with unofficial guides or vendors.
  • Stay hydrated and pace outdoor sightseeing around the heat, especially in summer.

Don't

  • Don't photograph people or military/government buildings without permission.
  • Don't drink tap water — stick to bottled or purified water nationwide.
  • Don't engage with unsolicited 'guides' at major sites without agreeing a price upfront.
  • Don't rush a Nile itinerary — the distances and heat genuinely demand a realistic pace.

Travel tips

Egypt travel tips

The things that most often go wrong on an Egypt trip, in rough order of how much they cost people.

  • Book major sites and a Nile cruise well ahead, especially for peak winter travel.
  • Carry small Egyptian pound notes for tipping, expected throughout your trip.
  • Agree prices upfront with taxi drivers, vendors and unofficial guides before accepting help.
  • Pack modest clothing for mosques, markets and areas outside resort zones.
  • Drink only bottled or purified water throughout the trip.
  • Pace outdoor sightseeing around the heat, especially at Luxor and Aswan outside winter.
  • Book domestic flights or overnight trains ahead to connect Cairo, Luxor and Aswan efficiently.
  • Hire a licensed guide at major sites for real historical context beyond what's posted.

FAQ

Visiting Egypt: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Egypt?

Ten to twelve days covers Cairo, a Nile cruise between Luxor and Aswan, and a Red Sea extension comfortably, which is the itinerary on this page. Four to five days is enough for Cairo and Giza alone, and genuinely still worthwhile.

What is the best time of year to visit Egypt?

October to April offers the most comfortable temperatures for sightseeing, especially in Luxor and Aswan, where summer heat can be genuinely dangerous.

Do you need a guide for the pyramids and temples?

Not required, but genuinely recommended — a licensed guide adds real historical context that's easy to miss exploring independently.

Is Egypt expensive?

Moderate overall, genuinely affordable outside a Nile cruise's own cost — mid-range travel runs about E£1,800–3,200 per person per day.

Is a Nile cruise worth it?

Yes, genuinely — it's the classic, comfortable way to see Luxor, Edfu, Kom Ombo and Aswan's temples without repeated hotel changes.

Is Egypt safe to visit?

Generally yes for tourists in main destinations, with genuinely persistent touting around major sites the main practical nuisance rather than serious danger.
