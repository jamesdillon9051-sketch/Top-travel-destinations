Welcome to Sharm El Sheikh

Sharm El Sheikh is genuinely Egypt's largest and best-developed Red Sea resort town, built along the southern tip of the Sinai Peninsula where the desert meets some of the most extraordinary coral reefs on Earth.

Its real draw is genuinely underwater — extraordinary visibility, healthy coral and a density of marine species that has made this coastline one of the world's premier diving destinations for decades.

Most visitors genuinely base themselves around Naama Bay or a nearby resort strip, splitting time between diving or snorkelling trips to Ras Mohammed National Park and simply relaxing by the Red Sea itself.

Why visit

Why visit Sharm El Sheikh?

Sharm El Sheikh's case rests on genuinely being one of the world's great diving and snorkelling destinations, with resort infrastructure to match.

  • Ras Mohammed is genuinely one of the world's best dive sites

    Extraordinary coral walls and visibility at the meeting point of two seas.

  • The coral reefs here are genuinely remarkably healthy

    Some of the best-preserved reef systems left in the Red Sea.

  • It's genuinely accessible to every experience level

    From beginner snorkellers to advanced technical divers, all well catered for.

  • The resort infrastructure is genuinely well developed

    A wide range of hotels, restaurants and dive centres to suit any budget.

  • It works genuinely well as a Red Sea extension to a Nile-focused trip

    A direct flight connects easily to Cairo or elsewhere in Egypt.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Sharm El Sheikh

Sharm El Sheikh's essential sights are genuinely mostly underwater, at some of the Red Sea's finest reef locations.

  1. 01

    Naama Bay

    Genuinely the town's main hub, a curving bay lined with hotels, restaurants and dive centres right on the water.

    Tip Genuinely a lively spot for an evening stroll, with restaurants spanning every cuisine and budget.

  2. 02

    Ras Mohammed National Park

    Genuinely one of the world's premier dive and snorkel sites, where the Gulf of Aqaba and Gulf of Suez meet in extraordinary biodiversity.

    Tip Genuinely book a full-day boat trip here rather than a rushed half-day for the best experience.

  3. 03

    The reef's coral formations

    Genuinely remarkably healthy and colourful, among the best-preserved coral systems remaining in the Red Sea.

    Tip Genuinely never touch or stand on coral — it's a fragile, slow-growing ecosystem.

  4. 04

    Clownfish in sea anemones

    Genuinely a reliable, delightful sighting for snorkellers and divers alike across the reef.

    Tip Genuinely watch quietly from a respectful distance — clownfish are territorial and can nip if disturbed.

  5. 05

    Hawksbill sea turtles

    Genuinely a real, if not guaranteed, highlight of many dives and snorkel trips around Ras Mohammed.

    Tip Genuinely never chase or touch a turtle — observe patiently and let it approach on its own terms.

  6. 06

    Naama Bay's beach

    Genuinely a relaxed stretch of sand right in the centre of town, easy access for a swim between other activities.

    Tip Genuinely most resort beaches are private to guests, though public access points exist nearby.

  7. 07

    The reef's moray eels

    Genuinely a common sighting for divers exploring the reef's crevices and coral overhangs.

    Tip Genuinely a good subject for underwater photography, if you keep a safe, respectful distance.

Things to do

Best things to do in Sharm El Sheikh

Sharm El Sheikh genuinely exists for its underwater world, though plenty of relaxation rounds out a visit.

  1. Dive or snorkel Ras Mohammed

    A genuinely essential Sharm El Sheikh experience, exploring some of the Red Sea's finest reef at this protected national park.

    Tip Genuinely book with a PADI or SSI certified dive centre, especially for anything beyond snorkelling.

  2. Spot clownfish in anemones

    A genuinely delightful, accessible sighting even for first-time snorkellers.

    Tip Genuinely bring an underwater camera or housing — this is one of the reef's most photogenic residents.

  3. Watch for sea turtles while snorkelling

    A genuinely memorable moment for many visitors, spotting a hawksbill turtle gliding gracefully over the reef.

    Tip Genuinely stay calm and still — sudden movements tend to send turtles swimming away quickly.

  4. Relax on Naama Bay's beach

    A genuinely easy way to balance out days of diving, simple beach time right in the centre of town.

    Tip Genuinely most resorts offer sun loungers and water sports right on the sand.

  5. Look for moray eels on a dive

    A genuinely exciting find for divers exploring the reef's nooks and coral formations.

    Tip Genuinely ask your dive guide to point out likely moray hiding spots along the route.

  6. Explore the reef's angelfish

    A genuinely colourful highlight of any dive or snorkel trip, angelfish among the reef's most striking residents.

    Tip Genuinely their vivid colours make them a rewarding subject for underwater photography.

Getting to and around Sharm El Sheikh

Getting around

Sharm El Sheikh is genuinely spread across a resort strip, with taxis and organised transfers the standard way around.

    Genuinely the standard way to reach dive and snorkel sites from your hotel.

    Most dive centres include hotel pickup as part of the trip.

  • Taxis

    Genuinely useful for reaching restaurants and Naama Bay from other resort areas.

Sharm El Sheikh is genuinely a year-round destination, with water temperatures staying warm even in winter.

  • Summer (June–August)

    Good

Travel tips

Sharm El Sheikh travel tips

Practical notes that make a Sharm El Sheikh visit noticeably smoother.

  • Book genuinely with a PADI or SSI certified dive centre for any diving beyond snorkelling.
  • Bring genuinely your own certification card if you're a qualified diver.
  • Pack genuinely reef-safe sunscreen to help protect the coral.
  • Never touch or stand on coral genuinely — it's a fragile, slow-growing ecosystem.
  • Stay genuinely hydrated and use sun protection, even on overcast days on the water.
  • Book a Ras Mohammed trip genuinely a day or two ahead through your resort or a dive centre.

FAQ

Sharm El Sheikh: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Sharm El Sheikh?

Four to five days genuinely allows for multiple dive or snorkel trips alongside real relaxation time.

Do you need to be a certified diver to visit?

No, genuinely not — excellent snorkelling is available for non-divers, and beginner dive courses are widely offered.

Is Ras Mohammed worth the trip?

Yes, genuinely — it's considered one of the world's premier dive and snorkel sites, with extraordinary visibility and marine life.

What is Sharm El Sheikh known for?

Genuinely world-class Red Sea diving and snorkelling, alongside a well-developed resort scene at Naama Bay.

Is Sharm El Sheikh good for beginners?

Yes, genuinely — the resort has extensive infrastructure for every experience level, from first-time snorkellers to advanced divers.

Can you combine Sharm El Sheikh with the Nile Valley?

Yes, genuinely easily — a direct flight connects to Cairo, making it a natural Red Sea extension to a Nile-focused Egypt trip.
