Welcome to Évora

Évora is the Alentejo region's historic capital, a genuinely well-preserved walled city whose layered history — Roman, Moorish, medieval — remains visible in a way few Portuguese cities can match, all recognised with UNESCO World Heritage status for the old town as a whole.

The city's most striking sight is genuinely its most literal: a 2nd-century Roman temple stands nearly intact at the heart of the old town, its Corinthian columns surviving centuries of reuse as a slaughterhouse and fortress before its true nature was rediscovered.

That ancient history sits alongside the genuinely unsettling Chapel of Bones, its interior walls and columns built from the skulls and bones of thousands of monks and locals, and a surrounding Alentejo countryside of cork oak plains and increasingly respected wine estates that reward slowing down beyond the city walls.

Why visit

Why visit Évora?

Évora's case is that no other Portuguese city holds this much visible ancient history within one genuinely walkable walled centre.

  • The Roman Temple is genuinely remarkable

    Standing nearly intact at the city's heart, one of the best-preserved Roman temples on the Iberian Peninsula.

  • The Chapel of Bones is genuinely unforgettable

    An interior built entirely from human bones, unlike anything else on a typical Portugal itinerary.

  • The old town is genuinely dense with layered history

    Roman, Moorish and medieval architecture sit within a compact, UNESCO-listed walled centre.

  • The Alentejo wine country is genuinely excellent

    Increasingly respected wine estates surround the city, considerably less crowded than the Douro Valley.

  • It's genuinely much quieter than Lisbon or Porto

    A relaxed, low-key pace with a fraction of the coastal cities' visitor numbers.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Évora

Évora's essential sights, spanning its Roman, medieval and religious layers of history.

  1. 01

    Roman Temple of Évora

    A 2nd-century temple, likely dedicated to the cult of Emperor Augustus, its Corinthian columns genuinely among the best preserved Roman remains anywhere in Portugal.

    Tip Visit at dusk when the temple is floodlit — the columns take on a genuinely striking presence against the darkening sky.

  2. 02

    Chapel of Bones (Capela dos Ossos)

    A genuinely macabre 16th-century chapel, its walls and columns built from the bones and skulls of thousands of monks and locals, intended as a meditation on mortality.

    Tip The chapel's Latin inscription at the entrance, translating roughly to 'we bones, here, await yours,' genuinely sets the tone.

  3. 03

    Évora Cathedral (Sé de Évora)

    A genuinely imposing granite Gothic cathedral, its rooftop terrace offering sweeping views over the old town's rooftops.

    Tip Climb to the cathedral's rooftop terrace for genuinely one of the best views in the city.

  4. 04

    University of Évora

    A genuinely historic university, its main courtyard lined with azulejo-tiled classrooms still in active academic use.

    Tip Free to wander the main courtyard and admire the tilework, even without a formal tour.

  5. 05

    Praça do Giraldo

    Évora's central square, genuinely the social heart of the city, ringed by cafés and arcaded buildings.

    Tip A good spot to simply sit with a coffee and watch the city's genuinely unhurried daily rhythm.

  6. 06

    The city walls and aqueduct

    Évora's medieval walls and the 16th-century Água de Prata aqueduct, genuinely worth a walk for a different perspective on the city's layered architecture.

    Tip Parts of the aqueduct have genuinely been built directly into, with houses incorporated along its arches.

Things to do

Best things to do in Évora

Beyond the Roman and religious sights, Évora rewards the surrounding Alentejo countryside and its wine culture.

  1. Taste Alentejo wine at a local estate

    The Alentejo has become genuinely one of Portugal's most respected wine regions, its estates producing excellent reds from the plains surrounding Évora.

    Tip Several wine estates near Évora offer genuinely worthwhile tours combining tastings with the region's cork oak landscape.

  2. Explore the cork oak countryside

    The Alentejo produces a genuinely significant share of the world's cork, its distinctive oak groves a defining feature of the landscape around Évora.

    Tip A countryside drive or guided tour explains the genuinely fascinating, sustainable cork harvesting process.

  3. Visit the Cromlech of the Almendres

    A genuinely significant prehistoric stone circle predating Stonehenge, a short drive from Évora and considerably less visited than its British counterpart.

    Tip A rental car or organised tour is genuinely necessary — public transport doesn't reach the site well.

  4. Wander Évora's old town streets

    The genuinely compact walled centre rewards slow, unhurried wandering beyond the headline sights.

    Tip Évora's whitewashed houses with yellow trim are a genuinely distinctive Alentejo architectural style worth noticing.

  5. Visit a nearby Alentejo village

    Villages like Monsaraz, a genuinely picturesque hilltop settlement, offer a worthwhile day trip into the wider Alentejo countryside.

    Tip Monsaraz overlooks the Alqueva reservoir, Europe's largest artificial lake, genuinely worth the detour for the view alone.

Local food

What to eat in Évora

Évora sits at the heart of Alentejo cuisine, genuinely distinct from the rest of Portugal for its bread-based dishes and excellent wine.

  • Açorda alentejana

    A genuinely simple, satisfying bread soup flavoured with garlic, coriander and olive oil, often topped with a poached egg, an Alentejo staple.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout Évora's old town.

  • Migas

    Fried breadcrumbs typically served alongside pork, a genuinely hearty Alentejo dish reflecting the region's tradition of using every part of the bread.

    Where to try it Traditional Alentejo restaurants throughout the city.

  • Alentejo wine

    Increasingly respected reds from the plains surrounding Évora, genuinely excellent and considerably less internationally known than Douro wines.

    Where to try it Wine estates around Évora, or wine bars throughout the old town.

  • Porco preto (black Iberian pork)

    Pork from free-range black pigs fed partly on acorns from the region's cork and holm oak forests, genuinely one of the Alentejo's finest products.

    Where to try it Traditional restaurants throughout Évora, often served simply grilled.

  • Queijo Serpa cheese

    A genuinely distinctive sheep's milk cheese from the wider Alentejo region, with a soft, creamy texture and protected geographic status.

    Where to try it Local markets and traditional restaurants throughout Évora.

Transport

Getting to and around Évora

Évora is an easy train or bus trip from Lisbon, or a short drive through the Alentejo countryside.

Getting there

  • Train from Lisbon

    Direct trains from Lisbon reach Évora in about 1.5 hours.

    A genuinely comfortable, straightforward connection with no changes required.

  • Bus from Lisbon

    Regular buses offer a comparable journey time to the train, often at a lower price.

    A reasonable alternative if train timings don't suit your schedule.

  • Rental car

    Offers the flexibility to explore the wider Alentejo countryside, including the Cromlech of the Almendres and nearby villages.

    Genuinely worthwhile if combining Évora with wine estates or countryside villages beyond the city itself.

Getting around

Évora's walled old town is genuinely compact and entirely walkable.

  • Walking

    The Roman Temple, cathedral and Chapel of Bones all sit within a genuinely compact, walkable historic centre.

    The old town's flat layout within the walls makes walking considerably easier than in Lisbon or Porto.

  • Rental car for the countryside

    Necessary for reaching the Cromlech of the Almendres, wine estates and nearby villages like Monsaraz.

    Public transport to these outlying sites is genuinely limited.

When to go

Best time to visit Évora

Évora's inland Alentejo location makes it genuinely hotter in summer than Portugal's coastal cities, favouring spring and autumn visits.

  • Spring (April–June)

    Best time

    Mild, increasingly warm weather, ideal for walking the old town and countryside before summer's inland heat.

  • Summer (July–August)

    Shoulder

    Genuinely intense heat, often exceeding 35°C, being considerably further inland than the coast.

  • Autumn (September–October)

    Best time

    Comfortable temperatures and the wine harvest in the surrounding countryside.

  • Winter (November–March)

    Good

    Mild and genuinely quiet, with the Alentejo countryside at its greenest after autumn rains.

Worth planning around: Midday hours outdoors in July and August, when the Alentejo's inland heat genuinely exceeds coastal Portugal by a significant margin.

Travel tips

Évora travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to an Évora trip.

  • Visit the Roman Temple at dusk when it's floodlit for a genuinely striking view.
  • Rent a car if you want to visit the Cromlech of the Almendres or nearby villages like Monsaraz.
  • Pack for genuine inland heat in summer — Évora runs considerably hotter than coastal Lisbon or Porto.
  • Book a wine estate tour ahead, particularly during the autumn harvest season.
  • Wear comfortable shoes for the old town's cobblestones, though the terrain itself is genuinely flatter than Lisbon or Porto.
  • Combine Évora with a countryside day trip if you have time — the wider Alentejo genuinely rewards it.

FAQ

Évora: frequently asked questions

Is Évora worth a day trip from Lisbon?

It's possible as a day trip, but an overnight stay is genuinely better — the Roman Temple lit at dusk and the surrounding wine country reward more time than a rushed single day allows.

How many days do you need in Évora?

Two days covers the old town's Roman and religious sights comfortably, with a countryside day for wine tasting or the Cromlech of the Almendres a worthwhile addition.

What is the Chapel of Bones?

A 16th-century chapel whose interior walls and columns are built from the bones and skulls of thousands of monks and locals, intended as a meditation on mortality — genuinely one of Portugal's most unusual sights.

Is Évora expensive?

No, genuinely one of Portugal's more affordable regions — mid-range travel runs roughly €65–120 per person per day, noticeably below Lisbon and comparable to Porto.

What is the best time of year to visit?

April to June and September to October offer the most comfortable weather. Évora's inland Alentejo location runs genuinely hotter than coastal Portugal in July and August.
