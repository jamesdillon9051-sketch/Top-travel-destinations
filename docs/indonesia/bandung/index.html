Welcome to Bandung

Bandung sits in a highland basin ringed by volcanoes, giving West Java's capital a noticeably cooler climate than Jakarta and making it a long-standing weekend escape for the capital's residents.

The surrounding countryside holds some of Java's most striking volcanic scenery — Kawah Putih's otherworldly turquoise crater lake and Tangkuban Perahu's smoking rim are both within easy day-trip distance, alongside photogenic tea plantations carpeting the highland slopes.

The city itself is the heart of Sundanese culture, with its own distinct language, traditional angklung bamboo music and a food scene locals will readily argue is Indonesia's best — a real claim worth testing for yourself.

Why visit

Why visit Bandung?

Bandung's case rests on dramatic volcanic scenery paired with some of Indonesia's best regional food.

  • Kawah Putih is otherworldly

    A pale turquoise crater lake with bone-white shores, looking more like another planet than West Java.

  • Tangkuban Perahu is an accessible active volcano

    Drivable right up to its smoking crater rim, unlike most volcanoes requiring a serious trek.

  • Sundanese food is a highlight of the whole trip

    Batagor, siomay and karedok are all Bandung specialties locals rightly consider Indonesia's best.

  • The climate is cool and refreshing

    Highland elevation keeps Bandung noticeably cooler than Jakarta or the coast, a real relief in the heat.

  • It's an easy trip from Jakarta

    A comfortable train ride connects the two cities directly, making Bandung an easy add-on.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Bandung

Bandung's essential sights combine dramatic volcanic craters with the city's own colonial and cultural landmarks.

  1. 01

    Kawah Putih (White Crater Lake)

    A striking volcanic crater lake, its water shifting between turquoise and pale green, ringed by bone-white mineral shores.

    Tip The colour of the water can shift with sulphur levels — check recent photos before visiting for the best display.

  2. 02

    Tangkuban Perahu

    An active volcano shaped, by local legend, like an overturned boat, with a paved road running right up to its smoking crater rim.

    Tip Arrive early morning for the clearest views before afternoon cloud typically rolls in.

  3. 03

    Gedung Sate

    Bandung's defining landmark, a striking colonial-era government building whose spire resembles a satay skewer, giving the building its name.

    Tip A small museum inside explains the building's unusual architecture and history.

  4. 04

    Braga Street

    An atmospheric street of Art Deco buildings from Bandung's colonial-era heyday as 'Paris of Java,' now home to cafés and galleries.

    Tip Evening brings the street to life, with buskers and a lively café scene.

  5. 05

    Saung Angklung Udjo

    A beloved cultural venue keeping traditional Sundanese angklung bamboo music alive through daily performances.

    Tip Book ahead for the afternoon performance — it's a deservedly popular show.

  6. 06

    Ciwidey Tea Plantations

    Photogenic rolling tea fields carpeting the highlands around Kawah Putih, easy to combine with a crater lake visit.

    Tip Morning light and mist often make for the most atmospheric photos here.

  7. 07

    Dago Hills

    Bandung's elevated northern district, with cafés and viewpoints looking back down over the whole city.

    Tip Sunset and after-dark visits both give great views over the city lights.

Things to do

Best things to do in Bandung

Bandung rewards mixing volcanic day trips with the city's café culture and food scene.

  1. Watch a traditional angklung performance

    A joyful, participatory show mixing traditional bamboo music with dance, often inviting the audience to join in.

    Tip Arrive a little early — the venue's grounds and small museum are worth a wander beforehand.

  2. Walk Braga Street's Art Deco architecture

    A relaxed evening wander among Bandung's colonial-era heyday architecture, now filled with cafés and small galleries.

    Tip Several buildings house small independent coffee shops worth a stop.

  3. Shop Bandung's factory outlets

    A long-standing local specialty, with factory outlet stores selling discounted local and export-surplus clothing throughout the city.

    Tip The Riau Street (Jalan RE Martadinata) area holds the highest concentration of outlets.

  4. Picnic among Ciwidey's tea plantations

    A peaceful way to spend an afternoon, walking or picnicking among the rolling green tea fields near Kawah Putih.

    Tip Combine with a Kawah Putih visit — they sit close together on the same day-trip route.

  5. Watch sunset from the Dago hills

    A popular local evening ritual, watching the city light up from one of Dago's elevated cafés or viewpoints.

    Tip Traffic up to Dago can be heavy in the evening — allow extra time.

  6. Soak in Ciwidey's hot springs

    A relaxing way to end a day of crater and tea plantation sightseeing, with several natural hot springs in the Ciwidey area.

    Tip Combine with a Kawah Putih day trip for an easy, efficient itinerary.

Local food

What to eat in Bandung

Bandung's food is a highlight in its own right, with Sundanese specialties locals rightly claim among Indonesia's best.

  • Batagor

    Bandung's most famous street snack, fried fish and tofu dumplings served with a rich peanut sauce.

    Where to try it Batagor stalls and restaurants throughout the city.

  • Siomay

    Batagor's steamed cousin, fish dumplings with vegetables served in the same rich peanut sauce.

    Where to try it Street vendors and restaurants throughout Bandung.

  • Karedok

    A refreshing Sundanese salad of raw vegetables in peanut sauce, gado-gado's uncooked Bandung cousin.

    Where to try it Sundanese restaurants throughout the city.

  • Nasi timbel

    A traditional Sundanese meal, rice steamed in banana leaf and served with fried chicken, tofu and fresh vegetables.

    Where to try it Traditional Sundanese restaurants throughout Bandung.

  • Surabi

    A traditional Sundanese pancake, cooked in a clay pan over charcoal and topped with either savoury or sweet toppings.

    Where to try it Traditional surabi vendors throughout the city.

  • Es cendol

    A refreshing iced dessert drink of green rice-flour jelly, coconut milk and palm sugar syrup, welcome in Bandung's cooler air.

    Where to try it Street vendors and dessert stalls throughout the city.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Bandung

Bandung offers a wide range of accommodation, from budget guesthouses to highland resort hotels.

  • City centre (near Braga and Gedung Sate)

    The most convenient base, with easy access to the city's colonial-era landmarks.

    Best for: Being central and walkable to major sights

  • Dago and the northern highlands

    Slightly further out, but rewards with noticeably cooler air and good city views.

    Best for: A cooler climate and elevated views

Transport

Getting to and around Bandung

Bandung is well connected to Jakarta, making it an easy add-on to a wider Java trip.

Getting there

  • Train from Jakarta

    The most popular way in, a comfortable 3-3.5 hour ride through scenic highland countryside.

    Executive-class trains run frequently between Jakarta and Bandung.

  • Domestic flight

    Available from other major Indonesian cities, landing at Husein Sastranegara airport.

    Flight options are more limited than the Jakarta train route.

  • Toll road drive from Jakarta

    A straightforward 2.5-3 hour drive via the Cipularang toll road.

    Weekend traffic can extend this noticeably — avoid Friday evening departures.

Getting around

Bandung's volcanic day trips require a car, while the city centre is manageable on foot or by app.

  • Ride-hailing apps (Gojek, Grab)

    The easiest way around the city centre itself.

    Widely available and reliably priced.

  • Private driver or car charter

    The standard way to reach Kawah Putih and Tangkuban Perahu, both some distance from the city.

    Typically booked through a hotel or driver for a fixed daily rate.

  • On foot

    Fine for exploring the city centre's colonial landmarks and Braga Street.

    Central sights sit within a comfortable walking radius of each other.

When to go

Best time to visit Bandung

Bandung's cool highland climate stays pleasant year-round, though dry season brings the clearest views at Tangkuban Perahu and Kawah Putih.

  • Dry season (April–October)

    Best time

    The clearest weather for crater views and outdoor exploring.

  • Shoulder months (April, October)

    Good

    Good weather odds with thinner weekend crowds than peak season.

  • Wet season (November–March)

    Good

    Still workable, with short, heavy afternoon downpours and lush green tea plantations.

  • Weekends year-round

    Good

    Busy with Jakarta day-trippers and weekend visitors — weekdays are noticeably quieter.

Worth planning around: Avoid weekend day trips to Kawah Putih and Tangkuban Perahu if possible — both get significantly busier with Jakarta visitors.

Itinerary

Two days in Bandung

Built around the volcanic craters and tea plantations on day one, the city itself on day two.

  1. Day1

    Craters and tea plantations

    Kawah Putih and the Ciwidey highlands.

    • Morning Kawah Putih's turquoise crater lake.
    • Afternoon The tea plantations around Ciwidey.
    • Evening Return to Bandung for dinner.
  2. Day2

    The city and its culture

    Colonial landmarks and Sundanese culture.

    • Morning Gedung Sate and Braga Street.
    • Afternoon A performance at Saung Angklung Udjo.
    • Evening Sunset and dinner around the Dago hills.

Travel tips

Bandung travel tips

Practical notes that make a Bandung trip run more smoothly.

  • Book the Jakarta-Bandung train ahead, especially on weekends.
  • Visit Kawah Putih and Tangkuban Perahu on weekdays if possible to avoid the busiest crowds.
  • Bring a light jacket — Bandung's highland air is noticeably cooler than Jakarta's.
  • Book Saung Angklung Udjo tickets ahead for the afternoon performance.
  • Allow extra time for Dago traffic in the evening.
  • Carry small rupiah notes for street food and market stalls.

FAQ

Bandung: frequently asked questions

Is Bandung worth visiting?

Yes — its volcanic scenery, cool climate and Sundanese food make it a worthwhile add-on to a Jakarta or wider Java trip.

How far is Bandung from Jakarta?

About 3 to 3.5 hours by train, or 2.5 to 3 hours by car outside peak traffic.

Can you visit Kawah Putih and Tangkuban Perahu in one day?

It's possible but ambitious — most visitors split them across two separate day trips given the driving distances involved.

How many days do you need in Bandung?

Two to three days covers the main craters, tea plantations and city sights at a comfortable pace.

Is Bandung cooler than Jakarta?

Yes, noticeably so — its highland elevation keeps temperatures several degrees cooler than the coast.

What is Bandung famous for?

Its volcanic craters, tea plantations, colonial Art Deco architecture and a Sundanese food scene widely considered among Indonesia's best.
