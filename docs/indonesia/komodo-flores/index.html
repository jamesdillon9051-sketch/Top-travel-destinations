Welcome to Komodo National Park & Flores

Komodo National Park is genuinely one of Indonesia's most dramatic corners, a scatter of rugged, savanna-covered islands between Flores and Sumbawa, home to the only place on Earth where Komodo dragons still roam wild.

The park's islands are genuinely as visually striking as its dragons — Padar's three-bayed viewpoint, Pink Beach's genuinely rose-tinted sand, and turquoise water that draws divers for some of Indonesia's richest reefs outside Raja Ampat.

Labuan Bajo, the harbour town on Flores that serves as the park's gateway, has genuinely grown fast in recent years, and Flores itself holds further rewards beyond the harbour — not least Kelimutu, a volcano with three crater lakes that change colour over time.

Why visit

Why visit Komodo National Park & Flores?

Komodo and Flores' case rests on genuinely wild wildlife and island scenery found almost nowhere else on Earth.

  • Komodo dragons are genuinely found wild nowhere else

    This is the only place on the planet these giant monitor lizards still live in their natural habitat.

  • Padar Island's viewpoint is genuinely one of Indonesia's iconic views

    Three curved bays of different coloured sand visible from a single hilltop, especially striking at sunrise.

  • Pink Beach is genuinely a rare natural phenomenon

    Its sand gets its rose tint from crushed red coral mixed with white sand, one of only a handful of such beaches worldwide.

  • The diving is genuinely world-class

    Strong currents feed the park's reefs, drawing manta rays and dense marine life close to the surface.

  • Kelimutu genuinely adds an entirely different landscape

    Three crater lakes on Flores that shift colour over months and years, a genuinely unique geological phenomenon.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Komodo National Park & Flores

Most of Komodo's essential sights are genuinely reached only by boat, making a multi-day trip the standard way to see them properly.

  1. 01

    Padar Island Viewpoint

    Genuinely one of Indonesia's most photographed views, a short but steep climb rewarded with three curved bays of black, white and pink sand spread below.

    Tip Arrive genuinely at sunrise, both for the light and to beat the heat of the climb.

  2. 02

    Pink Beach

    A genuinely rare rose-tinted beach, its colour coming from crushed red coral mixed into the white sand, with excellent snorkelling just offshore.

    Tip The genuinely pink tint shows best when the sand is wet, right at the waterline.

  3. 03

    Komodo Island

    The genuinely original home of the Komodo dragon, with ranger-guided walking trails offering a real chance of spotting these giant lizards wild.

    Tip Rangers genuinely carry forked sticks for safety — always stay close to your guide.

  4. 04

    Rinca Island

    Genuinely offering a higher dragon-sighting chance than Komodo island itself, with a shorter, easier ranger-led walking trail.

    Tip Dragons genuinely often rest near the ranger station kitchen — sightings here are almost guaranteed.

  5. 05

    Labuan Bajo

    The genuinely fast-growing gateway town to Komodo National Park, its harbour packed with liveaboard boats and a lively sunset waterfront.

    Tip The genuinely waterfront restaurants along the harbour are the best spot for a sunset dinner.

  6. 06

    Kelimutu Crater Lakes

    A genuinely unique volcanic phenomenon further into Flores, three crater lakes side by side that shift between different colours over months and years.

    Tip It's genuinely a long drive from Labuan Bajo — most visitors fly into or out of nearby Ende or Maumere instead.

  7. 07

    Manta Point

    A genuinely reliable manta ray cleaning station within the park, drawing these giant rays close enough for excellent snorkelling and diving.

    Tip Boat trips genuinely time visits around tidal currents — ask your operator about the best window.

Things to do

Best things to do in Komodo National Park & Flores

A Komodo trip genuinely revolves around boat-based island-hopping, mixing wildlife, beaches and diving over several days.

  1. Hike Padar Island at sunrise

    A genuinely essential Komodo experience, climbing to the viewpoint before the heat and crowds of midday.

    Tip Liveaboards genuinely often anchor overnight nearby specifically for an early Padar climb.

  2. See wild Komodo dragons with a ranger

    A genuinely unforgettable wildlife encounter, walking within safe but close distance of the world's largest living lizard.

    Tip Early morning genuinely tours see dragons more active before the day's heat sets in.

  3. Snorkel or dive with manta rays

    A genuinely thrilling encounter at the park's manta cleaning stations, where these giant rays circle close to the surface.

    Tip A genuinely wetsuit is worth renting even for snorkelling — currents here can be strong and cool.

  4. Relax on Pink Beach

    A genuinely worthwhile stop on any island-hopping itinerary, with good snorkelling right off the beach itself.

    Tip Bring genuinely reef-safe sunscreen — the beach sits within a protected marine park.

  5. Island-hop by liveaboard boat

    Genuinely the classic way to see Komodo properly, sleeping aboard a traditional wooden phinisi boat over two or three days.

    Tip Book genuinely a few months ahead in peak season — good boats fill up fast.

  6. Watch sunset in Labuan Bajo

    A genuinely relaxed way to end a day of island-hopping, with harbourside bars and restaurants facing directly into the sunset.

    Tip Rooftop bars genuinely along the hillside above the harbour give the best elevated sunset views.

Local food

What to eat in Komodo National Park & Flores

Flores' food is genuinely centred on fresh seafood, reflecting Labuan Bajo's identity as a working fishing harbour turned dive hub.

  • Ikan bakar (grilled fish)

    Genuinely the standard evening meal along the Labuan Bajo waterfront, whole fish grilled over charcoal and served with rice and sambal.

    Where to try it Waterfront restaurants along Labuan Bajo's harbour.

  • Sambal

    A genuinely essential condiment at every meal, a fiery chili sauce that varies noticeably from restaurant to restaurant.

    Where to try it Alongside nearly every meal throughout Flores.

  • Nasi goreng

    A genuinely reliable option aboard liveaboard boats and at harbourside warungs alike.

    Where to try it Warungs and liveaboard boat kitchens throughout the region.

  • Flores coffee

    Genuinely grown across Flores' volcanic highlands, particularly around Bajawa, with a distinct, full-bodied character.

    Where to try it Cafés in Labuan Bajo and highland towns further into Flores.

  • Jagung titi

    A genuinely traditional Flores snack, corn kernels hand-flattened and toasted, often eaten alongside coffee.

    Where to try it Markets and warungs in Flores' highland towns.

  • Fresh seafood catch

    Genuinely landed daily at Labuan Bajo's harbour, with the day's catch often visible right at the fish market each morning.

    Where to try it The fish market and harbourside restaurants in Labuan Bajo.

Transport

Getting to and around Komodo National Park & Flores

Labuan Bajo is genuinely reached almost exclusively by air, with Komodo National Park itself accessible only by boat.

Getting there

  • Domestic flight to Labuan Bajo

    Genuinely the standard way in, with direct flights from Bali, Jakarta and other major Indonesian cities.

    Komodo Airport sits just outside the town centre.

  • Liveaboard or day boat from Labuan Bajo

    The genuinely only way to reach Komodo National Park's islands themselves.

    Boats depart from Labuan Bajo's harbour daily in season.

  • Onward flight to Ende or Maumere for Kelimutu

    Genuinely the practical way to reach Kelimutu without a very long overland drive from Labuan Bajo.

    Short regional flights connect these towns within Flores.

Getting around

Getting around Komodo National Park genuinely means boats — there are no roads connecting its islands.

  • Liveaboard boat

    Genuinely the best way to see multiple islands without backtracking to Labuan Bajo each night.

    Typically booked for 2-4 days, visiting several islands and dive sites.

  • Day boat trips

    Genuinely suit visitors with less time, covering one or two islands in a single day from Labuan Bajo.

    Shared and private options are both widely available.

  • Car or scooter within Labuan Bajo

    Genuinely useful for getting around the town itself and to the airport.

    Ride-hailing apps have more limited coverage here than in Bali or Java.

When to go

Best time to visit Komodo National Park & Flores

Dry season genuinely brings calmer water for island-hopping, though visibility for diving actually peaks slightly later in the year.

  • Dry season (April–October)

    Best time

    Genuinely the most reliable weather and calmest seas for boat-based island-hopping.

  • Shoulder months (October–December)

    Good

    Genuinely often the best diving visibility of the year, with generally still-manageable seas.

  • Wet season (January–March)

    Good

    Genuinely rougher seas can disrupt boat trips, and some operators reduce their schedules.

  • Kelimutu visits

    Good

    Genuinely workable year-round, though the crater lakes can be shrouded in mist at any time of year.

Worth planning around: Avoid genuinely booking a tight liveaboard schedule deep in wet season (January–March), when rough seas most often disrupt boat plans.

Travel tips

Komodo National Park & Flores travel tips

A handful of practical notes make a Komodo trip go far more smoothly.

  • Book a genuinely reputable boat operator — safety standards vary noticeably between companies.
  • Bring genuinely reef-safe sunscreen, required within the marine park.
  • Never approach Komodo dragons genuinely without a ranger present.
  • Pack seasickness medication genuinely if prone to it — open-water crossings can get rough.
  • Book liveaboards genuinely months ahead for peak dry-season dates.
  • Bring genuinely cash — card payments are limited outside Labuan Bajo's main hotels.

FAQ

Komodo National Park & Flores: frequently asked questions

Is it safe to see Komodo dragons in the wild?

Yes, genuinely, with a park ranger present at all times — never attempt to see them without one.

How do you get to Komodo National Park?

Fly genuinely into Labuan Bajo on Flores, then take a boat — there's no other way into the park itself.

Is Komodo Island or Rinca Island better for seeing dragons?

Rinca genuinely offers a slightly higher sighting chance with an easier walk, though both islands are genuinely worthwhile.

How many days do you need for Komodo National Park?

Two to four days genuinely covers the park's highlights, whether as day trips from Labuan Bajo or a short liveaboard.

Is Pink Beach's sand really pink?

Yes, genuinely, though subtly — the rose tint comes from crushed red coral mixed into white sand, and shows best when wet.

Can you combine Komodo with Kelimutu on Flores?

Yes, genuinely, though it typically requires a separate regional flight to Ende or Maumere, since it's a long overland drive from Labuan Bajo.
