Welcome to Lombok & the Gili Islands

Lombok sits genuinely just a short boat ride from Bali, but feels like a different island in every meaningful way — quieter beaches, a distinct Sasak culture, and an active volcano, Mount Rinjani, that draws serious trekkers rather than day-trippers.

Its genuine headline draw for most visitors is the three Gili Islands off Lombok's northwest coast — Gili Trawangan, Gili Air and Gili Meno — all genuinely car-free, with horse carts and bicycles the only transport and a pace of life noticeably slower than Bali's coast.

The three Gilis each have genuinely distinct characters despite their small size and close proximity: Trawangan is the liveliest, with nightlife and dive shops; Air sits in the middle, popular with a slightly older crowd; and Meno is genuinely the quietest, favoured for its near-total lack of development.

Why visit

Why visit Lombok & the Gili Islands?

Lombok and the Gilis' case rests on genuinely offering Bali's beach appeal with a slower, quieter pace.

  • The Gili Islands are genuinely car-free

    No cars or scooters anywhere — horse carts and bicycles are the only transport, and the pace shows it.

  • It's genuinely quieter and less developed than Bali

    The same tropical island appeal, with noticeably fewer crowds outside Gili Trawangan's busier strip.

  • Mount Rinjani is genuinely a serious trekking destination

    Indonesia's second-highest volcano, with a multi-day trek to a stunning crater lake.

  • Sasak culture is genuinely distinct from Bali's Hindu traditions

    Predominantly Muslim, with its own architecture, weaving and village traditions worth exploring.

  • The diving and snorkelling are genuinely excellent

    Sea turtles are a near-guaranteed sighting around all three Gili Islands.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Lombok & the Gili Islands

Lombok and the Gilis' essential sights span three distinct islands plus the mainland's volcano and villages.

  1. 01

    Gili Trawangan

    Genuinely the largest and liveliest of the three Gilis, with beach bars, dive shops and a lap-the-island cycle route.

    Tip Rent genuinely a bicycle to circle the whole island — it takes about an hour at an easy pace.

  2. 02

    Gili Air

    A genuinely relaxed middle ground between Trawangan's energy and Meno's quiet, popular with a slightly older, more low-key crowd.

    Tip Gili Air's genuinely east coast has some of the best snorkelling right off the beach.

  3. 03

    Gili Meno

    Genuinely the quietest and least developed of the three, with a small population and a real sense of escape.

    Tip Genuinely the whole island can be walked in under two hours — a good half-day trip from Trawangan or Air.

  4. 04

    Mount Rinjani

    Genuinely Indonesia's second-highest volcano, its summit trek passing a stunning crescent-shaped crater lake, Segara Anak.

    Tip The genuinely full summit trek takes two to three days — book a licensed guide well ahead.

  5. 05

    Kuta Lombok

    A genuinely different Kuta from Bali's, a laid-back surf town in southern Lombok with dramatic hills and quieter beaches.

    Tip Don't genuinely confuse it with Bali's Kuta when booking — double-check which island your accommodation is on.

  6. 06

    Sade Traditional Sasak Village

    A genuinely still-inhabited traditional village, its thatched houses and rice barns built in centuries-old Sasak style.

    Tip A genuinely small entry donation is customary, and local guides offer short tours around the village.

  7. 07

    Tiu Kelep Waterfall

    A genuinely rewarding waterfall reached by a scenic riverside walk through Lombok's northern hills.

    Tip The genuinely walk involves several river crossings — wear shoes you don't mind getting wet.

Things to do

Best things to do in Lombok & the Gili Islands

Lombok and the Gilis genuinely reward a relaxed pace, mixing island time with one or two more active days.

  1. Snorkel with sea turtles

    A genuinely near-guaranteed encounter around all three Gili Islands, with turtles regularly spotted just off the beach.

    Tip Give turtles genuinely plenty of space — they're protected, and getting too close disturbs their feeding.

  2. Trek Mount Rinjani

    A genuinely serious multi-day trek for those wanting more than a beach holiday, ending at a spectacular crater lake.

    Tip Book genuinely a licensed guide and porter through an established trekking operator, not independently.

  3. Island-hop between the three Gilis

    A genuinely easy way to compare all three islands' distinct characters in a single day using the public boat service.

    Tip The genuinely public boats are far cheaper than chartering a private one between islands.

  4. Cycle around car-free Gili Trawangan

    A genuinely pleasant way to see the whole island, with no traffic to contend with anywhere on the loop.

    Tip Start genuinely in late afternoon to catch the island's famous sunset from the west side.

  5. Watch traditional weaving at Sade Village

    A genuinely fascinating look at traditional Sasak ikat weaving, still practised by hand in the village today.

    Tip Handwoven genuinely textiles here make a far more authentic souvenir than mass-produced market versions.

  6. Surf at Kuta Lombok's beaches

    A genuinely less crowded surf scene than Bali's south coast, with several breaks suited to intermediate and advanced surfers.

    Tip Selong Belanak, genuinely nearby, offers gentler waves better suited to beginners.

Local food

What to eat in Lombok & the Gili Islands

Lombok's food is genuinely famous for its heat — Sasak cuisine runs noticeably spicier than Bali's or Java's.

  • Ayam Taliwang

    Lombok's genuinely signature dish, chicken grilled or fried and coated in a fiery chili sambal, named after a district in Mataram.

    Where to try it Warungs throughout Lombok, especially around Mataram.

  • Plecing kangkung

    A genuinely essential Lombok side dish, blanched water spinach tossed in a raw chili and tomato sambal.

    Where to try it Warungs alongside Ayam Taliwang throughout Lombok.

  • Seafood barbecue on the Gilis

    A genuinely essential Gili evening ritual, fresh catch grilled beachside and served with rice and sambal as the sun sets.

    Where to try it Beachfront warungs on Gili Trawangan and Gili Air.

  • Fresh coconut

    A genuinely essential beach drink across all three Gilis, served straight from the shell.

    Where to try it Beach vendors throughout the Gili Islands.

  • Nasi goreng

    A genuinely reliable, familiar option at warungs across Lombok and the Gilis alike.

    Where to try it Warungs throughout Lombok and the Gili Islands.

  • Local Lombok coffee

    Genuinely grown on Lombok's volcanic slopes, served strong and traditionally unfiltered at village warungs.

    Where to try it Warungs in Mataram and around Mount Rinjani's trailheads.

Where to stay in Lombok & the Gili Islands

Accommodation genuinely varies widely by island — Trawangan has the most choice, Meno the least development.

  • Gili Trawangan

    Best for: The widest choice of hotels, dive shops and nightlife

  • Gili Air

    Fewer crowds than Trawangan, with a still-solid range of guesthouses and restaurants.

    Best for: A genuine balance of amenities and quiet

  • Gili Meno

    Best for: Genuine peace and quiet above all else

Transport

Getting to and around Lombok & the Gili Islands

Lombok is genuinely reached directly by air, with the Gilis a short further boat ride away.

Getting there

  • Direct flight to Lombok

    Genuinely available from Bali, Jakarta and other major Indonesian cities.

    Lombok International Airport sits in the island's south.

  • Fast boat from Bali

    A genuinely popular direct option connecting Bali to the Gili Islands without going via mainland Lombok.

    Crossings take about 1.5-2.5 hours depending on the operator and departure point.

  • Public boat from Bangsal, Lombok

    The genuinely cheapest way to reach the Gilis from mainland Lombok.

    Short crossings of 15-30 minutes to each of the three islands.

Getting around

The Gilis are genuinely car-free, while Lombok itself requires more conventional transport.

  • Bicycle or horse cart (Gilis)

    Genuinely the only motorised-free transport options on all three Gili Islands.

    Bicycles can be rented cheaply at guesthouses throughout the islands.

  • Scooter or car rental (Lombok)

    Genuinely the standard way to explore mainland Lombok's beaches and villages.

    An international driving permit is genuinely required and increasingly enforced.

  • Public or chartered boats (between islands)

    Genuinely necessary for moving between the Gilis and mainland Lombok.

    Public boats run scheduled routes; charters offer more flexibility at higher cost.

When to go

Best time to visit Lombok & the Gili Islands

Dry season genuinely brings the most reliable weather for both beach time and the Rinjani trek, which closes entirely in the wettest months.

  • Dry season (April–October)

    Best time

    Genuinely the most reliable weather, and the only season Mount Rinjani's trekking routes are open.

  • Shoulder months (April, October)

    Good

    Genuinely good weather odds with somewhat thinner crowds than peak season.

  • Wet season (November–March)

    Good

    Genuinely still workable for the Gili Islands, though Rinjani's trekking routes close for safety.

  • Peak season (July–August)

    Good

    Genuinely the busiest time on Gili Trawangan specifically, worth booking accommodation ahead for.

Worth planning around: Mount Rinjani's trekking routes genuinely close entirely from around January to March for safety during the wettest months.

Itinerary

Three days in the Gili Islands

Built around sampling all three islands' distinct characters plus a Lombok mainland day.

  1. Day1

    Gili Trawangan

    Arrival and the liveliest of the three islands.

    • Morning Boat from Bali or Bangsal to Gili Trawangan.
    • Afternoon Cycle the island's loop road.
    • Evening Sunset drinks on the west side of the island.
  2. Day2

    Gili Meno and Gili Air

    A quieter contrast to Trawangan's energy.

    • Morning Boat to Gili Meno for a peaceful morning.
    • Afternoon Continue to Gili Air for the afternoon.
    • Evening Dinner on Gili Air before returning to Trawangan.
  3. Day3

    Lombok mainland

    Sasak culture and a waterfall on the mainland.

    • Morning Boat back to Lombok and visit Sade Village.
    • Afternoon Tiu Kelep Waterfall.
    • Evening Onward travel or a final night in Lombok.

Travel tips

Lombok & the Gili Islands travel tips

A handful of practical notes make a Lombok and Gili trip go more smoothly.

  • Book genuinely a licensed guide well ahead for any Mount Rinjani trek.
  • Bring genuinely cash to the Gilis — ATMs are limited and unreliable, especially on Meno.
  • Pack genuinely reef-safe sunscreen for snorkelling around the islands.
  • Confirm genuinely which Kuta your accommodation means when booking — Bali and Lombok both have one.
  • Rent genuinely a bicycle rather than walking if exploring all of Gili Trawangan in one day.
  • Dress modestly genuinely when visiting Sade Village and other Sasak communities.

FAQ

Lombok & the Gili Islands: frequently asked questions

Which Gili Island should I choose?

Trawangan genuinely for nightlife and dive shops, Air for a quieter middle ground, and Meno for genuine peace and minimal development.

How do you get to the Gili Islands?

By genuinely fast boat direct from Bali, or via a short boat crossing from Bangsal on mainland Lombok.

Is Mount Rinjani difficult to climb?

Yes, genuinely a serious multi-day trek requiring real fitness and a licensed guide — not a casual day hike.

Are the Gili Islands really car-free?

Yes, genuinely — no cars or motorbikes are permitted on any of the three islands, only bicycles and horse carts.

How many days do you need for Lombok and the Gilis?

Three to five days genuinely covers the Gili Islands comfortably; add two to three more for the Rinjani trek.

Is Lombok safe to visit?

Yes, genuinely safe overall, with the same sensible precautions as anywhere in Indonesia — Sasak Muslim culture is genuinely welcoming to respectful visitors.
