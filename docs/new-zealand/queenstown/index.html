Welcome to Queenstown

Queenstown sits on the shore of Lake Wakatipu, a genuinely dramatic S-shaped glacial lake, directly beneath The Remarkables — a jagged mountain range that rises almost straight up from the water without the usual foothills softening the transition, giving the town one of the most immediately striking settings anywhere in New Zealand.

This is genuinely where commercial bungee jumping began: A.J. Hackett's first jump off the Kawarau Gorge Bridge in 1988 launched an entire industry, and Queenstown has stayed the world's reference point for adventure tourism ever since, with skydiving, jet boating and canyon swings all concentrated here at a density found nowhere else.

Beyond the adrenaline, Queenstown is genuinely the gateway to Central Otago's acclaimed Pinot Noir wine country and to historic Arrowtown, a beautifully preserved gold-rush town a short drive away — giving the area a real range beyond just its adventure-sports reputation.

Why visit

Why visit Queenstown?

Queenstown's case is a genuinely unmatched concentration of adventure sports, set in one of New Zealand's most dramatic mountain-and-lake landscapes.

  • It's genuinely the birthplace of commercial bungee jumping

    A.J. Hackett's first commercial jump at the Kawarau Gorge Bridge in 1988 launched the entire modern bungee industry.

  • The adventure sports concentration is genuinely unmatched

    Bungee jumping, skydiving, jet boating and canyon swings all operate here at a density found nowhere else in the world.

  • The setting is genuinely dramatic

    The Remarkables rise almost straight from Lake Wakatipu's shore, a genuinely striking mountain backdrop with no gentle foothills softening it.

  • Central Otago wine country is genuinely world class

    The Gibbston Valley and wider region produce acclaimed Pinot Noir, a real counterpoint to the town's adrenaline-focused reputation.

  • Arrowtown is genuinely one of the country's best-preserved gold-rush towns

    A short drive away, its heritage streetscape offers a genuinely different pace from Queenstown itself.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Queenstown

Queenstown's essential sights span its dramatic lake and mountain setting, adventure-sports history and nearby wine country.

  1. 01

    Lake Wakatipu waterfront

    The genuine heart of the town, its S-shaped glacial lake framed by mountains on every side, with a lakefront promenade, gardens and beach all within walking distance of the centre.

    Tip The lake's water level genuinely rises and falls with a real, if not fully understood, natural rhythm called the 'Wakatipu seiche'.

  2. 02

    The Remarkables

    A genuinely dramatic mountain range rising almost straight from the lake, among the few ranges in the world to run directly north-south, giving Queenstown its unmistakable skyline.

    Tip The range hosts a genuine ski field in winter, reachable by a spectacular access road from the town.

  3. 03

    The Skyline Gondola

    A genuinely steep gondola ride climbing 450 metres above the town centre in minutes, its summit offering sweeping views over the lake and Remarkables.

    Tip The summit's luge track is genuinely good fun for all ages — a fast, controllable ride back down part of the hill.

  4. 04

    The Kawarau Gorge Bridge

    The genuine site of the world's first commercial bungee jump in 1988, still operating today and still the classic first jump for most visitors.

    Tip A viewing platform lets non-jumpers watch for free — genuinely entertaining even without taking the leap yourself.

  5. 05

    Arrowtown

    A genuinely well-preserved 1860s gold-rush town about 20 minutes from Queenstown, its main street lined with original stone and wooden buildings, now cafés and small museums.

    Tip Autumn genuinely transforms the town, its historic avenue of trees turning a spectacular gold and red.

  6. 06

    Queenstown Gardens

    A genuinely peaceful green peninsula jutting into Lake Wakatipu right beside the town centre, with mature trees, a rose garden and lakeside walking paths.

    Tip A short, easy walk around the peninsula gives genuinely good views back at the town and mountains from the water.

  7. 07

    Shotover Canyon

    A genuinely dramatic, narrow river gorge cut by the Shotover River, its steep rock walls forming the setting for the famous Shotover Jet boat rides.

    Tip The canyon's history as a gold-mining river adds real context to what is otherwise a pure adrenaline experience.

Things to do

Best things to do in Queenstown

Queenstown's adventure-sports concentration and wine country reward genuinely active exploration.

  1. Bungee jump at the Kawarau Gorge

    The genuine original commercial bungee site, a 43-metre jump over the Kawarau River that started the entire modern industry in 1988.

    Tip Book ahead in peak summer — the most popular jump times genuinely fill up.

  2. Ride the Skyline Gondola and luge

    A genuinely accessible way to get the classic Queenstown view without the adrenaline of the bigger adventure activities, with an optional luge ride down.

    Tip Time the ride for sunset — the light over the lake and Remarkables is genuinely spectacular.

  3. Jet boat the Shotover River

    A genuinely thrilling, high-speed ride through the narrow Shotover Canyon, the boat's shallow draft allowing it to skim within inches of the rock walls.

    Tip Sit toward the back for genuinely the wettest, most thrilling ride.

  4. Ski Coronet Peak or The Remarkables

    In winter, Queenstown genuinely becomes a serious ski destination, with two major fields a short drive from town and a lively après-ski scene.

    Tip Book lift passes and rentals ahead during the July–September peak season.

  5. Taste wine in the Gibbston Valley

    A genuinely short drive from Queenstown reaches some of Central Otago's most acclaimed Pinot Noir wineries, several offering tastings with dramatic vineyard views.

    Tip Book a wine tour rather than driving yourself between wineries, given the tastings involved.

  6. Day-trip to Milford Sound

    A genuinely long but spectacular day trip, the drive alone passing through some of the South Island's most dramatic scenery before reaching the fiord itself.

    Tip Consider a small-plane or helicopter flight for a shorter, genuinely equally spectacular alternative to the long drive.

Local food

What to eat in Queenstown

Queenstown's food culture is genuinely built around its famous burger institution and acclaimed local wine country.

  • Fergburger

    A genuinely legendary Queenstown institution, its burgers drawing queues out the door at almost any hour — a real rite of passage for visitors, not just a marketing gimmick.

    Where to try it The original Fergburger on Shotover Street; expect a genuine wait, especially at peak meal times.

  • Central Otago Pinot Noir

    The world's southernmost significant wine region, genuinely producing Pinot Noir considered among New Zealand's finest, grown on the dramatic slopes around Queenstown.

    Where to try it Gibbston Valley wineries, or wine bars and restaurants throughout central Queenstown.

  • Flat white

    New Zealand's café culture treats the flat white as a serious daily ritual, and Queenstown's genuinely busy café scene keeps pace with visitor demand.

    Where to try it Independent cafés throughout the compact town centre.

  • Meat pie

    A genuinely serious national obsession — flaky pastry filled with steak, mince or other fillings, a good grab-and-go option between adventure activities.

    Where to try it Bakeries throughout central Queenstown.

  • Pavlova

    A meringue-based dessert with a genuinely crisp shell and marshmallow centre, a beloved national favourite found on café and restaurant menus.

    Where to try it Restaurants and cafés throughout central Queenstown.

  • Hokey pokey ice cream

    Vanilla ice cream studded with honeycomb toffee pieces, genuinely one of the most beloved local flavours, a good treat after a day of adventure activities.

    Where to try it Ice cream parlours throughout the town centre and lakefront.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Queenstown

Queenstown genuinely splits between the compact lakeside centre and quieter surrounding areas.

  • Town centre

    The most convenient base, genuinely close to everything, though also the busiest and most expensive area.

    Best for: Walking distance to the lake, restaurants and adventure-sport bookings

  • Frankton and the airport area

    A short drive from the centre, genuinely useful for visitors with an early flight or wanting more affordable rates.

    Best for: A quieter, often more affordable stay

  • Arrowtown

    About 20 minutes from Queenstown, genuinely offering a calmer alternative with easy access back into town.

    Best for: A quieter, historic-town base

Transport

Getting to and around Queenstown

Queenstown has its own airport with direct domestic and some international connections, genuinely convenient for a South Island trip.

Getting there

  • Domestic and some international flights

    Queenstown Airport connects directly to Auckland, Wellington and Christchurch, plus some direct flights from Australia.

    The approach is genuinely one of the most scenic commercial flights anywhere, weaving between mountains on the descent.

  • Rental car

    About 4–5 hours from Christchurch, and the genuinely best way to reach Milford Sound and the wider region at your own pace.

    Book well ahead in summer — rental cars in Queenstown genuinely sell out during peak season.

  • Bus

    InterCity coaches connect Queenstown to Christchurch and other South Island towns, a workable budget option.

    Considerably slower than flying or driving, but a functional alternative for visitors without a car.

Getting around

Queenstown's town centre is genuinely compact and walkable, with a car useful for wider exploration.

  • Walking

    The lakefront, Queenstown Gardens and most restaurants are all within a genuinely compact, walkable centre.

    Steep streets in places, but manageable for most visitors.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely useful for reaching Arrowtown, Gibbston Valley wineries and Milford Sound independently.

    Not necessary for the town centre itself, where parking can be limited in peak season.

  • Local buses and shuttles

    Connect the town centre to Arrowtown, the airport and nearby ski fields.

    A genuinely practical option for visitors without a rental car.

When to go

Best time to visit Queenstown

Queenstown genuinely works as a year-round destination, with summer and winter offering very different but equally strong reasons to visit.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    Warm weather ideal for bungee jumping, jet boating and lake activities — also the busiest and most expensive season.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Mild weather and genuinely spectacular autumn colour, especially around Arrowtown.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Best time

    Cold, but genuinely excellent for skiing at Coronet Peak and The Remarkables, with a lively après-ski scene.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Increasingly mild, a genuinely good shoulder season with thinner crowds than either peak.

Worth planning around: Book adventure activities, accommodation and rental cars many months ahead for the December–February and July–August peaks — both genuinely sell out.

Travel tips

Queenstown travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Queenstown trip.

  • Book adventure activities, accommodation and rental cars many months ahead for summer and ski-season peaks.
  • Budget realistically for adventure sports — bungee jumping, skydiving and jet boating all carry genuinely significant price tags.
  • Consider a scenic flight to Milford Sound rather than the long drive, if time is limited.
  • Expect a genuine queue at Fergburger — it's part of the experience, not a sign to look elsewhere.
  • Pack layers regardless of season — mountain weather changes genuinely fast.
  • Book a Gibbston Valley wine tour rather than driving yourself between tastings.

FAQ

Queenstown: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Queenstown?

Three to four days covers the town, at least one major adventure activity, Arrowtown and a Gibbston Valley wine tour comfortably. Add a day for Milford Sound if including that as a day trip.

Is Queenstown expensive?

Yes, genuinely one of New Zealand's pricier destinations — adventure activities, accommodation and dining all carry a premium, reflecting its status as the country's adventure-tourism hub.

What is Queenstown known for?

Genuinely the birthplace of commercial bungee jumping and the world's densest concentration of adventure sports — skydiving, jet boating and canyon swings alongside the original bungee jump at the Kawarau Gorge Bridge.

Is Queenstown good for skiing?

Yes, genuinely excellent — Coronet Peak and The Remarkables ski fields sit a short drive from town, with a lively winter après-ski scene from June to September.

Can you visit Milford Sound from Queenstown?

Yes, as a long but genuinely spectacular day trip by road, or a shorter scenic flight for visitors with less time — the drive itself passes through some of the South Island's most dramatic scenery.

What is Fergburger?

A legendary Queenstown burger institution, genuinely drawing queues out the door at almost any hour — a real rite of passage for visitors rather than just a tourist gimmick.
