Welcome to Christchurch

Christchurch was devastated by a series of earthquakes in 2010 and 2011, the most destructive of which killed 185 people and levelled much of the historic city centre — and the rebuilding that followed is as much a part of visiting today as any single sight, from the striking Transitional 'Cardboard' Cathedral to entire streets of ambitious new architecture.

Long before the earthquakes, Christchurch earned its nickname the 'Garden City' for real reason: Hagley Park and the Botanic Gardens at its centre are among the finest urban green spaces in the Southern Hemisphere, and the Avon River still winds gently through the middle of town exactly as it always has.

As the South Island's main gateway, Christchurch is also the country's connection point to Antarctica — the International Antarctic Centre sits right by the airport, a real reminder that Christchurch has supplied Antarctic expeditions for over a century.

Why visit

Why visit Christchurch?

Christchurch's case is a compelling combination of green space, a real post-disaster rebuild story, and the country's most tangible Antarctic connection.

  • The rebuild is part of the experience

    From the Transitional Cathedral to ambitious new public buildings, the post-earthquake rebuild gives Christchurch a real, ongoing story most cities don't have.

  • The Botanic Gardens are among the finest in the Southern Hemisphere

    Hagley Park's scale and the Gardens' plant collections reward considerably more than a quick walk-through.

  • It's the country's Antarctic gateway

    The International Antarctic Centre reflects a real, century-long connection between Christchurch and Antarctic exploration.

  • It's a practical South Island base

    The main airport and road connections make Christchurch the natural starting point for a wider South Island road trip.

  • Punting on the Avon is still a real local pastime

    Not just a tourist activity — the river through the Botanic Gardens remains a genuine part of everyday Christchurch life.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Christchurch

Christchurch's essential sights span its rebuilt centre, expansive parks and a genuine Antarctic connection.

  1. 01

    Cathedral Square

    The historic centre of the city, its earthquake-damaged Anglican cathedral still mid-restoration, with the heritage tram looping through the square as it always has.

    Tip The square tells the rebuild story as clearly as any single site — old damage, restoration work and new development sit side by side.

  2. 02

    Botanic Gardens and Hagley Park

    An expansive Victorian-era botanic garden set within the larger Hagley Park, its plant collections and riverside paths among the finest urban green spaces in the Southern Hemisphere.

    Tip Allow at least two hours to see the gardens properly — the grounds are larger than they first appear from any single entrance.

  3. 03

    International Antarctic Centre

    An engaging visitor centre reflecting Christchurch's century-long role supplying Antarctic expeditions, with penguin encounters, a snow and ice experience, and real Antarctic vehicles.

    Tip Time your visit to catch a penguin feeding session — check the current schedule when booking tickets.

  4. 04

    Christchurch Art Gallery

    A striking modern building, its collection spanning historic New Zealand and international art, and its role as an emergency headquarters after the 2011 earthquake now part of the city's own story.

    Tip Free general admission makes this an easy stop, with special exhibitions charged separately.

  5. 05

    The Transitional 'Cardboard' Cathedral

    A striking temporary cathedral built largely from cardboard tubes, designed by Japanese architect Shigeru Ban after the original cathedral's earthquake damage — a real symbol of the city's resilience.

    Tip The building is still in active use as a cathedral, not just a tourist curiosity — check for services before visiting if timing matters.

  6. 06

    The Avon River

    A gentle, willow-lined river winding through the city centre and Botanic Gardens, its punting tradition a real local pastime rather than a manufactured tourist activity.

    Tip The river path makes a pleasant walking or cycling route connecting several of the city's central sights.

  7. 07

    Akaroa

    A picturesque former French settlement on the Banks Peninsula, about 75 minutes from Christchurch, its harbour and hillside streets carrying a distinct, quieter character.

    Tip A full-day trip allows time for both the drive's scenic viewpoints and a proper look around the town itself.

Things to do

Best things to do in Christchurch

Christchurch's parks, river and rebuild story reward unhurried exploration.

  1. Ride the historic tram

    A restored heritage tram loops through the central city, a relaxed way to get oriented and see several key sights without walking the full circuit.

    Tip A day pass allows hopping on and off at any stop — good value if planning several central-city stops.

  2. Punt on the Avon River

    A relaxed way to see the Botanic Gardens from the water, poled along by a guide in traditional Edwardian dress.

    Tip Early morning or late afternoon trips offer the calmest water and best light for photos.

  3. Meet the penguins at the Antarctic Centre

    A hands-on Antarctic experience, including a snow and storm simulation and real rescued little blue penguins in a dedicated enclosure.

    Tip Combine with the Hagglund all-terrain vehicle ride for a fun, bumpy add-on to the standard visit.

  4. Walk or cycle Hagley Park

    The park's expansive paths make it a popular spot for locals to walk, run or cycle, not just a sightseeing stop for visitors.

    Tip Bike rental is available nearby — a pleasant way to cover more of the park's considerable size.

  5. Day-trip to Akaroa

    The drive itself, over the volcanic hills of the Banks Peninsula, is as much a highlight as the town at the end of it.

    Tip A harbour cruise from Akaroa offers a genuine chance to spot Hector's dolphins, the world's smallest dolphin species.

  6. Learn the rebuild story at the Cardboard Cathedral

    The Transitional Cathedral tells the story of the city's recovery better than any museum display, a working building rather than a static memorial.

    Tip Combine with a walk through the nearby container-based Re:START retail precinct, another genuine symbol of the city's post-quake resilience.

Local food

What to eat in Christchurch

Christchurch's food draws on excellent Canterbury lamb and South Island seafood alongside the usual New Zealand classics.

  • Whitebait fritters

    A seasonal South Island delicacy, tiny translucent fish bound together with egg and lightly fried into a fritter, prized enough to command premium prices in season.

    Where to try it Cafés and restaurants across Canterbury during the whitebait season, roughly August to November.

  • Canterbury lamb

    The Canterbury Plains surrounding Christchurch produce some of New Zealand's finest lamb, a real point of regional pride found on menus across the city.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout central Christchurch, especially those focused on modern New Zealand cuisine.

  • Flat white

    New Zealand's café culture treats the flat white as a serious daily ritual, and Christchurch's growing café scene is no exception.

    Where to try it Independent cafés throughout the rebuilt central city.

  • Fish and chips

    A beloved national tradition, best eaten from the paper in the Botanic Gardens or along New Brighton beach.

    Where to try it Independent fish and chip shops across the city, especially near New Brighton.

  • Pavlova

    A meringue-based dessert with a crisp shell and marshmallow centre, a beloved national favourite found on café menus citywide.

    Where to try it Cafés throughout the central city, especially at weekend brunch spots.

  • Meat pie

    A serious national obsession — flaky pastry filled with steak, mince or other fillings, sold at bakeries citywide.

    Where to try it Independent bakeries throughout the city, a genuine point of local pride.

Transport

Getting to and around Christchurch

Christchurch is the South Island's main gateway, with a well-connected international airport.

Getting there

  • International and domestic flights

    Christchurch Airport connects directly to Australia, the Pacific and Asia, alongside frequent flights to Auckland, Wellington and Queenstown.

    The airport sits close to the International Antarctic Centre, an easy first or last stop.

  • Rental car

    Christchurch is the natural starting point for a South Island road trip toward Queenstown, Milford Sound and the glaciers.

    Rental desks are available at the airport and throughout the city centre.

  • TranzAlpine train

    A scenic rail journey crosses the Southern Alps from Christchurch to Greymouth on the West Coast.

    Book ahead in summer — the TranzAlpine is popular and seats can sell out.

Getting around

Christchurch's central city is walkable and flat, with a heritage tram and buses covering the rest.

  • Walking

    Cathedral Square, the Art Gallery and the edge of the Botanic Gardens are all within a compact central area.

    The rebuilt city centre is flat and easy to navigate on foot.

  • Heritage tram

    A restored tram loops through the central city, a relaxed way to see several sights without a long walk.

    A day pass allows unlimited hop-on hop-off travel around the central loop.

  • Rental car

    Useful for reaching Akaroa or beginning a wider South Island road trip.

    Not necessary for the city centre itself, which is easy to explore without a car.

When to go

Best time to visit Christchurch

Christchurch's South Island climate is more seasonal than the North Island's, with warm summers and cool winters.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    Warm, settled weather, ideal for the Botanic Gardens and punting on the Avon.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Mild with spectacular autumn colour in the Botanic Gardens and Hagley Park.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Cold, with frosty mornings, though a good season to combine with nearby skifields.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Increasingly mild, with the Botanic Gardens' spring blooms a genuine highlight.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Christchurch's indoor and outdoor sights both work reasonably well across the seasons, with summer simply the most reliable for outdoor time.

Travel tips

Christchurch travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Christchurch trip.

  • Book International Antarctic Centre tickets online ahead, and check the penguin feeding schedule.
  • Consider Christchurch as your South Island road trip starting point — most major routes south begin here.
  • Book TranzAlpine train tickets well ahead in summer if planning the scenic crossing to the West Coast.
  • Allow a full day for Akaroa if visiting — the drive itself is part of the experience.
  • Try whitebait fritters in season (roughly August–November) — a distinctive South Island specialty.
  • Use a heritage tram day pass to cover the central city efficiently without extensive walking.

FAQ

Christchurch: frequently asked questions

Is Christchurch worth visiting, or just a transit stop?

Worth a day or two in its own right — the Botanic Gardens, International Antarctic Centre and the city's ongoing post-earthquake rebuild all offer real substance beyond just being a gateway to the rest of the South Island.

How many days do you need in Christchurch?

One to two days covers the central city, Botanic Gardens and Antarctic Centre comfortably. Add a full day if including Akaroa as a day trip.

What happened to Christchurch in the earthquakes?

A series of earthquakes in 2010 and 2011, the most destructive of which killed 185 people, devastated much of the historic city centre. The ongoing rebuild — including the striking Transitional 'Cardboard' Cathedral — is now part of what makes visiting the city interesting.

Is Christchurch a good base for a South Island road trip?

Yes, the natural starting point — its airport and road connections make it the practical gateway to Queenstown, Milford Sound and the West Coast glaciers.

What is the International Antarctic Centre?

A visitor centre reflecting Christchurch's genuine century-long role supplying Antarctic expeditions, with penguin encounters, a snow and storm simulation, and real Antarctic vehicles on display.

Is Akaroa worth a day trip from Christchurch?

Yes — about 75 minutes away on the Banks Peninsula, its harbour town and French-influenced history offer a distinctly different, quieter character from the city, with a real chance of spotting Hector's dolphins on a harbour cruise.
