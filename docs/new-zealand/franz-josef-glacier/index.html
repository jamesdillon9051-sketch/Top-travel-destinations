Welcome to Franz Josef and Fox Glacier

Franz Josef and Fox, twin glaciers a short drive apart on the South Island's West Coast, are genuinely unusual worldwide for how far they descend toward sea level and temperate rainforest — a combination of steep terrain and heavy Tasman Sea rainfall found in only a handful of places on Earth.

Both sit within Te Wahipounamu, the same UNESCO World Heritage area that covers Fiordland, and both are genuinely retreating overall even as they occasionally advance in wetter years, a visible, ongoing reminder of the wider glacier's response to a changing climate.

The two small townships that share the glaciers' names are genuinely quiet, practical bases rather than resort towns — most visitors are here for a specific activity, whether a valley walk, a scenic flight or a heli-hike onto the ice itself.

Why visit

Why visit Franz Josef and Fox Glacier?

Franz Josef and Fox's case rests on a genuinely rare landscape combination found in very few places worldwide.

  • The glaciers genuinely descend close to rainforest, a rare combination anywhere on Earth

    Steep terrain and heavy coastal rainfall bring the ice unusually close to temperate forest and the sea.

  • Lake Matheson's reflections are genuinely among the most photographed in New Zealand

    On a still morning, Aoraki/Mount Cook and Mount Tasman reflect perfectly in the lake's dark water.

  • The heli-hike is genuinely a rare chance to walk on the ice itself

    Retreat has made the glacier face largely unsafe to approach on foot, so helicopters now land hikers directly on the upper ice.

  • The West Coast setting is genuinely quieter than the rest of the South Island's big-name stops

    Sparse population and dramatic, wild scenery give it a different character from Queenstown or Milford Sound.

  • It's genuinely an easy, natural link on a South Island road trip

    The main highway connects it directly to Queenstown in the south and Abel Tasman and Nelson to the north.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Franz Josef and Fox Glacier

Franz Josef and Fox's essential sights span both glaciers, a famous mirror lake, and the rainforest and wetlands around them.

  1. 01

    Franz Josef Glacier

    The more visited of the two glaciers, its ice genuinely descending a steep valley almost to the surrounding rainforest.

    Tip The glacier face itself is genuinely off-limits to walkers now — a heli-hike or scenic flight is the way to get close.

  2. 02

    Fox Glacier

    A genuinely quieter, slightly larger neighbour to Franz Josef, a short drive south, with its own valley walk and viewpoints.

    Tip Visiting both glaciers genuinely works well as a single day if you're based between the two townships.

  3. 03

    Lake Matheson

    A genuinely famous mirror lake near Fox Glacier, its still water reflecting Aoraki/Mount Cook and Mount Tasman on calm mornings.

    Tip Arrive genuinely at dawn — wind picks up later and breaks the reflection within a few hours.

  4. 04

    The glacier heli-hike

    A genuinely rare chance to walk directly on glacier ice, landing above the crevassed lower sections that are no longer safely accessible on foot.

    Tip Book with real flexibility — heli-hikes are genuinely weather-dependent and cancel or delay often.

  5. 05

    Okarito Lagoon

    New Zealand's genuinely largest unmodified wetland, a short drive from Franz Josef, home to kiwi and a wealth of coastal birdlife.

    Tip A guided kayak trip at dawn or dusk is genuinely the best way to spot the resident white heron colony.

  6. 06

    The West Coast rainforest

    A genuinely lush temperate rainforest surrounds both glacier valleys, an elevated treetop walkway near Hokitika showing it from above.

    Tip It's a genuinely worthwhile stop on the drive between the glaciers and Hokitika.

  7. 07

    The glacier valley walk

    A genuinely easy walk up the valley floor toward the glacier terminal, showing the dramatic scale of the ice's historic retreat.

    Tip Markers along the track genuinely show past ice extent by year — a sobering, visible record of retreat.

Things to do

Best things to do in Franz Josef and Fox Glacier

Franz Josef and Fox genuinely reward getting close to the ice, on foot, by air or from the surrounding wetlands and forest.

  1. Heli-hike onto the glacier

    The genuinely essential Franz Josef and Fox experience, landing above the crevassed lower ice for a guided walk on the glacier itself.

    Tip Book on the first available day of your visit — genuinely leave a spare day in case of weather cancellation.

  2. Walk the glacier valley track

    A genuinely easy, flat walk suited to any fitness level, following the valley floor as close to the ice as ground access now allows.

    Tip Check current track status before setting out — sections genuinely close after heavy rain or rockfall.

  3. See Lake Matheson's dawn reflections

    A genuinely early start rewards you with the calmest water and the clearest mirror reflection of the peaks.

    Tip The full loop track takes genuinely about 1.5 hours — allow time beyond just the viewpoint.

  4. Kayak or spot birdlife at Okarito Lagoon

    A genuinely peaceful contrast to the glaciers, paddling New Zealand's largest unmodified wetland among herons and other coastal birds.

    Tip Guided dawn tours genuinely offer the best chance of a white heron sighting.

  5. Walk the West Coast Treetop Walk

    An elevated steel walkway genuinely lifts you into the rainforest canopy, a striking contrast to the ice just up the road.

    Tip A genuinely worthwhile detour on the drive north toward Hokitika.

  6. Soak in the Glacier Hot Pools

    A genuinely welcome way to end a cold day on the ice, natural-style hot pools set among rainforest right in Franz Josef township.

    Tip Evening sessions are genuinely popular in winter — book ahead if visiting then.

Transport

Getting to and around Franz Josef and Fox Glacier

Franz Josef and Fox sit genuinely remotely on the West Coast, reached by a long but spectacular drive from either direction.

Getting there

  • Rental car from Queenstown

    About 4-4.5 hours' drive via Haast Pass, genuinely one of the South Island's great road-trip routes.

    Fuel up before Haast — services are genuinely sparse along this stretch.

  • Rental car from Christchurch

    About 5.5 hours via Arthur's Pass, another genuinely dramatic mountain crossing.

    A natural link if arriving from Christchurch rather than the south.

  • Domestic flights

    Small regional flights connect Hokitika, about an hour north, to Christchurch.

    A genuinely useful option to skip part of the long drive.

Getting around

Both glacier townships are genuinely tiny and walkable, with a car or organised transfer needed to reach the ice and Lake Matheson.

  • Walking

    Both townships are genuinely small enough to cover on foot, including reaching the start of the valley walks.

    Lake Matheson and the glacier heli-hike bases are a short drive from Fox and Franz Josef townships respectively.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely the most flexible way to move between the two glaciers and Lake Matheson.

    The drive between the two townships takes about 25 minutes.

  • Helicopter and organised tours

    Genuinely essential for reaching the glacier ice itself, and widely available from both townships.

    Operators typically include transfers from your accommodation.

When to go

Best time to visit Franz Josef and Fox Glacier

The glaciers themselves are genuinely visible year-round, though flying conditions and daylight hours shift the experience by season.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    The most reliable flying conditions and longest daylight hours for heli-hikes and walks.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Genuinely settled and quieter than summer, with good flying conditions still common.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Genuinely dramatic snow-covered scenery, though heli-hikes cancel more often due to weather.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Increasingly settled, a genuinely good shoulder season before summer's crowds arrive.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — build a spare day into any visit regardless of season, since heli-hikes and scenic flights are genuinely weather-dependent throughout the year.

Travel tips

Franz Josef and Fox Glacier travel tips

Practical notes on weather, booking and driving that make a real difference to a Franz Josef and Fox trip.

  • Build a spare day into your visit — heli-hikes genuinely cancel or delay often due to weather.
  • Book your heli-hike for the first available day, holding your buffer day in reserve.
  • Arrive at Lake Matheson genuinely at dawn — wind picks up within a few hours and breaks the reflection.
  • Fill your fuel tank before driving Haast Pass — services are genuinely sparse along the route.
  • Pack sandfly repellent — they're genuinely persistent around both townships and the lagoon.
  • Check current valley walk track status before setting out — sections genuinely close after heavy rain.

FAQ

Franz Josef and Fox Glacier: frequently asked questions

Can you still walk right up to the glacier?

Not genuinely all the way — retreat has made the lower ice face unstable, so ground access stops short and a heli-hike is the way to get onto the ice itself.

Is the heli-hike worth the cost?

Yes, genuinely, for most visitors — it's a rare chance to walk on real glacier ice, and reviews consistently rate it among the best experiences in New Zealand.

How many days do you need at Franz Josef and Fox?

Two days genuinely covers both glaciers, Lake Matheson and a heli-hike, with a spare day advisable given how weather-dependent flying is.

Which is better, Franz Josef or Fox Glacier?

Genuinely similar in quality — Franz Josef has more infrastructure and operators, while Fox pairs naturally with Lake Matheson's reflections.

Is the drive from Queenstown difficult?

Long but genuinely manageable — about 4-4.5 hours via Haast Pass, with spectacular scenery throughout, though fuel stops are sparse.

Do the glaciers ever advance instead of retreat?

Occasionally, genuinely, in unusually wet years, though the long-term trend for both glaciers has been retreat.
