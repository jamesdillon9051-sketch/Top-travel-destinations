Welcome to Wellington

Wellington is genuinely one of the most compact national capitals anywhere, its city centre squeezed onto a narrow strip of flat land between a sheltered harbour and steep, wind-scoured hills — a geography that has kept the city walkable and dense rather than sprawling, even as it grew into New Zealand's political and cultural centre.

That compactness has produced a genuinely outsized café and arts culture for a city this size: Wellington regularly claims some of the best coffee anywhere in the world, and Peter Jackson's Weta Workshop turned the city into a real centre of the global film industry, its effects and props work visible in Wellington Airport's own Lord of the Rings-themed displays.

Wellington is also genuinely serious about conservation — Zealandia, a predator-fenced valley reserve barely ten minutes from Parliament, has helped native bird populations recover within city limits in a way few other capitals anywhere can claim.

Why visit

Why visit Wellington?

Wellington's case is a genuinely compact, walkable capital with outsized museums, café culture and film history for its size.

  • Te Papa is genuinely one of the world's best national museums

    Free entry and a genuinely engaging mix of Maori taonga, natural history and interactive exhibits make it easy to lose half a day here.

  • The café culture is genuinely world class

    Wellington regularly ranks among the best coffee cities anywhere, a real point of local pride rather than marketing.

  • Weta Workshop makes the film history genuinely tangible

    Peter Jackson's effects studio turned Wellington into a real global film hub, and workshop tours show the actual craft behind it.

  • Zealandia is genuinely unlike anything in most other capitals

    A predator-fenced valley reserve minutes from Parliament has helped native bird species recover within the city itself.

  • It's genuinely walkable and compact

    Most of the city's key sights sit within a easy walk of each other, a real contrast to more sprawling capitals.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Wellington

Wellington's essential sights fit within a genuinely compact, walkable harbourside centre.

  1. 01

    Te Papa Tongarewa

    New Zealand's national museum, genuinely free to enter, its collection spanning Maori taonga (treasures), natural history and interactive exhibits across six floors.

    Tip Allow at least half a day — the museum is genuinely larger and more absorbing than its compact exterior suggests.

  2. 02

    The Wellington Cable Car

    A genuinely historic funicular railway climbing from the city centre to Kelburn, its upper station offering sweeping harbour views and direct access to the Botanic Garden.

    Tip Ride up and walk back down through the Botanic Garden — a genuinely pleasant, mostly downhill route back to the centre.

  3. 03

    Wellington Botanic Garden

    A genuinely well-kept hillside garden connecting the Cable Car's upper station to the city centre, with themed plantings and sweeping views along the way down.

    Tip The rose garden and duck pond near the bottom are a genuinely pleasant final stretch before re-entering the city.

  4. 04

    The Beehive and Parliament

    New Zealand's genuinely distinctive executive wing, its rounded 'Beehive' shape a real architectural landmark beside the more traditional Parliament House and Library.

    Tip Free guided tours of Parliament run regularly — a genuinely worthwhile look at how the country's government actually works.

  5. 05

    Zealandia

    A genuinely pioneering predator-fenced valley reserve, its native bird and reptile populations recovering within a ten-minute drive of Parliament, unlike almost anywhere else this close to a capital city.

    Tip A daytime visit shows the landscape and some birdlife; a guided night tour is genuinely the best way to spot kiwi.

  6. 06

    Cuba Street

    Wellington's genuinely bohemian pedestrian strip, lined with independent cafés, vintage shops and street art, a real showcase of the city's creative character.

    Tip The Bucket Fountain is a genuinely beloved, slightly odd local landmark near the street's northern end — worth a quick look.

  7. 07

    Mount Victoria lookout

    A genuinely sweeping viewpoint over the harbour, city and surrounding hills, reachable by a short drive or a steeper walk from the city centre.

    Tip Sunset here is genuinely spectacular, with the harbour catching the last light below the city skyline.

Things to do

Best things to do in Wellington

Wellington's compact size and creative culture reward genuinely unhurried exploration on foot.

  1. Ride the Cable Car and walk down through the Botanic Garden

    A genuinely classic Wellington half-day: ride up for the views, then take a leisurely downhill walk back through the Botanic Garden.

    Tip The full walk down takes about 30–45 minutes at an unhurried pace, with several places to stop along the way.

  2. Tour Weta Workshop

    Guided tours genuinely show the practical effects, prop-making and creature design behind The Lord of the Rings, The Hobbit and numerous other major films.

    Tip Book tours online ahead — sessions are genuinely limited in size and popular time slots sell out.

  3. Explore Cuba Street's cafés and bars

    Genuinely the best single stretch to experience Wellington's celebrated café culture and creative, independent retail scene.

    Tip Wellington's coffee reputation is genuinely earned — try a flat white at more than one café to compare.

  4. Walk the waterfront

    A genuinely pleasant, flat promenade connects the city centre to Oriental Bay, passing public art, swimming spots and waterfront bars along the way.

    Tip Continue as far as Oriental Bay for a genuinely good beach-side lunch spot with harbour views.

  5. Spot native wildlife on a Zealandia night tour

    A guided evening tour genuinely offers one of the more reliable chances to spot a wild kiwi anywhere in the country, alongside other native nocturnal species.

    Tip Book well ahead — night tours are genuinely popular and run with limited group sizes.

  6. Day-trip to the Wairarapa wine region

    Martinborough and the wider Wairarapa, genuinely a well-regarded pinot noir wine region, sit about an hour's drive or train ride from Wellington.

    Tip The Wairarapa Wine Train runs seasonally from Wellington — a genuinely relaxed way to visit without driving.

Local food

What to eat in Wellington

Wellington's food and drink culture is genuinely built around its café scene, alongside the usual excellent New Zealand classics.

  • Flat white

    Wellington's café scene treats the flat white as a genuinely serious craft, regularly ranking among the best coffee cities in the world for its density of quality independent roasters.

    Where to try it Independent cafés throughout Cuba Street and the wider city centre.

  • Craft beer

    Wellington genuinely has one of New Zealand's strongest craft beer scenes, with numerous small breweries and dedicated tap rooms across the city.

    Where to try it Craft beer bars around Courtenay Place and the wider city centre.

  • Fish and chips

    A genuinely beloved national tradition, best eaten from the paper along the waterfront at Oriental Bay.

    Where to try it Independent fish and chip shops around the waterfront and Oriental Bay.

  • Meat pie

    A genuinely serious national obsession — flaky pastry filled with steak, mince or other fillings, sold at bakeries citywide.

    Where to try it Independent bakeries throughout the city centre.

  • Pavlova

    A meringue-based dessert with a genuinely crisp shell and marshmallow centre, a beloved national favourite found on café menus citywide.

    Where to try it Cafés throughout the city centre, especially at weekend brunch spots.

  • Green-lipped mussels

    A genuinely distinctive New Zealand species, widely available on Wellington restaurant menus, served simply steamed or in a range of sauces.

    Where to try it Seafood restaurants around the waterfront and Courtenay Place.

Transport

Getting to and around Wellington

Wellington sits at the genuine crossing point between the North and South Islands, with an airport, ferry terminal and rail links.

Getting there

  • Domestic flights

    Regular flights connect Wellington to Auckland, Christchurch and Queenstown efficiently.

    Wellington Airport's short runway, exposed to genuinely strong local winds, is a well-known point of local pride and occasional traveller nerves.

  • Interislander ferry

    Connects Wellington to Picton across Cook Strait, a genuinely scenic crossing through the Marlborough Sounds.

    Book vehicle space well ahead in summer — sailings can sell out.

  • Train

    The Capital Connection and other regional services connect Wellington to nearby towns including the Wairarapa.

    A genuinely relaxed way to reach day-trip destinations without driving.

Getting around

Wellington's city centre is genuinely compact and walkable, with the Cable Car and buses covering the hillier areas.

  • Walking

    Te Papa, the waterfront and Cuba Street are all within a genuinely compact, flat central area.

    Some neighbourhoods climb genuinely steep hills — comfortable shoes help.

  • Cable Car and buses

    The Cable Car reaches the Botanic Garden and Kelburn, while a citywide bus network covers everywhere else.

    A Snapper card covers bus fares — tap on and off for each journey.

  • Rental car

    Useful for day trips to the Wairarapa or Kapiti Coast, though genuinely unnecessary for the city itself.

    Wellington's steep, narrow streets make a car more hassle than help within the city centre.

When to go

Best time to visit Wellington

Wellington is genuinely famous for its wind in any season, though summer offers the calmest, most reliable conditions.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    The warmest, calmest weather, ideal for the waterfront and outdoor café culture.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Mild and genuinely settled, with noticeably thinner crowds than summer.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Cold and genuinely windy, though museums and indoor sights work well regardless of weather.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Increasingly mild, though genuinely still prone to the city's famous wind.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly, though Wellington's wind is genuinely a year-round feature of the city rather than confined to one season — pack accordingly.

Travel tips

Wellington travel tips

Practical notes on booking, timing and transport that make a real difference to a Wellington trip.

  • Book Weta Workshop tours and Zealandia night tours online ahead — both are genuinely popular and limited in size.
  • Pack a windproof layer regardless of season — Wellington's wind is genuinely a year-round feature of the city.
  • Book Interislander ferry vehicle space well ahead in summer if driving across Cook Strait.
  • Allow at least half a day for Te Papa — it's genuinely larger and more absorbing than it first appears.
  • Try coffee at more than one Cuba Street café — Wellington's reputation for it is genuinely well earned.
  • Combine Wellington with a day trip to the Wairarapa wine region if you have an extra day to spare.

FAQ

Wellington: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in Wellington?

Two days covers Te Papa, the Cable Car and Weta Workshop comfortably, with time for a Zealandia night tour and a walk along the waterfront.

Is Wellington worth visiting?

Yes, genuinely — it offers a free, world-class national museum, an outsized café and arts culture for its size, and a genuinely pioneering wildlife sanctuary minutes from Parliament.

What is Zealandia?

A predator-fenced valley wildlife reserve barely ten minutes from central Wellington, where native bird and reptile populations have genuinely recovered within city limits — a night tour offers one of the more reliable chances to spot a wild kiwi.

Is Wellington windy?

Yes, genuinely famously so, in any season — pack a windproof layer regardless of when you visit, since it's a real year-round feature of the city rather than confined to winter.

Can you visit Weta Workshop without booking ahead?

It's genuinely risky not to — guided tours run in limited group sizes and popular time slots regularly sell out, especially in summer.

How do you get from Wellington to the South Island?

The Interislander ferry crosses Cook Strait to Picton in a genuinely scenic journey through the Marlborough Sounds, or a domestic flight connects Wellington to Christchurch or Queenstown quickly.
