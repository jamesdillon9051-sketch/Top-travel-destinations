Welcome to Bay of Islands

The Bay of Islands is genuinely where modern New Zealand began — the Treaty of Waitangi, the country's founding document, was signed here in 1840 on the lawn in front of the Treaty House, and the bay's early role as a whaling and trading port shaped the nation long before Auckland or Wellington existed.

That history sits alongside 144 islands scattered across genuinely sheltered, island-studded water, drawing sailors, game fishers and cruise passengers to a coastline that has quietly functioned as New Zealand's boating playground for well over a century.

It's a genuinely slower, warmer corner of the country than the South Island's mountain scenery — the appeal here is history, water and a relaxed subtropical pace rather than dramatic landscapes.

Why visit

Why visit Bay of Islands?

The Bay of Islands' case rests on a genuinely rare combination of national history and a beautiful, boat-friendly coastline.

  • Waitangi is genuinely New Zealand's most significant historic site

    The country's founding treaty between the British Crown and Maori chiefs was signed on these grounds in 1840.

  • The Hole in the Rock is genuinely one of the North Island's most photographed sights

    A natural arch worn clean through solid rock, reachable by scenic cruise from Paihia or Russell.

  • Russell is genuinely New Zealand's first capital and a beautifully preserved colonial township

    Once a rough whaling port nicknamed the 'hellhole of the Pacific,' now a quiet, historic seaside village.

  • The sailing culture is genuinely woven into daily life here, not a tourist add-on

    The bay has hosted serious yachting and game fishing for well over a century.

  • It's genuinely a warmer, more relaxed pace than much of the rest of the country

    Northland's subtropical climate makes the Bay of Islands pleasant to visit across most of the year.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Bay of Islands

The Bay of Islands' essential sights span its founding history, its historic townships, and its scattered islands offshore.

  1. 01

    Waitangi Treaty Grounds

    The genuine birthplace of modern New Zealand, where the Treaty of Waitangi was signed in 1840, with the Treaty House, a carved meeting house and the world's largest ceremonial war canoe on site.

    Tip A guided tour genuinely adds far more historical context than walking the grounds alone.

  2. 02

    Russell

    New Zealand's first capital and a genuinely rowdy 19th-century whaling port, now a beautifully preserved, quiet township reached by a short ferry from Paihia.

    Tip Climb Maiki (Flagstaff) Hill for a genuinely excellent view over the whole bay.

  3. 03

    The Hole in the Rock

    A genuinely striking natural arch worn through Motukokako Island by wind and sea, with calm-weather cruises passing directly through it.

    Tip Passage through the hole genuinely depends on swell conditions — it isn't guaranteed on every cruise.

  4. 04

    Kerikeri's Stone Store and Kemp House

    New Zealand's oldest stone building and oldest wooden building sit genuinely side by side here, remnants of one of the country's earliest mission stations.

    Tip The Kerikeri Basin walk genuinely links the historic buildings to a waterfall a short stroll away.

  5. 05

    Cape Brett Track and Lighthouse

    A genuinely demanding full-day or overnight track to a working lighthouse at the bay's outermost point, with dramatic coastal views throughout.

    Tip Most visitors genuinely see the cape by boat instead — the track is a serious hike, not a casual walk.

  6. 06

    Paihia

    The bay's genuine hub town, its waterfront lined with cruise operators, cafés and the short ferry crossing to Russell.

    Tip Most cruises and activities genuinely depart directly from the Paihia wharf.

  7. 07

    The bay's 144 islands

    A genuinely vast, sheltered scattering of islands and inlets, with Urupukapuka the largest, offering beaches and walking tracks reached only by boat.

    Tip A full-day cruise genuinely covers far more of the bay than a short harbour trip.

Things to do

Best things to do in Bay of Islands

The Bay of Islands genuinely centres on the water, with history-rich towns to explore between cruises.

  1. Cruise to the Hole in the Rock

    The genuinely essential Bay of Islands excursion, a half-day cruise out to Cape Brett and, conditions allowing, straight through the rock arch.

    Tip Sit outside on the upper deck for genuinely the best photos as the boat passes through.

  2. Sail the Bay of Islands

    A genuinely serious sailing destination for well over a century, with day charters and multi-day yacht trips widely available from Paihia and Opua.

    Tip A skippered half-day sail genuinely needs no prior sailing experience.

  3. Swim or cruise with dolphins

    Common dolphins are genuinely frequent visitors to the bay, with dedicated dolphin-watching and swim tours running from Paihia.

    Tip Swimming is genuinely weather- and dolphin-behaviour-dependent, so treat it as a bonus rather than a guarantee.

  4. Walk the Cape Brett Track

    A genuinely serious 16km one-way tramp to the lighthouse, usually done as an overnight trip with a stay in the DOC hut.

    Tip This is a genuinely strenuous full-day hike each way — fit trampers only.

  5. Explore the Waitangi Treaty Grounds

    A genuinely essential few hours spent on the site where New Zealand's founding document was signed, including a Maori cultural performance.

    Tip Time your visit around one of the scheduled cultural performances for genuinely the fullest experience.

  6. Wander historic Russell

    A genuinely easy, pleasant afternoon spent among New Zealand's oldest church, its first capital's colonial buildings, and a quiet waterfront.

    Tip The passenger ferry from Paihia genuinely runs regularly throughout the day.

Local food

What to eat in Bay of Islands

The Bay of Islands' food genuinely leans on fresh local seafood, alongside the standard New Zealand classics found countrywide.

  • Green-lipped mussels

    A genuinely distinctive native species, farmed in sheltered northern waters and served simply steamed at waterfront restaurants.

    Where to try it Waterfront restaurants in Paihia and Russell.

  • Fish and chips

    Genuinely best eaten on the Paihia waterfront, fresh off the day's catch and eaten looking out over the bay.

    Where to try it Waterfront takeaways in Paihia and Russell.

  • Pavlova

    A genuinely beloved national dessert, its crisp meringue shell and soft centre a fixture on café menus across the bay.

    Where to try it Cafés in Paihia and Kerikeri.

  • Kumara

    A genuinely important Maori and New Zealand staple, this native sweet potato appears roasted, mashed or as chips on menus throughout Northland.

    Where to try it Restaurants throughout Paihia, Russell and Kerikeri.

  • Manuka honey

    Northland's warm climate genuinely makes it prime manuka country, and local honey is sold direct from producers around Kerikeri.

    Where to try it Farm shops and gift stores around Kerikeri.

  • Flat white

    Genuinely essential before a day out on the water, and Paihia's waterfront cafés keep a steady, reliable standard.

    Where to try it Cafés along the Paihia waterfront.

Transport

Getting to and around Bay of Islands

The Bay of Islands sits a genuinely comfortable drive north of Auckland, with a small local airport for quicker access.

Getting there

  • Rental car from Auckland

    About 3h15 drive from Auckland, genuinely straightforward on the main state highway north.

    A worthwhile stop is Whangarei's Town Basin, roughly the halfway point.

  • Domestic flights

    Short flights connect Auckland to the Bay of Islands Airport at Kerikeri.

    A genuinely quick option if you'd rather skip the drive.

  • Bus

    InterCity coaches run from Auckland to Paihia.

    A workable, if slower, budget option for visitors without a car.

Getting around

Paihia and Russell are genuinely linked by a short, frequent passenger ferry, while a car helps reach Kerikeri and the wider region.

  • Paihia–Russell passenger ferry

    A genuinely quick, frequent crossing running throughout the day.

    Far simpler than driving around the harbour to reach Russell.

  • Rental car

    Genuinely useful for reaching Kerikeri and Northland's wider sights independently.

    Most key towns sit within a 30-minute drive of each other.

  • Cruises and organised tours

    Widely available from Paihia for the Hole in the Rock, dolphin watching and Cape Brett.

    The genuinely standard way most visitors see the bay's outer islands.

When to go

Best time to visit Bay of Islands

Northland's subtropical climate genuinely keeps the Bay of Islands pleasant across a wider window than much of the rest of the country.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    Warm, busy and genuinely the peak season for sailing and cruises.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Genuinely settled and warm, with noticeably thinner crowds than summer.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Mild by New Zealand standards, though genuinely wetter, with quieter towns and lower prices.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Increasingly warm and genuinely pleasant, ahead of the summer peak.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly — Northland's climate genuinely stays mild year-round, though summer weekends and school holidays bring the biggest crowds.

Travel tips

Bay of Islands travel tips

Practical notes on booking, ferries and timing that make a real difference to a Bay of Islands trip.

  • Book Hole in the Rock cruises ahead in summer — the best morning departures genuinely fill up.
  • Check whether your cruise actually passes through the arch — it's genuinely subject to swell conditions.
  • Catch the passenger ferry rather than driving to Russell — it's genuinely quicker and removes parking hassle.
  • Bring sun protection even on overcast days — Northland's subtropical sun is genuinely stronger than further south.
  • Book Cape Brett Hut well ahead if attempting the full track — it's genuinely popular in summer.
  • Combine the Bay of Islands with Auckland on a northern road trip rather than treating it as an isolated stop.

FAQ

Bay of Islands: frequently asked questions

How many days do you need in the Bay of Islands?

Three days covers Waitangi, a Hole in the Rock cruise and Russell comfortably. A day trip from Auckland is possible but genuinely rushed.

Is the Bay of Islands worth visiting for history?

Yes, genuinely — Waitangi is New Zealand's single most significant historic site, and Russell was the country's first capital.

Does the cruise always go through the Hole in the Rock?

Not guaranteed — passage genuinely depends on swell and tide conditions on the day, though most cruises attempt it.

Is Russell or Paihia the better base?

Paihia is genuinely more convenient for cruises and restaurants; Russell offers a quieter, more historic stay a short ferry ride away.

Can you swim with dolphins in the Bay of Islands?

Yes, on dedicated tours, though it's genuinely dependent on finding dolphins and their behaviour on the day — treat it as a possibility, not a guarantee.

Do you need a car in the Bay of Islands?

It genuinely helps for reaching Kerikeri and wider Northland, though Paihia and Russell themselves are easily covered on foot and by ferry.
