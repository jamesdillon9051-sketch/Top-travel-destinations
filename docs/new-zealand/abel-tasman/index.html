Welcome to Abel Tasman National Park

Abel Tasman is genuinely New Zealand's smallest national park, but its combination of golden-sand beaches, clear sheltered water and native coastal bush has made it one of the country's most popular — genuinely more accessible than the South Island's bigger, more remote wilderness areas.

Its defining feature is the Abel Tasman Coast Track, one of New Zealand's Great Walks, though the park is genuinely just as rewarding for a single day: a water taxi can drop you at a beach in the morning and pick you up further along the coast in the afternoon, no multi-day hike required.

Kaiteriteri and Marahau, the two small gateway towns, genuinely give the park a relaxed, beach-holiday feel that's distinct from the more dramatic mountain scenery found elsewhere on the South Island.

Why visit

Why visit Abel Tasman National Park?

Abel Tasman's case rests on genuinely making South Island wilderness easy — golden beaches and coastal bush without a demanding multi-day commitment.

  • It's genuinely the most accessible of New Zealand's Great Walks

    Water taxis let you sample any stretch of the Coast Track in a single day, no overnight gear required.

  • The beaches are genuinely golden-sand and unusually warm for the South Island

    Sheltered bays like Anchorage and Bark Bay stay warm enough to swim through much of the year.

  • Split Apple Rock is genuinely one of the South Island's most photographed natural curiosities

    A giant granite boulder split cleanly in two, sitting just offshore.

  • The kayaking is genuinely among the best in the country

    Calm, sheltered water and a dramatic coastline make it an easy, rewarding paddle for any skill level.

  • It's genuinely a short, easy add-on to a Nelson or Marlborough trip

    Close enough to Nelson Airport to combine easily with the rest of the northern South Island.

Main attractions

Top things to see in Abel Tasman National Park

Abel Tasman's essential sights span its golden bays, its coastal track, and a scattering of natural curiosities along the shore.

  1. 01

    Anchorage Bay

    A genuinely classic Abel Tasman beach, its wide golden sand and calm water a popular water-taxi drop-off and the first night's stop on the Coast Track.

    Tip Arrive on an early water taxi for genuinely the quietest hours before day-trip crowds build.

  2. 02

    Split Apple Rock

    A genuinely striking granite boulder sitting just offshore, split cleanly down the middle by centuries of frost and erosion.

    Tip It's genuinely visible from a short beach walk near Kaiteriteri, no boat required.

  3. 03

    The Abel Tasman Coast Track

    A genuinely gentle Great Walk by New Zealand standards, 60km of well-formed coastal trail linking golden beaches and native bush.

    Tip Walking just one section between water-taxi stops genuinely gives a full taste without committing to all three to five days.

  4. 04

    Cleopatra's Pool

    A genuinely fun natural rock pool fed by a stream, with a smooth rock chute that doubles as a natural water slide.

    Tip A short detour off the main track near Torrent Bay — genuinely worth the extra 20 minutes.

  5. 05

    Tonga Island Marine Reserve

    A genuinely protected marine reserve home to a resident New Zealand fur seal colony, easily seen by kayak or water taxi.

    Tip Seals are genuinely most active and visible on calm, sunny mornings.

  6. 06

    Kaiteriteri Beach

    The park's genuine gateway beach, a golden crescent with its own resort-town holiday atmosphere and the main water-taxi departure point.

    Tip It's genuinely worth a swim here even before or after your time in the park itself.

  7. 07

    Bark Bay

    A genuinely quieter beach further up the coast, its tidal lagoon and swing bridge a favourite lunch stop for kayakers and walkers alike.

    Tip Time your visit around the tide — the lagoon crossing is genuinely easier near low water.

Things to do

Best things to do in Abel Tasman National Park

Abel Tasman genuinely rewards getting onto the water and the track alike, with water taxis making both easy to combine.

  1. Walk a section of the Coast Track

    A genuinely easy, well-formed trail — even a single 2-3 hour stretch between water-taxi stops covers the park's best coastal scenery.

    Tip The Anchorage to Torrent Bay stretch is genuinely one of the most scenic and manageable short sections.

  2. Kayak the coastline

    Guided and freedom kayak trips genuinely let you paddle right up to Split Apple Rock and Tonga Island's seal colony.

    Tip A half-day guided trip genuinely needs no prior kayaking experience.

  3. Swim and slide at Cleopatra's Pool

    A genuinely refreshing detour off the main track, cool freshwater and a natural rock slide that's popular with kids and adults alike.

    Tip Water levels genuinely vary with recent rainfall — the slide works best after some rain upstream.

  4. Take a water taxi to a remote beach

    Genuinely the park's defining convenience — get dropped at one beach and walk or get picked up from another, no return leg required.

    Tip Book both legs of a drop-off and pick-up together — timing genuinely matters more than it seems.

  5. Spot fur seals at Tonga Island

    A genuinely reliable wildlife stop by kayak or boat cruise, with seals regularly hauled out on the rocks.

    Tip Keep a genuinely respectful distance — this is a protected marine reserve.

  6. Relax at Kaiteriteri Beach

    A genuinely easy, low-key way to bookend a day in the park, with a proper swimming beach and cafés right at the water taxi terminal.

    Tip Arrive early in summer — genuinely the car park fills up fast on warm days.

Local food

What to eat in Abel Tasman National Park

Abel Tasman's food options are genuinely simple and beach-town casual, centred on Kaiteriteri and Marahau rather than the park itself.

  • Green-lipped mussels

    The wider Nelson-Marlborough region is genuinely mussel-farming country, and they appear steamed and simply prepared on local menus.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Motueka and Nelson.

  • Whitebait fritters

    A genuine seasonal treat when local rivers are running, tiny native fish bound in a light egg batter.

    Where to try it Cafés in Motueka during the spring whitebait season.

  • Fish and chips

    A genuinely reliable, casual choice after a day on the track or the water, sold at both gateway towns.

    Where to try it Takeaways in Kaiteriteri and Marahau.

  • Hokey pokey ice cream

    A genuinely perfect match for a hot beach afternoon, vanilla ice cream studded with honeycomb toffee.

    Where to try it Kiosks at Kaiteriteri Beach.

  • Manuka honey

    The Tasman region's genuinely abundant native bush makes it good manuka country, sold direct from small producers nearby.

    Where to try it Farm shops around Motueka.

  • Flat white

    Genuinely essential before an early water taxi, and both gateway towns keep a steady café standard.

    Where to try it Cafés in Kaiteriteri and Marahau.

Accommodation

Where to stay in Abel Tasman National Park

Kaiteriteri and Marahau are genuinely the two main gateway bases, both right at the water-taxi departure points.

  • Kaiteriteri

    Genuinely the busier, more developed of the two gateway towns, with its own popular swimming beach.

    Best for: A beach-resort atmosphere with the widest range of accommodation

  • Marahau

    A genuinely quieter, smaller settlement right at the park boundary.

    Best for: The closest access to the start of the Coast Track

  • Motueka

    A genuinely practical small town with a wider choice of budget accommodation and services.

    Best for: A cheaper, more local base a short drive away

Transport

Getting to and around Abel Tasman National Park

Abel Tasman is genuinely reached via Nelson, with Kaiteriteri and Marahau both under an hour's drive from the airport.

Getting there

  • Rental car from Nelson

    About 50-60 minutes' drive from Nelson Airport to Kaiteriteri or Marahau.

    A genuinely straightforward, well-signposted drive.

  • Shuttle bus

    Scheduled shuttles connect Nelson to both gateway towns.

    A genuinely easy option for visitors without a rental car.

  • Rental car or bus from Picton

    About 2 hours' drive, useful if arriving via the Interislander ferry.

    A workable link for visitors road-tripping up from the North Island.

Getting around

Within the park itself, boats genuinely do most of the work, since there's no road access beyond the gateway towns.

  • Water taxi

    Genuinely the standard way to reach any beach or track section without walking the full length.

    Frequent scheduled services run from both Kaiteriteri and Marahau.

  • Walking

    The Coast Track itself is genuinely the only way to move between bays on land.

    Well-formed and gentle by Great Walk standards, suitable for most fitness levels.

  • Kayak

    A genuinely flexible, self-paced way to explore the coastline and reach Tonga Island.

    Both guided trips and independent rentals are widely available.

When to go

Best time to visit Abel Tasman National Park

Abel Tasman's genuinely sheltered position makes it one of the sunniest, driest parts of the South Island.

  • Summer (December–February)

    Best time

    Warm and genuinely reliable for swimming, though the busiest season on the track and beaches.

  • Autumn (March–May)

    Best time

    Genuinely settled weather with noticeably thinner crowds than summer.

  • Winter (June–August)

    Good

    Cooler and quieter, though genuinely still mild enough for walking without snow.

  • Spring (September–November)

    Good

    Increasingly warm, a genuinely good shoulder season before summer's crowds arrive.

Worth planning around: Nothing strongly, though genuinely book water taxis and Great Walk huts well ahead for the December–February peak.

Itinerary

Two days in Abel Tasman

Built around a full day exploring the coast by boat and track, with time on Kaiteriteri's beach either side.

  1. Day1

    Water taxi and Coast Track

    The essential Abel Tasman day.

    • Morning Water taxi to Anchorage Bay or Bark Bay.
    • Afternoon Walk a scenic section of the Coast Track back toward Marahau, with a stop at Cleopatra's Pool.
    • Evening Relax in Kaiteriteri or Marahau.
  2. Day2

    Kayaking and the coast

    A slower day on the water.

    • Morning A guided kayak trip past Split Apple Rock to Tonga Island.
    • Afternoon Swim and relax at Kaiteriteri Beach.
    • Evening Dinner in Kaiteriteri.

Travel tips

Abel Tasman National Park travel tips

Practical notes on booking, tides and timing that make a real difference to an Abel Tasman trip.

  • Book water taxis ahead in summer — the popular morning departures genuinely sell out.
  • Check tide times before planning a walk near Torrent Bay's lagoon crossing.
  • Bring plenty of sun protection — the sheltered coast is genuinely sunnier than much of the South Island.
  • Pack light, waterproof day gear even in summer — coastal weather can genuinely change quickly.
  • Book Great Walk huts and campsites well ahead if walking the full track in peak season.
  • Combine a water-taxi drop-off with a walk back rather than a return boat trip for genuinely better value.

FAQ

Abel Tasman National Park: frequently asked questions

Do you need to walk the full Coast Track to enjoy Abel Tasman?

No, genuinely not — water taxis let you sample any single section in a day, and this is how most visitors experience the park.

How many days do you need in Abel Tasman?

Two days genuinely covers a water taxi and walk combination plus kayaking comfortably. The full Coast Track takes three to five days.

Is Abel Tasman good for kids?

Yes, genuinely — the short walking sections, calm swimming beaches and Cleopatra's Pool's rock slide all suit families well.

Can you see Split Apple Rock without a boat?

Yes, genuinely — it's visible from a short beach walk near Kaiteriteri, though kayaking gets you closer.

Is Kaiteriteri or Marahau the better base?

Kaiteriteri genuinely has more of a resort-town feel and swimming beach; Marahau sits right at the Coast Track's start for an earlier departure.

Do you need a car in Abel Tasman?

Not within the park itself — water taxis and the track handle all movement — though a car genuinely helps reach Nelson and Motueka.
