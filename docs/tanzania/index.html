Why go to Tanzania

Tanzania sits on the coast of East Africa, between Kenya and Mozambique, and holds more than a quarter of its land in national parks and reserves. It is home to the Serengeti, whose annual wildebeest migration is one of the planet's great wildlife spectacles, to the Ngorongoro Crater, a collapsed volcano whose floor shelters thousands of animals, and to Mount Kilimanjaro, at 5,895 metres the highest mountain in Africa and the tallest free-standing mountain in the world.

The country is also a meeting point of cultures. The Swahili coast has traded with Arabia, India and Persia for a thousand years, and Zanzibar's Stone Town is one of the best-preserved Swahili trading towns. Inland, more than 120 ethnic groups include the Maasai, who still herd cattle across the northern plains. About 65 million people live here, most of them speaking Swahili, and the country has been stable and peaceful since independence in 1961.

Tanzania is a rewarding but expensive place to travel. Park fees, flights between parks and lodges add up, and a classic safari is a major budget. The reward is wild Africa on a vast scale: the northern circuit around Arusha is the best known, the southern and western parks are quieter, and Zanzibar and Mafia Island make an easy beach finish.

Experiences

Best things to do in Tanzania

The experiences that recur across the country and make a Tanzanian trip feel like one.

  1. Go on a classic safari

    Game drives in open-roofed vehicles are the core Tanzanian experience, with the Big Five and enormous herds across the northern circuit. See the Serengeti and Ngorongoro.

    Tip Book a driver-guide with a good reputation, and build in at least three nights in the Serengeti.

  2. Watch the wildebeest migration

    About 1.5 million wildebeest, with zebras and gazelles, follow the rains in a loop around the Serengeti-Mara ecosystem. Where they are depends on the month. See the Serengeti.

    Tip Ask your operator where the herds are when you book, and be flexible.

  3. Climb Kilimanjaro

    Hundreds of thousands of trekkers have walked to the roof of Africa. It needs no technical skill, but it does need time and acclimatisation. See Kilimanjaro.

    Tip Choose a route of seven days or more to improve your odds of reaching the summit.

  4. Walk Stone Town's alleys

    Carved doors, spice markets and a seafront night food market in a UNESCO-listed Swahili town. See Stone Town.

    Tip Go with a local guide, and eat at Forodhani Gardens at dusk.

  5. Relax on a Zanzibar beach

    White sand and warm, clear water make a classic end to a safari. See Zanzibar&#39;s beaches and Mafia Island.

    Tip Choose the north for swimming at any tide, and the east for kitesurfing.

  6. Take a boat or walking safari

    The southern parks allow what the north mostly does not: safaris by boat and on foot with a ranger. See Nyerere and Ruaha.

    Tip Fly in, since the roads are long.

  7. Meet the Maasai

    The Maasai herders live around the Serengeti and Ngorongoro, and community visits can offer insight, if done respectfully. See Ngorongoro.

    Tip Choose community-run visits, ask about fees, and ask before photographing people.

Food and drink

Tanzania foods to try

Tanzanian food is built on staples and grills, with Swahili spices on the coast and Indian influence in the cities. Meals are generous and cheap away from the lodges.

  • Ugali

    A stiff maize porridge eaten by hand with stews, greens or grilled meat, and the staple of the mainland.

    Where to try it Local restaurants everywhere, especially at lunch.

  • Urojo (Zanzibar mix)

    A tangy Zanzibari street soup of potato, fried fritters, cassava and chutneys, with lime and chilli.

    Where to try it Stalls in Stone Town.

  • Nyama choma and mishkaki

    Grilled meat, goat, beef or chicken, eaten with ugali, kachumbari salad and a cold beer, and meat skewers called mishkaki.

    Where to try it Roadside grills and bars across the country.

  • Mandazi

    Lightly sweet fried dough, flavoured with cardamom and coconut, eaten at breakfast with chai.

    Where to try it Street stalls and cafés.

  • Pilau and coastal curries

    Spiced rice with meat, and seafood in coconut sauce, a legacy of the Swahili coast's trade with India and Arabia.

    Where to try it Restaurants in Zanzibar and Dar es Salaam.

  • Chapati

    Layered flatbread, an Indian import, served with beans or stew, and the standard accompaniment at lunch stops.

    Where to try it Local eateries.

Before you go

Practical travel information for Tanzania

The essentials, current for 2026. Tanzania is welcoming and well organised for visitors, though costs, health preparation and visa rules need planning.

Capital
Dodoma (official capital); Dar es Salaam is the largest city
Language
Swahili and English are both official. English is widely spoken in tourism, and a few words of Swahili, such as 'jambo' and 'asante', are warmly received.
Currency
Tanzanian shilling (TZS). Roughly 2,300–2,700 shillings to the US dollar in recent years. US dollars are widely quoted for park fees, lodges and tours.
Cards and cash
Cards are accepted at lodges and many hotels, often with a surcharge. Park fees are generally paid by card or electronically in advance. Carry US dollars in recent, undamaged notes, and shillings for local purchases. ATMs are common in Arusha, Dar es Salaam and Stone Town.
Entry and visas
Most nationalities need a visa, which can be applied for online as an e-visa before travel; fees depend on nationality and rules change, so use the official immigration site. A yellow fever certificate is required if you arrive from a country with yellow fever risk. Zanzibar has its own immigration check on arrival.
Power sockets
Type D and G (three-pin), 230 V, 50 Hz. A UK plug often fits. Bring an adaptor.
Emergency number
112 for emergencies. Your safari operator or lodge will have its own emergency and flying-doctor arrangements, so check that your insurance covers evacuation.
Tipping
Tipping is expected for safari staff. Typical guidance is about US$15–25 per day for a driver-guide and US$5–10 for a cook or camp staff, per group, and a set scale for Kilimanjaro porters and guides. Round up in restaurants.
Safety
Tanzania is generally safe for visitors, but petty theft and scams happen in Arusha, Dar es Salaam and Stone Town, so avoid walking alone at night and keep valuables out of sight. Take malaria prophylaxis, use insect repellent, and drink only bottled or filtered water. Plastic bags are banned, so do not bring them. Do not wear camouflage clothing, and do not photograph government buildings, soldiers or airports. Drones require a permit.
Internet and SIM
Good in towns and along main roads, with Vodacom, Airtel and others selling cheap SIMs. Coverage is patchy in the parks and Kilimanjaro.
Time zone
East Africa Time, UTC+3 all year, with no daylight saving.

When to go

Best time to visit Tanzania

Tanzania's dry season, from late June to October, is the classic safari window. January and February suit the calving herds in the southern Serengeti. The long rains of March to May are the quietest and cheapest.

  • Dry season (June–October)

    Best time

    Clear skies and animals gathering at water. The migration reaches the northern Serengeti and the Mara River crossings from about July to October. Busiest and most expensive.

  • Short rains (November–December)

    Good

    Brief afternoon showers, green landscapes, migratory birds and fewer visitors. The herds return to the southern plains.

  • Calving season (January–February)

    Best time

    Hot, dry weather and hundreds of thousands of wildebeest calves in the southern Serengeti, with predators close behind. Also a good time for Zanzibar and for Kilimanjaro.

  • Long rains (March–May)

    Think twice

    Heavy rain, muddy roads and some lodges closed. Lowest prices and empty parks, but Kilimanjaro trails are wet and Zanzibar is humid.

Worth planning around: April and May, the peak of the long rains, unless you want the lowest prices and don't mind mud.

Trip length

How long to spend in Tanzania

A good safari needs time, because distances are long. A week covers the northern circuit, and two weeks allows a safari plus beach days on Zanzibar.

  • 6–7 days

    A northern-circuit safari: Tarangire, the Serengeti and Ngorongoro.

    The essentials, with little rest.

  • 10–12 days

    A northern safari plus three or four days on Zanzibar.

    The classic first trip.

  • 12–14 days

    The itinerary on this page, covering the north and Zanzibar at a comfortable pace.

    Our recommendation.

  • 3 weeks or more

    Adds Kilimanjaro or a southern-circuit safari to Nyerere and Ruaha.

    For climbers and repeat visitors.

Getting around

Transportation in Tanzania

Safaris use 4x4 vehicles with a driver-guide, and small planes link the parks. Roads between towns are improving but slow, and public transport is for budget travellers with time.

  • Safari vehicle with driver-guide

    The standard way to see the northern circuit, in a 4x4 with a pop-up roof, arranged by a safari operator. Prices include park transfers.

    Choose a reputable licensed operator.

  • Domestic flights

    Coastal Aviation, Auric Air and others run light aircraft between Arusha, the Serengeti, Dar es Salaam, Zanzibar and the southern parks.

    Baggage limits are strict, with soft bags only, about 15 kg.

  • Long-distance buses

    Cheap and frequent between towns such as Arusha, Moshi and Dar es Salaam, though slow and sometimes crowded.

    Travel in daylight.

  • Ferries to Zanzibar

    Fast ferries link Dar es Salaam and Zanzibar in about two hours, and the flight takes about 25 minutes.

    Book with a reputable company.

  • Dala-dalas and boda-bodas

    Minibuses and motorcycle taxis for short hops in towns. Cheap, though not always safe.

    Agree the fare first, and wear a helmet.

Culture

Culture and etiquette in Tanzania

Tanzanians are friendly, polite and tolerant, and the country is known for peaceful coexistence between its many ethnic groups and religions. Greetings matter, life moves at a relaxed pace, and modest dress is respected, especially on the Muslim coast and Zanzibar.

  • Greet people properly

    Always say 'hujambo' or 'habari' and ask how someone is before you ask for anything. Handshakes are common, and elders are shown respect.

  • Use your right hand

    Eat, give and receive with the right hand. The left is considered unclean.

  • Dress modestly

    Cover shoulders and knees in towns and villages, especially in Zanzibar and on the coast, and when visiting mosques.

  • Ask before taking photos

    Always ask permission to photograph people, including Maasai, and be prepared to pay if asked.

  • Respect Ramadan

    During Ramadan, many Muslims fast in daylight. Be discreet about eating and drinking in public, especially on Zanzibar.

  • Follow ranger guidance

    On safari, stay in your vehicle, keep quiet and do not feed or approach animals.

Do

  • Learn a few Swahili words: jambo (hello), asante (thank you), pole pole (slowly)
  • Carry small notes for tips
  • Book safaris with licensed, reputable operators
  • Bring a soft duffel bag for light aircraft
  • Take malaria prophylaxis and a yellow fever certificate if required
  • Pack neutral-coloured clothing for safari

Don't

  • Don't bring plastic bags into the country
  • Don't wear camouflage clothing
  • Don't photograph soldiers, police, airports or government buildings
  • Don't litter in the parks, or feed wildlife
  • Don't use a drone without a permit
  • Don't drink tap water

Travel tips

Tanzania travel tips

The things that most often catch visitors out in Tanzania, several of which are specific to how safaris and travel here work.

  • Book safaris well ahead for June to October and the Christmas period.
  • Ask your operator how many other vehicles typically gather at sightings, and choose smaller camps.
  • Pack a soft bag, not a hard suitcase, if you fly between parks, and keep to the weight limit.
  • Take malaria precautions and check whether you need a yellow fever certificate.
  • Bring recent US dollar notes, since older or damaged notes are often refused.
  • Allow at least three nights in the Serengeti, since drives are long.
  • Choose Kilimanjaro operators that treat porters fairly, and tip on the recommended scale.
  • Dress modestly in towns and in Zanzibar.

FAQ

Visiting Tanzania: frequently asked questions

Is Tanzania safe for tourists?

Generally yes. Safaris are well organised, but take care against petty theft and scams in cities, avoid night walks and follow your guide's instructions in the parks.

How many days do you need in Tanzania?

Ten to fourteen days for a first trip, covering a northern safari and Zanzibar.

When is the best time to visit Tanzania?

June to October for the dry-season safari, and January and February for the calving herds. The long rains from March to May are quiet and cheap.

Do I need a visa for Tanzania?

Most visitors do. An e-visa can be applied for online. Check the official site, since fees and rules change.

Is a Tanzanian safari expensive?

Yes. Park fees, lodges and vehicles add up. Camping safaris cost less, and the low season has lower prices.

Can I see the Great Migration all year?

The herds are always on the move around the Serengeti, but where they are depends on the month. Ask your operator.
